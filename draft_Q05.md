## STUDY MODE

### S1. How the parts connect [basic science]
Composition decides setting, and setting decides properties and handling. The powder is an ion-leachable glass, the liquid is a polymeric acid in water; mixing starts an acid-base reaction in which the acid frees calcium (then aluminium) ions that cross-link the polyacid chains [SRC:A27] [SRC:G12] [SRC:G13]. The powder:liquid ratio and the glass particle size then decide which clinical job the cement is suited to, which is the logic of classification.

### S2. Classification [classification]
The standard system is Wilson and McLean (used in Mount's work): Type I luting, Type II restorative (II.1 aesthetic, II.2 reinforced), Type III lining and base [SRC:A187] [SRC:A188] [SRC:A189] [SRC:A190]. Sikri also lists a classification by McLean (traditional, resin-modified, polyacid-modified composite), by application (adding Type IV fissure sealant, V orthodontic, VI core build-up) and a newer one (traditional, metal-modified, light-cure, hybrid) [SRC:G10]. Only the Wilson and McLean system is needed for full marks; the others add detail.

### S3. Composition and manufacture [basic science]
- **Powder:** calcium (or strontium) fluoroaluminosilicate glass, silica about 29%, alumina 16.6%, calcium fluoride about 34% in Mount and Hume's and Craig's tables [SRC:A29] [SRC:B104]; Sikri's table gives different ranges (silica 35-50%, alumina 20-30%) [SRC:G7]. Fluoride is a flux and an oxide scavenger in the melt [SRC:A38]. Strontium or lanthanum give radiopacity [SRC:A33].
- **Manufacture:** the ingredients are fused at about 1,100-1,500 C and the molten glass is plunged into water, which cracks it so it can be ground and sieved to 4-50 micrometres [SRC:G6] [SRC:B161] [SRC:A30]. Reactivity depends on fusion temperature and heat history [SRC:A32]. Restorative cements use coarser glass; luting and lining cements finer [SRC:A31].
- **Liquid:** a 40-55% aqueous solution of acrylic-itaconic or acrylic-maleic copolymer, with tartaric acid 5-15% [SRC:A40] [SRC:A42]. Itaconic acid keeps the liquid from gelling and lowers viscosity; tartaric acid sharpens the set and extends working time [SRC:G9].
- **Anhydrous (water-settable) cements:** the polyacid is vacuum-dried and mixed into the powder; the liquid is water or dilute tartaric acid [SRC:A43].

### S4. Properties [treatment relevance]
Working time is 2-3 minutes, thermal expansion is close to dentine, solubility is high in the first 24 hours, and pH starts near 2 and rises to 6-7 [SRC:G17]. Adhesion is chemical, fluoride is released and recharged, and the cement is brittle and moisture-sensitive (Q4, Q16, Q10 give detail) [SRC:G19] [SRC:G20]. Biocompatibility is acceptable because the large polyacid molecules do not travel down dentinal tubules [SRC:G18].

### S5. Manipulation [treatment]
Isolate, prepare, condition, dispense, mix, place under a matrix, protect, finish after 24 hours [SRC:G41] [SRC:G43] [SRC:G44] [SRC:G45] [SRC:G46] [SRC:G47] [SRC:G48]. The aim of mixing is only to wet the powder particles; a plastic spatula and a cooled slab help [SRC:G45].

### S6. Cavity preparation [treatment]
GIC needs minimal preparation because it bonds: remove caries, keep sound tooth, and do not break proximal contacts [SRC:G42]. Preparation follows the lesion, and the outline is dictated by caries, discolouration and aesthetics [SRC:G42]. For lesions in low-load sites, GIC is the first choice, and a stronger auto-cure cement is used if the load is heavier [SRC:A336].

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 34 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Glass ionomer cement (glass polyalkenoate cement) is a water-based cement formed by acid-base reaction between ion-leachable fluoroaluminosilicate glass powder and an aqueous polyalkenoic acid [SRC:A27] [SRC:A28]. It was introduced by Wilson and Kent in the early 1970s [SRC:G3].

### 2. Classification
**A. Wilson and McLean (Mount and Hume's version):**
| Type | Use | Powder:liquid ratio | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | luting crowns, bridges, inlays, orthodontic appliances | about 1.5:1 | fast set, thin film [SRC:A187] |
| II.1 | aesthetic restorative (auto or dual cure) | 2.8:1 to 6.8:1 | translucent, not for heavy load [SRC:A188] |
| II.2 | reinforced restorative | 3:1 to 4:1 | fast set, immediate trimming, radiopaque [SRC:A189] |
| III | lining and base | lining 1.5:1; base 3:1 or more | thermal barrier, dentine substitute [SRC:A190] |
**B. By McLean:** traditional GIC, resin-modified GIC, polyacid-modified composite (compomer) [SRC:G10].
**C. By application:** adds Type IV fissure sealant, Type V orthodontic cement, Type VI core build-up [SRC:G10].
**D. By modification:** traditional, metal-modified, light-cure, hybrid [SRC:G10].

### 3. Composition
- **Powder:** silica, alumina, calcium fluoride, sodium fluoride, aluminium fluoride, aluminium phosphate; strontium or lanthanum for radiopacity [SRC:G7] [SRC:A33].
- **Liquid:** 40-55% copolymer of polyacrylic and itaconic (or maleic) acid, tartaric acid 5-15%, water [SRC:A40] [SRC:A42].
- **Function of each:** silica gives translucency and hardness, alumina forms the aluminium polyacrylate matrix, fluoride lowers fusion temperature and releases to tooth, itaconic acid prevents gelling, tartaric acid sharpens the set [SRC:B166] [SRC:A38] [SRC:G9].

### 3A. Powder constituents and what each does (Bhat)
| Constituent | Approximate weight | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Silica | 29% | hardness and translucency; does not react [SRC:B166] |
| Alumina | 16.5% | forms the aluminium polyacrylate matrix [SRC:B166] |
| Calcium fluoride | 34.3% | flux; fluoride release [SRC:B166] |
| Aluminium fluoride | 7.3% | flux, anticariogenic [SRC:B166] |
| Sodium fluoride | 3% | flux [SRC:B166] |
| Aluminium phosphate | 9.9% | controls setting time [SRC:B166] |

### 4. Manufacture
Fuse the ingredients at about 1,100-1,500 C, plunge the melt into water (shock cooling), grind and sieve to 4-50 micrometres; restorative powders about 50 micrometres, luting about 20 micrometres [SRC:G6] [SRC:A30].

### 5. Properties
- **Chemical:** adhesion by ion exchange; fluoride release and recharge [SRC:G21] [SRC:G23].
- **Physical:** working time 2-3 minutes; thermal expansion close to dentine; opaque (about 90%) [SRC:G17] [SRC:G20].
- **Mechanical:** compressive strength 16,000-22,000 psi; tensile 900-960 psi; hardness 48 KHN; brittle [SRC:G20].
- **Biological:** initial pH about 2 rising to 6-7; mild pulp irritant; protect very deep dentine with calcium hydroxide [SRC:G17] [SRC:G18].
- **Weaknesses:** moisture sensitive, high early solubility, poor aesthetics [SRC:G14].

### 5A. Setting in brief and advantages and disadvantages
- **Setting:** acid attacks the glass; calcium ions released first form calcium polysalts (initial set); aluminium ions follow and form a tighter network over 24 hours; full reaction can take up to 7 days [SRC:G12] [SRC:G13].
- **Advantages:** chemical adhesion, fluoride release, biocompatibility, low solubility after the full set, easy manipulation [SRC:G14].
- **Disadvantages:** opaque surface, surface cracks, debonding when poorly manipulated, technique sensitivity, high early solubility, poor strength, unsuitable for large lesions [SRC:G14].

### 6. Manipulation
1. Isolate with rubber dam or cord [SRC:G41].
2. Condition with 10% polyacrylic acid for 10 seconds (Mount) or 10-20 seconds (Sikri); wash; dry lightly [SRC:A120] [SRC:G43].
3. Dispense: fluff the powder, level the scoop, dispense liquid without bubbles [SRC:G44].
4. Mix: plastic spatula, two halves, first half wet in 10-15 seconds, finish in about 30 seconds [SRC:G45].
5. Slump test: cement should break and slump back; working time 60-90 seconds [SRC:G45].
6. Place under a matrix, avoiding voids [SRC:G46].
7. Seal at once with resin bonding agent; varnish washes away [SRC:G48].
8. Finish after 24 hours with Sof-lex discs in moist conditions [SRC:G47].

### 7. Uses
Luting; Class III and V restorations; root caries; deciduous teeth; liner and base; sandwich technique; ART and transitional restorations; core build-up in limited cases; fissure sealant; orthodontic bands [SRC:G11].

### 8. Cavity preparation
- **Class III:** access through the lingual marginal ridge with a small inverted-cone bur; preserve labial enamel [SRC:G42].
- **Class V:** outline by caries, discolouration and aesthetics; retention grooves on occlusal and gingival walls if the lesion is wide [SRC:G42].
- **Proximal lesions:** do not break the contact; keep unsupported enamel; gingival extent set by the lesion, controlling sulcular fluid [SRC:G42].
- **Occlusal lesions:** minimal cavity; GIC is the best initial restoration, laminated with composite or amalgam if the load is heavy [SRC:A336].
- **Surface treatment:** pumice slurry, then conditioning [SRC:A101].

### 9. Conclusion
Classification follows use (luting, restorative, lining), composition is glass plus polyacid, and success depends on powder:liquid ratio, moisture control and surface protection.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Types: I luting (1.5:1), II.1 aesthetic, II.2 reinforced (3-4:1), III lining and base [SRC:A187] [SRC:A190].
- Powder: calcium fluoroaluminosilicate glass; liquid: 40-55% polyacrylic-itaconic copolymer with tartaric acid 5-15% [SRC:A40] [SRC:A42].
- Fusion about 1,100-1,500 C, shock cooled, ground to 4-50 micrometres [SRC:G6].
- Condition 10 s with 10% polyacrylic acid; never acid-etch; seal at once; finish at 24 h [SRC:A121] [SRC:G48].
- Minimal cavity: keep contacts, no undercuts needed [SRC:G42].
