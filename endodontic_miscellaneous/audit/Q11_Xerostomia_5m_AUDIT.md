## Evidence and uncertainty notes

- **[RECALL] / unverified:** none; all statements trace to a located phrase or are reasoning stated as such.
- **Interpretation / synthesis:** see Conflicts and Note below.
- **Conflicting sources:** Conflict or difference: Pickard's says an adverse oral environment can affect the set and physical properties of some materials, e.g. GICs in xerostomia, whereas Summitt's reports glass-ionomer restorations in the same xerostomic patient did not develop recurrent caries over the study period (locator q11_sum_gi). The 200-word answer states only the Pickard point under 'Fluoride; GIC setting may be affected'; the Summitt contrast was cut for length. The caries-risk 'low flow rate' threshold differs: Summitt's gives less than 0.1 to 0.2 mL/min (some authorities) and elsewhere less than 0.2 and 0.1 mL/min; Kidd and Fejerskov give normal values only (0.3-0.5 unstimulated, 1-2 stimulated). Endodontic relevance of xerostomia is stated only where Cohen (Lin and Huang) and Torabinejad (Aminoshariaei and Fouad) say it; management of endodontic care in xerostomic patients beyond caries control is not given by the books consulted. Saliva substitutes: Summitt's cautions that some commercial products can demineralise dentine; Bonsor and Pearson list moisturising rinses, gels, toothpastes and sugar-free gum.
