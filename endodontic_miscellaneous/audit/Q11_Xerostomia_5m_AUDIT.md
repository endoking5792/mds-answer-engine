## Evidence and uncertainty notes

- **[RECALL] / unverified:** none; all statements trace to a located phrase or are reasoning stated as such.
- **Interpretation / synthesis:** see Conflicts and Note below.
- **Conflicting sources:** Conflict or difference: Pickard's says an adverse oral environment can affect the set and physical properties of some materials, e.g. GICs in xerostomia, whereas Summitt's reports glass-ionomer restorations in the same xerostomic patient did not develop recurrent caries over the study period (locator q11_sum_gi). - **Conflicts shown, not ranked:** (1) Definition: Kidd and Fejerskov treat xerostomia as the symptom and hyposalivation as the measured flow, whereas Mount et al and Ingle ch.37 use xerostomia for reduced function or dry mouth, and Ingle notes that in one-third of cases there is no real flow reduction (u4_ing37_third). (2) Resting flow: Kidd and Fejerskov 0.3 to 0.5 ml/min; Mount et al 0.3 to 0.4 mL/min (u4_mnt_norm).
