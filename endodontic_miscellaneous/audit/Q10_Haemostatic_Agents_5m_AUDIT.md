## Evidence and uncertainty notes

- **[RECALL] / unverified:** none; all statements trace to a located phrase or are reasoning stated as such.
- **Interpretation / synthesis:** see Conflicts and Note below.
- **Conflicting sources:** Conflicts: Cohen Ch.11 and Mahmoud say bone wax is no longer recommended; Jain says it should not be used; Harty lists it as an item to avoid. Ferric sulfate: Cohen Ch.11 reports good healing if curetted and irrigated, and Jain calls Cut-Trol caustic and to be avoided despite good haemostasis; not shown in the 200-word answer. Surgicel: Cohen says it is primarily a physical barrier and retards healing; Ingle says it caused the most intense inflammatory reaction in an animal study. Gutmann's perforation advice (small amounts of epinephrine or ferric sulfate, avoid strong agents) is for access perforations and is a different setting from root-end surgery. The pulp-exposure line (NaOCl 1.5 to 5.25%) is Cohen Ch.24's statement of current regard, not an endodontic-surgery recommendation. The 'perforation, root-end surgery, bleeding canal' use list in the brief: bleeding canal was not given its own line because no book located gave a haemostatic-agent protocol for canal bleeding beyond sodium hypochlorite irrigation and calcium hydroxide dressing.

## Second-reader audit U4 (see audit/AUDIT_u4.md)

- **Corrected:** table cautery cell now says damage rises with heat and duration (Cohen: 'proportional to both temperature and the duration'; the book adds that cautery in the crypt itself has not been studied, u4_coh_cautns). - **Added (answer):** ferric sulfate dark reddish-brown stain hampers visualisation (Jain et al, u4_jain_fscol).
- **Conflicts shown, not ranked:** ferric sulfate: Cohen Ch.11 near-normal healing if the wound is curetted and irrigated (u4_coh_fscur); Mahmoud generally favourable if completely removed; Jain et al call Cut-Trol caustic to bone and 'should be avoided' despite good haemostasis (u4_jain_cut) and give 15.5% to 20% (u4_jain_fsconc). Racellet size: Cohen and Ingle say #3 (0.55 mg), Mahmoud says Racellet #2, Gutmann lists #2 as 1.15 mg and #3 as 0.55 mg (u4_mah_r2, u4_gut_p); the answer uses the #3 figure as Cohen gives it. Calcium sulfate: Mahmoud reports it superior to gauze and ferric sulfate in one small clinical study (u4_mah_cs), Cohen only that healing proceeds normally. Epinephrine strength: Mahmoud cites a small periodontal flap study with twice the blood loss using 1:100,000 compared with 1:50,000 (u4_mah_150); not shown in the answer. Cardiovascular concern with epinephrine pellets: Ingle says marked changes may follow direct application to bone but concludes the systemic effect of pellets is minimal (u4_ing_cv), Cohen finds no change; Ingle reports Surgicel gave the most intense inflammatory reaction in an animal study (u4_ing_surg).
- **Corroboration:** Torabinejad 6e Ch.20 lists ferric sulfate, aluminium chloride, collagen, haemostatic gauze, racemic epinephrine and electrocautery and tells the surgeon to weigh each one's effect on healing (u4_tor20_list); Sikri 2e Ch.32 lists general methods (pressure, packing, haemostat), not agent-specific data.
- **Slips:** none found; no number in the table is unsupported.
- **Open point:** the 'Uses' lines mix three settings (surgery, access perforation, vital pulp therapy); each is cited to its own book, and NaOCl is the pulp-exposure agent only (Cohen Ch.24).
