## Evidence and uncertainty notes

- **[RECALL] / unverified:** none stated as fact without a locator. The Conclusion sentence and the table headings are synthesis. The WHO row (initial hyperaemia, acute, chronic ulcerative or hyperplastic; necrosis; degeneration; secondary dentine) is Sikri's account of the WHO scheme; Sikri's own list for Grossman's older scheme also includes hyperaemia and atrophic degeneration, which the current Grossman 14e chapter omits (not shown in the answer for length).
- **Interpretation / synthesis:** 'pulpotomy an emerging alternative' summarises Cohen 24 (a 'paradigm shift', CSC pulpotomy in adult teeth with irreversible pulpitis) and Ingle 27 (favourable outcomes; CEM trial). The management table sets the three positions side by side without ranking; Torabinejad 6e (Walton, Ruparel) treats pulpotomy or partial pulpectomy as emergency options when time is short, Grossman 14e as an emergency procedure, Cohen 24 as a definitive alternative.
- **Conflicting sources:** (1) Percussion in irreversible pulpitis: Grossman 'negative'; Sikri 'can be tender', indicating periapical spread or a crack; Cohen 1 a thickened ligament in advanced disease. Shown in the answer. (2) Partial pulpectomy: Torabinejad 6e discourages it for severely inflamed teeth, whereas Grossman 14e simply lists pulpectomy. (3) Classification schemes differ (AAE clinical, Grossman, WHO, Abbott). (4) Intrapulpal pressure: Grossman and Sikri say increased pressure causes necrosis (Sikri: local only); Ingle 5 shows pressure stays normal 2 mm away; not included in the answer.
- **Numerical claims:** 10-16% false sensibility tests (Cohen 24, q02_coh24_false); over 90% pain reduction after pulpotomy or partial pulpectomy (Torabinejad 6e Ch.9); about 40% silent progression to necrosis (Torabinejad 6e Ch.1).
- **Literature evidence:** none opened; the CEM trial is known only through Ingle 27 and Cohen 24.
