# Hertwigs epithelial root sheath.

**Marks:** 5 · Short note

**Origin.** After crown completion, the cervical loop, where inner and outer enamel epithelia unite, proliferates as the **Hertwig's epithelial root sheath**, a two-layered epithelial wall (Gopikrishna, 2021; Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).

**Formation.** It **maps out the shape of the root(s)**: a simple tube for one root, more complex for several (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Its tip turns inward as the **epithelial diaphragm**, delineating the apical foramen or foramina and, in multirooted teeth, guiding furca formation (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Function.** Inner enamel epithelium below the future cementoenamel junction induces papilla cells to become **odontoblasts** (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Fate.** As dentine forms, the basement membrane disintegrates, follicle cells become **cementoblasts**, and leftover epithelial cells persist as **cell rests of Malassez** (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Clinical significance.**
- Rests may proliferate into **periradicular cysts** under chronic inflammation; a sheath defect leaves a gap forming an **accessory canal** (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Taurodontism:** the diaphragm fails to invaginate at the proper level (Versiani, Cleghorn and Christie, 2021).
- Trauma to the sheath causes **partial arrest of root formation** (Andreasen, Andreasen and Andersson, 2007).
- Its cells resist destruction in inflammation, supporting root development in an **immature apex** (Gopikrishna, 2021).
