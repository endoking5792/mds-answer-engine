# Considerations for medically compromised patients requiring endodontic treatment.

**Marks:** 25 · Essay

## Introduction

About 25-30% of dental patients report a medical condition of potential relevance (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). Systemic disease may accelerate pulpal pathosis or alter treatment outcome (Aminoshariae and Fouad, 2021). The aim is safe treatment: identify the risk, then modify the procedure.

## General principles

- **History and interview.** History-taking is active: "a good history is taken, not given" (Mustard and Gulabivala, 2014); use a questionnaire supplemented by an interview, covering medications, allergies, previous dental problems and compliance (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). Allergy is the commonest positive finding; hypersensitivity to gutta percha, amide anaesthetics, hypochlorite and nickel has been reported (Aminoshariae and Fouad, 2021). Ginkgo, garlic, ginger, ginseng, feverfew and vitamin E inhibit platelet aggregation (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). Review cardiovascular, endocrine, respiratory, hepatic and renal systems (Aminoshariae and Fouad, 2021). Severe apprehension may need premedication or sedation (Aminoshariae and Fouad, 2021).
- **Examination.** Record blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate and temperature (Johnson and Epstein, 2019); INR for warfarin, HbA1c for diabetes (Aminoshariae and Fouad, 2021).
- **Risk assessment.** ASA II is well-controlled disease; ASA III or above almost always needs medical consultation (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). ASA is only a guide: gather more from patient and physician (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021). The Multi-Dimension Risk Assessment Model scores 1 to 4 for physical status, procedural stress and anxiety; uncomplicated nonsurgical root canal treatment scores 2, extractions or surgical root canal 3; a total of 10-12 means consultation strongly advised and possible hospital treatment (Johnson and Epstein, 2019).
- **Consultation.** Be specific, document it; responsibility for safe treatment rests with the dentist (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). Some patients should be treated in hospital (Wynne, 2025).
- **Stress reduction.** Morning, short appointments; anxiolytic premedication or nitrous oxide (Johnson and Epstein, 2019); adequate pain control is essential (Johnson and Epstein, 2019).
- **Local anaesthesia.** Up to 0.036 mg epinephrine (about two cartridges of 1:100,000) is accepted in cardiovascular disease (Johnson and Epstein, 2019; Rosenberg and Malek, 2021). Avoid or use extreme caution after infarction within a month, stroke within 6 months, or bypass within 3 months (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). For intraosseous injection use 3% mepivacaine without vasoconstrictor (Johnson and Epstein, 2019).
- **Antibiotics.** Prophylaxis only for the highest-risk endocarditis groups (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021); none for prosthetic joints (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). In abscess, measure temperature (Aminoshariae and Fouad, 2021).
- **Infection control and emergencies.** Standard precautions, as for any patient, including those with HIV (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021). Angina, hypoglycaemia, anaesthetic reactions and seizures are the commonest office emergencies (Johnson and Epstein, 2019).

## Condition-specific modifications

| Condition | Modification |
|---|---|
| **Hypertension** | SBP below 160 and DBP below 100 tolerates routine care; above 180/110 only emergency pain or infection care after consultation (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Ischaemic heart disease** | Morning, short visits, anxiolytic, limited vasoconstrictor; patient brings anti-anginal drug; orofacial pain is the sole symptom of infarction in about 6% (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Heart failure** | Upright chair; if uncompensated, consult and avoid vasoconstrictor (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Valvular disease** | Prophylaxis for prosthetic valve, previous endocarditis or select congenital disease (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021). |
| **Pacemaker** | Electronic apex locator and pulp tester should be safe (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Diabetes** | Avoid overlap with meals; hypoglycaemia: 15 g oral carbohydrate, or 1 mg glucagon injection; consider antibiotics for surgery in poor control (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Anticoagulants** | Check INR is in range (warfarin 2.0-3.5), especially for nerve block; low-dose aspirin lengthens bleeding time (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Bleeding disorders** | Haematologist consultation; inferior alveolar block only after factor cover (Johnson and Epstein, 2019; Mustard and Gulabivala, 2014). |
| **HIV** | Healing equals uninfected patients; modify only if platelets below 50,000 or neutrophils below 1,000; no routine antibiotics (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Cancer therapy** | Treat symptomatic nonvital teeth one week before; proceed if neutrophils exceed 1,000 and platelets 50,000 (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Radiotherapy** | Fibrosis limits mouth opening; dry mouth causes caries (Aminoshariae and Fouad, 2021). |
| **Antiresorptives** | Surgery risks necrosis; prefer nonsurgical endodontics to extraction (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Transplant** | Eradicate dental disease, including endodontically, beforehand (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Renal disease** | Treat the day after dialysis; adjust antibiotic dose (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Liver disease** | Blood count, PT or INR beforehand (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Corticosteroids** | Nonsurgical treatment generally needs no supplement; surgery about 25 mg hydrocortisone equivalent (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Asthma** | Bring inhaler; nitrous oxide contraindicated in severe asthma (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Epilepsy** | If seizure: stop, remove instruments; many antibiotics are contraindicated with seizure drugs (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Sickle cell** | Treat asymptomatic necrotic teeth early (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Pregnancy** | Pain or infection: treat in any trimester; elective care in second (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |

## Where books differ

| Issue | Books |
|---|---|
| Elective care after infarction | Under 1 month (Johnson and Epstein, 2019); 30 days (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021); at least 6 months, some 1 year (Mustard and Gulabivala, 2014). |
| Pacemakers | Locator and pulp tester should be safe (Johnson and Epstein, 2019); devices sensitive to electromagnetic signals (Wynne, 2025). |
| Gutta-percha allergy | Reports exist (Aminoshariae and Fouad, 2021); no proven cases (Mustard and Gulabivala, 2014). |
| Neutrophils for cancer patients | Above 1,000 (Johnson and Epstein, 2019); above 2000 (Wynne, 2025). |
| HIV antibiotics | Routine use contraindicated (Johnson and Epstein, 2019); cover when granulocytes exceed 500 (Wynne, 2025). |

## Conclusion

History, risk grading, consultation and stress reduction come first; the condition then dictates anaesthetic, drug, bleeding and timing changes.
