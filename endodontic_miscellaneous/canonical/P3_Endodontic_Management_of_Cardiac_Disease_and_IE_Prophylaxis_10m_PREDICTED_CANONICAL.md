# Endodontic management of the patient with cardiac disease, infective endocarditis prophylaxis

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay (predicted)

**Answer blueprint:** Assessment → General modifications → Condition table → Endocarditis prophylaxis (at-risk groups, regimen, endodontic procedures, NICE) → Conclusion

## Assessment and general modifications

Physician consultation is mandatory before treatment (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021); measure blood pressure when an invasive procedure is planned (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). Use morning, short appointments with anxiolytic or nitrous oxide cover (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). Epinephrine: 0.036 mg, about two cartridges of 1:100,000 (Johnson and Epstein, 2019); Wynne (2025) gives 0.04 mg. Avoid it, or use extreme caution, after infarction within a month, stroke within 6 months or bypass within 3 months (Johnson and Epstein, 2019).

| Condition | Management |
|---|---|
| **Hypertension** | Above 180/110: emergency care only (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Angina, infarction** | Unstable angina: no elective treatment (Mustard and Gulabivala, 2014). Elective care after infarction waits 30 days (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021) or at least 6 months (Mustard and Gulabivala, 2014). |
| **Heart failure** | Upright chair; if uncompensated avoid vasoconstrictor (Johnson and Epstein, 2019). |
| **Pacemaker** | Locator and pulp tester should be safe (Johnson and Epstein, 2019); devices are sensitive to electromagnetic signals (Wynne, 2025). |
| **Warfarin** | INR 4.0 or less and stable, excluding inferior alveolar block (Escudier, 2017); INR 3.5 or less (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021). |

## Infective endocarditis prophylaxis

- **At risk:** prosthetic valve, previous endocarditis, select congenital disease (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021): unrepaired cyanotic disease, or repair with prosthetic material in the first 6 months (Mustard and Gulabivala, 2014). Not mitral valve prolapse (Johnson and Epstein, 2019).
- **Regimen**, single dose 30-60 minutes before (Wynne, 2025): adults amoxicillin 2.0 g, children 50 mg/kg; penicillin-allergic adults cephalexin 2 g, clindamycin 600 mg or azithromycin 500 mg. If missed, up to 2 hours after (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021).
- **Endodontic procedures:**

| Book | View |
|---|---|
| Johnson and Epstein (2019) | Instrumentation within the canal carries no significant risk; beyond the apex, intraligamentary or intraosseous injection and surgery do. |
| Wynne (2025) | Nonsurgical root canal treatment manipulates the periapex: at risk. |

- **NICE:** prophylaxis not required (Mustard and Gulabivala, 2014); chlorhexidine not offered (Escudier, 2017), though Wynne (2025) finds 0.2% chlorhexidine rinse reduces bacteraemia.

## Conclusion

Grade the cardiac risk, limit stress and epinephrine, and confine prophylaxis to the highest-risk groups.
