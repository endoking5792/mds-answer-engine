# Discuss Endo-Ortho relationship.

**Marks:** 20 · Long essay

**Answer blueprint:** Introduction → Effect on the pulp → Root resorption → Endodontic treatment before and during orthodontics → Root-filled teeth → Traumatized teeth → Pulp testing and monitoring → Forced eruption → Conclusion

## Introduction
Orthodontic movement inevitably causes biological reactions in periodontal and pulpal tissues, so the specialties form an **endodontic-orthodontic continuum** (Sikri, 2019). It is inseparable when orthodontics needs endodontic intervention (resorption, loss of vitality), when endodontics needs orthodontics (crown loss, forced eruption), and when endodontic treatment must halt resorption before movement (Sikri, 2019).

## Effect of orthodontic movement on the pulp
- Forces above capillary pressure cause sterile necrosis in the periodontal ligament (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014).
- Respiration of pulp cells falls by a mean 27.4%, more in older patients; initial hyperaemia follows (Sikri, 2019).
- Overall, neurovascular disturbance gives inflammatory then degenerative change, particularly with mature apices; immature roots are less affected (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014).
- Clinical effects are usually minimal: altered responses, pulpitis, necrosis (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014). Weine and Potashnick (2004) record canines moved into position later showing periapical radiolucency or canal narrowing, and cite orthodontists' rotations of 720 and even 1080 degrees without damage (Sikri prints 72, apparently a slip).

## Root resorption
- Types: surface, inflammatory, rarely replacement (Sikri, 2019).
- Frequency: up to 1 to 2 mm in the majority; about 15% lose more than 2.5 mm; apical resorption is four times commoner than lateral (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014).
- Risk: blunt or pipette roots (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014); adults (Sikri, 2019).
- Mechanism: pulpal neuropeptides raise blood supply and osteoclast precursors (Sikri, 2019). Orthodontic treatment is the most frequent predisposing factor for cervical resorption; CBCT shows resorption more accurately than radiographs (Sikri, 2019).
- Treating afterwards: blunted apices cause overfilling, so shorten the working length (Weine and Potashnick, 2004; Sikri, 2019).
- Resorption present before movement: halt it first with calcium hydroxide (Weine and Potashnick, 2004); pulp extirpation may arrest progressive resorption (Gutmann and Manjarres, 2019).

## Endodontic treatment before and during orthodontics
- Teeth needing endodontic therapy are best treated before movement (Weine and Potashnick, 2004).
- During treatment, remove wires for rubber dam where possible (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014); lingual brackets may shift the access (Sikri, 2019).
- Options: complete debridement, delay obturation under calcium hydroxide (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014); Weine and Potashnick (2004) see no contraindication to completing treatment. Ingle's chapter resumes movement after 10 days to 2 weeks (Gutmann and Manjarres, 2019).
- Timing: forces 2 to 4 weeks after endodontic treatment; three to six months after surgery (Sikri, 2019). Orthodontics is not a cause of endodontic failure (Sikri, 2019).

## Root-filled teeth
| Issue | Statement |
|---|---|
| Movement | Root-treated teeth move the same distances (Gutmann and Manjarres, 2019); an overfill can cause focal ankylosis (Sikri, 2019) |
| Resorption | Studies: most (n = 4) no difference; some (n = 2) greater in root-treated teeth (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014); less in root-treated teeth (Gutmann and Manjarres, 2019); Sikri (2019) gives both views |

Evidence conflicts and more research is needed (Gutmann and Manjarres, 2019).

## Traumatized teeth
- Teeth with a clinically unrecognised compromised pulp are more likely to become necrotic with movement (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014).
- Repaired root fractures can be moved (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014); ankylosed teeth only if the area is small.
- Intruded mature teeth: keep calcium hydroxide until movement ends (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014).

## Pulp testing and monitoring
- Test every tooth beforehand by electric and thermal tests (Gutmann and Manjarres, 2019); a symptom-free tooth may not have a healthy pulp.
- Bands and archwires make electric testing impossible (Weine and Potashnick, 2004; Sikri, 2019); cold testing is more reliable after movement (Gutmann and Manjarres, 2019).
- Review moved teeth yearly (Weine and Potashnick, 2004).

## Forced eruption
Indications: crown loss from caries, resorption near the crest, infrabony defects (Sikri, 2019).

| Principle | Statement |
|---|---|
| Advantages | No bone cutting, no resorption, defects eliminated without surgery (Sikri, 2019); better crown-root ratio than surgery alone (Weine and Potashnick, 2004) |
| Endodontics | Complete first; gutta-percha, never silver points (Weine and Potashnick, 2004); obturation may wait (Sikri, 2019) |
| Biological width | A further 1 or 2 mm sound structure above the attachment (Weine and Potashnick, 2004); crest to tooth minimum 3.5 mm (Sikri, 2019) |
| Periodontium | Keep healthy; movement into an infrabony pocket is inadvisable (Weine and Potashnick, 2004) |
| Teeth | Posterior teeth not recommended (Sikri, 2019) |
| Technique | Wire loop cemented in the canal if only the root remains (Gulabivala and Naini, 2014); retention 8 to 12 weeks (Sikri, 2019) |

## Conclusion
Orthodontic forces affect pulp and root, yet well-planned endodontic care permits safe movement; assessment, monitoring and consent are essential.
