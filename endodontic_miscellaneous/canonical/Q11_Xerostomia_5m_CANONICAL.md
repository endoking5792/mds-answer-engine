# Xerostomia.

**Marks:** 5 · Short note

**Definition.** **Xerostomia** is the patient's awareness of dry mouth; some with reduced flow (**hyposalivation**) do not realise it (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). Normal flow is 0.3 to 0.5 ml/min unstimulated and 1 to 2 ml/min stimulated (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016); some authorities set hypofunction below 0.1 to 0.2 mL/min (Hilton, Ferracane and Broome, 2013).

**Causes.** Head and neck **radiation** and **Sjögren's syndrome** are the most common causes of severe xerostomia; antidepressants, tranquillizers, antihypertensives and diuretics also reduce flow (Banerjee and Watson, 2011).

**Effects.** Saliva lubricates and clears sugars and acids, so xerostomia raises cariogenic bacteria and caries activity (Hilton, Ferracane and Broome, 2013).

**Endodontic relevance.** Caries risks pulpal infection and apical periodontitis (Lin and Huang, 2021); after radiotherapy dry mouth causes recurrent caries, compromising prognosis (Aminoshariaei and Fouad, 2021); in the elderly, medication-related xerostomia with root caries may raise endodontic demand (Levy, Talim and Rotstein, 2019).

**Management.**
1. Stimulate natural flow; pilocarpine or cevimeline after medical consultation (Hilton, Ferracane and Broome, 2013).
2. Saliva substitutes, rinses and sugar-free gum (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013); some products demineralise dentine (Hilton, Ferracane and Broome, 2013).
3. Fluoride toothpaste (5000 ppm); varnish 3-monthly (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
