# Periapical pathology and immunologic consideration in endodontics.

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Periapical pathology → Innate response → Adaptive response → Mediators and bone resorption → Immunopathology → Healing and persistence.

## Periapical pathology

Periradicular lesions are the host response to irritants leaving an infected canal: asymptomatic or symptomatic apical periodontitis, acute or chronic abscess, and condensing osteitis (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019). Histologically they are granuloma, cyst or abscess; a cyst arises within a granuloma, and both heal equally well if irritants are controlled (Sedgley, Silva and Fouad, 2021). The thin ligament cannot stop the infection, so bone resorption makes room for a soft tissue lesion where immune reactions concentrate (Sedgley et al., 2021).

## Innate response

Acute apical periodontitis is an immediate defence without specificity or memory (Lin and Huang, 2021). Macrophages and complement act first; C3a and C5a make mast cells release histamine (Ricucci et al., 2019). Neutrophils are the first leukocytes to arrive (Ricucci et al., 2019) and the principal effector cells (Lin and Huang, 2021).

## Adaptive response

Chronic apical periodontitis is an adaptive response with specificity and memory (Lin and Huang, 2021). Antigens reach regional lymph nodes for lymphocyte activation (Ricucci et al., 2019); plasma cells secrete antibodies specific for canal bacteria (Lin and Huang, 2021).

| T cell | Role (Ricucci et al., 2019) |
|---|---|
| TH1 | Lesion expansion (IL-1, TNF-α, IL-6) |
| TH2 | IL-4, IL-10, TGF-β; healing |
| TH17 | IL-17; neutrophil attraction; exacerbation |

## Mediators and bone resorption

IL-1, IL-6 and TNF-α drive resorption; IL-4 and IL-10 limit it (Sedgley et al., 2021). RANKL binds RANK to form osteoclasts; osteoprotegerin is a decoy receptor, and a raised RANKL/OPG ratio marks pathological resorption (Ricucci et al., 2019).

## Immunopathology

The response is a double-edged sword: it confines infection but causes pain, liquefaction and bone resorption (Ricucci et al., 2019). Activated neutrophils release oxygen radicals and lysosomal enzymes (Ricucci et al., 2019). Antigen-antibody complexes can stimulate bone destruction experimentally, but complement probably has no major pathogenic role, anaphylactic-type reactions are of unclear significance, and antibody protects against dissemination (Sasaki and Stashenko, 2012).

## Healing and persistence

Chronic inflammation is "good news and bad news": defences hold but cannot eliminate irritants; normal tissue returns if they are removed (Lin and Huang, 2021). Exacerbation may follow virulent strains, greater microbial load or disrupted host response; about 50% of persistent lesions contain bacteria (Sedgley et al., 2021).
