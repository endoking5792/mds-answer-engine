## STUDY MODE

### S1. Setting reaction of conventional GIC [basic science]
Mixing starts an acid-base reaction between the polyacid and the glass. (1) **Dissolution:** the acid attacks the glass surface, freeing calcium or strontium, aluminium and fluoride ions; about 20-30% of the glass decomposes, forming a sol (Mount et al, 2016). (2) **Gelation or hardening:** calcium (strontium) ions bind the carboxylate groups first, giving a clinically hard surface in about 4-10 minutes; aluminium ions then cross-link the chains over the next 24 hours (Mount et al, 2016). (3) **Maturation:** progressive hydration of the salts improves properties sharply (Mount et al, 2016). The result is unreacted glass cores in a siliceous gel inside a cross-linked polysalt matrix (Mount et al, 2016). Sikri describes the same two chemical stages, with a secondary aluminium stage needing at least 24 hours and a full reaction up to 7 days (Sikri, 2020).

### S2. Water balance [clinical relevance]
About 24% of the set cement is water, either loosely bound (easily lost early) or tightly bound in the hydration shell of the salts (Mount et al, 2016). The cement is most vulnerable to water in stage 2, and remains vulnerable to water loss for some time (Mount et al, 2016). Early calcium polyacrylate chains can wash out in water, and dehydration cracks the surface (Mount et al, 2016). This is why the cement is sealed at once.

### S3. Resin-modified and tri-cure GIC
Resin-modified GIC sets by acid-base neutralisation plus free-radical polymerisation of methacrylate groups, so it snap-sets with light and then continues to mature (Mount et al, 2016). Cross-linking can be acid-base, light (camphorquinone) or redox (Mount et al, 2016). Sikri adds that tri-cure materials use a chemical dark cure as well, important where light does not reach (Sikri, 2020). Light only hardens to the depth of penetration, so build up in increments (Mount et al, 2016).

### S4. Properties [treatment relevance]
Physical: working time 2-3 minutes, thermal expansion near dentine, opaque, high early solubility (Sikri, 2020). Mechanical: compressive strength 16,000-22,000 psi, tensile strength 900-960 psi, hardness 48 KHN, brittle (Sikri, 2020). Biological: fresh pH very low but dentine buffers it; mild pulp response that resolves in 10-20 days (Mount et al, 2016); ion exchange adhesion; fluoride release (Sikri, 2020).

## EXAM MODE

### 1. Introduction
Glass ionomer cement hardens by an acid-base reaction between ion-leachable fluoroaluminosilicate glass and aqueous polyalkenoic acid, in which water is the transfer medium (Sikri, 2020).

### 2. Setting reaction of conventional GIC
| Stage | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Dissolution | acid attacks glass, releasing Ca, Al, F and Na ions; sol forms (Mount et al, 2016) | seconds to minutes |
| 2 Gelation | Ca (Sr) then Al ions cross-link polyacid chains; initial hard set (Mount et al, 2016) | about 4-10 minutes |
| 3 Maturation | Al cross-links, hydration, silica gel formation (Mount et al, 2016) | 24 hours; up to 7 days (Sikri, 2020) |
- **Set cement:** unreacted glass in siliceous gel inside a polysalt matrix (Mount et al, 2016).
- **Calcium cross-links** form first, are fragile and vulnerable to early water; **aluminium** later gives insoluble tight bonds (Mount et al, 2016).
- **Water:** about 24% of the set cement; early water uptake or loss damages it (Mount et al, 2016).

### 2A. Factors affecting the rate of set
Glass composition, fusion and annealing temperature, particle size, tartaric acid content and powder:liquid ratio; high ambient temperature speeds the set and low temperature slows it; the liquid must never be refrigerated (Mount et al, 2016).

### 3. Resin-modified GIC
Acid-base reaction plus light-cured polymerisation of methacrylate (HEMA); a snap set with light, then maturation; also redox cure in some (Mount et al, 2016; Sikri, 2020).

### 4. Tri-cure GIC
Acid-base, light cure and chemical redox dark cure; allows bulk placement where light cannot reach (Bhat et al, 2019; Sikri, 2020).

### 5. Physical properties
- Working time 2-3 minutes; thermal expansion close to dentine (Sikri, 2020).
- Opaque; solubility 0.3-0.5% (traditional); film thickness 22-25 micrometres (Sikri, 2020).
- Setting shrinkage about 3%, developing slowly (Mount et al, 2016).

### 6. Mechanical properties
- Compressive strength 16,000-22,000 psi; tensile 900-960 psi; hardness 48 KHN (Sikri, 2020).
- Brittle, low tensile strength, low wear resistance (Sikri, 2020).
- Resin-modified and cermet types are stronger in tension (Sikri, 2020).

### 7. Biological properties
- Adhesion to enamel and dentine; fluoride release and recharge (Sikri, 2020).
- Pulp: fresh pH about 1-2 but dentine buffers; mild inflammation resolving in 10-20 days (Mount et al, 2016); do not place on direct pulp exposure (Sikri, 2020).
- Plaque does not thrive on it; tissue tolerance is high (Mount et al, 2016; Mount, 1994).

### 8. Conclusion
Setting is a three-stage acid-base process whose product depends on water balance; the properties follow from it and decide technique.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Stages: dissolution, gelation (Ca/Sr then Al), maturation (Mount et al, 2016).
- Hard set about 4-10 minutes; maturation 24 hours to a week (Mount et al, 2016; Sikri, 2020).
- About 24% water; protect early (Mount et al, 2016).
- RMGIC: acid-base plus light; tri-cure adds redox (Mount et al, 2016; Sikri, 2020).
