## STUDY MODE

Impressions carry micro-organisms from the oral cavity (real species named: *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Escherichia coli*, *Candida albicans* survive on impression surfaces — Sikri, 2017) and must be disinfected before reaching the laboratory, since lab personnel handling untreated impressions/casts are an infection-control risk.

**General method**: immersion or spraying with a disinfectant for a brief, defined period — **prolonged immersion distorts** many materials and can alter surface hardness, so exposure time is itself a controlled variable, not just the choice of agent (Sikri, 2017; Ricketts, 2011).

**Commonly used agents** (Sikri, 2017; Philips', via Module 3 study material):
- 2% glutaraldehyde (e.g., "Cidex")
- 2.5% (or 1%, per Ricketts, 2011) sodium hypochlorite
- Chlorine compounds, iodophors, phenolics (for polysulfide/silicone specifically)
- Chlorhexidine 0.5% ("Hibitane"), benzalkonium chloride 1% ("Benzalkon"), and other proprietary surface disinfectants.

**Material-specific guidance** (a real, complete reference table exists in Philips', cited in the Module 3 study material, and corroborated by Sikri and Ricketts):
- **Alginate**: immerse WITH CAUTION, under 10 minutes only, using chlorine compounds or iodophors — short glutaraldehyde exposure is acceptable for the material but inadequate for true disinfection.
- **Agar**: must NOT be immersed in alkaline glutaraldehyde at all.
- **Polysulfide and silicones**: immersion is fine with glutaraldehyde, chlorine compounds, iodophors, or phenolics — but not beyond 30 minutes.
- **Polyether**: immerse WITH CAUTION — it both absorbs water/fluid from the disinfectant solution and simultaneously leaches its own water-soluble plasticiser, so prolonged immersion is doubly damaging; store dry (<50% relative humidity), never left soaking long.
- **Plaster impression material**: a specific 10-minute soak in sodium hypochlorite.
- **Impression compound**: 2% alkaline glutaraldehyde, immersed for the required time, then rinsed and poured immediately.

**A real, quantified effect**: 2% glutaraldehyde produces a slight expanding effect (0.03%) on rubber-base impression dimensional stability; disinfectants also increase polysulfide's wettability but reduce dimensional stability of hydrophilic silicones specifically, and adversely affect polyether if used by immersion (Sikri, 2017).

**Newer methods**: microwave energy and UV light have also been used to disinfect impressions (Sikri, 2017).

**Practical recommendation** (Ricketts, 2011): the minimum disinfection standard recommended is immersion in 1% sodium hypochlorite for at least 10 minutes, following the specific manufacturer's instructions for both the impression material and the disinfectant used.

## EXAM MODE

**(Estimated writing time: ~8 minutes)**

### Why disinfection is needed
Impressions carry oral micro-organisms (*S. aureus*, *E. coli*, *C. albicans*) and must be disinfected before lab transfer to prevent cross-infection.

### General principle
Spray or brief immersion (3-10 minutes typically; never prolonged, since this distorts the material and can alter surface hardness).

### Common disinfectants
2% glutaraldehyde; 1-2.5% sodium hypochlorite; chlorine compounds/iodophors/phenolics; 0.5% chlorhexidine; 1% benzalkonium chloride.

### Material-specific notes
- Alginate — chlorine compounds/iodophors, <10 min, cautiously.
- Agar — NEVER alkaline glutaraldehyde.
- Polysulfide/silicones — glutaraldehyde/chlorine/iodophor/phenolic fine, <30 min.
- Polyether — cautious immersion only (absorbs water + leaches plasticiser); prefer spraying; store dry.
- Plaster — 10-min NaOCl soak.
- Impression compound — 2% alkaline glutaraldehyde, rinse, pour immediately.

### Newer methods
Microwave energy, UV light.

### Minimum recommended standard
Immersion in 1% sodium hypochlorite for ≥10 minutes, per manufacturer instructions (Ricketts, 2011).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Organisms surviving on impressions: *S. aureus*, *E. coli*, *C. albicans*.
- Standard agents: 2% glutaraldehyde, 1-2.5% NaOCl.
- Agar: NEVER alkaline glutaraldehyde. Polyether: avoid prolonged immersion (absorbs water + leaches plasticiser) — spray preferred.
- Minimum recommended: 1% NaOCl, ≥10 minutes.
- Prolonged immersion = dimensional distortion risk for most materials — time is as important as agent choice.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Naming only one disinfectant (usually glutaraldehyde) without material-specific exceptions (agar's absolute glutaraldehyde contraindication, polyether's water-absorption problem).
2. Omitting the named organisms that justify disinfection in the first place.
3. Not mentioning that immersion TIME itself is a controlled variable, not just agent selection.
4. Forgetting plaster/impression-compound-specific disinfection notes (students often cover elastomers/hydrocolloids only).
