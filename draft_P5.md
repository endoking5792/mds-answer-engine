## STUDY MODE

### S1. What ART is [basic science]
ART removes caries with hand instruments only, without anaesthesia or expensive equipment, and restores the cavity with a chemically adhesive glass ionomer, which bonds and releases fluoride [SRC:C1135]. It was first tried in the mid 1980s, and single-surface restorations have done better than multi-surface ones, with marked operator effects [SRC:G59].

### S2. Why high-viscosity GIC [treatment relevance]
High-viscosity (condensable) GIC has polyacrylic acid in the powder and finer glass (about 3 micrometres), giving amalgam-like handling and better abrasion resistance, though strength is little changed [SRC:B262]. Bhat lists easy packing, less early moisture sensitivity, non-stickiness, rapid immediate finishing, better wear resistance and low solubility [SRC:B198]. Examples are Fuji IX and Ketac Molar [SRC:B262].

### S3. Evidence [prognosis]
A systematic review of 14 RCTs found no significant difference in survival between ART/high-viscosity GIC and traditional restorations for primary molars (single and multiple surfaces) and single-surface permanent restorations at 1, 2, 3 and 5 years; only 2 of 14 reports were at low risk of bias [LIT:Frencken2021]. A different review (67 articles, including non-randomised studies) found the conventional technique better than ART for single-surface occlusal and multi-surface GIC restorations [LIT:Ruengrungsom2018]. An umbrella review graded the evidence as conflicting and long-term performance questionable [LIT:Panetta2024]. The honest reading is that ART is best supported for single-surface cavities and primary teeth.

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Atraumatic restorative treatment (ART) is a minimal intervention technique in which caries is removed with hand instruments and the cavity is restored with high-viscosity glass ionomer cement [SRC:G59] [SRC:C1135].

### 2. High-viscosity GIC
- **Composition:** polyacrylic acid in the powder, finer glass (about 3 micrometres) [SRC:B262].
- **Properties:** packable, less early moisture sensitivity, non-sticky, immediate finishing, better wear resistance, low solubility [SRC:B198].
- **Examples:** Fuji IX, Ketac Molar, Chemflex, Hi-Fi [SRC:B262].
- **Limit:** strength little different from conventional GIC [SRC:B262].

### 3. Indications
Single-surface cavities, primary teeth, patients and settings without equipment or electricity, rampant caries stabilisation, and anxious children [SRC:G61] [SRC:G59] [SRC:C279].

### 4. Contraindications
Heavy occlusal load in multi-surface permanent restorations; pulp involvement; inaccessible cavities [SRC:C128] [SRC:G59].

### 5. Technique
1. Examine; isolate with cotton rolls [SRC:G60].
2. Access with hatchet or sharp excavator; break undermined enamel [SRC:G60].
3. Excavate soft dentine; clean [SRC:G60].
4. Condition with weak polyacrylic acid [SRC:G60].
5. Fill the cavity and confluent fissures with GIC [SRC:G60].
6. Press with a petroleum-jelly-coated finger; cover with varnish or petroleum jelly [SRC:G60].
Routine ART needs no lining; undermined dentine becomes supported by GIC [SRC:G60].

### 6. Advantages and disadvantages
- **Advantages:** no equipment or electricity, minimal discomfort, little training, low cost, less time, fluoride effect, a more radical approach remains possible [SRC:G61].
- **Disadvantages:** operator dependence, poorer success in multi-surface restorations, wear, need for maintenance [SRC:G59] [SRC:C1135].

### 7. Evidence
No significant difference from conventional restorations in primary molars and single-surface permanent cavities up to 5 years; evidence base partly at high risk of bias [LIT:Frencken2021]. A second review found conventional technique better for single-surface occlusal and multi-surface GIC restorations [LIT:Ruengrungsom2018]. Overall evidence conflicting [LIT:Panetta2024].

### 8. Modifications
Single-step ART (one-stage partial removal): a better seal, with arrested caries-affected dentine sealed from nutrients [SRC:C286]. Occlusal resurfacing of GIC with composite after 2-3 years if worn [SRC:C287].

### 9. Conclusion
ART with high-viscosity GIC is an effective, low-cost approach for single-surface cavities and primary teeth; it needs maintenance and is not a substitute for conventional restorations in heavily loaded multi-surface permanent teeth.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
