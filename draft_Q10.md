## STUDY MODE

### S1. Setting reaction of conventional GIC [basic science]
Mixing starts an acid-base reaction between the polyacid and the glass. (1) **Dissolution:** the acid attacks the glass surface, freeing calcium or strontium, aluminium and fluoride ions; about 20-30% of the glass decomposes, forming a sol [SRC:A70]. (2) **Gelation or hardening:** calcium (strontium) ions bind the carboxylate groups first, giving a clinically hard surface in about 4-10 minutes; aluminium ions then cross-link the chains over the next 24 hours [SRC:A71]. (3) **Maturation:** progressive hydration of the salts improves properties sharply [SRC:A73]. The result is unreacted glass cores in a siliceous gel inside a cross-linked polysalt matrix [SRC:A78]. Sikri describes the same two chemical stages, with a secondary aluminium stage needing at least 24 hours and a full reaction up to 7 days [SRC:G12] [SRC:G13].

### S2. Water balance [clinical relevance]
About 24% of the set cement is water, either loosely bound (easily lost early) or tightly bound in the hydration shell of the salts [SRC:A79]. The cement is most vulnerable to water in stage 2, and remains vulnerable to water loss for some time [SRC:A80]. Early calcium polyacrylate chains can wash out in water, and dehydration cracks the surface [SRC:A89]. This is why the cement is sealed at once.

### S3. Resin-modified and tri-cure GIC
Resin-modified GIC sets by acid-base neutralisation plus free-radical polymerisation of methacrylate groups, so it snap-sets with light and then continues to mature [SRC:A84] [SRC:A87]. Cross-linking can be acid-base, light (camphorquinone) or redox [SRC:A85]. Sikri adds that tri-cure materials use a chemical dark cure as well, important where light does not reach [SRC:G16]. Light only hardens to the depth of penetration, so build up in increments [SRC:A88].

### S4. Properties [treatment relevance]
Physical: working time 2-3 minutes, thermal expansion near dentine, opaque, high early solubility [SRC:G17] [SRC:G20]. Mechanical: compressive strength 16,000-22,000 psi, tensile strength 900-960 psi, hardness 48 KHN, brittle [SRC:G20]. Biological: fresh pH very low but dentine buffers it; mild pulp response that resolves in 10-20 days [SRC:A458]; ion exchange adhesion; fluoride release [SRC:G21] [SRC:G23].

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Glass ionomer cement hardens by an acid-base reaction between ion-leachable fluoroaluminosilicate glass and aqueous polyalkenoic acid, in which water is the transfer medium [SRC:G12].

### 2. Setting reaction of conventional GIC
| Stage | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Dissolution | acid attacks glass, releasing Ca, Al, F and Na ions; sol forms [SRC:A70] | seconds to minutes |
| 2 Gelation | Ca (Sr) then Al ions cross-link polyacid chains; initial hard set [SRC:A71] | about 4-10 minutes |
| 3 Maturation | Al cross-links, hydration, silica gel formation [SRC:A73] | 24 hours; up to 7 days [SRC:G13] |
- **Set cement:** unreacted glass in siliceous gel inside a polysalt matrix [SRC:A78].
- **Calcium cross-links** form first, are fragile and vulnerable to early water; **aluminium** later gives insoluble tight bonds [SRC:A74].
- **Water:** about 24% of the set cement; early water uptake or loss damages it [SRC:A79] [SRC:A80].

### 2A. Factors affecting the rate of set
Glass composition, fusion and annealing temperature, particle size, tartaric acid content and powder:liquid ratio; high ambient temperature speeds the set and low temperature slows it; the liquid must never be refrigerated [SRC:A81] [SRC:A83] [SRC:A82].

### 3. Resin-modified GIC
Acid-base reaction plus light-cured polymerisation of methacrylate (HEMA); a snap set with light, then maturation; also redox cure in some [SRC:A84] [SRC:A85] [SRC:G16].

### 4. Tri-cure GIC
Acid-base, light cure and chemical redox dark cure; allows bulk placement where light cannot reach [SRC:B190] [SRC:G16].

### 5. Physical properties
- Working time 2-3 minutes; thermal expansion close to dentine [SRC:G17].
- Opaque; solubility 0.3-0.5% (traditional); film thickness 22-25 micrometres [SRC:G20].
- Setting shrinkage about 3%, developing slowly [SRC:A93].

### 6. Mechanical properties
- Compressive strength 16,000-22,000 psi; tensile 900-960 psi; hardness 48 KHN [SRC:G20].
- Brittle, low tensile strength, low wear resistance [SRC:G14].
- Resin-modified and cermet types are stronger in tension [SRC:G20].

### 7. Biological properties
- Adhesion to enamel and dentine; fluoride release and recharge [SRC:G21] [SRC:G23].
- Pulp: fresh pH about 1-2 but dentine buffers; mild inflammation resolving in 10-20 days [SRC:A458]; do not place on direct pulp exposure [SRC:G18].
- Plaque does not thrive on it; tissue tolerance is high [SRC:A461] [SRC:A131].

### 8. Conclusion
Setting is a three-stage acid-base process whose product depends on water balance; the properties follow from it and decide technique.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
