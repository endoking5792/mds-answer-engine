## 1. Canal impediments

### Explanation

Cohen's defines the two commonest: "A ledge is a dentinal shelf that is created by shaping instruments that straighten and dig into the convex side of the canal wall", and "a blockage refers mainly to a root canal area that is filled with densely compacted debris or collagenous pulp remnants"; "another reason for a perceived blockage may be a very abrupt canal curvature" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]. Peters and Arias: "a loose resistance indicates the presence of a ledge or acute curve, while so-called rubbery resistance suggests the presence of compacted soft tissue" (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC], citing West's four conditions requiring modified technique (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC].

Gutmann and Lovdahl: loss of working length is mostly "due to the packing of dentin chips in the apical third" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]; choose "a small but stiff instrument (e.g., No. 15 K-file)" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC] with "a 45-degree curve ... at the apical 3 to 4 mm" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC], always "with a lubricant in the canal" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]; the file "should not be removed until it can be used in small-amplitude strokes" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]; chelating agents soften the plug (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC], but persisting may create "a false canal" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]; if bypass fails, shape "at a new working length coronal to the blockage" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC] and consider surgery (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]. Pulp-tissue blockage is "very easy to prevent by filling the pulp chamber with viscous lubricants" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** A canal impediment is anything that prevents a file from reaching working length in a canal that was, or should be, patent.

**Types**
1. *Anatomical:* abrupt or apical curvature and dilaceration (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021); exit of the foramen at 90° to the canal (Peters and Arias, 2025); calcifications and pulp stones; canal division or merging.
2. *Iatrogenic:* blockage with compacted dentine chips or pulp tissue; ledge, a dentinal shelf on the convex wall (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021); separated instrument; old filling materials, posts, cements.

**Diagnosis by tactile feel:** loose resistance = ledge or curve; rubbery resistance = packed soft tissue (Peters and Arias, 2025); a hard stop may mean calcification, a fragment or a ledge (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019). Confirm with an angled radiograph or CBCT.

**Management**
- *Blockage of debris:* irrigate with NaOCl and use a lubricant or chelator (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011; Weine, 2004, by contrast, advises against chelators in a ledged or blocked canal); a small stiff #10-#15 K-file or C+ file with a 45° bend in the apical 3-4 mm, rotated gently to find the catch, then small-amplitude in-and-out movements; do not withdraw until loose; confirm with a radiograph; then a Hedström file on the outstroke (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).
- *Ledge or curve:* sharp 30-40° precurve near the tip, directional stop, stainless steel pilot files; rotaries are unsuitable in ledged canals (Weine, 2004; Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019; Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021).
- *Calcification:* ultrasonic tips under the microscope, EDTA gel, C+ files (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- *Separated instrument or filling material:* bypass, retrieval with ultrasonics, or solvents (see Procedural Errors topic).
- *If not negotiable:* complete shaping and filling at a new working length coronal to the impediment, review, and consider periradicular surgery or replantation (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).

**Abrupt apical exit:** place a short bend in the last millimetre of a #08 or #10 file and rotate gently to find the passive path; once past, use a #10 with the same bend in push-pull to reduce the impediment; the more difficult the canal, the more important patency becomes (Peters and Arias, 2025).

**Prevention:** viscous lubricant in the chamber of vital teeth (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021); copious irrigation; recapitulation; patency; never force files; precurved small files in curved canals.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Ledge = dentinal shelf on convex wall; blockage = compacted debris or pulp.
- Loose vs rubbery resistance.
- 45° bend in the apical 3-4 mm; lubricant; do not remove until loose.
