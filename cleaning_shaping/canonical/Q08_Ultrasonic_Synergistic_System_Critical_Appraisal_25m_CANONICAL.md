## 1. The concept and its origin

### Explanation

Ultrasonics in endodontics began when "in 1957 Richman reported on the use of a barbed broach connected to an ultrasonic delivery system" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; Hülsmann and Schäfer date the "introduction of ultrasonics and sonics systems by Richman in 1957" (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]. Endodontology: "almost 20 years elapsed before the use of ultrasonic technology was again pursued (Martin, 1976)" (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]; Weine: "beginning in 1980, Martin and Cunningham and their group" published a series of studies (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. They "coined the term endosonics" and stated that the equipment "creates a synergistic system whereby canal preparation and cleaning, irrigation and disinfection, and canal packing and filling are all accomplished with the same group of devices" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. That is the "synergistic system" of the question. Endodontology describes the same ambition as replacement of hand instrumentation and "synergistic action with irrigating solutions" (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC].

Frequencies: "sonic" is 16 Hz to 20 kHz and "ultrasonic" 20 kHz to 1 GHz in physics (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]; endodontic ultrasonic units work "at levels of 20 to 30 kHz" (Weine, 2004) [SRC] or "between 20 000 Hz and 40 000 Hz" (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]; sonic units "at 1500-6000 Hz" (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Piezoelectric units "are much more powerful than electromagnetic units" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Grossman's gives a lower figure for sonic handpieces, "2-3 kHz when used inside root canals", and notes that "the sonic system uses water as an irrigant and requires special instruments" (Rispi Sonic, Shaper Sonic, Trio Sonic) (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; the two books differ on the sonic frequency.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Ultrasonic devices were introduced to endodontics by Richman in 1957 (Weine, 2004). Martin and Cunningham, from 1976-1980, coined the term "endosonics" and described ultrasonics as a **synergistic system**: one group of devices that prepares and cleans the canal, irrigates and disinfects it, and helps fill it, with the irrigant energised by the vibrating file (Weine, 2004; Baumann and Beer, 2010). The statement in the question reflects this claim. A critical appraisal must ask whether ultrasonics is efficient for both parts of biomechanical preparation: mechanical shaping and chemical cleaning.

**Equipment**
- Electromagnetic (magnetostrictive, e.g. Cavi-Endo) or piezoelectric units (e.g. Enac, EMS); piezoelectric units are more powerful (Weine, 2004) and produce less heat (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Ultrasonic: about 20-40 kHz; sonic: 1500-6000 Hz (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- An endodontic file (K-file, diamond file) or smooth wire is held in an adapter; irrigant (water or NaOCl) flows down the file into the canal.

## 2. Mechanism of action

### Explanation

Martin and Cunningham's explanation was "implosion or cavitation" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; Grossman's describes cavitation as "the growth and collapse of bubbles" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Ahmad and colleagues disagreed: the power was "too low to produce cavitation" and the canal too narrow, so the mechanism was acoustic streaming, "a stream of liquid to produce eddies and flows of oscillation" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; Grossman's: "small but intense eddy currents" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], "however, the root canal diameter does not permit cavitation significantly" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Endodontology: dental units "are too weak to create large cavitation effects" and "most important and effective is the third mechanism of action", acoustic microstreaming (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]; the file oscillates "with a series of nodes and antinodes" (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC], and wall contact "reduces acoustic microstreaming" (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Peters, Arias and Shabahang agree on microstreaming as the main mechanism (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]. Grossman's adds that the apical third must first be enlarged "to at least the size of a No. 30 to No. 40 file" for free oscillation (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Mechanism of action**
1. **Cavitation:** growth and implosion of bubbles in the irrigant, disrupting tissue and bacteria (Martin and Cunningham's original explanation) (Weine, 2004; Gopikrishna, 2021).
2. **Acoustic (micro)streaming:** small, intense eddy currents around the oscillating file that create shear stress on the canal wall (Ahmad and colleagues) (Weine, 2004).
3. **Heating and energising the irrigant,** increasing NaOCl activity.
4. **Mechanical cutting** by file contact with the wall.

**Appraisal of the mechanism.** The books now agree that endodontic units are too weak and canals too narrow for significant cavitation; acoustic microstreaming is the main effect (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Gopikrishna, 2021; Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021). Microstreaming needs the file to oscillate freely: wall contact damps it, so the canal must already be enlarged to about #30-40 (Gopikrishna, 2021). This finding undermines the "synergy" claim, because the mechanism works best only when the file does not touch, and therefore does not shape, the wall.

## 3. Appraisal of ultrasonics as shaping (the "mechanical" part)

### Explanation

Weine reports early optimism, but "my initial investigations indicated that this was not consistently true"; Chenail and Teplitsky, after enlarging to size 30, "found that zipping was a constant feature" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Schulz-Bongert and Weine found that ultrasonics alone in curves of 30° and more produced "severe zipping", whereas ultrasonics above the elbow "in combination with hand filing at the apex" gave minimal zipping (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Length control is a problem: "it is impossible to use rubber stops" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Grossman's: sonic and ultrasonic instruments "have been reported to cause transportation" and "are not indicated for shaping" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Peters, Arias and Shabahang: ultrasonic shaping of curved canals "is no longer recommended" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]; Endodontology: "ultrasonic root canal preparation is no longer justified" (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]; Cohen's: ultrasonic instrumentation with wall contact "may result in the uncontrolled cutting of the root canal walls without effective disinfection" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]. Endodontology adds that sonic devices cause fewer fractures, steps and losses of length than ultrasonic ones (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**As a shaping method: the statement is not supported**
- Ultrasonic files used to enlarge curved canals beyond size 30 consistently zipped and transported them; with ultrasonics alone in curves of 30° or more zipping was severe (Weine, 2004).
- Strip perforations, ledges, steps and loss of working length were frequent; rubber stops cannot be used, so length control is poor (Weine, 2004; Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- Cutting is uncontrolled and does not disinfect where the file touches the wall (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021).
- Current verdict: ultrasonic shaping is "no longer recommended" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021), "no longer justified" (Baumann and Beer, 2010), and ultrasonics are "not indicated for shaping" (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Sonic versus ultrasonic shaping:** sonic handpieces (1500-6000 Hz in Baumann and Beer, 2010; 2-3 kHz in Gopikrishna, 2021) drive special rasp-like files (Rispisonic, Shapersonic) with water as irrigant. They remove dentine faster and cause fewer fractures, steps and losses of working length than ultrasonic files, but control of dentine removal and tactile sense are poorer, so they too were never widely accepted for shaping (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- A limited role remains: ultrasonics used coronal to the curve for flaring, combined with hand filing apically, gave minimal zipping (Weine, 2004).

## 4. Appraisal of ultrasonics as cleaning (the "bio" part)

### Explanation

Weine: early reports of better debridement than hand instruments were followed by "more recent papers" that "cast some doubt on those claims" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; smear layer removal may be easier, but where the tip touches "produce a new smear layer" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; less material is pushed through the apex, giving "fewer postoperative flare-ups" in one report (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; NaOCl may cause "clogging or corrosion" of the units (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Weine still uses "several minutes of ultrasonic cleaning, leaving the tip centered" after shaping (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Endodontology: there is "no direct antibacterial effect" without irrigant (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC].

The concept that survived is passive ultrasonic irrigation, "described as early as 1985 by Weller et al." (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]; "passive" means the "noncutting action" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC], used once the canal "has a final apical size and taper" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC], often with "a smooth wire, which does not affect the canal wall shape" (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Endodontology's verdict: "in retrospect, this approach was not correct. Only passive ultrasonic irrigation (PUI) has stood the test of time" (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Cohen's adds caution: PUI improves debridement "in vitro; the results in vivo present some controversy" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; continuous ultrasonic irrigation is a newer variant (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; and "cost-effectiveness still needs to be scientifically demonstrated" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**As a cleaning method: the statement is partly supported**
- Energised NaOCl removes more debris, pulp remnants and bacteria from isthmuses and fins than syringe irrigation in vitro; the antibacterial effect depends on the irrigant, not on ultrasound itself (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- Early claims of superior debridement over hand instrumentation were later doubted; file contact creates a new smear layer; NaOCl can corrode units (Weine, 2004).
- Less debris may be pushed periapically, with fewer flare-ups in one report (Weine, 2004).
- **Passive ultrasonic irrigation (Weller, 1985):** after shaping is complete, a small file or smooth wire oscillates freely in fresh NaOCl without touching the walls; this is the part of the concept that has "stood the test of time" (Baumann and Beer, 2010). In vivo benefit is still debated (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021), and cost-effectiveness is unproven (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021). Continuous ultrasonic irrigation delivers and activates irrigant together.

## 5. Present role and conclusion

### Explanation

Endodontology describes "a total paradigm shift": ultrasonics are now used mainly for obstructions, retreatment and surgery (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]; with ultrasonics and the microscope, success in removing fractured instruments rose "from 68%" to 87% (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Present uses of ultrasonics (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021)**
- Refining access, removing pulp stones and calcifications, locating orifices (MB2).
- Activating irrigants (PUI, continuous ultrasonic irrigation).
- Removing posts, separated instruments, silver points and cements in retreatment (success in instrument removal rose from 68% to 87%).
- Thermoplasticising gutta-percha; placing MTA; root-end preparation in microsurgery.

**Critical appraisal: summary**

| Part of the claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Efficient shaping of root canals | Not supported: transportation, zipping, ledges, poor length control; abandoned |
| Efficient cleaning and disinfection | Partly supported: improves irrigant action in vitro, mainly through acoustic microstreaming as PUI after shaping |
| Synergy (one system for shaping, cleaning, filling) | Not supported: shaping and activation need different conditions (wall contact versus free oscillation) |

**Conclusion.** The statement was a reasonable hypothesis in the 1980s, when Martin and Cunningham proposed it, but it is only half true. Ultrasonics is not an efficient method of mechanical preparation and is no longer used for shaping. Its value lies in the "bio" part, energising irrigants after the canal has been shaped by hand or NiTi instruments, and in many adjunctive procedures. The modern synergy is between NiTi shaping, NaOCl and passive ultrasonic activation, not within the ultrasonic unit alone.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

- Richman 1957; Martin and Cunningham coined "endosonics" and the "synergistic system".
- Ultrasonic 20-40 kHz; sonic 1500-6000 Hz; piezoelectric more powerful than magnetostrictive.
- Mechanism: cavitation (Martin) disputed; acoustic microstreaming (Ahmad) accepted as main effect.
- Free oscillation needs an apical size of about #30-40; wall contact damps streaming.
- Ultrasonic shaping causes zipping and is no longer recommended.
- PUI (Weller 1985): only part to stand the test of time.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW

1. Oscillating file with nodes and antinodes and eddy currents (acoustic microstreaming).
2. PUI: smooth wire centred in a shaped canal filled with NaOCl, not touching the walls.
3. Zipped curved canal after ultrasonic enlargement.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

- Agreeing with the statement without appraisal.
- Describing cavitation as the main mechanism without noting the dispute.
- Confusing ultrasonic instrumentation (cutting) with passive ultrasonic irrigation.
- Omitting the present uses of ultrasonics.

## MNEMONIC

**"CAPS" for the appraisal:** **C**avitation weak; **A**coustic streaming main; **P**assive use only (PUI); **S**haping abandoned.
