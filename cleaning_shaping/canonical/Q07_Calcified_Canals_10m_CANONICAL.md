## 1. Causes and nature

### Explanation

Gutmann and Lovdahl: "pulpal calcifications are signs of pathosis, not the cause of them" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]; when irritants act "slowly over a long period, both the pulp chamber and the pulp canal system undergo calcific changes", whereas rapid bacterial or traumatic insult leaves a patent necrotic canal (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]; calcification also follows "vital pulp treatment with calcium hydroxide" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]. Grossman's: "reactions of the pulp to inflammatory changes have caused calcification and blockage" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Mineralisation "generally occurs in a coronal to apical direction" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]. Gutmann and Lovdahl add that "the canal space is always located in the cross-sectional center of the root" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Causes:** age (secondary dentine) and slow, long-standing irritation from caries and repeated restorations (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011); pulp inflammation (Gopikrishna, 2021); vital pulp therapy with calcium hydroxide (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011); trauma (pulp canal obliteration) (Johnson, 2025); attrition and periodontal disease [RECALL]. Calcifications are signs of pathosis, not its cause (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011); mineralisation generally proceeds coronal to apical (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021), so the apical canal may remain negotiable [RECALL].

**Types:** pulp stones (free, attached, embedded) in the chamber [RECALL]; diffuse (linear) calcification along the canal; apparently complete radiographic obliteration, where histology may still show small areas of residual tissue (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).

**Problems:** canal not visible on the radiograph; receded orifices; access may perforate the floor or the labial root surface; files cannot be advanced, causing ledges, false canals, instrument fracture and loss of working length.

## 2. Management

### Explanation

Assessment: angled radiographs (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], bite-wings for chamber depth (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC], limited-field CBCT (Johnson, 2025) [SRC]; the chamber roof lies at the CEJ even when receded (Johnson, 2025) [SRC]. Access: change the bur "parallel ... to the long axis" after 3-4 mm in incisors (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]; ultrasonics are "indispensable" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC], also for "troughing" (Johnson, 2025) [SRC]; transillumination at the CEJ (Johnson, 2025) [SRC]; radiographs with a bur in place (Johnson, 2025) [SRC]. Under ×30 every speck "can begin to look like the orifice" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]; magnification is essential (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]. Negotiation: explorer or small round burs (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], EDTA gel (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; EDTA was introduced for "narrow and calcified root canals" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC], though prolonged use erodes dentine (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; C+ or C-Pilot files (Gopikrishna, 2021; Schäfer, 2017) [SRC], which allow "more pressure" with "excellent tactile feedback" (Schäfer, 2017) [SRC]; ultrasonic tips with radiographic monitoring (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; small crown-down and step-back sequences (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; 0.5 mm clipped increments (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. "Sometimes the canal is never found" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Management**
1. *Assessment:* parallel and angled radiographs, bite-wing for chamber depth, limited-field CBCT (Johnson, 2025); case difficulty and referral.
2. *Access:* access may be started before the rubber dam to keep root angulation visible [RECALL]; access to the depth of a normal chamber floor, guided by the CEJ (chamber roof at CEJ level) and the long axis; in incisors turn the bur parallel to the long axis after 3-4 mm (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011); long-shank burs; check direction with a radiograph with a bur in place.
3. *Locating orifices:* microscope (alternate high and low magnification), DG-16 explorer, transillumination, colour of dentine and dentinal map, ultrasonic tips to remove calcifications and trough, NaOCl bubble ("champagne") test [RECALL] (Johnson, 2025; Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).
4. *Negotiation:* #06-#10 precurved K-files or stiff C+/C-Pilot files with EDTA gel or viscous chelator; watch-winding, no forcing; apex locator; radiographic checks to avoid perforation (Gopikrishna, 2021; Schäfer, 2017).
5. *Enlargement:* intermediate sizes, small crown-down and step-back sequences, glide path before NiTi (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021; Weine, 2004).
6. *If not negotiable:* fill to the obstruction and review; periradicular surgery if disease persists. Guided endodontics with CBCT-planned templates [RECALL].
7. Asymptomatic calcified teeth without periapical disease are usually reviewed rather than treated [RECALL].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Calcification = sign, not cause, of pathosis; progresses coronal to apical.
- Chamber roof at the level of the CEJ.
- C+ / C-Pilot files and EDTA gel; ultrasonic troughing; microscope.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
1. Receded calcified chamber in a molar with the normal chamber outline and access to its depth.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Forcing large files or chelators, creating false canals and perforations.
