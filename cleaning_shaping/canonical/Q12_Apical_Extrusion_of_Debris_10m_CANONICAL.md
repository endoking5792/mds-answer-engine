## 1. Apical extrusion of debris

### Explanation

"Avoiding extrusion of some debris during root canal preparation is virtually impossible" (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; all techniques extruded debris "in 100% of the cases" (Rôças and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]. Extrusion of infected debris "is allegedly one of the principal causes of postoperative pain" (Rôças and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]: in asymptomatic apical periodontitis "a balance has been established" between bacteria and host (Rôças and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC], which extrusion upsets. The extent of pain depends on "the number of bacterial cells extruded" (quantitative) and their "virulence" (qualitative) (Rôças and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]; symptomatic teeth are at higher risk (Rôças and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]. Over-instrumentation can also cause bacteraemia, important in compromised patients (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC].

File systems: "crown-down techniques usually extrude less debris" (Rôças and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC], as Grossman's also claims (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], though Cohen's says the benefit of coronal flaring on extrusion "has not been confirmed" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]. Balanced force extruded less than step-back or ultrasonic (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; ultrasonic preparation was associated with more extrusion (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC], whereas Weine reported fewer flare-ups with ultrasonic cleaning (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. A meta-analysis showed "a trend" of more extrusion with reciprocating single files (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; cross-section mattered most, larger areas extruding more than S-shaped designs (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; WaveOne may act like a "piston" (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC], with "about 26.6% of patients" in severe pain in one trial (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; rotary single files do not appear to increase it (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; reciprocating single files extrude more "than rotary full-sequence systems" (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]. Schäfer reports conflicting studies (Schäfer, 2017) [SRC]; Grossman's warns of extrusion with misused reciprocating files (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

Irrigation: needle binding "should be avoided under all circumstances" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; side-vented needles 1 mm short (Siqueira et al, 2022) [SRC] at "4 to 15 ml/min" (Siqueira et al, 2022) [SRC]; EndoActivator extruded a minimal amount (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; EndoVac "is unlikely to extrude" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]. Patency does not appear to increase pain (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Concept.** Apical extrusion is the expulsion of dentine chips, pulp tissue, bacteria, irrigant and, in retreatment, filling material beyond the foramen during preparation. Some extrusion occurs with every technique (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019; Rôças and Siqueira, 2022). In apical periodontitis, extruded infected debris disturbs the balance between bacteria and host and is a principal cause of postoperative pain and flare-ups; the risk depends on the number and virulence of the bacteria extruded and is higher in symptomatic teeth (Rôças and Siqueira, 2022). It may also cause bacteraemia (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019).

**Comparison of file systems and techniques**

| Technique / system | Relative extrusion |
|---|---|
| Linear filing, step-back | More |
| Crown-down (hand or NiTi), coronal flaring | Less (Rôças and Siqueira, 2022); benefit not confirmed (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) |
| Balanced force | Less than step-back or ultrasonic |
| Ultrasonic preparation | More |
| Full-sequence rotary NiTi | Less |
| Rotary single file | Not increased |
| Reciprocating single file (WaveOne, Reciproc) | Trend to more; "piston effect"; more pain in some trials; data conflicting |
(Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019; Schäfer, 2017)

Instrument cross-section and chip space matter more than motion: large cross-sections extrude more than S-shaped designs (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019).

**Comparison of irrigation protocols:** open-ended or bound needles and high pressure extrude most; side-vented needles 1 mm short at 4-15 ml/min are safer (Siqueira et al, 2022); sonic activation (EndoActivator) extrudes minimally; apical negative pressure (EndoVac) is the safest (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021).

**Factors increasing extrusion:** over-instrumentation beyond the foramen; loss of the apical constriction (resorption, open apex); linear filing; large cross-section files with little chip space; forcing files or using them too long at length; high irrigant pressure and bound needles; larger apical sizes; and, in some studies, more instruments in the sequence (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019); larger master apical files raise the risk of extruding irrigant and filling material (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Prevention:** accurate electronic working length kept within the canal; coronal pre-enlargement and crown-down sequences; patency with small files used passively (it does not increase pain; Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021); full-sequence rotary or balanced force rather than linear filing; light pecking strokes; loose, moving side-vented needles or negative pressure; extra care in symptomatic teeth and medically compromised patients.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Extrusion occurs with all techniques; crown-down extrudes less.
- Reciprocating single files: trend to more extrusion.
- EndoVac safest irrigation.
