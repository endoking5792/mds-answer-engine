## 1. Balanced force technique

### Explanation

"Roane and Sabala are credited with developing the balanced force technique which employs a new K-type file design known as Flex-R file" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Sikri explains the principle with the wood-screw analogy, "All K-files function much like wood screws" (Sikri, 2019) [SRC], and lists its aims, including to "minimize canal straightening" (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Grossman's describes it as "positioning and preloading an instrument through clockwise rotation and then shaping the canal with counterclockwise rotation" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], with counterclockwise rotation of "180-270°" and enough apical pressure to hold depth (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Cohen's gives the same 180-270° (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]. Other books differ: clockwise "less than 180 degrees" and counterclockwise "at least 120 degrees" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]; clockwise 180° (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC] and counterclockwise 120° (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; quarter turn clockwise (Sikri, 2019) [SRC] and three-quarter counterclockwise (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]; quarter clockwise and "a half turn" counterclockwise (Schäfer, 2017) [SRC]. Because the files are not precurved, Cohen's warns that "every linear outward stroke essentially is a filing stroke" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC].

Evaluation: it "creates the least canal aberrations with K-files" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC], "the most effective hand instrumentation technique" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC], it "has shown to reduce canal transportation and ledging" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], and it extruded less debris than step-back or ultrasonic techniques (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; it is the one technique with evidence of superiority (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]. Roane advised minimum sizes of 45 in curved and 80 in single-rooted teeth (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Hülsmann and Schäfer report this as a claim (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]. Sikri describes Roane's "apical control zone" (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** The balanced force technique was developed by Roane and Sabala (Gopikrishna, 2021) and published with Duncanson in 1985 (Sikri, 2019) for preparing curved canals with hand files. It aims to reduce instrument breakage, minimise canal straightening and eliminate apical zipping (Sikri, 2019).

**Instrument.** Flex-R file: a triangular K-type file with a non-cutting (Batt) tip and rounded transition; flexible K-files or NiTi hand files can also be used. Files are not precurved.

**Principle.** A K-file behaves like a wood screw (Sikri, 2019). A clockwise turn draws it into the canal and engages dentine (preloading); a counterclockwise turn with apical pressure holding the file at the same depth shears off the engaged dentine. Because the tip does not cut and the file is held centred by the balance of forces, it follows the canal curvature rather than straightening it.

**Technique**
1. Insert the file passively to resistance.
2. Rotate clockwise to engage dentine with light apical pressure.
3. Rotate counterclockwise with apical pressure to keep depth; dentine is cut with a characteristic "click".
4. Repeat two or three cycles, advancing apically.
5. Remove with a clockwise rotation to load debris; clean, irrigate, recapitulate.
6. Coronal third first prepared crown-down (Gates-Glidden or orifice shapers); files used in descending sizes, then apical enlargement.

**Rotation angles differ between books**

| Source | Clockwise | Counterclockwise |
|---|---|---|
| Gopikrishna, 2021; Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021 | 90° | 180-270° |
| Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021 | <180° | ≥120° |
| Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019 | 180° | 120° |
| Sikri, 2019 | ¼ turn | ¾ turn |
| Schäfer, 2017 | ¼ turn | ½ turn |

**Apical control zone (Roane):** larger apical preparation (about size 45 in curved, 80 in straight canals) stepped back in 0.5 mm increments to create a resistance form for obturation (Gopikrishna, 2021; Sikri, 2019).

**Advantages:** best centring and least transportation of hand techniques; less ledging; less debris extrusion (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021; Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019).

**Disadvantages:** technique sensitive; straightening if withdrawn repeatedly (each outward stroke is a filing stroke); perforations and fractures reported.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Roane 1985; Flex-R file with non-cutting tip; not precurved.
- Clockwise to engage, counterclockwise with apical pressure to cut, clockwise to remove.
- Best hand technique for centring; claimed size 45 in curved canals.
