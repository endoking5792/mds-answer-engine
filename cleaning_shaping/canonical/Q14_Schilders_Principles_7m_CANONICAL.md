## 1. Schilder's principles

### Explanation

"In 1974, Schilder introduced the concept of “cleaning and shaping” root canals" (Jain, 2016) [SRC], set out as five mechanical and five biological objectives (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; Weine notes Schilder "dubbed these procedures cleansing and shaping" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. "Cleaning" means "removing all contents of the root canal system before and during shaping" (Jain, 2016) [SRC]. Jain lists the shape as "a continuously tapering funnel" that "should maintain the path of the original canal" (Jain, 2016) [SRC]; Grossman's adds the "quality of flow" for plasticised gutta-percha (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], which Sikri calls the "concept of flow" (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Biological lists vary: Ingle's includes space "for intracanal medicaments" and "completion of preparation in one appointment" (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; Grossman's, "creation of sufficient space for optimal obturation" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Sikri, "removal of substrate harboring microorganisms" (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Endodontology quotes Schilder's ideal: "a three-dimensional, continuously tapering cone in multiple planes" (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. These concepts "are still maintained today" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC], though the taper "was aimed at facilitating obturation rather than targeting antimicrobial efficacy" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** In 1974 Herbert Schilder described root canal preparation as "cleaning and shaping": cleaning is the removal of all canal contents before and during shaping; shaping gives the canal a specific form that can be filled in three dimensions (Jain, 2016; Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019).

**Mechanical objectives**
1. A continuously tapering funnel from the apex to the access cavity.
2. Cross-sectional diameter narrower at every point apically.
3. The preparation flows with the shape of the original canal (concept of "flow").
4. The apical foramen stays in its original position.
5. The apical opening is kept as small as practical.

**Biological objectives**
1. Confine instrumentation to the roots themselves.
2. Do not force necrotic debris beyond the foramen.
3. Remove all tissue and debris from the root canal space.
4. Create sufficient space for intracanal medicaments.
5. Complete the preparation in one appointment.
(Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019)

Grossman's replaces items 4 and 5 with space for optimal obturation (Gopikrishna, 2021); Sikri lists removal of the substrate harbouring microorganisms (Sikri, 2019).

**Significance.** Schilder's ideal shape is "a three-dimensional, continuously tapering cone in multiple planes" (Baumann and Beer, 2010). The funnel lets irrigants reach the apex and allows warm gutta-percha to be compacted against an apical matrix without overfilling. The principles remain the basis of modern preparation and are still maintained today, but the uniform taper was designed for obturation more than for disinfection, and preservation of dentine is now added as a further aim (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021).
