## 1. Glide path

### Explanation

Jain quotes West: "a smooth radicular tunnel from the orifice of the canal to the physiologic terminus of the root canal" (Jain, 2016) [SRC]; Grossman's: "creating a patent path up to the working length without altering or blocking the original root canal anatomy" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Cohen's: the term has been used "for more than a decade" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; it "minimizes the so-called screw-in or threading-in effect" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC], because "taper lock may occur if the canal cross section is smaller than the tip of the instrument" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; the canal should be shaped "at least to a #15 size" before rotary NiTi to length (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]. Tests differ: a straight #15 (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC] or a straight, unbent #10 checked from 1-2 and 5-6 mm short (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC]. Ingle's lists objectives, beginning with "early exploration" (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC], and notes reciprocating systems question whether a glide path is needed (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]. Gutmann and Lovdahl add that developing the path yields "additional important information" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC] and use K-files "No. 06 to 20" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC].

On precurving, Grossman's uses "a precurved ISO size 10 or ISO size 8" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], whereas Peters, Arias and Shabahang say glide path files "should not be precurved" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]. Rotary glide path files are used "only after a size #10 K-file is placed to working length" (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC]; they give better anatomy and "less incidence of postoperative pain" (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; Jain defends manual glide paths for tactile control and fracture safety (Jain, 2016) [SRC] while noting stainless steel's cutting tip may ledge curved canals (Jain, 2016) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** A glide path is "a smooth radicular tunnel from the orifice of the canal to the physiologic terminus" (West, in Jain, 2016): a patent path to working length that does not alter the original anatomy (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Objectives:** explore canal length, curvature, width and contents early; prevent taper lock, screw-in and torsional fracture of NiTi rotaries; reduce transportation; give the clinician information about curves, junctions and obstructions (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021; Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019; Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).

**Armamentarium**
- *Manual:* #06, #08, #10, #15 (to #20) stainless steel K-files; C+ / C-Pilot files for calcified canals; Golden Mediums (#12, #17) for the #10-#15 jump (Gopikrishna, 2021; Peters and Arias, 2025).
- *Engine-driven:* PathFiles, Scout RaCe, G-Files (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021); ProGlider (Gopikrishna, 2021); TruNatomy Glider (Peters and Arias, 2025).
- *Adjuncts:* viscous chelator (EDTA gel) or NaOCl, apex locator, magnification.

**Steps**
1. Straight-line access; flood the chamber with irrigant or chelator.
2. Scout the orifice with a #10 K-file to orient (Peters and Arias, 2025); orifice modification.
3. Negotiate with a #08-#10 K-file in watch-winding with light pressure; precurve only if an impediment is felt.
4. Determine working length with the apex locator and radiograph; establish patency.
5. Enlarge to #15 (and #20 if needed) with watch-winding or balanced force; use a #12 or step back #20-#25 if #15 will not reach length (Peters and Arias, 2025).
6. Or, once #10 reaches length, use a rotary glide path file.
7. Confirm: a straight, unbent #10 (Peters and Arias, 2025) or #15 (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) K-file slides passively to length.

**Precurving – books differ:** Grossman's establishes the glide path with a precurved #10 or #08 file (Gopikrishna, 2021); Peters, Arias and Shabahang (2021) state that the final glide path file should not be precurved, so that the rotary that follows will track the same straight path. In practice the negotiating file may be curved to pass an impediment, but the glide path is confirmed with a straight file. Stainless steel is preferred because it gives tactile feedback (Schäfer, 2017).

**Manual versus rotary:** manual gives tactile control and lower fracture risk but may ledge curves (Jain, 2016); rotary preserves anatomy better with less postoperative pain (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- West's definition; at least #15 before NiTi to length.
- Rotary glide path only after #10 reaches length.

1. Curved canal with a #10 then #15 file to length, and a rotary file following the path.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Using rotary glide path files in an unexplored canal.

## MNEMONIC
**"Scout, Negotiate, Measure, Widen, Confirm."**
