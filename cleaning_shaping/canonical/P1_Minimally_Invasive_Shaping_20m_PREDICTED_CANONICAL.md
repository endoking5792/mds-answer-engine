## 1. The question and the position taken

### Explanation

Arguments for: root-filled teeth are lost mainly to fracture (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021; Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC]; pericervical dentine "(4 mm above and below the crestal bone) is crucial" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]; "the more dentin is removed ... the less resistant to fracture the root becomes" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]; excessive flaring reduces strength (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC]; overflared canals are "potentially weaker and subject to fracture during compaction" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]; stainless steel step-back shapes are "larger than needed in the coronal third" (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC]; limited maximum flute diameter is advised (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC], under 1 mm in recent files (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC], as in TruNatomy (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; conservative access may increase fracture resistance (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; 0.3 mm of wall is called critical (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; heat-treated files allow "minimal orifice modification" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC].

Arguments against too small: tapers were set by obturation (spreader 1-2 mm, plugger 3-5 mm from length; Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021; 7% for warm vertical, Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; irrigant acts about 1 mm beyond the needle (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC], so the minimum is about #25/.06 (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC] or #25/.08-#30/.06 (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC]; larger preparations reduce bacteria (Siqueira et al, 2022) [SRC].

Reconciliation: "preparation should dictate the obturation technique" (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]; shaping "should be tooth dependent" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]; size criteria should be irrigant delivery (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]; single-cone hydraulic obturation can work in conservative shapes (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC]; no size predicts outcome (Peters and Arias, 2025; Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; large sizes can pack dentine into anatomy (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC]; a variably tapered #30 can reach 9% taper while a #50 K-file is 2% (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC], and "larger, however, is not always better" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Minimally invasive shaping preserves as much coronal and radicular dentine as possible, especially the pericervical dentine 4 mm above and below the crestal bone, by using conservative access, limited coronal flaring and files with smaller tapers and maximum flute diameters (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021). **Position defended:** yes, preserving pericervical dentine justifies smaller *coronal* tapers, provided the apical third is still shaped enough to deliver irrigants; it does not justify under-preparation of the apical canal.

**Arguments for smaller tapers**
1. Fracture and non-restorable caries, not persistent infection, are now the main causes of loss of root-filled teeth (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021; Peters and Arias, 2025).
2. Pericervical dentine distributes functional stress; finite element studies show lower stresses even when small amounts are kept (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021).
3. Root strength falls as dentine is removed; overflared canals fracture during compaction and restoration (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).
4. Old step-back and Gates-Glidden shapes were larger than needed coronally (Peters and Arias, 2025).
5. Modern tools make conservation possible: heat-treated flexible files needing minimal orifice modification, maximum flute diameters under 1 mm (TruNatomy 0.8 mm), conservative access (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021; Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Arguments for larger tapers**
1. Traditional tapers were needed for obturation: spreader within 1-2 mm, plugger within 3-5 mm of length, at least 7% taper for warm vertical compaction (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021; Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019).
2. Irrigant exchange reaches only about 1 mm beyond the needle tip (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021); too little taper keeps the needle away from the apex.
3. Larger apical preparations reduce bacteria in infected canals (Siqueira et al, 2022).

**Reconciling the two**
- Shaping should be tooth-dependent and set by irrigant delivery, not by the obturation technique (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021); preparation dictates obturation (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019).
- A minimum of about #25/.06 (or #25/.08, #30/.06) admits a 30-gauge needle (Peters and Arias, 2025).
- Variably tapered files give apical taper without coronal excess: a #30 file may carry 9% taper apically while a #50 K-file has only 2% (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).
- No apical size has been shown to give better outcomes (Peters and Arias, 2025); large sizes can pack debris into anatomy.
- Well-executed single-cone obturation with calcium silicate sealers works in conservative shapes (Peters and Arias, 2025).
- Activated irrigation compensates for smaller shapes in isthmuses and fins.

**Limits of the evidence:** support comes mainly from laboratory and finite element studies; long-term clinical trials of minimally invasive shaping are lacking.

**Conclusion.** Healing and retention are both goals (Peters and Arias, 2025). The defensible approach is "small coronal, sufficient apical": conserve pericervical dentine with limited coronal taper, shape the apical third to admit the needle and irrigant, and adapt the tools to the canal (Peters and Arias, 2025).

## 2. A minimally invasive shaping protocol and its risks

### Explanation

Peters, Arias and Shabahang accept early coronal flaring when it is done with a limited-flute-diameter file, because it gives "earlier access of disinfecting irrigation solutions, better tactile control of hand instruments during negotiation" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]. Cohen's notes that canal walls of "1 mm and below" exist before any shaping (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC], so the margin for dentine removal is small. Peters and Arias give practical steps: scout with a #10 before flaring (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC], avoid Gates-Glidden drills on curved orifices such as MB2 (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC], and prepare oval canals as two paths rather than one wide round shape (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC], since a rotating file can cut only a round canal (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**A minimally invasive shaping protocol**
1. *Planning:* CBCT where anatomy is complex, to locate canals so that access need not be enlarged in search of them.
2. *Access:* conservative outline preserving the pericervical dentine and, where possible, part of the chamber roof; magnification and ultrasonic tips to locate orifices.
3. *Orifice:* scout with a #10 K-file first; selective removal of only the restrictive dentine triangle with a small NiTi orifice shaper; no Gates-Glidden drills in curved orifices (Peters and Arias, 2025).
4. *Body:* heat-treated files with small maximum flute diameter and regressive or limited taper; anticurvature movements away from the danger zone, where walls may be 1 mm or less before shaping (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021).
5. *Apex:* shape to at least the size that admits a 30-gauge side-vented needle; gauge and enlarge only if the canal is larger than the file.
6. *Oval canals:* two paths, adaptive files and activated irrigation rather than one large round preparation.
7. *Disinfection:* NaOCl reservoir, long contact, final EDTA, sonic or ultrasonic activation.
8. *Obturation:* single-cone or warm techniques matched to the conservative shape.

**Risks of going too small** [Likely; drawn from the principles above rather than from a single source]
- Residual infected tissue and biofilm in untouched walls and recesses.
- Irrigation needle cannot reach the apical third; debris packing and blockage.
- Missed canals because of a restricted view.
- Difficulty in obturation with lateral or warm vertical compaction.
- Instrument fracture in narrow access because of increased curvature of the file path.
These risks are why minimal shaping must be paired with good irrigation and adequate apical size.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

- Minimum #25/.06 for a 30-gauge needle.
- Evidence mostly in vitro.
