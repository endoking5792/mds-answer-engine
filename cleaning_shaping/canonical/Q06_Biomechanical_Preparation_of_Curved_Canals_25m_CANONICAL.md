## 1. Introduction and assessment of curvature

### Explanation

"There are few straight root canals" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; many curves run buccolingually and are hidden, for example "palatal roots of maxillary molars often have a buccal curve" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. When curvature reaches 30° "the complexity of the case increases markedly" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Weine estimates curvature by the method described "originally by Schneider" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]: lines along the coronal and apical segments meet, and "the interior angle is the estimate of the degree of canal curvature" (Weine, 2004) [SRC], which gives the mesiodistal curve "only without taking into consideration any buccolingual curvature" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Hülsmann and Schäfer stress that a curve is three-dimensional, "with only two planes of the curve visible in the radiograph" (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Most root canals are curved, often in more than one plane, and many curves (palatal root of maxillary molars, lingual curves of incisors) are buccolingual and invisible on a periapical radiograph (Weine, 2004; Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021). The aim of biomechanical preparation in a curved canal is the same as in a straight one, a continuously tapering shape that follows the original canal with the foramen unmoved, but files tend to straighten and the risk of error rises sharply once the curve exceeds 30° (Weine, 2004).

**Assessment of curvature**
- *Schneider's method:* draw a line along the coronal canal and another through the apical segment; the interior angle is the degree of curvature (Weine, 2004). Mild below 30°, moderate 30-60°, severe above 60°.
- Also assess the radius of curvature, the distance of the curve (the "elbow") from the apex, double (S-shaped) curves, and buccolingual curves, using angled radiographs, CBCT and the shape of the first file withdrawn from the canal.

## 2. Problems in curved canals: why canals are transported

### Explanation

Siqueira and colleagues: "when passed along the curvature, the instrument tends to straighten to return to its original shape", pressing on the inner wall coronally and the outer wall apically; "these effects are more pronounced with greater curvatures and with larger and stiffer instruments" (Siqueira et al, 2022) [SRC]. Peters, Arias and Shabahang: "the restoring force of the file ... exceeds the threshold for cutting dentin" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]. Schäfer: straightening increases with file size and curvature (Schäfer, 2017) [SRC].

Weine's plastic block studies showed that after preparation "the narrowest site was never at the apex but was a few millimeters short" (Weine, 2004) [SRC], an "hourglass shape" (Weine, 2004) [SRC], because "each file attempted to straighten within the canal, whether precurved or not" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; he named the narrow site "the elbow" and the wider apical area "the zip" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. With stainless steel, transportation occurs beyond a No. 20 file (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC] or "already begins with a No. 25 file" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]; the two statements sit in the same chapter. In multirooted teeth the inner (furcal) wall is thin: the "danger zone" is closer to the canal than the outer "safety zone" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]. Siqueira and colleagues point out that the old advice to keep curved canals small "has nothing to do with the minimum sizes needed to promote cleaning" but with stainless steel's limits (Siqueira et al, 2022) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Mechanism of transportation**
- A file bent around a curve stores a restoring force; as it tries to straighten it cuts more on the inner wall coronal to the curve and on the outer wall apical to it (Siqueira et al, 2022; Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021).
- The effect grows with curvature, file size and stiffness (Schäfer, 2017); stainless steel transports curved canals from about size 20-25 (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021).

**Resulting errors (detail in Procedural Errors topic)**
- *Elbow:* the narrowest part of the preparation, a few millimetres short of the apex, giving an hourglass shape.
- *Zip:* teardrop-shaped widening of the apex towards the outer curve (Weine, 2004).
- *Apical perforation* from continued zipping; *ledge*; *loss of working length*; *blockage*.
- *Strip perforation* of the thin inner (furcal) "danger zone" of mesial roots of molars during coronal flaring (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021).
- *Instrument fracture* at the point of maximum flexion, usually the middle of the curve (Jain, 2016).

## 3. Principles of preparing curved canals

### Explanation

Enlarging curved canals beyond a No. 25 "can be done only when an adequate coronal flare is developed" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]; small curved canals should be enlarged no more than "three sizes larger than the IAF" (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]. Weine's principles: "it is best to enter canals only with files that have been precurved" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; early flaring removes "the triangle of dentin that often protects the orifice of the curved canal" (Weine, 2004) [SRC] and "reducing the curvature lessens the chances" of zipping (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; Hülsmann and Schäfer agree that flaring outside the curve "can reduce the degree of the curvature" (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]. Working length must be rechecked "for every three increments of file width" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. After each larger file used short of length, a small file goes back to length (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Weine's view of crown-down in curves is critical: "larger, less flexible files stay straight and often will ledge and block the apical portion" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; he uses a "temporary master apical file (TMAF)" between flaring files (Weine, 2004) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Principles**
1. **Straight-line access and early coronal flaring:** remove the dentine triangle over the orifice and flare away from the curve; this reduces the angle and increases the radius of the curve (Weine, 2004; Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019). Keep Gates-Glidden drills coronal to the curve (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021).
2. **Anticurvature filing:** direct files towards the bulky outer (safety) wall and away from the thin furcal (danger) wall (Gopikrishna, 2021).
3. **Precurved, small stainless steel files** to negotiate, with a gradual curve along the flutes and a directional stop (Weine, 2004).
4. **Glide path:** a straight #10-#15 K-file passing smoothly to length before rotary NiTi (Peters and Arias, 2025; Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021).
5. **Incremental enlargement:** intermediate sizes (clipped files, Golden Mediums); never force or ream a binding file.
6. **Limit apical enlargement** in proportion to the curve and instrument flexibility; with stainless steel do not exceed three sizes above the initial apical file (Peters, Arias and Shabahang, 2021).
7. **Remeasure** working length after every three sizes, because straightening shortens it (Weine, 2004).
8. **Recapitulate** with a small file to length after every larger instrument; maintain patency.
9. **Copious irrigation and lubrication.**

## 4. Techniques for curved canals

### Explanation

**Balanced force.** Grossman's: it "has shown to reduce canal transportation and ledging" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Peters, Peters and Basrani: general agreement that it creates the least aberrations (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]; Roane claimed sizes of "45 in curved canals and 80 in straight canals" (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC]. Hülsmann and Schäfer note that BF is the only technique with evidence of less straightening (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019) [SRC].

**Weine's customised files.** To prevent zipping, he removes flutes from the outer side of a clipped, precurved file near its tip: "I drag the nail file against the outer portion of the precurved endodontic file to remove the cutting flutes" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; above 30° "flute removal is mandatory, in my opinion" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. To remove the elbow, "all that is needed is to provide adequate canal flaring" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Non-ISO tapers should not be used "to the apex of curved canals due to the problem of file breakage" (Weine, 2004) [SRC], at "between 300 and 350 rpm" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Above 60°, "it is difficult to enlarge these canals safely apically beyond size 25" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. In bayonet canals: protect the apical curve and straighten the coronal one (Weine, 2004) [SRC], and keep the first file in the canal "until it is very loose" (Weine, 2004) [SRC].

**Flexible instruments.** Weine: "the more flexible files will alter canal shape less" (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; three lines of development: design, metal, and non-zipping tips (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Siqueira and colleagues: a no. 45 NiTi bends like a no. 25 stainless steel file (Siqueira et al, 2022) [SRC]; heat-treated files improve centring (Jain, 2016) [SRC]. Peters and Arias add staging: divide very curved canals "in two" (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC], and if a glide path cannot be secured use hand versions of rotary files (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Techniques**

*A. Hand techniques with stainless steel*
- **Step-back with precurved files:** apical preparation to a modest master apical file, then 0.5-1 mm steps back with recapitulation; keeps stiff large files away from the apical curve.
- **Balanced force (Roane, 1985):** non-precurved Flex-R or K-files rotated clockwise to engage and counterclockwise with apical pressure to cut; best centring and least transportation of hand techniques (Gopikrishna, 2021; Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021); Roane claimed size 45 in curved canals.
- **Crown-down / double flare:** coronal flaring first; Weine warns that large stiff files in a crown-down sequence may ledge the curve, so a small file must go to length between them (Weine, 2004).
- **Weine's customised-file technique:** early flaring with non-ISO tapers to the elbow; a clipped, precurved temporary master apical file with flutes removed on the outer side near the tip (30°+) used to length after each flaring file; apical enlargement with flexible files to #25-30; step-back flaring (Weine, 2004).
- **Anticurvature filing** for mesial roots of molars.

*B. Engine-driven NiTi*
- Glide path first; crown-down or single-length sequence; light pecking; flexible heat-treated files (CM, Blue, Gold) that respect the curve; reciprocation reduces cyclic stress.
- Use each file for a short time; discard after severely curved canals; fracture occurs at the point of maximum flexion (Jain, 2016).

**Management by degree of curvature (Weine, 2004)**

| Curvature | Approach |
|---|---|
| Below 30° | Routine step-back or NiTi; flexible files apically |
| 30-60° | Early flaring to the elbow; precurved, clipped files with outer flutes removed apically; apical size #25-30 |
| Above 60° | 0.5 mm clips, many intermediate sizes; apical size usually not beyond #25; experienced operator |
| S-shaped | Straighten the coronal curve, protect the apical curve; keep the first file in until loose; prepare in two stages (Peters and Arias, 2025) |

## 5. Obturation of curved canals

### Explanation

Peters and Arias describe the tapered apical "resistance form" created by NiTi, which "allows a more predictable filling technique" than an apical stop (Peters and Arias, 2025) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Obturation:** a tapered resistance form at the apex (Peters and Arias, 2025); a master cone with apical tug-back; lateral compaction with a NiTi spreader or warm vertical compaction; carrier-based or single-cone hydraulic techniques follow curves well. Avoid overfilling a zipped apex.

**Conclusion.** Curved canals are prepared safely by assessing the curve, flaring coronally to reduce it, negotiating with precurved small files and a glide path, enlarging gradually with flexible instruments and balanced force or NiTi, limiting apical size to what the curve allows, and rechecking length.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

- Schneider's method: interior angle between coronal and apical axes; mesiodistal only.
- Restoring force → inner wall cut coronally, outer wall apically → elbow and zip.
- Stainless steel transports from about #20-25; beyond #25 only with coronal flare.
- Small curved canals: no more than three sizes above initial apical file.
- Remeasure every three sizes.
- Balanced force: least transportation of hand techniques.
- Weine: remove outer flutes near the tip for curves of 30°+; above 60° rarely beyond #25.
- NiTi #45 bends like stainless steel #25.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

- Writing only "use NiTi" without principles.
- Omitting assessment of curvature and buccolingual curves.
- Forgetting remeasurement of working length.
- Using large stiff files to length in a curve.

## MNEMONIC

**"CURVE":** **C**oronal flaring; **U**se precurved small files; **R**emeasure length; **V**ery gradual (incremental) enlargement; **E**ngage flexible instruments (NiTi, balanced force).
