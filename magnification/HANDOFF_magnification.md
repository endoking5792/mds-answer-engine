## Disagreements kept side by side (not ranked)
- First dental use of the microscope: 1975 (Baumann and Beer) vs 1977 (Rubinstein; Lins et al) vs 1978 (Rubinstein, start of development; Bharti and Bhasin) vs 1981 (Sikri; Grossman's).
- Nylen 1921: monocular (Rubinstein; Baumann and Beer) vs binocular (Bharti and Bhasin).
- Carr: ergonomic DOM 1991 (Grossman's); first publication 1992 (Lins et al; Bharti and Bhasin); began using in 1990 (Rubinstein).
- Mandatory microscopy training: January 1996 decision / January 1997 effective (Rubinstein) vs early 1998 (Lins et al).
- Endoscope rod lens for nonsurgical use: 2.7 mm, 3 cm (Sikri) vs 4 mm, 30°, 4 cm (Lins et al; Rubinstein).
- Orascope canal preparation: coronal half to 80-90 (Sikri) vs cervical 15 mm to #90 (Lins et al).
- Loupe limits: Galilean up to 4.5× (Sikri), 2-4.5× (Grossman's), 2.5-3.5× (Bharti and Bhasin); practical loupe limit ×3.5-×4.5 (Rubinstein); maximum 6× (Sikri).
- Magnification ranges: Grossman's 2.5-5 / 5-12 / 12-20×; Rubinstein (surgery) 2.5-8 / 10-16 / 20-30×; Baumann and Beer want capacity to ×30.
- Objective lens: 200 mm recommended (Sikri; Rubinstein) vs longest the operator's height allows (Lins et al); Grossman's lists 200/250/300.
- Operator position: 12 o'clock for almost all teeth (Sikri) vs 12 o'clock maxilla, 8-9 o'clock mandible (Baumann and Beer).
- Light source: xenon/metal halide/halogen (Sikri), LED (Grossman's), xenon 420 klx vs halogen 100 klx (Baumann and Beer).
