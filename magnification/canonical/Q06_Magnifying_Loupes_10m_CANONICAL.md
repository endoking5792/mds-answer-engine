## Explanation

Loupes were introduced to surgery by Saemisch in 1876 (Rubinstein, 2017) [SRC]. Types: simple, compound, Galilean and prism; simple loupes have spherical and chromatic aberration; achromatic compound lenses give colour-perfect images; Galilean loupes are limited to 2.5-3.5× with a blurred periphery; prism loupes contain Pechan or Schmidt prisms (Bharti and Bhasin, 2020) [SRC]. Grossman's: Galilean 2-4.5×, most recommended; Keplerian up to 6×; flip-up versus TTL (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Sikri: Galilean up to 4.5×, prism up to 6×; less than 25° head tilt; fixed magnification; headgear light casts shadows (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Rubinstein: prism loupes are low-power telescopes and the most advanced loupes; practical limit ×3.5-×4.5; headlamp up to four times operatory light; convergence fatigue (Rubinstein, 2017) [SRC]. Ritter et al: 2-4× for most; 2-2.5× for quadrants; working distance 13-14 in; lower power means more depth; higher power is heavier; LED headlamps (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Resolution: 80 µm at 2.5× and 50 µm at 6× (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Loupes are binocular magnifiers: two monocular lens systems mounted side by side on spectacles or a headband, angled inwards (convergent) to focus on one point. They were introduced to surgery by Saemisch in 1876 (Rubinstein, 2017). They improve resolution from 200 µm (the eye) to about 80 µm at 2.5× and 50 µm at 6× (Sikri, 2019).

**Types by optics:**

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| Simple (single-lens, diopter) | Cheap; spherical and chromatic aberration (Bharti and Bhasin, 2020) |
| Compound | Several lenses with air spaces; achromatic, colour-correct image |
| Galilean | Two to three lenses, light; 2-4.5× (Gopikrishna, 2021); limited to 2.5-3.5× with a blurred periphery (Bharti and Bhasin, 2020) |
| Prism (Keplerian) | Most advanced; low-power telescopes with Pechan or Schmidt prisms; larger field, more depth, longer working distance; up to 6×; heavier (Rubinstein, 2017; Gopikrishna, 2021) |

**Types by mounting (Gopikrishna, 2021):**
- **Flip-up:** IPD and declination adjustable, shareable, cheaper, heavier.
- **Through-the-lens (TTL):** custom-made for one operator, fixed, lighter, dearer.

**Selection parameters (Ritter et al, 2019):**
- **Power:** most dentists use 2-4×; 2-2.5× shows multiple quadrants.
- **Working distance:** about 13-14 in (33-35 cm) for average height.
- **Depth of focus:** greater at lower power.
- Declination angle and IPD.
- Integrated LED headlamp.

**Advantages:**
- inexpensive and easy to learn;
- posture improved, with less than 25° of forward head tilt (Sikri, 2019);
- stereoscopic image;
- ideal for training before moving to the microscope (Bharti and Bhasin, 2020).

**Limitations:**
- one fixed magnification (Sikri, 2019);
- practical limit of ×3.5-×4.5 (Rubinstein, 2017), maximum 6× (Sikri, 2019);
- heavier at higher power;
- headgear light can cast shadows;
- convergent optics cause eye fatigue in long procedures (Rubinstein, 2017);
- no documentation.

A fibre-optic headlamp raises light up to four times that of the operatory light (Rubinstein, 2017).

**Conclusion.** Loupes are the most common and most affordable magnification aid. Prism loupes at 3.5-4.5× with an LED headlamp suit most restorative and nonsurgical work. Work needing higher, variable magnification or documentation calls for the operating microscope.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Galilean 2-4.5×; prism up to 6×; practical limit ×3.5-×4.5.
- TTL = custom and light; flip-up = adjustable and heavier.
- Working distance 13-14 in; head tilt under 25°.
