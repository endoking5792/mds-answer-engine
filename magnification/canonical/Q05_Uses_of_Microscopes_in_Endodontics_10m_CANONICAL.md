## Explanation

The unaided eye resolves 0.2 mm (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]; the microscope gives coaxial, shadow-free light and parallel optics (Gopikrishna, 2021; Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Uses: caries and cracks, MB2 73% to 93%, middle mesial canals, sealer and dryness checks, retreatment and post cement, isthmus and osteotomy size, documentation and ergonomics (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]; vertical fracture on the canal wall and surgical success above 90% against below 60% (Lins et al, 2016) [SRC]; troughing (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Effectiveness and limits: endodontists' use of the microscope at 90% by 2008; access is not improved (Rubinstein, 2017) [SRC]; slower procedures, learning curve and cost (Sikri, 2019; Lins et al, 2016; Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** The eye resolves only 0.2 mm (Sikri, 2019). The dental operating microscope gives variable magnification of about 2-20×, coaxial shadow-free light and parallel (Galilean) optics, so the operator can see detail and work upright (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Levels used (Gopikrishna, 2021):**
- low (2.5-5×): examination and initial access;
- medium (5-12×): most procedures;
- high (12-20×): used sparingly, e.g. instrument retrieval.

**Uses:**
1. **Diagnosis:**
   - incipient caries and enamel and floor cracks (Sikri, 2019);
   - vertical root fracture seen on the canal wall, avoiding exploratory surgery (Lins et al, 2016).
2. **Access and canal location:**
   - pulp stones removed and conservative access;
   - MB2 detection rose from 73% to 93% (Sikri, 2019);
   - middle mesial canals found more often;
   - calcified canals traced by ultrasonic troughing (Gopikrishna, 2021).
3. **Cleaning, shaping and obturation:** remnants removed; dryness and sealer distribution checked (Sikri, 2019).
4. **Retreatment and mishaps:** ledges bypassed, separated instruments removed, perforations repaired, and post cement removed, all more predictably (Sikri, 2019).
5. **Surgery:**
   - isthmus, fins and lateral canals seen on the resected root;
   - osteotomy of about 0.5 cm against more than 1.0 cm (Sikri, 2019).
6. **Documentation:** education, referral and legal records.
7. **Ergonomics:** upright, neutral posture (Sikri, 2019).

**How effective are they?**

| Evidence in the books | Comment |
|---|---|
| MB2 73% → 93% (Sikri, 2019) | Better detection of anatomy |
| Surgery above 90% vs below 60% (Lins et al, 2016) | Includes ultrasonics and new materials, not magnification alone |
| 90% of endodontists used them by 2008 (Rubinstein, 2017) | Accepted standard |

**Limits:**
- slower at first (Sikri, 2019);
- steep learning curve (Lins et al, 2016);
- high cost (Baumann and Beer, 2010);
- does not improve physical access (Rubinstein, 2017);
- four-handed work needed.

**Conclusion.** Microscopes are highly effective for seeing and treating complex anatomy, retreatment and surgery. Their benefit depends on training and on use with ultrasonics and micro-instruments. Endodontic specialty programmes now teach their use as standard, and every endodontist is expected to use one (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- MB2 73% → 93%.
- Microsurgery: osteotomy 0.5 cm; >90% vs <60%.
- Coaxial light, parallel optics, 2-20×.
