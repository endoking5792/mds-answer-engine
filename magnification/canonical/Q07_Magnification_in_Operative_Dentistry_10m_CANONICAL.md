## ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Operative dentistry needs fine detail (margins, minute caries, cracks), but the eye resolves only 0.2 mm, and bending forward to see causes neck strain and Tension Neck syndrome (Sikri, 2019). Some form of magnification is recommended for every dentist providing restorative care (Hilton et al, 2013). Magnification must go with good illumination (Bharti and Bhasin, 2020).

**Devices:**
1. **Loupes** (1.5-10×; most dentists use 2-4×). Lower power (2-2.5×) shows several quadrants; higher power (3-4×) shows a few teeth, but is heavier, narrower and needs more light (Ritter et al, 2019; Bharti and Bhasin, 2020).
   - Types: single-lens diopter (up to 2.5×), Galilean and prismatic binocular loupes. Telescopes are among the finest magnifiers (Hilton et al, 2013).
   - Mounting: flip-up, or fixed through-the-lens.
   - Working distance: 13-14 in for average height (Ritter et al, 2019); Summitt's advises 10-14 in.
   - Small LED headlamps add light.
2. **Dental operating microscope:** 2-40× (Bharti and Bhasin, 2020); typically 2.5-20× in five or six steps (Ritter et al, 2019).

**Uses in operative dentistry:**
- **Caries diagnosis:** visual caries detection improves by 50% with a prism loupe or microscope (Hilton et al, 2013); incipient lesions are seen (Sikri, 2019).
- Detecting enamel cracks and checking that caries removal is complete.
- **Minimally invasive preparation:** minimal removal of sound tooth structure (Ritter et al, 2019).
- **Restoration quality:** finishing porcelain margins, adaptation of margins, removal of excess cement and adhesive.
- Inspection of old restorations for marginal gaps and secondary caries.
- **Ergonomics:** upright posture, less fatigue; no prescription spectacles needed with the microscope (Bharti and Bhasin, 2020).
- Documentation and patient communication; an internal marketing tool.

**Limitations:** cost, equipment size and perceived lack of value have limited microscope use in operative practice (Ritter et al, 2019). Loupes have fixed power and a learning curve.

**Training.** Students should first learn with loupes, which is now standard in preclinical courses, before moving to the microscope (Bharti and Bhasin, 2020).

**Conclusion.** Magnification with good light lets the operative dentist diagnose earlier, prepare more conservatively and finish restorations more precisely, while protecting posture. Loupes are the everyday standard; the microscope is for detail-oriented work.
