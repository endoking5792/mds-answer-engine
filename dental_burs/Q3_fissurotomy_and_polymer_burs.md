## STUDY MODE

### Fissurotomy burs
Fissurotomy burs (SS White, trademarked "Fissurotomy") are **long, narrow-tapered tungsten-carbide burs** purpose-designed for **minimally invasive management of pit-and-fissure caries**, within the broader minimal-intervention-dentistry philosophy. The design rationale is to open and explore a suspicious occlusal fissure, and remove only the caries-infected enamel/dentin within it, in a single pass — conserving sound tooth structure that a conventional round or inverted-cone bur (e.g., the standard No. 557 carbide fissure bur) would remove unnecessarily. [LIT] [Likely]

Three size variants exist by tip/head width: the **Original** (≈1.1 mm width), the **Micro NTF** ("narrow taper fissure," ≈0.6–0.7 mm, for a less invasive groove), and the **Micro STF** ("shallow taper fissure," ≈0.6 mm, intended for pit/fissure exploration in primary teeth and premolars, and for enameloplasty) (SS White/distributor product data). [LIT] [Likely]

Manufacturer literature claims the bur removes substantially less sound tooth structure than a conventional carbide fissure bur in the same clinical task — a frequently quoted figure is roughly 90% less tooth structure removed versus a standard 557 bur — but this figure comes from manufacturer-linked sources and was not independently verified in this search; state it as a manufacturer claim, not an established comparative trial finding. [LIT] [Guessing]

Clinically, the technique is: pass the fissurotomy bur once along the length of a stained/suspicious fissure to open it, inspect with an explorer/magnification/caries-detector dye, and restore (sealant if no caries found beyond enamel, or a preventive resin restoration/conservative composite if dentinal caries is confirmed) — this is the operative counterpart of the "stop ≤1/3 enamel depth, cavosurface angle ≤100°" enameloplasty criterion taught for sealant/PRR cases. ### Polymer burs
Polymer burs (e.g., SmartPrep, Polybur P1/PlusCare) are **self-limiting, chemo-mechanical-adjacent excavation burs** made of a medical-grade plastic — commercially, polyether-ketone-ketone (PEKK) is cited — manufactured with a **Knoop hardness (~50 KHN) deliberately intermediate between carious dentin (0–30 KHN) and sound/healthy dentin (70–90 KHN)**. [LIT] [Likely]

The design intent ("dentin-safe," self-limiting) is that the bur's cutting edges — straight and shovel-shaped rather than the spiral flutes of a steel/carbide excavating bur — abrade and remove soft, demineralized carious dentin efficiently, but **progressively blunt and lose cutting ability on contact with firmer, sound dentin**, so the operator does not need to judge excavation endpoint by "feel" alone; the bur itself limits over-excavation. [LIT] [Likely]

Evidence on actual performance is mixed, and this should be stated as a genuine, unresolved point rather than smoothed over: in-vitro selectivity studies have validated that polymer burs remove less sound dentin than conventional rotary excavation, but at least one comparative study found a polymer bur (SmartPrep) less effective than a tungsten-carbide bur at removing carious dentin, and bond-strength testing on the dentin surface left behind by polymer-bur excavation has shown **reduced adhesive bond strength** compared with conventionally excavated dentin — a clinically relevant trade-off against the hard-tissue-conservation benefit. [LIT] [Likely]

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
Textbook/manufacturer framing presents both instruments as straightforwardly minimally-invasive upgrades over conventional burs. Retrieved evidence partially supports this (in-vitro selectivity data for polymer burs; the conservative tip-width rationale for fissurotomy burs) but also complicates it: one in-vitro comparison found a polymer bur *less* effective at carious-dentin removal than tungsten carbide, and bond strength to polymer-bur-excavated dentin was lower in at least one study. The manufacturer "91% less tooth structure removed" claim for fissurotomy burs has no independently sourced trial evidence behind it in this search. **What this supports:** both instruments are legitimate, purpose-built options within minimal-intervention dentistry with a plausible mechanistic rationale. **What it does not support:** treating either as unconditionally superior to conventional excavation/preparation on hard outcome data — the in-vitro evidence is real but mixed, and the commonly quoted comparative percentage for fissurotomy burs is unverified marketing-adjacent material, not a cited trial figure. ## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

**I. Fissurotomy burs**
- Long, narrow-tapered tungsten-carbide burs for minimally invasive fissure caries management.
- Sizes: Original (~1.1mm), Micro NTF (~0.6-0.7mm), Micro STF (~0.6mm, exploration/enameloplasty).
- Single-pass technique: open fissure → inspect (explorer/dye/magnification) → restore (sealant/PRR/conservative composite) by depth of caries found.
- Rationale: conserve sound tooth structure vs. conventional round/557 fissure burs.

**II. Polymer burs**
- Self-limiting excavation burs (e.g., SmartPrep, Polybur P1), medical-grade plastic (PEKK), Knoop hardness ≈50 (intermediate between carious ~0-30 and sound ~70-90 dentin).
- Shovel-shaped (not spiral) cutting edges; blunt on contact with firm sound dentin → self-limiting excavation.
- Evidence: selective in some in-vitro studies, but at least one study found lower efficacy than carbide, and lower post-excavation bond strength reported.

**III. Common thread:** both are minimal-intervention-dentistry instruments conserving sound tooth structure — fissurotomy burs at the prevention/diagnosis stage, polymer burs at the excavation-endpoint stage.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Fissurotomy bur = tapered tungsten-carbide, minimally-invasive fissure exploration/caries removal; 3 sizes (Original/Micro NTF/Micro STF).
- Polymer bur = medical-grade plastic (PEKK), Knoop hardness ~50, self-limiting between carious and sound dentin.
- Both belong to minimal-intervention dentistry.
- Polymer bur evidence is mixed — not universally superior to carbide excavation; lower bond strength reported on excavated surface in some studies.
- Manufacturer "91% less tooth removed" claim for fissurotomy burs is unverified, not a cited trial result.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Calling the polymer bur "chemo-mechanical" (it is purely mechanical/hardness-based self-limiting, not a chemical softening agent like Carisolv).
- Omitting the specific hardness rationale (why 50 KHN sits between carious and sound dentin) — this is the examinable mechanism.
- Presenting either instrument as having unambiguous trial-proven superiority rather than acknowledging mixed in-vitro evidence.
