## STUDY MODE

### S1. Concept [orientation]
Minimal intervention dentistry (MID) means care that deals with the cause of disease and rests on prevention and control; minimal invasive dentistry is the conservative restorative part of that plan [SRC:G57]. The old teaching of "extension for prevention" became "prevention for extension" [SRC:G57]. The core principles are recognising risk factors, minimising them (diet, saliva, pH) and remineralising early lesions with fluoride or CPP-ACP [SRC:G58].

### S2. What the restorative part needs from a material [basic science]
When the aim is to keep sound and remineralisable tissue, the material should: bond without needing mechanical retention [SRC:G63]; seal against bacteria and so stop caries beneath it [SRC:A354]; release fluoride and ideally be rechargeable [SRC:G64]; be easy to place in a small, awkward cavity with little or no special equipment [SRC:G61]; tolerate moisture and be biocompatible [SRC:A332] [SRC:G18]; and be strong enough for its site [SRC:G64]. No material meets all of them, and adhesive GIC is the closest for low-load sites, with composite and bonded amalgam where strength matters [SRC:G63]. [Likely]

### S3. How the types of GIC fit [classification]
- **High-viscosity (Fuji IX type) for ART:** hand excavation then adhesive GIC, with undermined dentine becoming supported and no lining needed [SRC:G59] [SRC:G60]. Pickard says the provisional restoration need not be fully replaced as arrested caries-affected dentine is sealed from nutrients [SRC:C286].
- **Type II.2 reinforced:** tunnel and Class II minimal restorations, with a radiopaque capsulated cement [SRC:A356] [SRC:A357].
- **Type II.1 and resin-modified:** aesthetic minimal Class III and V; resin-modified GIC for transitional restorations [SRC:C279].
- **Type I luting:** Hall technique crowns on carious primary molars with no excavation [SRC:G62].
- **Type III liner or base and sealant:** GIC as dentine sealant and fissure sealant, especially where moisture is hard to control [SRC:A320] [SRC:A332].

### S4. Mount's minimal cavity designs [treatment]
Because GIC bonds, cavities can be minimal: remove infected dentine only, leave affected dentine on the axial floor if the margin is sealed, and let demineralised enamel remineralise with fluoride [SRC:A352] [SRC:A353] [SRC:A354]. The tunnel is entered from the occlusal fossa medial to the marginal ridge, without retention, and filled with a radiopaque cement in at least two increments [SRC:A356] [SRC:A360]. Tunnel restorations showed apparently remineralised enamel at 3 years in the author's cases [SRC:A362]. The minimal concept applies only to new lesions in early dentine involvement and is a first stage that precedes conventional design [SRC:A326].

### S5. Evidence and limits [prognosis]
A systematic review of 14 RCTs found ART with high-viscosity GIC did not differ significantly from conventional restorations in single-surface cavities and primary teeth, with only 2 of 14 trials at low risk of bias [LIT:Frencken2021]. Sikri notes single-surface ART restorations succeed better than multisurface ones and that operator effects are marked [SRC:G59]. ART needs maintenance care [SRC:C1135].

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 34 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Minimal intervention dentistry (MID) is a patient-care philosophy that treats the cause of disease and rests on prevention and control; minimal invasive dentistry is its conservative restorative component [SRC:G57].

### 2. Principles
1. Recognise risk factors (caries risk assessment) [SRC:G58].
2. Minimise them: reduce cariogenic diet, manage saliva and pH [SRC:G58].
3. Remineralise early lesions with fluoride or CPP-ACP [SRC:G58].
4. Remove only infected tissue; keep remineralisable tissue [SRC:G57].
5. Restore minimally with adhesive materials; repair rather than replace [SRC:G57] [SRC:G63].

### 2A. Traditional against minimal intervention approach
| Feature | Traditional | Minimal intervention |
|---|---|---|
| Philosophy | extension for prevention | prevention for extension [SRC:G57] |
| Focus | filling teeth | cause and risk of disease [SRC:G57] |
| Caries removal | extend to self-cleansing areas | infected dentine only; keep affected, remineralisable tissue [SRC:A354] |
| Cavity | mechanical retention, resistance form | no retention needed for adhesive materials [SRC:G63] |
| Follow-up | replace when failed | repair, maintain and recall [SRC:G57] |

### 3. Techniques of minimal invasive removal
Hand instruments (ART), air abrasion, ultrasonics, chemo-mechanical agents, lasers, and magnification [SRC:G59].

### 4. Ideal restorative material for MID
| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| Chemical adhesion | no mechanical retention cuts [SRC:G63] |
| Bacterial seal | arrests caries beneath [SRC:A354] |
| Fluoride release and recharge | remineralisation [SRC:G64] |
| Easy placement, moisture tolerance | small cavities, little equipment [SRC:G61] [SRC:A332] |
| Biocompatibility | placed near pulp [SRC:G18] |
| Adequate strength | site-dependent [SRC:G64] |
Examples of adhesive materials: GIC, resin-modified GIC, composite with bonding agents, bonded amalgam [SRC:G63]. This list is a synthesis from several sources. [Likely]

### 5. Types of GIC in MID
| Type | Use in MID |
|---|---|
| High-viscosity, Type II.2 | ART, tunnel and Class II minimal restorations [SRC:G59] [SRC:A356] |
| Type II.1 and resin-modified | aesthetic Class III and V, transitional restorations [SRC:C279] |
| Type I luting | Hall technique crowns [SRC:G62] |
| Type III lining, base | dentine sealant under restorations [SRC:A320] |
| GIC sealant | pit and fissure sealing, especially when moisture control is difficult [SRC:A332] [SRC:C280] |

### 6. Atraumatic restorative treatment
1. Isolate with cotton rolls; access with hatchet or sharp excavator.
2. Remove undermined enamel and excavate soft dentine.
3. Clean; condition with weak polyacrylic acid.
4. Fill with GIC, including confluent fissures; press with a petroleum-jelly-coated finger.
5. Cover with varnish or petroleum jelly [SRC:G60].
**Advantages:** no equipment, no electricity, low cost, minimal discomfort, fluoride effect [SRC:G61]. **Evidence:** no significant difference from conventional restorations in single-surface cavities [LIT:Frencken2021].

### 6A. Single-step and stepwise caries removal
Pickard describes single-step ART with chemically adhesive high-viscosity GIC after hand excavation: a better adaptive seal, and arrested caries-affected dentine stays sealed from nutrients [SRC:C286]. Systematic reviews are cited that one-stage partial caries removal gives significantly fewer pulp exposures than two-step stepwise excavation [SRC:C288].

### 6B. GIC sealants in the minimal intervention plan
GIC sealants are recommended for caries-active individuals (long-term fluoride release and a persistent ion-exchange layer after bulk loss), partially erupted teeth, difficult moisture control and when no light is available [SRC:A332]. A weak low-powder mix is contraindicated because of wear [SRC:A328].

### 7. Mount's minimal cavity designs
- Tunnel (occlusal approach) for Class II: enter medial to the marginal ridge; no retention; radiopaque cement [SRC:A356].
- Proximal approach only when the lesion becomes accessible during adjacent preparation [SRC:A357].
- Buccal or lingual tunnel in older patients [SRC:A358].
- Remove infected dentine; leave affected dentine if sealed [SRC:A354].

### 8. Transitional and stabilising restorations
Rampant caries: GIC or resin-modified GIC strengthens remaining tooth and removes the bacterial reservoir [SRC:C2123]; interim therapeutic restoration uses conventional or resin-modified GIC with maintenance [SRC:C1134].

### 8A. Preventive restorations (Pickard)
- **Preventive resin restoration:** minimal excavation of occlusal caries to the enamel-dentine junction, restoration with composite or GIC, then an overlying fissure sealant [SRC:C2125].
- **GIC fissure sealant protocol:** debride, wash and dry; condition enamel with 10% polyacrylic acid for 15 seconds; wash and dry for 10 seconds; apply GIC or RM-GIC; auto-cure or light-cure [SRC:C2101] [SRC:C2129].
- **Moisture-tolerant interim cover:** partially erupted molars can be protected with a light-cured glass-ionomer varnish [SRC:C1131].

### 9. Limitations
Single-surface better than multisurface; operator-dependent; not for heavy load; needs maintenance and recall [SRC:G59] [SRC:C128].

### 10. Conclusion
MID shifts the focus from filling teeth to controlling disease; adhesive, fluoride-releasing GIC fits it well in low-load sites, and its types are matched to ART, tunnels, transitional restorations, luting and sealing. Success depends on case selection, moisture control, protection of the cement and recall care.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- MID = cause-based care; minimal invasive = conservative restorative part [SRC:G57].
- ART: hand excavation plus high-viscosity GIC; first tried in the mid 1980s [SRC:G59].
- Hall technique: GIC-luted metal crown, no excavation [SRC:G62].
- Tunnel: enter medial to the marginal ridge [SRC:A356].
- Ideal material: adhesive, fluoride-releasing, moisture-tolerant [SRC:G63] [SRC:G64].
