## STUDY MODE

### S1. How to organise "advances"
Every advance answers one of GIC's weaknesses: slow set and early water sensitivity, low strength and wear, poor aesthetics, and a fluoride benefit that is debated. A good answer groups developments by the problem they solve, and says which have clinical evidence and which are laboratory-only [basic science].

### S2. Advances that fix handling and strength
- **Resin-modified GIC** added 15-25% resin (HEMA) so the cement sets by light first and is protected from water earlier [SRC:A201]; **tri-cure** adds a chemical dark cure for bulk placement [SRC:B190].
- **High-viscosity (condensable) GIC**: polyacrylic acid added to the powder and a smaller grain size (about 3 micrometres) give amalgam-like handling and better abrasion resistance, though strength is little changed; examples Fuji IX, Ketac Molar, Chemflex [SRC:B262]; used with minimal preparation in ART [SRC:B197].
- **Fast-setting materials** reach a carvable stage in about 90 seconds; developments aim at ceramic nano-fillers or N-vinyl pyrrolidone polyacids for load-bearing use [SRC:C270].
- **Heat-assisted auto-cure**: a dye absorbs heat from the curing light to speed the acid-base reaction without adding resin [SRC:A206].
- **Reinforcement**: hollow glass fibres (5-10% by weight) improved properties [SRC:G36]; hydroxyapatite or fluorapatite nano-fillers raised compressive, tensile and flexural strength in one commercial GIC [SRC:B296].

### S3. Advances in aesthetics and surface
- **Nano-ionomer** (RMGIC with nanomers and nanoclusters, from 2007): better polish and optics, fluoride release not compromised [SRC:B469]; van Noort says the data are sparse and wear and fluoride claims come from the laboratory only [SRC:B270].
- **Resin coat on high-viscosity GIC** (glass hybrid): one 5-year trial found moderate-to-large two-surface Class II glass hybrid restorations survived as well as nano-hybrid composite (94.5% against 94.4%), with composite ahead on success scores [LIT:Miletic2024].
- **Zirconomer**: zirconium oxide nanoparticles for strength and colour stability, with rough texture and cracks at the zirconium-glass interface [SRC:G39].

### S4. Advances in biological action [treatment of caries]
- **Antibacterial additives**: chlorhexidine (CHX hexametaphosphate gives long release without harm to strength), triclosan, quaternary ammonium salts, nanoparticles of silver, zinc and titanium dioxide, bioactive glass, plant extracts [SRC:G37]; results are equivocal and clinical benefit is unproven [SRC:A444] [LIT:Danelon2025] [LIT:Guo2024].
- **Remineralising and bioactive additives**: hydroxyapatite nanoparticles (8% by weight) [SRC:G27]; hydroxyapatite-modified GIC is promising in the laboratory [LIT:Zhang2025]; CPP-ACP is equivocal [SRC:A444].
- **Fluoride recharge and low-pH "smart" materials** that release fluoride at low pH [SRC:G26] [SRC:G40].
- **Glass carbomer**: acid-washed glass, silicone oil coating and hydroxyapatite; long-term studies did not confirm superior efficacy [SRC:G38].
- **Therapeutic ion release**: Pickard expects more use of ionic exchange to release remineralising and antibacterial ions [SRC:C270].

### S5. What is new but not a true GIC
Alkasite and bioactive resin restoratives, and giomers and compomers, are resin-based [SRC:A208]. Alkasite materials release hydroxide, calcium and fluoride ions and are suited to Class I, II and V cavities [LIT:Bonchev2025].

### S6. Where the evidence stands
A meta-analysis of alkasite studies found performance comparable to composite and "superior" to GIC [LIT:Laporte2026]; one small 2-year RCT found a bioactive resin similar to bulk-fill composite [LIT:Raghip2025]. An in-vitro study found a bioactive resin had lower bond strength to dentine than a resin-modified GIC [LIT:AlHasan2022]. For ART with high-viscosity GIC, a systematic review found no significant survival difference in single-surface cavities [LIT:Frencken2021].

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

### Claim. Do the newer ion-releasing and bioactive materials outperform GIC?
- **Textbook position.** Mount's Atlas says the essential elements of true GIC are the ionic bond and fluoride release, and that formulas which reduce them are not true GICs [SRC:A449]; Pickard expects more ion release in future [SRC:C270].
- **Retrieved evidence.** An alkasite meta-analysis found performance comparable to composite and superior to GIC, but moderate risk of bias in several studies and no pooled numbers in the abstract [LIT:Laporte2026]; a 2-year RCT in 22 patients found a bioactive resin similar to bulk-fill composite [LIT:Raghip2025].
- **Interpretation and limit.** The new materials look promising against GIC, but evidence is short-term, small and abstract-level; the claimed remineralisation is mostly in-vitro. [Likely]
- **Conclusion.** Say they are promising and resin-based, not proven replacements; GIC keeps its place where adhesion, fluoride recharge and moisture tolerance matter.

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 43 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Glass ionomer cement, introduced by Wilson and Kent in the early 1970s, has been modified to overcome brittleness, slow setting, water sensitivity, poor aesthetics and a debated anticaries effect [SRC:G3] [SRC:B263]. The research aim is to increase fracture resistance without losing the ion-exchange adhesion and fluoride release [SRC:A445].

### 2. Advances in handling and setting
- **Resin-modified GIC:** 15-25% resin (HEMA); light-set first, then acid-base reaction [SRC:A201] [SRC:G16].
- **Tri-cure:** adds a chemical (redox) dark cure for bulk placement where light is poor [SRC:B190].
- **Fast-setting:** carvable in about 90 seconds [SRC:C270].
- **Heat-assisted auto-cure:** a dye absorbs heat from the curing light and hastens the reaction, with no added resin [SRC:A206].
- **Capsulated and syringe delivery:** improve mixing consistency and reduce voids [SRC:G46].

### 3. Advances in strength and wear
- **High-viscosity GIC:** polyacrylic acid in the powder, grain size about 3 micrometres; amalgam-like handling; better abrasion resistance; examples Fuji IX, Ketac Molar [SRC:B262]; used in ART [SRC:B197].
- **Metal-modified:** silver cermet and glass cermet [SRC:G30].
- **Fibre reinforcement:** hollow glass fibres 5-10% by weight [SRC:G36].
- **Nano-fillers:** hydroxyapatite or fluorapatite nano-fillers raised strength; titania improved toughness at 3% but higher amounts weakened the cement [SRC:B296].
- **Zirconomer:** zirconia nanoparticles; colour-stable, but rough and prone to interface cracks [SRC:G39].

### 3A. High-viscosity GIC in more detail
- **Advantages:** easily packable and condensable, less early moisture sensitivity, non-sticky, immediate finishing, better wear resistance, low solubility [SRC:B198].
- **Indications by product type:** Fuji VII for deciduous molars and core build-up; Fuji VIII for anterior aesthetic restorations; Fuji IX mostly posterior restorations [SRC:B198].
- **Limit:** strength is little changed from conventional GIC, and GIC is still not recommended for Class II in adults [SRC:B262].
- **Fluoride-rich variant:** Fuji VII is a high-fluoride non-resin auto-cure GIC marketed for caries prevention [SRC:B197].
- **Faster hardening** comes from glass particle size and distribution, and one maker coats the powder with polymer for easier mixing [SRC:B465].

### 4. Advances in aesthetics
- **Nano-ionomer** (since 2007): nanomers and nanoclusters in RMGIC; better polish and optics [SRC:B469]; evidence mostly laboratory [SRC:B270].
- **Resin coat** (glass hybrid): seals and improves wear; moderate Class II survival comparable to composite over 5 years [LIT:Miletic2024].

### 5. Advances in biological action
| Approach | Example | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Antibacterial | chlorhexidine, triclosan, quaternary ammonium, silver and zinc nanoparticles | laboratory, equivocal [SRC:G37] [SRC:A444] |
| Remineralising | hydroxyapatite nanoparticles, CPP-ACP | promising in vitro [SRC:G27] [LIT:Zhang2025] |
| Fluoride recharge | two-part recharge material; low-pH "smart" release | concept [SRC:G26] [SRC:G40] |
| Glass carbomer | acid-washed glass, silicone oil, hydroxyapatite | not confirmed long term [SRC:G38] |

### 5A. Advances in biocompatibility of resin-modified materials
- HEMA released from RMGIC is linked to cytotoxicity in laboratory tests [LIT:Agha2021].
- Experimental RMGICs gave 89-98% cell viability from their extracts against 4% for a commercial RMGIC liner, with higher fluoride release (137 against 88 ppm), but they had lower monomer conversion; this is cell-culture evidence on experimental materials [LIT:Potiprapanpong2023].
- True GIC can be told from resin-based materials by setting in the dark, so the "dark-set" test is a way to classify a new product [SRC:A210].

### 6. Newer materials that are not true GICs
- **Compomer:** resin with glass and carboxylic groups; no ion-exchange bond [SRC:A208] [SRC:G34].
- **Giomer:** pre-reacted glass-ionomer filler in resin [SRC:G35].
- **Alkasite:** hydroxide, calcium and fluoride release; Class I, II and V; comparable to composite and better than GIC in a meta-analysis of studies at moderate risk of bias [LIT:Bonchev2025] [LIT:Laporte2026].
- **Bioactive resin (ACTIVA type):** similar to bulk-fill composite at 2 years in a small RCT; lower dentine bond than RMGIC in vitro [LIT:Raghip2025] [LIT:AlHasan2022].

### 6A. Quick comparison of the main modified forms
| Form | What changed | Main gain | Main limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resin-modified (light, dual, tri-cure) | 15-25% resin added [SRC:A201] | early water resistance, command set | HEMA, shrinkage, water uptake [SRC:B364] |
| High-viscosity | finer glass, polyacid in powder [SRC:B262] | packable, ART | strength little changed |
| Cermet (silver, glass) | metal sintered into glass [SRC:G30] | abrasion resistance | grey colour, interface failure [SRC:G31] |
| Nano-ionomer | nanofillers in RMGIC [SRC:B469] | polish, optics | sparse data [SRC:B270] |
| Glass carbomer, zirconomer | glass or nanoparticle changes [SRC:G38] [SRC:G39] | strength claimed | not confirmed long term |

### 7. Clinical evidence for the advances
- ART with high-viscosity GIC: no significant difference from conventional restorations in single-surface cavities up to 5 years [LIT:Frencken2021].
- Glass hybrid in moderate two-surface Class II: 5-year survival 94.5% against 94.4% for composite [LIT:Miletic2024].
- Antibacterial and nano-additives: laboratory only [LIT:Danelon2025] [LIT:Guo2024].

### 8. Future directions
- Fracture resistance without loss of ion exchange [SRC:A445]; higher molecular weight polyacids and dispersed-phase reinforcement [SRC:A448].
- Amino-acid-modified, non-HEMA GICs and ultrasound-assisted hardening [SRC:E447].
- Bioactive orthodontic RMGIC needing more study [SRC:E450].
- Greater use of ion exchange to release remineralising and antibacterial ions [SRC:C270].

### 9. Conclusion
The main advances are resin modification, high-viscosity and fast-setting materials, nano-fillers, and bioactive or antibacterial additives. Clinical evidence is strongest for high-viscosity GIC in ART and for glass hybrid in moderate cavities; most additive claims remain laboratory findings. For examination, state each advance with the problem it solves, one example, and its level of evidence, and keep resin-based look-alikes separate from true glass ionomers.
