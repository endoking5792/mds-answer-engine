## STUDY MODE

**Terms.** *Osteogenic* materials contain living bone-forming cells (autograft). *Osteoinductive* materials stimulate progenitor cells in the adjacent tissue to form bone; allograft demineralised bone and the BMP family are the examples. *Osteoconductive* materials only provide a scaffold into which bone grows, and are slowly resorbed and remodelled (xenograft, alloplast) (Kinaia et al, 2012; Torabinejad et al, 2019) [SRC]. The two properties can be combined in one graft (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. In Ingle's list of ideal root-end filling properties the word bioinductive means promoting cementogenesis, a different sense from bone induction (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. A 5-mark "bio-inductive materials in root end surgery" answer should therefore cover both senses.

**Why grafts are discussed at all.** The aim of periapical surgery is regeneration, not repair. In a large defect, non-osteogenic connective tissue and epithelium grow into the crypt faster than bone, which results in repair (Torabinejad et al, 2019) [SRC]. Grafts and membranes try to exclude these tissues and keep space for PDL and bone cells (Torabinejad et al, 2019) [SRC]. Graft materials have two roles: a mechanical substructure supporting the membrane and soft tissue, and a biological stimulus to bone formation (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Materials.** Autografts from ramus, chin or iliac crest; allografts such as demineralised freeze-dried bone (DFDBA); xenografts (bovine bone mineral); ceramics and alloplasts (calcium sulfate, hydroxyapatite, bioactive glass); and bioactive proteins such as BMP (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Demineralised bone matrix contains bone morphogenetic proteins and is osteoinductive; mixed with cancellous chips it becomes a syringeable graft that fits a small crypt (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Freeze-dried allograft has been used after removal of periapical lesions, with bone regeneration but not full PDL regeneration (Kinaia et al, 2012) [SRC]. Of the growth factors, BMP-2 has been linked to ankylosis and BMP-7 has not (Kinaia et al, 2012) [SRC]. Enamel matrix derivative (amelogenin) and PDGF-BB are safe, well studied mediators with short half-lives; their effect on endodontic surgery is still a proposal rather than established practice (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC].

**Membranes.** Resorbable membranes are preferred because a second operation is not needed (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. They extend 2 to 3 mm beyond the crypt margin, are supported by a graft so they do not collapse, are fully covered by tissue, and compression is omitted; smoking contraindicates GTR (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Who needs them.** Kim and Kratchman's classes A to C usually heal without grafts or barriers; classes D, E and F (periodontal communication or loss of buccal bone) usually need them (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Apicomarginal defects lower complete healing by about 20% or more (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC], and lesions of 15 mm or more do worse (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Where an ordinary apical osteotomy is made and the buccal bone over the root is intact, a resorbable membrane had no benefit (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Pecora's randomised trial of 20 lesions of at least 10 mm found faster healing and better bone quality at 3 to 12 months under an e-PTFE membrane than in uncovered controls (Pecora et al, 1995) [LIT, abstract].

> **SOURCE CONFLICT, the Pecora membrane.** *Johnson, Fayad and Berman* (Cohen's Ch.11) describe the membrane as nonresorbable; *Torabinejad, Lundgren, Tatakis and Fayad* (Ch.15) describe it as resorbable. The PubMed abstract of the trial states that large e-PTFE (Gore-Tex) membranes were used, which are nonresorbable, so Cohen's is correct and Ch.15 is not (checked at abstract level, 2026).

**Evidence.** Biologically a clot is a better space filler than any graft, and membranes have no clear proven benefit routinely (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. A 2024 meta-analysis of 8 randomised trials found significantly better healing with grafting with or without a membrane (odds ratio 2.18), but no particular graft, membrane or combination was significant, and the authors call for more trials (Flynn et al, 2024) [LIT, abstract]. Lieblich cautions that a foreign body placed in an infected area is more likely to fail, and notes a trend to better results with resorbable membranes in large through-and-through lesions (Lieblich, 2012) [SRC].

**Bioinductive root-end fillings.** MTA promotes cementum deposition and osteocalcin expression over the filling, which Ingle's counts as bioinductive (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]; other possible cementum-promoting materials are Diaket, CEM cement, EndoSequence RRM and Quick-Set (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019) [SRC].

**Further textbook points (second audit against the remaining books, 2026-10-18).** 
*Bone biology.* Bone heals in about 3 to 4 months after surgery, aided by the regional acceleratory phenomenon, a burst of remodelling in the injured area (Huja and Roberts, 2019) [SRC]. Reduced remodelling with potent bisphosphonates is why osteonecrosis of the jaw is weighed beforehand (Huja and Roberts, 2019) [SRC].

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

### Definitions
- **Osteogenic:** carries bone-forming cells (autograft).
- **Osteoinductive:** recruits and stimulates progenitor cells to form bone (demineralised allograft, BMP).
- **Osteoconductive:** a scaffold for ingrowth of bone, slowly resorbed (xenograft, alloplast) (Kinaia et al, 2012; Torabinejad et al, 2019).
- **Bioinductive** (Ingle's): promotes cementogenesis (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

### Rationale
Large defects fill with soft tissue and heal by repair. Grafts and membranes keep epithelium and connective tissue out and maintain space so PDL and bone regenerate (Torabinejad et al, 2019).

### Materials
| Type | Examples | Property |
|---|---|---|
| Autograft | Ramus, chin, iliac crest | Osteogenic |
| Allograft | DFDBA, demineralised bone matrix | Osteoinductive (BMP) |
| Xenograft | Bovine bone mineral | Osteoconductive |
| Alloplast | Calcium sulfate, hydroxyapatite, bioactive glass | Osteoconductive |
| Biologic mediators | BMP, enamel matrix derivative, PDGF | Inductive; limited endodontic data |
(Johnson et al, 2021; Jurosky, 2019).

### Membranes
Resorbable (collagen, polylactic acid) are preferred; nonresorbable need re-entry (Johnson et al, 2021). Extend 2 to 3 mm beyond the crypt, support with graft, cover completely, no compression.

### Indications
- Through-and-through defects, large lesions, apicomarginal defects, loss of buccal plate, endodontic-periodontal lesions: Kim and Kratchman classes D to F (Johnson et al, 2021).
- Not needed with small lesions and intact buccal bone.

### Bioinductive root-end fillings
MTA and other tricalcium silicate cements stimulate cementum and bone over the filling (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019); see Q08.

### Evidence and cautions
- Meta-analysis of 8 trials: better healing with grafting (odds ratio 2.18), but no specific material proven (Flynn et al, 2024).
- A clot is a better space filler biologically; routine use is not supported (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).
- Smoking contraindicates GTR; discuss cost and the animal or human origin of the material (Johnson et al, 2021).
- BMP-2 may cause ankylosis (Kinaia et al, 2012).
- A membrane or other foreign body placed in an already infected area is more likely to fail; resorbable membranes show a trend to better results in large through-and-through lesions (Lieblich, 2012).

- Bone heals in about 3 to 4 months; the regional acceleratory phenomenon speeds remodelling (Huja and Roberts, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
Osteogenic, osteoinductive, osteoconductive; Kim and Kratchman classes D to F; resorbable membrane 2 to 3 mm beyond crypt; odds ratio 2.18 (Flynn); no routine use; bioinductive filling means MTA and cementum.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Mixing up osteoinductive and osteoconductive.
- Forgetting that "bioinductive" also refers to root-end filling materials.
- Stating that grafts are routine in every case.
