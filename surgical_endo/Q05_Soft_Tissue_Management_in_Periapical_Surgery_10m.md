## STUDY MODE

**Why it matters.** Handling of the tissues, meaning incision, raising the flap, handling during surgery and suturing, decides the final result, mainly gingival recession and attachment loss (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. "Pink esthetics" (soft tissue and bone) is as important as "white esthetics" after surgery (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Microsurgery does not speed epithelial healing in itself; it shortens the distance epithelium must migrate by giving exact adaptation of wound edges (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC].

**Assess before cutting.** Probing depth around all teeth, noting furcations; an inflamed gingiva reads deeper than the true histologic attachment (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Reduce plaque first: hygienist care and 0.2% chlorhexidine twice daily from a week before to 2 weeks after (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Measure attached gingiva: probing depth subtracted from the distance from margin to mucogingival junction; a submarginal incision needs at least 2 mm (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Note biotype (thin biotypes recede more), restoration margins (recession can expose a crown margin) and papilla height (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. The papilla is present nearly 100% of the time if contact point to bone is 5 mm or less, 56% at 6 mm, 27% at 7 mm or more (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]; once cut, 22 of 32 excised papillae in one study did not regenerate to original shape and height (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC].

> **SOURCE CONFLICT, attached gingiva needed for a submarginal flap.** *Velvart and Peters* (Torabinejad Ch.9): at least 2 mm coronal to the incision (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. *Endodontology* (Ch.40): at least 2 mm (Endodontology, Ch.40) [SRC]. *Rubinstein, Fayad and Torabinejad* (Principles and Practice Ch.20): 4 mm for a papilla-based incision (Rubinstein et al, 2021) [SRC]. *Gulabivala and Ng* (Ch.9): 3 to 5 mm for the Luebke-Ochsenbein flap (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC]. For the exam, quote 2 mm and say the range is 2 to 5 mm.

**Incision.** Keep the blood supply: verticals parallel to the tooth and never converging, flap length to base within 2:1, not over a bony defect and not over a root eminence (Velvart and Peters, 2019; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Single sharp stroke to bone (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. A sulcular flap loses about 0.5 to 0.62 mm of crestal bone and papilla height; papilla-base flaps preserve the papilla (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC].

**Elevation and retraction.** Begin in the vertical incision and undermine, keeping the periosteum intact so mucosa cannot enter the bony crypt (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Cut a small bone groove as a rest for the retractor (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Keep the flap and non-reflected tissue moist, since dehydration shrinks the flap and makes closure tense (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. The longer a flap stays retracted, the greater the morbidity (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC].

**Closure.** Reposition exactly and compress for 2 to 3 minutes, which leaves a thin fibrin clot instead of a thick coagulum (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Interrupted sutures adapt better than continuous (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]; monofilament is preferred and silk collects plaque (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Remove sutures at 4 days, the earliest sensible time (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Healing is fastest in free gingiva (7 days), slower in attached gingiva (14) and alveolar mucosa (28), which is why dehiscence is commoner in mucosa (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. *Gulabivala and Ng* (Ch.9) give about 5 to 10 minutes of digital pressure once all sutures are placed (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC].

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

### Importance
Handling of soft tissue controls recession, papilla loss and scarring; "pink esthetics" matters as much as the endodontic result (Velvart and Peters, 2019).

### Pre-operative
- Probing depths, furcations; hygiene phase; 0.2% chlorhexidine twice daily from 1 week before to 2 weeks after.
- Attached gingiva width (at least 2 mm for submarginal incision); biotype; restoration margins; papilla height (papilla present if contact point to bone is 5 mm or less).

### Incision
- One sharp stroke to bone; verticals on interdental bone, parallel, not converging; flap length to base 2:1 at most; never over a bony defect or root eminence (Gopikrishna, 2021; Velvart and Peters, 2019).
- Choose sulcular (access), submarginal (crowned anteriors) or papilla-base (esthetics) flap (see Q04).

### Elevation and retraction
- Undermining elevation; periosteum kept intact.
- Retractor on solid bone in a prepared groove, not on the base of the flap.
- Keep flap moist with saline; minimise retraction time.

### Closure
- Reposition, compress 2-3 minutes with moist gauze.
- Interrupted monofilament sutures, over sound bone; knots away from the incision.
- Remove at 4 days (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

### Problems and how handling prevents them
| Problem | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Recession, especially in thin biotype or crowned teeth | Papilla-base or submarginal flap; minimal retraction; counsel the patient (Velvart and Peters, 2019) |
| Papilla loss | Papilla-base incision; avoid cutting through the middle of the papilla |
| Necrosis of non-reflected tissue | Keep blood supply; never over-dry; verticals parallel (Velvart and Peters, 2019) |
| Flap shrinkage and tension | Keep moist; avoid long retraction (worse in submarginal flaps) |
| Hematoma under the flap | Control bleeding before suturing, but do not dry the crypt completely (Gopikrishna, 2021) |
| Scar | Sharp single incision; careful adaptation of edges |

### Healing
First intention in about 5 days; free gingiva 7 days, attached 14, alveolar mucosa 28 (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
Papilla 5 mm rule; 2 mm attached gingiva; intact periosteum; moist flap; compression 2-3 minutes; sutures out at 4 days.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
Papilla-base incision (shallow curve at the papilla base, then to bone).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Leaving the flap to dry out.
- Suturing over the bony defect.
- Leaving silk or continuous sutures for a week.
- Not discussing recession risk with the patient.
