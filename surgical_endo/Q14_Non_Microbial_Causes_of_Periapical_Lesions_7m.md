## STUDY MODE

**Starting point.** Bacteria are the primary cause of apical periodontitis, and newly introduced, persistent or recurrent microorganisms are the main reason for failed treatment (Setzer et al, 2021) [SRC]. Apical periodontitis can still be caused by other irritants. Lin and Huang separate exogenous factors (microbes and their toxins, chemical agents, mechanical irritation, foreign bodies, trauma) from endogenous factors (the host's own metabolic products such as urate and cholesterol crystals) (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. They also distinguish inflammation, which is the reaction to irritants, from infection, the presence of microbes in the tissue; inflammation can occur without infection (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

**Mechanical and chemical injury.** Overinstrumentation, overfilling, irrigants, intracanal medicaments and filling materials, and trauma can all damage periapical tissue depending on the severity of injury and the toxicity of the chemicals (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Overinstrumentation with overfilling can promote foreign body reactions and also carries microorganisms into the periapex (Setzer et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Foreign body reaction.** Persistent disease can occur without detectable microbes in the presence of foreign material; lentil beans and cellulose fibres from paper points are examples (Roda et al, 2021) [SRC]. Gutta-percha and sealer are usually well tolerated unless the tissue was inoculated with bacteria (Roda et al, 2021) [SRC]. Lin and Huang caution that many studies did not exclude bacterial contamination, so foreign bodies may act as carriers, and foreign bodies favour infection by lowering the dose of bacteria needed (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

**Cholesterol crystals.** Crystals arise from disintegrating cell membranes and are found in about 18% to 44% of lesions; they are surrounded by foamy macrophages and foreign-body giant cells (Lin and Huang, 2021; Said-Al-Naief, 2019) [SRC]. Degraded proteins and cholesterol may act as antigens (Said-Al-Naief, 2019) [SRC]. The cholesterol may come from bleeding into the lesion (Said-Al-Naief, 2019) [SRC].

**True cysts.** A cyst is an epithelium-lined cavity formed from the epithelial rests of Malassez, which proliferate under chronic inflammation; cyst formation may be an attempt to separate the stimulus from bone (Roda et al, 2021) [SRC]. Incidence is quoted as 15% to 42% of periapical lesions in Cohen's Ch.10 and 15% to 20% in Ch.16 (Roda et al, 2021; Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Whether true cysts heal without surgery is disputed; see the conflict box in Q12. A cyst cannot form by itself and is not a separate disease from apical periodontitis (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

**Scar.** A periapical scar is the ultimate healing attempt of the tissue and occurs in 6.6% to 12% of lesions (Said-Al-Naief, 2019) [SRC].

**Five reasons for persistence.** Said-Al-Naief lists intraradicular infection, extraradicular infection, a cystic lesion, foreign body reaction and scar; Roda et al list four groups (intraradicular microorganisms, extraradicular infection, foreign body reaction and true cysts) (Said-Al-Naief, 2019; Roda et al, 2021) [SRC]. Three of these are non-microbial in nature.

**Lesions that are not of endodontic origin.** Trauma, foreign bodies, host metabolic by-products, advanced periodontal disease, fibro-osseous lesions and benign and malignant tumours can mimic apical periodontitis (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. The odontogenic keratocyst is the most common mimic (Said-Al-Naief, 2019) [SRC]; others include globulomaxillary and nasopalatine cysts and tumours (Gutmann, 2017) [SRC]. A cystic ameloblastoma was found in a lesion on a vital tooth (Lieblich, 2012) [SRC]. Extraradicular infection is microbial and does not belong here; Lin and Huang say it can be diagnosed only after biopsy and that independent periapical infection is not consistently present (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

**Further textbook points (second audit against the remaining books, 2026-10-18).** 
*Non-microbial causes questioned.* Siqueira and Rocas' group argue that most "non-microbial" persistence reflects undetected extraradicular bacteria (Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]. Cholesterol crystals and foreign bodies are treated as contributory, not primary (Siqueira, 2022; Fouad, 2020) [SRC].

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~12 minutes)*

### Introduction
Bacteria are the main cause of apical periodontitis, but some periapical lesions or persistent lesions arise from other irritants or are not of endodontic origin (Setzer et al, 2021; Lin and Huang, 2021).

### Non-microbial causes within an endodontic lesion
**1. Mechanical and chemical irritation**
- Overinstrumentation, overfilling, irrigants, medicaments, filling materials, trauma (Lin and Huang, 2021).

**2. Foreign body reaction**
- Gutta-percha, sealer, paper-point cellulose fibres, food (lentil beans); persists without detectable microbes (Roda et al, 2021).
- Usually well tolerated; bacteria may be carried with the material (Lin and Huang, 2021).

**3. Cholesterol crystals**
- Host-derived, from disintegrated cell membranes; found in 18% to 44% of lesions; foamy macrophages and giant cells; may act as antigens (Lin and Huang, 2021; Said-Al-Naief, 2019).

**4. True cysts**
- Epithelial rests of Malassez proliferate under inflammation; cyst closed to the canal; self-sustaining and perhaps unresponsive to canal treatment (Roda et al, 2021).

**5. Periapical scar**
- Dense fibrous tissue; the end point of healing; 6.6% to 12% (Said-Al-Naief, 2019).

### Non-endodontic lesions
- Periodontal disease, trauma, fibro-osseous lesions, benign and malignant tumours (Lin and Huang, 2021).
- Odontogenic keratocyst, ameloblastoma, nasopalatine and globulomaxillary cysts (Said-Al-Naief, 2019; Gutmann, 2017).
- Clue: vital tooth, atypical radiolucency, nerve anaesthesia, root resorption; always biopsy (Said-Al-Naief, 2019; Lieblich, 2012).

### Management
Remove the cause: retreat the canal; remove foreign material and cyst surgically; histology of all removed tissue (Johnson et al, 2021).

- Siqueira's group questions the independence of non-microbial causes (Siqueira, 2022).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
Foreign body, cholesterol, true cyst, scar; cholesterol in 18 to 44%; scar 6.6 to 12%; keratocyst is the commonest mimic.
