## STUDY MODE

**What the question wants.** A numbered sequence from patient preparation to recall, with the reason for each step and the modern (microsurgical) version beside the traditional one. Torabinejad's chapter divides apical microsurgery into flap design, reflection, retraction, osteotomy, curettage, biopsy, hemostasis, apical resection, inspection, apical preparation, evaluation, drying, material selection, placement, finishing and closure (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Grossman's gives the short form: curettage, root-tip resection, root-end preparation and filling (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

**1. Preparation.** Case selection and CBCT planning come first, because CBCT locates the exact position of the apical periodontitis (Rubinstein et al, 2021) [SRC]. Consent should stress that the operation is exploratory, since a root fracture or a large dehiscence may change the plan during surgery (Lieblich, 2012) [SRC]. The mouth is rinsed with 0.12% chlorhexidine before incision to cut the bacterial count (Rubinstein et al, 2021) [SRC]; Velvart and Peters use 0.2% twice daily from a week before to two weeks after (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Patients on daily 81 mg aspirin or fish oil should stop 7 days before to restore platelet function, in consultation with the physician (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Evidence on prophylactic antibiotics is not settled (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC].

**2. Anaesthesia and hemostasis.** Profound anaesthesia with a vasoconstrictor is the first and most important hemostatic measure (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]; Grossman's names lidocaine with epinephrine as the usual choice (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], and a long-acting agent such as bupivacaine helps with postoperative pain (Niemczyk, 2010) [SRC]. Where bleeding persists, direct pressure remains the most important measure and topical agents are secondary (Ogle et al, 2011) [SRC]. Local agents include ferric sulfate and buffered aluminium chloride gel (Niemczyk, 2010) [SRC]. Bone wax seals cancellous bone mechanically but stays in the jaw and inhibited bone regeneration in a cited study, so many authors avoid it (Ogle et al, 2011) [SRC]. A synthetic water-soluble alternative that dissolves in about 48 hours is described (Ostene) (Ogle et al, 2011) [SRC].

> **SOURCE CONFLICT, epinephrine concentration.** *Endodontology* (Ch.40): 1:50,000 recommended (Endodontology, Ch.40) [SRC]. *Johnson and Abedi* (Torabinejad Ch.8): 1:50,000 is effective for hemostasis in surgery, usually one cartridge of 2% lidocaine with 1:50,000 (Johnson and Abedi, 2019) [SRC]. *Tsesis et al* (2025): 1:100,000 is usually sufficient (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. Higher concentrations give better hemostasis but are limited in cardiovascular patients (see Q03).

**3. Flap.** The flap must give access with an adequate blood supply, avoid incisions over the bony defect, avoid acute corners, include the periosteum and never split a papilla (Rubinstein et al, 2021) [SRC]. Choice of flap (sulcular, papilla-base, submarginal) is taken in Q04 and tissue handling in Q05.

**4. Osteotomy.** Bone is removed with a round or surgical-length bur under saline, or piezosurgery, as small as is practical but large enough to curette and reach the root end (Rubinstein et al, 2021) [SRC]. About 4 mm is the ideal diameter with ultrasonic tips (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], and smaller osteotomies heal faster (Floratos and Kim, 2017) [SRC].

**5. Curettage and biopsy.** Granulation tissue is removed to give vision and access; foreign material in the crypt must go. Tissue that is removed should be examined (Rubinstein et al, 2021; Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Lieblich notes the practical controversy: guidelines exist for not submitting tissue in clear, typical cases, and distinguishing granuloma from cyst does not change outcome (Lieblich, 2012) [SRC]. See Q11. On order, *Gulabivala and Ng* (Ch.9) advise curetting first and resecting later so the root end stays in clear view (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC]; *Gutmann* (Harty's Ch.10) says curettage can often be done before or together with resection (Gutmann, 2017) [SRC].

**6. Root-end resection.** At least 3 mm of the apex is removed to include 93% of lateral canals and 98% of apical ramifications (Gopikrishna, 2021; Niemczyk, 2010) [SRC], at close to zero bevel, because a bevel exposes more dentinal tubules and increases leakage (Floratos and Kim, 2017) [SRC].

**7. Inspection.** The cut surface is dried and inspected at high magnification (methylene blue if needed) for cracks, isthmuses and missed canals, with 1% methylene blue to outline the root if needed (Gutmann, 2017); Floratos and Kim call inspection the key stage of microsurgery (Floratos and Kim, 2017) [SRC].

**8. Root-end preparation.** An ultrasonic tip about 0.25 mm wide and 3 mm long cuts a Class I cavity along the long axis to 3 mm (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Compared with burs it needs less bone removal, stays centred in the canal and perforates less often (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Details in Q07. A micromirror then checks that the facial wall is clean of gutta-percha (Rubinstein et al, 2021) [SRC].

**9. Drying and filling.** The cavity is rinsed and dried with a microtip (Stropko) irrigator (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Tricalcium silicate cement/MTA is Grossman's material of choice (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; zinc oxide eugenol cements are delivered as small cones and condensed in increments, while MTA is carried in a sleeve or carrier and must not be pulled out when withdrawing the plugger (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Materials are compared in Q08.

**10. Radiograph, closure and instructions.** A radiograph before closure confirms complete resection and the filling (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. The flap is replaced and compressed with moist gauze for at least 3 minutes to leave a thin clot (Rubinstein et al, 2021) [SRC]. *Gulabivala and Ng* (Ch.9) add that once all sutures are placed the flap is compressed again with digital pressure for about 5 to 10 minutes (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC]. Monofilament sutures are preferred; silk collects bacteria (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Removal time is quoted differently. **SOURCE CONFLICT, suture removal:** *Gutmann* (Harty's Ch.10) 48 to 72 hours; *Niemczyk* (2010) 1 to 3 days in uncomplicated cases; *Johnson, Fayad and Berman* (Cohen's Ch.11) 2 to 4 days; *Jurosky* (Torabinejad Ch.14) consider 5 days; *Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman's* chapter four days as the earliest reasonable time; *Rubinstein and Fayad* (Torabinejad Ch.10) up to 7 days; *Rubinstein, Fayad and Torabinejad* (Principles and Practice Ch.20) 3 to 7 days [SRC]. Further sources: *Gulabivala and Ng* (Ch.9) 7 days when wound strength is assured (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC]; *Endodontology* (Ch.40) 3 to 5 days (Endodontology, Ch.40) [SRC]; *Weine* (Ch.9) an absolute maximum of 7 days (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; *Barnes* (1984, the older colour manual) 5 to 7 days (Barnes, 1984) [SRC]; *Johnson, McNally et al* (Current Therapy, Ch.9) 2 to 4 days (Johnson, McNally et al, Current Therapy Ch.9) [SRC]. No source gives a trial; the range is 1 to 7 days and I have not picked one. Written postoperative instructions are given (Rubinstein et al, 2021) [SRC].

**11. Follow-up.** A film at 3 months compared with the postoperative one; if bone fill is not yet clear, again at 6 months (Lieblich, 2012) [SRC]. In Rubinstein and Kim's series complete healing took 3 months in 25.3% of cases, 6 months in 34%, 9 months in 15.4% and 12 months in 25.3% (Lieblich, 2012) [SRC].

**Further textbook points (second audit against the remaining books, 2026-10-18).** 
> **SOURCE CONFLICT, haemostasis and suture timing.** Epinephrine: 1:50,000 injected slowly with no contraindication even in compromised patients (*Castellucci*), 1:80,000 (*Barnes*, older text), against the dose-limited wording above. Ferric sulfate has a pH of 0.21 (*Castellucci*), and all agree it must be removed completely. Sutures out at 24 to 48 hours (*Castellucci*), 3 to 4 days (*Weine*) or up to 7 days (Castellucci, 2003; Weine, 2004) [SRC].

*Steps not stressed above.* Weine and Gutmann stress that curettage alone is not the end of the operation, and that a K-file can be used with the ultrasonic tip for the preparation (Weine, 2004; Gutmann (Problem Solving Ch.16)) [SRC]. A retained cotton pellet gives an acute foreign-body reaction, so pellets are counted (Gutmann (Problem Solving Ch.16)) [SRC]. MTA sets in about 3 hours (Castellucci, 2003) [SRC]. Postoperative: ice for 4 to 6 hours, a telephone call at 24 hours, and an analgesic regimen of ibuprofen 600 mg with acetaminophen 1000 mg (Evans et al, 2012; Gutmann (Problem Solving Ch.16)) [SRC]. Barnes lists common reasons for failure: an unsealed canal, a missed canal or a fracture (Barnes, 1984 (older text)) [SRC].

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim: the modern sequence (microscope, 3 mm resection, ultrasonic preparation, bioceramic filling) is what raises success.** Textbooks give 91.4% to 94.4% for microsurgery (Rubinstein et al, 2021) [SRC], and the Setzer meta-analysis found 94% against 59% for traditional surgery (Setzer et al, 2010) [LIT, abstract]. The one randomised trial quoted found no difference between Super-EBA and ProRoot MTA (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC], which suggests the whole technique, not one material, carries the gain. Limitation: the pooled studies differ in case mix and criteria, so the gap between 94% and 59% is not a clean causal estimate [Likely].

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~40 minutes)*

### Definition and aim
Apical (periradicular) surgery means surgical access to the root apex, removal of diseased tissue, resection of the root end and sealing of the canal with a root-end filling; the modern form is apical microsurgery. Its aim is to remove the irritant that cannot be reached from the crown and to seal the canal so the natural tooth can be kept (Rubinstein et al, 2021).

### Steps (in order)

**1. Pre-operative preparation**
- Case selection (nonsurgical retreatment not possible or unlikely to help), periapical film and CBCT to locate the lesion and neighbouring structures (Rubinstein et al, 2021).
- Explain that the operation is exploratory: a root fracture may change the plan; written consent (Lieblich, 2012).
- Medical review; stop daily aspirin or fish oil 7 days before if the physician agrees (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019); chlorhexidine 0.12% rinse before incision (Rubinstein et al, 2021).

**2. Anaesthesia and hemostasis**
- Lidocaine with epinephrine, profound, infiltrated slowly; bupivacaine for postoperative comfort (Niemczyk, 2010; Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Pressure first (Ogle et al, 2011); ferric sulfate or buffered aluminium chloride gel when needed, ferric sulfate fully removed afterwards (Niemczyk, 2010). Bone wax is avoided because it stays in the jaw and impairs bone regeneration; Ostene dissolves in about 48 hours (Ogle et al, 2011).

**3. Flap**
- Sulcular, papilla-base or submarginal. Wide base, no acute corners, not over the bony defect, papilla not split, periosteum included, verticals parallel (Rubinstein et al, 2021).
- Reflect from the vertical incision with undermining elevation; retractor on sound bone, flap kept moist.

**4. Osteotomy**
- Round bur or surgical-length bur under saline, or piezosurgery near the sinus or nerve; about 4 mm window, as small as practical (Gopikrishna, 2021; Rubinstein et al, 2021). Small osteotomies heal faster (Floratos and Kim, 2017).

**5. Curettage and biopsy**
- Peel granulation tissue off the crypt wall with a curette; remove foreign material (Rubinstein et al, 2021).
- Send tissue for histology unless the case is clearly typical and documented (Gopikrishna, 2021; Lieblich, 2012).

**6. Root-end resection**
- At least 3 mm removes 93% of lateral canals and 98% of apical ramifications (Gopikrishna, 2021); not more than 3 mm, to keep root length and strength (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).
- Bevel almost nil (0 to 10 degrees), because a bevel exposes dentinal tubules and increases leakage (Floratos and Kim, 2017).

**7. Inspection of the cut root**
- Dry, magnify, stain with methylene blue; look for cracks, isthmus, missed canal, gap in the old filling (Floratos and Kim, 2017; Gutmann, 2017).

**8. Root-end preparation**
- Ultrasonic tip (about 0.25 mm wide, 3 mm long) cuts a Class I cavity along the long axis, 3 mm deep, with irrigation and low power (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021).
- Less bone removal, centred preparation, fewer perforations than burs (Johnson et al, 2021). Diamond-coated tip last (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).
- Micromirror check: no gutta-percha on the facial wall (Rubinstein et al, 2021).

**9. Drying and root-end filling**
- Rinse; dry with a microtip (Stropko) irrigator or paper points (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).
- MTA or tricalcium silicate cement is the material of choice (Gopikrishna, 2021); Super-EBA or IRM are alternatives.
- Place small increments, condense with a plugger, wipe excess; MTA needs a carrier and a dry field (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).

**10. Radiograph and closure**
- Radiograph before closure: root tip removed, filling in place (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Replace the flap, compress with moist gauze for at least 3 minutes (Rubinstein et al, 2021).
- Interrupted monofilament sutures; silk is avoided (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

**11. Post-operative care**
- Ice for the first day, soft diet, do not lift the lip, saline or chlorhexidine rinses, written instructions (Niemczyk, 2010; Rubinstein et al, 2021).
- Sutures out at 2 to 4 days in most texts, range 1 to 7 days: Gutmann 48 to 72 hours, Johnson et al 2 to 4 days, Jurosky 5 days, Rubinstein et al up to 7 days (authors disagree).

**12. Follow-up**
- Radiograph at 3 months, 6 months, then 12 months; success means bone fill, no symptoms, no sinus tract (Lieblich, 2012).

### Microsurgical versus traditional version of the steps
| Step | Traditional | Microsurgical |
|---|---|---|
| Osteotomy | Large | About 4 mm |
| Resection | Steep bevel, 45 to 60 degrees | 0 to 10 degrees |
| Inspection | Naked eye | Microscope, micromirror |
| Preparation | Bur in a micro-handpiece | Ultrasonic tip |
| Filling | Amalgam | MTA or bioceramic |
| Success | About 59% | About 94% (Setzer et al, 2010) |

### Precautions that run through the steps
- Heat: bone and root are cooled with saline throughout; excessive heat during the osteotomy damages bone (Tsesis et al, 2025).
- Sinus and nerve: keep the root apex and filling material out of the maxillary sinus; use a short osteotomy and, near vital structures, piezosurgery (Tsesis et al, 2025; Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).
- Ultrasonic tip: if resistance is met the tip is probably directed lingually; step back, check alignment with the long axis, otherwise the root may be perforated (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).
- Magnification: use the microscope at low power to orient, high power to inspect.

### Outcome
Success of 91 to 94% is quoted for microsurgery (Rubinstein et al, 2021).

- **Haemostasis:** epinephrine concentration differs by author (1:50,000 Castellucci; 1:80,000 Barnes); ferric sulfate must be removed fully.
- **Postoperative:** ice 4 to 6 hours, review call at 24 hours (Evans et al, 2012).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
3 mm resection (93% lateral canals, 98% ramifications); 0 to 10 degree bevel; 3 mm ultrasonic Class I cavity; about 4 mm osteotomy; compress 3 minutes; MTA is the material of choice; 94% against 59%.
