## STUDY MODE

**What is wounded.** A flapped periapical operation produces three wounds: the incisional wound made by the scalpel, the dissectional wound where the periosteal elevator separates the flap from bone, and the excisional wound where bone and root are cut with a bur (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC]. Healing is by primary intention when the edges are closely approximated with minimal clot, which restores the normal architecture (regeneration); it is by secondary intention when the edges are apart and a thick clot fills the gap, which gives granulation tissue and repair, with possible scar (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC]. A thin clot greatly enhances healing, which is why the flap is compressed after suturing (Jurosky, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Phases (soft tissue incisional wound).**
1. *Hemostasis and inflammation.* Clotting converts fibrinogen to fibrin and the clot gives the first fragile attachment and a path for cells. Neutrophils clean the wound and are predominant at 24 to 48 hours; macrophages then take over and direct the reconstruction (Jurosky, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. **SOURCE CONFLICT, macrophage timing:** *Johnson, Fayad and Berman* (Cohen's Ch.11) put the macrophage peak at day 3 to 4; *Jurosky* (Torabinejad Ch.14) says macrophages are the predominant cell within 12 hours in oral wounds, earlier than in skin (days 3 to 4). Both are kept; Jurosky's figure comes from oral studies, Cohen's may refer to general wound healing [Guessing]. In oral wounds inflammation resolves quickly, which reduces scarring because TGF-beta, a scarring factor, is short-lived (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC].
2. *Epithelial healing.* Epithelial cells stream from the wound edge across the fibrin; the seal forms in about a day (21 to 28 hours in volunteers, 24 to 48 hours in monkeys) and a mature barrier by 36 to 72 hours (Jurosky, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Early epithelialization is the key to rapid healing (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC].
3. *Connective tissue healing.* Granulomatous tissue becomes granulation tissue as fibroblasts replace inflammatory cells; type III then type I collagen is laid down and angiogenesis proceeds from the periphery (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC].
4. *Maturation and remodelling.* Fibroblast number and vascularity fall and collagen cross-links (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC]. Healing is fastest in free gingiva (7 days) and slowest in alveolar mucosa (28 days) (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC].

**Periosteum and bone.** The periosteum does not survive flap reflection; it re-forms by about day 14 (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC]. In the excisional bony wound a disorganized clot is replaced by granulation tissue from the endosteum; woven bone is deposited directly on devitalised bone without prior resorption, appears at 12 to 14 days, and the defect is filled by about 16 weeks (Jurosky, 2019; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Maturation is complete by 16 to 20 weeks (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC].

**The resected root end.** Cohen's states that cementogenesis starts at 10 to 12 days, cementum covers the root end by about 28 days, and the PDL is re-established at about 8 weeks (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. In a dog study cementum was seen on resected root ends at 16 days, but a functional attachment had not re-formed by 45 days, whereas citric acid demineralisation brought cementogenesis forward to 12 days (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC]. These animal timings are not directly transferable to patients [Likely].

**Repair.** Grossman's describes initial repair across the incision by first intention in about 5 days, and says repair of the periradicular tissue is usually complete within a year, with progress visible on a radiograph at 6 months (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. A radiolucent "surgical defect" can persist when the periosteum was destroyed or the palatal plate was lost, because the defect is filled with fibrous tissue rather than bone (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Andreasen and Rud's three types of repair after root resection are: complete repair with restored PDL, repair with scar adjacent to the PDL, and scar tissue with moderate inflammation (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

**What delays healing.** Prolonged infection or hypoxia, smoking (tissue hypoxia is the main mechanism), diabetes, malnutrition, steroids and chemotherapy, and long-term NSAIDs (Jurosky, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Intravenous bisphosphonates carry the risk of osteonecrosis of the jaw (Jurosky, 2019) [SRC].

**Further textbook points (second audit against the remaining books, 2026-10-18).** 
*Regional acceleratory phenomenon.* Surgery triggers a regional acceleratory phenomenon that speeds remodelling for several months in the operated area (Huja and Roberts, 2019) [SRC].

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

### Wounds created
- Incisional (scalpel), dissectional (flap elevation), excisional (osteotomy and root resection) (Jurosky, 2019).

### Types of healing
- **Primary intention:** edges approximated, thin clot, regeneration.
- **Secondary intention:** thick clot, granulation tissue, repair, scar (Jurosky, 2019).

### Phases
1. **Hemostasis and inflammation (hours to 3 days):** fibrin clot; neutrophils predominate at 24 to 48 hours; macrophages follow (peak day 3 to 4 in Cohen's; earlier in oral wounds in Jurosky) (Jurosky, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021).
2. **Epithelial healing (1 to 3 days):** epithelial seal in about 1 day, barrier at 36 to 72 hours.
3. **Connective tissue healing (to about 2 weeks):** fibroblasts, type III then type I collagen, angiogenesis.
4. **Maturation and remodelling (weeks to months):** collagen cross-links, fewer fibroblasts, vessels regress.

### Soft tissue
First intention within about 5 days (Gopikrishna, 2021); free gingiva 7 days, attached gingiva 14, alveolar mucosa 28 (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

### Bone
- Granulation tissue from endosteum within 2 to 4 days; woven bone at 12 to 14 days, deposited directly on devitalised bone.
- Periosteum re-forms by day 14.
- Defect filled by about 16 weeks; remodelled by 16 to 20 weeks (Jurosky, 2019; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

### Root end and periodontal apparatus
- Cementum begins at 10 to 12 days and covers the root end by about 28 days; PDL by about 8 weeks (Johnson et al, 2021).
- Citric acid demineralisation speeded cementogenesis in dogs (Jurosky, 2019).

### Repair (Andreasen and Rud)
1. Complete repair with PDL restored.
2. Repair with scar next to the PDL.
3. Scar tissue with inflammation (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Radiographic repair is expected by 6 months and complete in about a year; a "surgical defect" of fibrous tissue may persist.

### Factors that delay healing
Infection, hypoxia, smoking, diabetes, malnutrition, steroids, chemotherapy, long-term NSAIDs, IV bisphosphonates, thick clot, dehydration of the flap, trauma (Jurosky, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021).

### How the surgeon helps healing
- Sharp incision, flap kept moist, minimal retraction.
- Small osteotomy, cooled bur, no frictional heat.
- Close approximation, compression for at least 3 minutes.
- Early suture removal (about 5 days) (Jurosky, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
Three wounds; primary versus secondary intention; seal at 1 day; woven bone at 12 to 14 days; bone filled at 16 weeks; cementum 28 days; PDL 8 weeks; three types of repair; thin clot.
