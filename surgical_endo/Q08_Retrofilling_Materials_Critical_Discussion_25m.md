## STUDY MODE

**What an ideal material must do.** It seals the canal against bacteria and their by-products, is biocompatible, nonresorbable, dimensionally stable, insoluble, unaffected by moisture, easy to use, radiopaque and non-staining (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Ingle's adds a last item, bioinductive (promotes cementogenesis), and states plainly that no material meets all of them (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Parirokh and Shabahang add reasonable working and setting times (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. This is the yardstick for the critical comparison.

**Amalgam.** Suggested by Farrar in 1884 and the standard until 1959, when Omnell found a cytotoxic zinc carbonate precipitate and zinc-free amalgam was used (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Faults: corrosion, dimensional change, amalgam tattoo and tissue argyria, persistent apical inflammation and mercury (Gopikrishna, 2021; Gutmann, 2017) [SRC]. Clinical figures are poor: success of apical surgery sealed with amalgam fell to 57.7% after 10 years in one study (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC], and IRM gave 91% against 75% for amalgam over long periods (Gutmann, 2017) [SRC]. Grossman's no longer recommends it (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

**Zinc oxide eugenol cements.** IRM has more than 75% zinc oxide and about 20% polymethacrylate with eugenol liquid; it seals better than amalgam and is tolerated, but forms no cementum (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Super-EBA has 60% zinc oxide, 34% aluminium oxide and 6% resin, mixed with 37.5% eugenol and 62.5% o-methoxybenzoic acid; it seals like IRM, was less leaky than amalgam and always gave less persistent disease than amalgam, but it disintegrates in an acid environment and has no cementogenic ability (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Mixed to a thick clay-like consistency, both are easy to place (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC].

**Glass ionomer.** Light-cured GIC seals and adapts better than chemically cured GIC; its seal is generally better than amalgam and similar to IRM, but moisture and blood contamination during setting adversely affected the outcome clinically (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. In a comparison with amalgam success was 90% at 1 year and 85% at 5 years for both (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Composite resin and hybrids.** Retroplast, a dual-cure composite with a dentin bonding agent, gave complete healing in 74% against 59% for amalgam at 1 year in 388 cases (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC], and 33 of 34 cases healed at 10 years (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. It requires meticulous hemostasis and a dry field (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Geristore leaked less than IRM or amalgam in vitro (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC], but in dogs the cemental covering was never more than 25% of the surface and 10 of 18 root-end filled teeth developed abscesses (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Diaket sealed well and a tissue barrier formed across it; Cohen's reports no cementum, whereas Parirokh and Shabahang list Diaket among materials that may promote cementum formation (Johnson et al, 2021; Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. I have left the two statements side by side. It is no longer available in the United States (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Mineral trioxide aggregate.** Developed at Loma Linda; calcium silicates, bismuth oxide, calcium carbonate, sulfate and aluminate. Hydration yields a gel that hardens to crystals in an amorphous matrix (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Properties: longest setting time (see the conflict below), low initial compressive strength, pH 10.2 rising to 12.5, less cytotoxic than amalgam, Super-EBA or IRM, setting unaffected by blood (Johnson et al, 2021; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Biologically it stands out for cementum deposition over the filling (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019; Lieblich, 2012) [SRC], with osteocalcin expression and cementoblast attachment (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. It is the material of choice in Grossman's, Ingle's and Torabinejad (Gopikrishna, 2021; Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. Disadvantages: long setting time, high cost, discoloration potential and handling difficulty (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; it needs a carrier and it should not be pulled out with the plugger (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Moisture is needed for set (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. EDTA conditioning is advised against with MTA (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

*Materials.* MTA sets in about 3 hours (Castellucci, 2003) [SRC]. ## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim: MTA is the best root-end filling.** *Textbook:* strong. In vitro studies mostly show better sealing and marginal adaptation than SuperEBA, IRM, amalgam and GIC (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; MTA is the most popular material among American Board diplomates, 61.4% (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. *Clinical evidence:* weaker than the laboratory data. A randomised trial found no difference between Super-EBA and ProRoot MTA, and the textbook infers that surgical technique matters more than the material (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]; in the human comparison with IRM, persistent disease was 16% against 24% at 12 months, not significantly different (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Systematic reviews disagree: one found no effect of material, two found IRM or MTA better than amalgam, GIC or gutta-percha, one found only MTA beneficial, and the authors note most primary studies were small, retrospective and short (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. *Conclusion:* amalgam, GIC and gutta-percha are inferior; between MTA and IRM or Super-EBA the clinical benefit is unproven, and MTA's real advantage is biological (cementum, handling aside) [Likely].

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~40 minutes)*

### Introduction
A root-end filling seals the resected, prepared root end so that bacteria and their products cannot pass from the canal into the periradicular tissue. It improves the outcome of apical surgery, and the choice of material has been debated for a century (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019).

### Ideal requirements
- Seals against bacteria and by-products; biocompatible; nonresorbable, insoluble, dimensionally stable.
- Unaffected by moisture or blood; radiopaque; non-staining; easy to handle; reasonable working and setting time.
- Bioinductive, that is promotes cementogenesis and regeneration of the PDL.
- No material satisfies all of these (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021; Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019).

### Materials used
Gutta-percha, amalgam, polycarboxylate and zinc phosphate cements, Cavit, gold foil, zinc oxide eugenol cements (IRM, Super-EBA), glass ionomer, Diaket, composite resin (Retroplast), resin-ionomer (Geristore), MTA, and newer bioceramics (Johnson et al, 2021).

### Individual materials
**1. Amalgam**
- Suggested by Farrar in 1884; zinc-free amalgam used after 1959 because of zinc carbonate toxicity (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).
- Faults: corrosion, dimensional change, amalgam tattoo, argyria, apical inflammation, mercury (Gopikrishna, 2021; Gutmann, 2017).
- Success fell to 57.7% at 10 years in one study (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019); IRM 91% against amalgam 75% (Gutmann, 2017).
- Not recommended today.

**2. Zinc oxide eugenol cements**
- IRM: powder over 75% ZnO with polymethacrylate; liquid eugenol. Super-EBA: powder 60% ZnO, 34% alumina, 6% resin; liquid 37.5% eugenol and 62.5% o-methoxybenzoic acid (Johnson et al, 2021).
- Seal better than amalgam, well tolerated, easy to mix to a clay-like consistency and place (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).
- Finishing with a carbide bur gives better marginal adaptation than burnishing (Johnson et al, 2021).
- No cementum formation; Super-EBA dissolves in an acid environment; Super-EBA gave less persistent disease than amalgam in every comparison (Johnson et al, 2021).

**3. Glass ionomer cement**
- Light-cured seals and adapts better than chemically cured; seal better than amalgam and similar to IRM.
- Moisture and blood contamination worsen the result; success 90% at 1 year and 85% at 5 years, the same as amalgam (Johnson et al, 2021).

**4. Composites and hybrids**
- Retroplast: bonded dual-cure composite; complete healing 74% against 59% for amalgam at 1 year; a 10-year follow-up was good. Needs meticulous hemostasis and a dry field (Johnson et al, 2021).
- Geristore: leaks less than IRM or amalgam in vitro, but periradicular healing is unpredictable (Johnson et al, 2021).

**5. Diaket**
- Polyvinyl resin with good seal and a tissue barrier over it; cementum formation is disputed (none in Cohen's; possible in Parirokh and Shabahang); no longer available in the USA (Johnson et al, 2021; Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019).

**6. Mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA)**
- Calcium silicates with bismuth oxide, sulfate and aluminate; hydration gives a gel that hardens; needs moisture to set (Johnson et al, 2021; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).
- pH about 12.5 after setting; setting time 2 to 4 hours; low early strength (Johnson et al, 2021; Lieblich, 2012).
- Best sealing and marginal adaptation; least cytotoxic; setting not affected by blood; induces cementum, osteocalcin expression and bone (Johnson et al, 2021; Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019).
- Mixed firm but not crumbly with saline or local anaesthetic; the field is kept dry and the material is not irrigated away before closure (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).
- Disadvantages: long set, handling, cost, discoloration; use a carrier, condense gently, do not condition the root with EDTA (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021).

**7. Newer bioceramics**
- EndoSequence Root Repair Material (putty or syringe) and Biodentine: biocompatible and antibacterial like MTA in vitro; long-term clinical data are lacking; Biodentine is poorly radiopaque (Johnson et al, 2021; Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).

### Comparison
| Material | Seal | Cementum | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amalgam | Poor over time | No | Corrosion, tattoo, mercury |
| IRM, Super-EBA | Better than amalgam | No | Eugenol; dissolves in acid |
| Glass ionomer | Moderate | No | Moisture sensitive |
| Composite | Good in vitro | Some | Needs a dry field |
| MTA | Best in vitro | Yes | Handling, slow set |
| Bioceramics | Like MTA in vitro | Possible | Little clinical evidence |

### Critical appraisal
- Laboratory evidence favours MTA over IRM, Super-EBA, amalgam and GIC for sealing and adaptation (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019).
- Clinically the gap is smaller: a randomised trial found no difference between MTA and Super-EBA, and persistent disease with MTA against IRM was 16% against 24% at 12 months, not significant (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021).
- Systematic reviews disagree: one found no effect of the material, others found MTA or IRM better than amalgam, GIC or gutta-percha. Primary studies were small, retrospective and short (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2019).
- Surgical technique may matter as much as the material (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).
- Amalgam is clearly inferior; MTA's advantages are biological, mainly cementum and a hydrophilic set in a moist field.

### Conclusion
MTA or a tricalcium silicate cement is the material of choice (Gopikrishna, 2021). IRM and Super-EBA are acceptable alternatives, and amalgam should not be used.

- **EDTA conditioning** of the root end has no proven benefit (Evans et al, 2012).
