## STUDY MODE

**The problem it addresses.** Microsurgery depends on a small osteotomy (about 4 mm), a resection of about 3 mm with almost no bevel, and a preparation along the long axis (Gopikrishna, 2021; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Reaching the apex through bone with no visual landmark, close to the sinus, mental foramen or neighbouring roots, is where freehand work goes wrong. CBCT is already the planning tool that shows the lesion and its relations (Rubinstein et al, 2021; Evans et al, 2012) [SRC]. Guidance transfers that plan to the patient.

**Two systems.** *Static guided* surgery uses a template made from CBCT, with or without an intraoral scan, and 3D printed, which sets the drilling path. *Dynamic guided* surgery uses an optical triangulation tracking system and gives real-time navigation of the bur (Wei et al, 2023) [LIT, abstract]. Augmented reality is a third, less developed route (Rewthamrongsris et al, 2026) [LIT, abstract].

**Claimed uses.** Teeth with pulp obliteration, anatomical abnormalities, retreatment, posterior teeth needing endodontic microsurgery, and tooth autotransplantation, described as precise, time-saving and minimally invasive compared with freehand work, on the basis of case reports and basic research (Wei et al, 2023) [LIT, abstract].

**Evidence.** The 2026 meta-analysis found 33 studies, of which 1 was a nonrandomised trial, 1 a case series and 31 in vitro or cadaver studies. Static and dynamic guidance both gave significantly lower three-dimensional deviation at the bur tip than freehand work; dynamic navigation had significantly greater resection angle deviation than static guides; static guides needed the least operating time (Rewthamrongsris et al, 2026) [LIT, abstract]. The 2025 systematic review of dynamic navigation included 14 articles (12 case reports and 2 case series), 7 of them for endodontic surgery, with a mean follow-up of 9.9 months; it concluded that the method is reliable for complex cases but limited by high cost and a steep learning curve, and called for randomised trials (Kapoor et al, 2025) [LIT, abstract].

**Interpretation.** Accuracy gains are shown in laboratory and cadaver models; clinical outcome (healing and survival) has not been shown to improve. This follows the study designs and should not be read as proof of benefit [Likely]. Cost, learning curve, the need for a CBCT, and the fixed path of a static guide in a sinus or nerve area are practical limits [Guessing].

## EXAM MODE

### Why guidance
Endodontic microsurgery needs a small osteotomy, a 3 mm resection and a long-axis preparation (Gopikrishna, 2021; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019). Guidance reproduces a CBCT plan at the surgical site and protects the sinus, nerve and neighbouring roots.

### Types
| | Static guide | Dynamic navigation |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | 3D-printed template from CBCT, with or without intraoral scan | Optical triangulation tracking, real-time display |
| Operating time | Shortest | Longer |
| Resection angle | Less deviation | Greater deviation |
(Wei et al, 2023; Rewthamrongsris et al, 2026)

Augmented reality is a third, less developed method (Rewthamrongsris et al, 2026).

### Workflow
1. CBCT, with or without an intraoral scan.
2. Plan osteotomy site, depth and root-end resection level in software.
3. Fabricate the guide (static) or register the patient to the tracker (dynamic).
4. Perform the osteotomy and resection through the guide or on screen.
5. Complete the root-end preparation and filling as in standard microsurgery.

### Indications
Obliterated canals, anatomical abnormality, retreatment, posterior teeth needing microsurgery, and autotransplantation (Wei et al, 2023).

### Accuracy measures used in studies
Three-dimensional deviation at the bur tip, error in drilled root length, and angular deviation of the resection (Rewthamrongsris et al, 2026). Static and dynamic guidance both reduced the first; they did not differ in root length error.

### Context
Unguided microsurgery already reports success of 91 to 94% (Rubinstein et al, 2021), so any benefit of guidance is likely to be greatest in difficult anatomy rather than in routine cases [Guessing].

### Advantages and limits
- Greater accuracy than freehand in laboratory and cadaver studies; the least operating time with static guides (Rewthamrongsris et al, 2026).
- Cost, learning curve and need for CBCT; most clinical reports are case reports with a mean follow-up of about 10 months (Kapoor et al, 2025).

### Status of the evidence
Mostly in vitro and cadaver studies, with very few clinical studies; clinical benefit is not yet proved, and long-term trials are needed (Rewthamrongsris et al, 2026; Kapoor et al, 2025).
