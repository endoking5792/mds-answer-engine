## STUDY MODE

**Aim.** The cavity must be deep enough for the filling to resist displacement and to seal every tubule and canal exit exposed by the cut. The ideal is a Class I cavity along the long axis of the root, at least 3 mm deep (Johnson et al, 2021; Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Gilheany's work showed the needed depth depends on the bevel: 1 mm at 0 degrees, 2.1 mm at 30 and 2.5 mm at 45, and a 3.5 mm radiographic depth was advised to cover errors of angulation (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Harty's gives 2 to 4 mm as generally sufficient (Gutmann, 2017) [SRC].

**Bur method (traditional).** A bur in a micro-handpiece was used. Problems: poor access in a narrow crypt, risk of perforating the root, insufficient depth, and no way of cleaning an isthmus (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC].

**Ultrasonic method (current).** Tips of about 0.25 mm diameter and 3 mm length, driven at 28,000 to 40,000 cycles per second, abrade dentin and soften gutta-percha under continuous irrigation (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Ingle's calls it the current standard of practice (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Tips follow the direction of the canals and clean better than burs (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Advantages over burs: less bone removal for access, a conservative preparation centred in the canal, less perforation risk, deeper and more consistent cavities with less bevel (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC], more parallel walls and cleaner surfaces (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC], and less smear layer (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. In one randomised trial of 399 patients success was 80.5% with ultrasonic tips against 70.9% with burs (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC].

**Technique.** Start at low power and raise it until the stroke is smooth and chatter-free; tips differ between units (tip specificity) (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Use light, repeated strokes, because ultrasonics work by vibration, not pressure (Floratos and Kim, 2017) [SRC]. If resistance is met the tip is probably turned lingually, and continuing may perforate (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. Diamond-coated tips cut faster and remove gutta-percha better, while stainless steel leaves cleaner walls (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. A back-action tip (70 to 80 degrees) clears the facial wall (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019) [SRC]. A micromirror must confirm a clean cavity (Rubinstein et al, 2021) [SRC].

**The crack problem.** Three types are described: intracanal, extracanal and communicating (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. In vitro studies show more microfractures after ultrasonic preparation, lower with low power; cadaver and in vivo studies do not (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Ingle's finds no evidence that the cracks lower the success rate (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Heat is a second concern, so every tip needs an irrigation port (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Alternatives.** An Er:YAG laser produced less leakage than ultrasonics in an in vitro comparison (Karlovic et al, 2005) [LIT, abstract; in vitro only]. For bonded fillings the preparation is shallow and scalloped (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Further textbook points (second audit against the remaining books, 2026-10-18).** 
*Alternative preparations.* Weine and Barnes describe slot and figure-of-eight preparations for flat or oval roots and an amalgam retention mechanism of cavity and slot design; amalgam is now historical (Weine, 2004; Barnes, 1984 (older text)) [SRC]. Gutmann describes the K-file with an ultrasonic tip as a way of cleaning the canal (Gutmann (Problem Solving Ch.16)) [SRC]. The ultrasonic tip may cause cracks: see the Q01 conflict (Tawil) (von Arx and White, 2019) [SRC].

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

### Root-end preparation
**Aim:** a clean, retentive Class I cavity along the long axis of the root, 3 mm deep, which holds the filling and seals all canal exits (Johnson et al, 2021; Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Depth needed rises with the bevel:** 1 mm at 0 degrees, 2.1 mm at 30 degrees, 2.5 mm at 45 degrees (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).

**Methods**
1. Bur in a micro-handpiece (old): poor access, perforation risk, shallow, isthmus cannot be treated (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).
2. Ultrasonic tips (current standard).

### Ultrasonic root-end preparation
- Tips about 0.25 mm wide, 3 mm long, 28,000 to 40,000 cycles per second, with irrigation (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019). Stainless steel, diamond-coated or zirconium nitride.
- Power low at first; light brush strokes; check alignment with the long axis (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).
- Diamond-coated tips last; they cut faster (Johnson et al, 2021).

**Advantages**
- Less bone removed; smaller osteotomy.
- Centred in the canal; fewer perforations.
- Deeper, parallel-walled, cleaner cavity; less bevel needed.
- Isthmus can be prepared.
- Less smear layer (Johnson et al, 2021; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).
- Success 80.5% against 70.9% with burs (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

**Disadvantages**
- Microfractures in vitro, lessened by low power and irrigation; clinical effect not shown (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).
- Heat if irrigation is poor; tip fracture (Johnson et al, 2021).

### Isthmus
An isthmus is present in premolars and molars in roughly 80% to 90% of cases at the 3 mm level, so the preparation must join the canals through it (Floratos and Kim, 2017).

### After preparation
- Rinse and dry with a microtip irrigator; inspect with a micromirror for debris on the facial wall (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).
- Fill with MTA or tricalcium silicate cement (Gopikrishna, 2021): carry small increments, condense with a plugger that fits the cavity, do not pull the material out, wipe the excess and check the margin (Rubinstein and Fayad, 2019).
- Take a radiograph before closing.

### Bonded technique
Shallow, scalloped preparation over the whole root face, filled in a dome (Johnson et al, 2021).

- Root-end cracks: Tawil found healing 30% to 32% with cracks vs 95% to 97% without (von Arx and White, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
3 mm Class I; 0.25 mm tip; 28,000 to 40,000 cycles per second; 80.5% against 70.9%; low power plus irrigation; micromirror check.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
Resected root with a 3 mm deep cavity along the long axis, and the same cavity wrongly angled towards the lingual wall.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Describing only burs.
- Forgetting the fracture and heat problems.
- Forgetting to inspect with a micromirror.
