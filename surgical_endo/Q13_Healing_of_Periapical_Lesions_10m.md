## STUDY MODE

**Principle.** Satisfactory wound healing is the goal of treatment, and the aim of endodontic therapy is to create conditions in which it can happen (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Healing begins as soon as the irritants (microbial and nonmicrobial) in the canal or periapical tissue are removed; inflammatory mediators are then no longer produced and the existing ones are inactivated by the body's control mechanisms (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. What actually switches inflammation off is not well known (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

**Mechanism.** Activated macrophages kill bacteria and clear the lesion, which Lin and Huang call biologic debridement (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Fibrovascular granulation tissue forms; hyperplastic cells are deleted by apoptosis; the extracellular matrix is remodelled by metalloproteinases (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Healing is largely regeneration and to a small degree repair (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Cells of the viable periodontal ligament from adjacent root surfaces cover the denuded root; cementum growth factors (IGF-1, FGFs, EGF, BMP, TGF-beta, PDGF) sequestered after cemental resorption induce PDL stem cells to form cementoid tissue (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Bone regenerates from osteoprogenitor cells in the endosteum and the inner periosteum (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. The new PDL then remodels, with Sharpey fibres in new cementum and new bone (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Lin and Huang compare the process to guided tissue regeneration in periodontics, since both remove irritants and create a favourable microenvironment (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

**Repair by scar.** If both cortical plates are destroyed by a large lesion, the periosteum below the mucosa may also be destroyed and the lesion may repair with fibrous scar, which is why membranes and grafts are sometimes advised in surgery (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. A periapical scar is described as the ultimate healing attempt of the tissue and its incidence is 6.6% to 12% (Said-Al-Naief, 2019) [SRC].

**Nonsurgical versus surgical.** The mechanisms are similar, but surgical debridement is quick, whereas biologic debridement takes time; surgery may produce faster bone healing but a higher risk of late failure (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. After nonsurgical treatment 78% of apical periodontitis lesions heal (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

**Cysts.** Lin and Huang say no study has shown whether cysts regress after nonsurgical treatment, and that a true cyst may regress by apoptosis if infection is controlled, although they also describe true cysts as less likely to heal (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. See the conflict box in Q12.

**Reactive bone.** Lesions with reactive bone formation (focal condensing osteitis) heal after treatment, with remodelling of the dense bone (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

**Evaluating healing.** *Ørstavik* (Endodontic Radiology Ch.16): density changes may appear after a few weeks and quite regularly at 3 to 6 months, about 1 year is needed to judge the overall outcome, and complete healing can be seen as late as 17 to 27 years after surgery (Ørstavik, 2012) [SRC]. His periapical index treats a score above 2 as disease and reports 1-year failure of about 5 to 10% for filled vital teeth against 20 to 30% for teeth with apical periodontitis (Ørstavik, 2012) [SRC]. After surgery, 6-month results favoured surgery, but at 1 to 2 years retreatment was as good or better (Kvist and Reit, quoted in Ørstavik, 2012) [SRC]. The AAE replaces success and failure with healed (functional, asymptomatic, no or minimal radiographic pathosis), healing (asymptomatic and functional but with pathosis present) and nonhealed (nonfunctional, symptomatic) (Setzer et al, 2021) [SRC]. Six months is a reasonable first review. A lesion that is unchanged or larger after 1 year is unlikely to resolve; if it is smaller at 6 months it is healing and review continues; larger lesions heal more slowly; late healing and late failure both occur (Setzer et al, 2021) [SRC]. With the periapical index complete healing may need 4 to 5 years, particularly after surgery (Setzer et al, 2021) [SRC]. CBCT detects more lesions and increases the share of uncertain and unsatisfactory healing at 1 year (Setzer et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Factors that delay healing.** Persistent infection, foreign material, poor nutrition and diabetes (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. A filling more than 2 mm short lowers healing from 94% to 68% (Setzer et al, 2021) [SRC]. HIV patients heal as well as others (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

### Definition
Healing of a periapical lesion is the resolution of the inflammation and the restoration of bone, cementum and periodontal ligament after the irritants have been removed by root canal treatment or surgery (Lin and Huang, 2021).

### Stages
1. **Removal of irritants:** inflammatory mediators fall and are inactivated.
2. **Biologic debridement:** activated macrophages kill bacteria and clear necrotic tissue.
3. **Granulation tissue:** fibroblasts, new vessels; unwanted cells removed by apoptosis.
4. **Regeneration:**
   - Bone from endosteal and periosteal osteoprogenitor cells.
   - Cementum: PDL stem cells form cementoid tissue under cementum growth factors.
   - PDL: fibres insert into new cementum and bone.
5. **Remodelling** to a mature PDL (Lin and Huang, 2021).

### Regeneration or repair
Mostly regeneration with some repair (Lin and Huang, 2021). Repair by fibrous scar occurs when both cortical plates and the periosteum are lost; a periapical scar is found in 6.6% to 12% of lesions (Lin and Huang, 2021; Said-Al-Naief, 2019).

### Healing in special lesions
- Abscess and sinus tract: heal without systemic antibiotics.
- Cysts: pocket cysts probably heal; true cysts disputed (Lin and Huang, 2021).
- Condensing osteitis: heals, bone remodels.

### Nonsurgical against surgical healing
Surgery debrides faster and bone heals quicker, but late failure may be higher; nonsurgical healing relies on macrophages and takes longer (Lin and Huang, 2021).

### Clinical and radiographic criteria
- AAE terms: healed = functional, asymptomatic, no or minimal radiographic pathosis; healing = asymptomatic and functional but pathosis present; nonhealed = nonfunctional, symptomatic (Setzer et al, 2021).
- First review at 6 months; unchanged or larger at 1 year means failure; smaller at 6 months means continue review; PAI may need 4 to 5 years.

### Factors affecting healing
- Local: infection, foreign body, apical extent of filling (94% when within 0 to 2 mm, 68% when more than 2 mm short), lesion size, coronal leakage.
- Systemic: diabetes lowers the chance of healing; HIV patients heal as well as others (Lin and Huang, 2021; Setzer et al, 2021).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
Biologic debridement; mostly regeneration; 78% healing; review at 6 months and 1 year; filling within 2 mm gives 94%; scar 6.6% to 12%.
