## STUDY MODE

**What a flap must do.** The goal is access to the root end with soft tissue that heals by primary intention and keeps its esthetics (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. The soft-tissue handling, meaning the incision, flap raising, handling and suturing, is responsible for the final outcome, mainly the amount of gingival recession and attachment loss (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Ingle's lists the properties of a well-designed flap: easy to make and reflect, adequate access, no loss of esthetics, no loss of blood flow to the flap or gingival margin and papilla, and no impingement on important anatomy (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC].

**Rules that apply to every flap.** Do not place an incision over a bony defect, and keep about 5 mm between the defect and incision (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Vertical incisions go on the interdental bone, never on the radicular eminence (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], because the mucosa over an eminence is thin and tears easily (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Vertical incisions should be parallel to the long axis of the tooth and never converge (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC], because vessels run roughly along the teeth. Circulation fails when the flap length to base ratio exceeds 2:1 (Velvart and Peters, 2019; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. A larger flap is generally preferred because it prevents tearing and trauma by retractors (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC].

**Classification** (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]: full mucoperiosteal flaps (triangular, rectangular, trapezoidal, horizontal or envelope, papilla-based) and limited mucoperiosteal flaps (submarginal curved or semilunar, and submarginal scalloped rectangular, the Luebke-Ochsenbein flap).

**Triangular (one vertical incision).** Good access, minimal vascular disruption, easy repositioning, suited to marginal problems (perforation, cervical resorption, short roots), and convertible to rectangular if access is poor; the drawback is recession risk from the marginal incision and limited apical reflection (Velvart and Peters, 2019; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. For palatal roots the release goes between canine and first premolar to avoid the greater palatine bundle (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC].

**Rectangular (two verticals).** The most-used design in endodontic surgery and good in any region, especially for long roots and shallow vestibules (Velvart and Peters, 2019; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. A trapezoid (broad base) is argued by some for blood supply; Kim argues against it on limited evidence and because it cuts vessels obliquely (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]; Velvart and Peters conclude that vertical incisions must not converge (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. This is a real disagreement over shape that I have shown on both sides.

**Sulcular full-thickness flap.** Grossman's: the design of choice for endodontic microsurgery (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], giving the best access, but laborious to reposition and a cause of recession and loss of the papilla in the esthetic zone (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Human studies show about 0.5 to 0.62 mm crestal bone loss after full-thickness flaps (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. In one study none of 17 papilla sites stayed at preoperative height after a sulcular flap (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC].

**Semilunar flap.** Fits drainage only (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; it gives limited access, severs vessels horizontally, lies over a bone defect and is under constant tension, so Velvart and Peters call it obsolete (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC].

**Submarginal (Luebke-Ochsenbein) flap.** Scalloped horizontal incision in attached gingiva with two vertical releases, designed by Ochsenbein and Luebke in 1974 (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Grossman's: 3 to 5 mm from the gingival attachment and 45 degrees to the cortical plate (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Advantages: the gingival margin around the crown is untouched, the scallop guides exact repositioning, and it is preferred for crowned anterior teeth (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Limits: needs at least 2 mm of attached gingiva (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]; unusable with short roots, large lesions, sinus tracts above the incision, or a frenum in the way (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]; the incision may cross the osteotomy; there is no access to the root under the band of gingiva; scar lines are common (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Von Arx found significantly less recession than with sulcular and papilla-base incisions in the first year, but the difference was not significant at 5 years (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC].

**Papilla-base flap (Velvart).** Two vertical incisions joined by a papilla-base incision and a sulcular incision in the cervical area; designed to prevent papilla recession (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Technique: a shallow incision about 1.5 mm deep at the base of the papilla, then a second incision to crestal bone, which makes a split-thickness flap at the papilla base and full thickness apically (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. The reported mean papilla height change at one month was 0.05 ± 0.39 mm, and virtually no recession at 3 months, compared with 1.25 mm for sulcular incision (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]; none of the operated sites showed any measurable loss of papilla height at 3 months (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. It needs a skilled surgeon and a blade no wider than 2.5 mm (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC].

**Modified papillary design.** Sabeti and colleagues: horizontal papillary incisions at the CEJ extending two teeth either side, a beveled c-shaped vertical incision with cutback, giving flexibility and less scarring (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC].

**Factors that choose the design.** Tissue biotype, lip line, restorations and crown margins, access, mucogingival status, periodontal condition, soft tissue thickness, vestibule depth and frenum, lesion location and extent, number of teeth, root length, nearness to vital structures (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Thin biotypes recede more (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]. Papilla presence depends on the distance from contact point to bone: at 5 mm or less nearly 100%, at 6 mm 56%, at 7 mm 27% or less (Velvart and Peters, 2019; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Insufficient blood supply and excessive drying can necrose the non-reflected tissue (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC].

**Elevation and retraction.** Start in the vertical incision, undermine the periosteum without tearing it, since an intact periosteum keeps mucosa out of the bony crypt (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]; make a small groove in bone as a rest for the retractor (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]; keep the flap and any non-reflected tissue moist (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC].

**Evidence.** Ingle's records disagreement: Harrison and Jurosky, and Chindia and Valdehaug, found no difference in healing between intrasulcular and submarginal incisions, while Kramper and colleagues found persistent inflammation and bone loss of 0.5 to 1 mm with intrasulcular incisions; it also warns that periodontal flap data may not transfer to endodontic surgery (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. A narrative review for the anterior maxilla concludes that flap choice depends on marginal periodontium, lesion extent, restoration margin and esthetic demand, and that recession may be created while solving the endodontic problem (von Arx and Salvi, 2008) [LIT, abstract]. A 2026 technique report describes a dual-layer coronally advanced and trapdoor flap for the esthetic zone, with a single case and no comparative data (Kuo et al, 2026) [LIT, abstract].

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim: papilla-base and submarginal flaps give less recession than sulcular flaps.** Textbook: Velvart and Peters and Ingle's report that papilla loss is greatest with sulcular incisions and least with papilla-base (Velvart and Peters, 2019; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Retrieved evidence: the cited von Arx study found less recession with submarginal incisions at first but no significant difference at 5 years (Velvart and Peters, 2019) [SRC]; Ingle's cites an RCT showing lower gingival margin height with full-thickness than papilla-based flaps at one month, yet no difference in bleeding, attachment or probing depth (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019) [SRC]. Strength: small clinical studies, short follow-up for the papilla-base data, and the original technique reports come from its originator. Conclusion: papilla-base reduces early recession in the studies cited; whether it changes long-term outcome is not shown [Likely]. The sources disagree on whether the sulcular flap or the papilla-based one is "the design of choice": Grossman's names the sulcular flap as the choice for microsurgery and the papilla-based for recession-free healing (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

## EXAM MODE

### Aim and principles
Provide access to the root end with healing by primary intention and preserved esthetics (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).
- One sharp incision to bone; never incise over a bony defect (keep about 5 mm clear) or over a root eminence.
- Verticals on interdental bone, parallel, never converging; length to base ratio not more than 2:1.
- Keep papilla and attached gingiva; keep tissue moist; avoid tension; use a retractor resting on bone (Gopikrishna, 2021; Velvart and Peters, 2019).

### Classification (Grossman)
1. **Full mucoperiosteal flaps:** triangular, rectangular, trapezoidal, horizontal (envelope), papilla-based.
2. **Limited mucoperiosteal flaps:** submarginal curved (semilunar), submarginal scalloped rectangular (Luebke-Ochsenbein).

### Comparison
| Flap | Incision | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triangular | Sulcular + 1 vertical | Easy, little vascular disruption | Limited apical access; recession |
| Rectangular | Sulcular + 2 verticals | Best access, long roots, any region | Recession; harder repositioning |
| Sulcular full thickness | Sulcular | Choice for microsurgery; full access | Papilla loss; recession; 0.5-0.62 mm bone loss |
| Luebke-Ochsenbein | Scalloped, in attached gingiva, 2 verticals | Marginal gingiva intact; crowned anteriors | Needs at least 2 mm attached gingiva; scar; no access under the band |
| Papilla-base | 1.5 mm shallow cut at papilla base, then to bone | Least papilla recession | Skilled technique; narrow blade |
| Semilunar | Curved in mucosa | Drainage only | Poor access; cuts vessels; obsolete |

### Description of each flap
**1. Triangular flap.** One vertical releasing incision plus a sulcular horizontal incision. Easy to reposition and keeps most of the blood supply; suited to marginal lesions, perforations, cervical resorption and short roots; can be converted to rectangular by adding a second vertical. Weakness: limited apical reflection and recession from the marginal incision. For palatal roots the vertical release lies between canine and first premolar to avoid the greater palatine bundle (Velvart and Peters, 2019).

**2. Rectangular flap.** Two vertical incisions at least one tooth away from the operated tooth, joined by a sulcular incision. The commonest flap in endodontic surgery; excellent access in any region and for long roots with a shallow vestibule. Vertical incisions run parallel to the vessels, so the blood supply is disturbed least (Velvart and Peters, 2019; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

**3. Trapezoidal flap.** A broad-based rectangular flap. Proposed for blood supply, but Kim argues against it because it cuts vessels obliquely, and Velvart and Peters state that verticals must never converge (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019; Velvart and Peters, 2019).

**4. Sulcular (envelope) full-thickness flap.** Horizontal incision from the sulcus through the periodontal ligament fibres to crestal bone, passing through the mid-col so buccal and lingual papillae are separated; the vertical is placed between root eminences. Best access, so it is the flap of choice in microsurgery; it can leave recession and a flattened papilla, which matters in the anterior esthetic zone (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**5. Luebke-Ochsenbein (submarginal scalloped rectangular) flap.** Two parallel vertical incisions with a scalloped horizontal incision in the attached gingiva, 3-5 mm from the gingival attachment and cut at 45 degrees to the cortical plate, so the widest cut surface adapts when repositioned. The marginal gingiva and crown margin stay untouched. Needs at least 2 mm of attached gingiva above the incision, healthy periodontium and no excessive pocket depth. Scarring is common (Gopikrishna, 2021; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

**6. Papilla-base flap (Velvart).** Used to avoid recession of the papilla. Shallow curved incision 1.5 mm deep across the papilla base, then a second incision to bone, so the papilla stays attached and only the base is split-thickness; full thickness from there apically. (Velvart and Peters, 2019).

**7. Semilunar flap.** Curved incision in the alveolar mucosa; drainage only, and obsolete for access (Gopikrishna, 2021; Velvart and Peters, 2019).

### Why the vertical incisions are placed the way they are
Vessels run roughly along the long axis of the tooth, so parallel verticals sever the fewest. The vertical incision starts paramedial to the papilla and at a right angle to the gingival margin; a vertical through the middle of the papilla leaves poorly supplied tissue that may necrose (Velvart and Peters, 2019).

### Choosing the flap
Biotype, lip line, crown margins, attached gingiva, lesion, root length, vital structures (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019). Papilla survives when contact point to crest is 5 mm or less (nearly 100%); 6 mm, 56%; 7 mm, 27% (Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

### Flap handling
Undermine with periosteum intact; bone groove as retractor rest; keep flap moist; short retraction lowers morbidity (Velvart and Peters, 2019; Torabinejad, Sabeti and Glickman, 2019).

### Evidence
Sulcular flaps lose papilla height; papilla-base incisions showed no measurable loss at 3 months in the originator's study; submarginal flaps show less recession early but not at 5 years (Velvart and Peters, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
Verticals on interdental bone; 2:1 ratio; Luebke-Ochsenbein needs 2 mm attached gingiva; sulcular flap is the microsurgery choice but recedes; papilla-base for esthetics; semilunar obsolete.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Drawing the vertical incision over the root eminence.
- Giving the semilunar flap as a standard access flap.
- Using Luebke-Ochsenbein without checking attached gingiva width.
- Calling the Luebke-Ochsenbein flap recession-proof.
- Forgetting that the incision must lie on sound bone, not over the defect.
