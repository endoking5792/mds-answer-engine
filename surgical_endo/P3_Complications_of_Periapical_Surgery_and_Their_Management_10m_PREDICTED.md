## STUDY MODE

**Definition.** A complication is any undesirable, unintended and direct result of surgery that would not have occurred had the surgery gone as well as could reasonably be hoped (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. Serious complications are rare; careful case selection, minimal trauma and good patient management keep the rate low (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Soft tissue.** Gingival recession follows incorrect repositioning, poor blood circulation or flap contraction; thin biotypes are at higher risk (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. Flap necrosis follows insufficient blood supply, so the flap base must be wider than the free margin and the tissue must not be crushed (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. Wound margins may separate within the first week through tissue failure or poor suturing (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. Teeth with periodontal lesions have a success rate that may be below 80%, against more than 90% without them (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC].

**Bone and root end.** Excess heat during the osteotomy interrupts blood flow and causes osteonecrosis, so only light brushing strokes with copious irrigation are used (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. Excess root resection can reduce the crown-root ratio, and inadequate resection or poor visualisation is the main cause of missed canals (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. Ultrasonic tips can fracture and cause dentinal cracks; a light touch, moderate power and irrigation reduce this (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC].

**Bleeding.** Mild bleeding is common and severe haemorrhage is rare, but bleeding disorders increase the risk (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. Steady pressure for 20 to 30 minutes controls most postoperative bleeding; if it persists, pressure and local anaesthetic with 1:50,000 epinephrine, then the sutures are removed and the vessel is crushed or cauterised (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Hematoma or ecchymosis may last up to 2 weeks and need no treatment (Tsesis et al, 2025; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. A secondary bleeding phase (rebound) may follow vasoconstrictor infiltration, so root-end work that needs a dry field is done first (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC].

**Nerve injury.** Direct trauma is the most frequent cause; most inferior alveolar nerve injuries are reported in second mandibular molars (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. CBCT shows the relation of the lesion and root apices to the nerve (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. Most patients recover sensation within 3 to 4 months unless the nerve is cut; if there is no sign of recovery by 10 to 12 weeks, refer for neuromicrosurgical assessment (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Sinus.** Perforation of the Schneiderian membrane is reported in 9.6 to 50% of cases; it is not detrimental to outcome so long as foreign material and the root apex are kept out of the sinus (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. Routine antibiotics and decongestants for sinus exposure are not supported by evidence (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Pain, swelling and infection.** Pain is worst in the first 24 hours and swelling peaks at 1 to 2 days (Tsesis et al, 2025; Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Preemptive analgesia is advised; in one report a single 8 mg dose of oral dexamethasone before surgery was followed by 76% of patients being pain-free at day 1 (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC]. NSAIDs are the usual drug (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. There is no consensus on antibiotics; they are considered mainly for immunocompromised patients or systemic signs, and the chapter relies on antiseptic rinses (Tsesis et al, 2025) [SRC].

**Further textbook points (second audit against the remaining books, 2026-10-18).** 
*Correction.* Dexamethasone is given before surgery and for 2 days afterwards, not as a single dose (Reiser, 2019) [SRC]. Infection after surgery is uncommon, and antibiotics are reserved for systemic involvement (Reiser, 2019) [SRC].

## EXAM MODE

### Definition
Any undesirable, unintended and direct result of surgery that would not have occurred had it gone as well as hoped (Tsesis et al, 2025).

### Classification with prevention and management
| Complication | Cause | Prevention and management |
|---|---|---|
| Recession, papilla loss | Faulty repositioning, poor blood supply, thin biotype | Preserve root-attached tissue, tension-free repositioning (Tsesis et al, 2025) |
| Flap necrosis | Insufficient blood supply, crushing | Wide base, no crushing, short operating time (Tsesis et al, 2025) |
| Wound dehiscence | Poor suturing, tissue failure | Passive sutures for at least 3 days; re-close or let heal (Tsesis et al, 2025) |
| Bone necrosis | Heat from the bur | Light strokes, copious irrigation (Tsesis et al, 2025) |
| Missed canal, crack, tip fracture | Poor resection or visualisation; ultrasonic energy | Adequate resection, magnification; low power, irrigation (Tsesis et al, 2025) |
| Haemorrhage, hematoma | Vessel injury, bleeding disorder | Pressure 20 to 30 minutes, vasoconstrictor, ligation or cautery; hematoma resolves by itself in 2 weeks (Johnson et al, 2021) |
| Nerve injury | Compression, stretching, cutting, heat | CBCT planning; observe; refer if no recovery by 10 to 12 weeks (Johnson et al, 2021) |
| Sinus perforation | Apex near the sinus | Keep apex and materials out of the sinus; no routine antibiotics (Tsesis et al, 2025; Johnson et al, 2021) |
| Pain, swelling | Surgical trauma, infection | Preemptive NSAID or dexamethasone (Johnson et al, 2021; Tsesis et al, 2025) |
| Infection | Contamination | Antiseptic rinse; antibiotics only for immunocompromised patients or systemic signs (Tsesis et al, 2025) |

### Timing of postoperative symptoms
Pain peaks on the day of surgery; swelling peaks on days 1 to 2; ecchymosis may persist for up to 2 weeks (Johnson et al, 2021; Tsesis et al, 2025).

### Prevention in general
Case selection and CBCT; a well-designed flap; a small osteotomy; microsurgical technique; thorough preoperative history including bleeding disorders; clear written instructions and follow-up (Tsesis et al, 2025; Johnson et al, 2021).

- Dexamethasone: before surgery and for 2 days after (Reiser, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
Recession in thin biotypes; heat necrosis of bone; rebound bleeding; inferior alveolar nerve injury in second molars; sinus perforation harmless if no material enters; swelling peaks at days 1 to 2.
