## STUDY MODE

### Why it is a problem
Gutta-percha is thermolabile and cannot be heat sterilised (Sikri, 2019; Gopikrishna, 2021). Cones are sterilised by the manufacturer, but contamination was found in 5-8% of cones taken straight from the package and in almost 20% after opening, from the clinical environment and handling (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Gutta-percha has a smooth surface and contains zinc oxide, which keep contamination low (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### Methods
| Agent | Result in the textbooks |
|---|---|
| **5.25% NaOCl, 1 min** | Standard method; also killed *Bacillus subtilis* spores in the study cited by Cohen's (Berman et al, 2021; Sikri, 2019; Gopikrishna, 2021). Ingle's: 5-6% NaOCl for 60 s eliminates Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria and even *B. subtilis* spores (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). Lower concentrations have also been tried but higher is preferred (Sikri, 2019). |
| **Rinse after NaOCl** | Hydrogen peroxide or ethyl alcohol (Sikri, 2019) to remove NaOCl crystals. |
| **2% chlorhexidine** | Not a true conflict (Niranjan, 2026-10-16): effective within 5 min (Sikri, 2019) against vegetative organisms, but not effective against *B. subtilis* spores specifically (Berman et al, 2021) — compatible claims about different microbial targets, not a disagreement. |
| **2% glutaraldehyde, 70% ethyl alcohol** | Not effective against *B. subtilis* spores (Berman et al, 2021). |
| **10% polyvinylpyrrolidone-iodine** | Effective in 30 s to 1 min (Sikri, 2019). |
| **Paraformaldehyde vapour** | Effective in 1 h (Sikri, 2019). |

### Practical points
- Immerse for 1 min, then rinse; the procedure should not alter the structure of the cone (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
- Notch the master cone at the reference point and dip it in 5% NaOCl before use (Sikri, 2019).
- "There is no evidence on whether this step can affect the clinical outcome, [but] it should be considered good practice" (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
- Store cool and dry; the points become brittle in heat and humidity (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).
- Coated cones and carrier obturators: follow the manufacturer's instructions, since disinfectants may affect coatings [Likely; not stated in the books read].

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 300; about 14 min at 22 words/min)**

### Introduction
Gutta-percha is thermolabile and softens with heat, so it cannot be sterilised by autoclave or dry heat. Commercial cones are sterilised by the manufacturer, but they become contaminated during storage and handling, so chairside disinfection before use is advised.

### Why disinfection is needed
- Contamination was found in 5-8% of cones taken directly from the original package, and in almost 20% after the package had been opened, from the clinical environment and improper handling.
- Gutta-percha has a smooth surface, which resists bacterial adherence, and contains zinc oxide, which has some antibacterial effect; this is why contamination stays low.

### Methods and findings
| Agent | Time | Result in the textbooks |
|---|---|---|
| **5.25% sodium hypochlorite** | 1 min | The standard method; also killed *Bacillus subtilis* spores in the study cited by Cohen's; Ingle's gives 5-6% for 60 s, eliminating Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms and spores |
| Rinse after NaOCl | | Hydrogen peroxide or ethyl alcohol to remove hypochlorite |
| **2% chlorhexidine** | 5 min | Effective against vegetative organisms (Sikri); not effective against spores specifically (Cohen's) — not a true conflict |
| 2% glutaraldehyde, 70% ethyl alcohol | | Not effective against spores (Cohen's) |
| 10% polyvinylpyrrolidone-iodine | 30 s to 1 min | Effective (Sikri) |
| Paraformaldehyde vapour | 1 h | Effective (Sikri) |

### Practical protocol
1. Take the master cone from the package with sterile forceps.
2. Notch it at the reference point and immerse in 5.25% NaOCl for one minute.
3. Rinse with alcohol or hydrogen peroxide and dry.
4. Use immediately; do not return it to the pack.
The procedure should not alter the structure of the cone. There is no evidence that it changes clinical outcome, but it is regarded as good practice.

### Conclusion
Gutta-percha cannot be heat sterilised. Cold chemical disinfection with 5.25% sodium hypochlorite for one minute is the accepted chairside method, supported by laboratory evidence of spore kill; other agents are less reliable or conflicting.
