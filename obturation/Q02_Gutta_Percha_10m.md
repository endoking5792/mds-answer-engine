## STUDY MODE

### Source and chemistry
Gutta-percha is the dried sap of *Palaquium* trees (Sikri, 2019; Gopikrishna, 2021), the trans-isomer of polyisoprene, used in endodontics for over 100 years (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Berman et al, 2021). Hill (1847) made "Hill's stopping"; Bowman (1867) used it as a root filling; S.S. White made points from 1887 (Gopikrishna, 2021).

### Composition (commercial points)
| Source | Gutta-percha (matrix) | Zinc oxide (filler) | Radiopacifier (metal sulfates) | Wax/resin (plasticiser) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sikri | 18-22% | 59-67% | 1-18% | 1-4% |
| Ingle's | 18-22% | 59-76% | 1-18% | 1-4% |
| Azim and Aksel | 19-22% | 50-79% | 1-17% | 1-4% |
| Cohen's | about 20% | about 65% | about 10% | about 5% |
Zinc oxide is the filler and also confers weak bacteriostatic activity; barium sulfate gives radiopacity (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). Figures differ between books; give a range.

### Crystalline phases
- **Beta (β):** commercial form at room temperature; solid, compactable, elongatable, brittle with age, does not stick (Weine; Sikri, 2019; Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Alpha (α):** tacky, flowable under pressure. **Beta-to-alpha transition: 42-49 °C is the preferred figure** — three sources agree exactly (Weine; Sikri, 2019; Torabinejad et al), checked word for word against print, 2026-10-16. Azim and Aksel (2022) give a single outlier figure, about 70 °C, with no range; Cohen's and Grossman's describe no distinct transition point at all, just alpha melting above 65 °C (which may describe the alpha-to-liquid melt rather than the beta-to-alpha solid transition).
- **Third phase:** gamma at 56-62 °C (Weine) or amorphous above 53-59 °C (Torabinejad et al) — two sources agree this phase exists just above the beta-to-alpha range, though they name and bound it slightly differently; Weine's own text admits its properties "are not well known." Keep both figures, treat the phase's existence (not its exact bounds) as the more solid claim.
- Cooled slowly, alpha recrystallises; routine cooling gives beta (Berman et al, 2021). Alpha shrinks on cooling, so thermoplastic techniques need compaction during cooling (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). Alpha-phase points suit thermoplastic techniques, beta-phase suits lateral condensation (Gopikrishna, 2021).

### Forms
Standardised ISO cones (taper 0.02, sizes 15/25-140) and greater-taper cones (0.04, 0.06, variable-taper to match rotary systems); non-standardised (extra-fine to extra-large); pellets for injection; carrier-coated; medicated; GuttaFlow powder (Sikri, 2019; Gopikrishna, 2021). Standardised cones suit lateral condensation, non-standardised suit vertical compaction (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). ISO and ANSI tolerances mean cone size varies from the file (Berman et al, 2021).

### Advantages
Compactable once softened, adapts to walls; stable once set (shrinks only when heat- or solvent-softened); very low solubility; well tolerated, probably the most inert dental material; weak bacteriostasis; radiopaque; easily disinfected; removable with heat or solvents (chloroform, eucalyptol); poor heat conductor (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Gopikrishna, 2021).

### Disadvantages
Lacks rigidity (small sizes buckle); no adhesion to dentin, so a sealer is mandatory; displaced by pressure (overextension); shrinks on cooling; becomes brittle if stored in heat and humidity (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Sikri, 2019). Room-temperature gutta-percha cannot be compacted or made to flow, and compaction forces pass to the canal wall and may fracture the root (Berman et al, 2021).

### Medicated and modified
Calcium hydroxide, iodoform or chlorhexidine points are "not encouraged" (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019); effectiveness not shown, and to act the drug must leach out, harming the seal (Berman et al, 2021). GuttaCore: cross-linked gutta-percha core (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Nanodiamond-embedded gutta-percha is in clinical trials (Berman et al, 2021).

### Introduction
Gutta-percha is the most widely used core material in root canal obturation and has been used for more than 100 years. It is the dried sap of *Palaquium* trees, chemically the trans-isomer of polyisoprene.

### Composition of commercial points
| Component | Role | Proportion (Ingle's) |
|---|---|---|
| Gutta-percha | Matrix | 18-22% |
| Zinc oxide | Filler; weak bacteriostatic effect | 59-76% |
| Metal sulfates (barium sulfate) | Radiopacity | 1-18% |
| Waxes and resins | Plasticisers | 1-4% |
Other books give slightly different ranges (Sikri 59-67% zinc oxide; Azim and Aksel 50-79%; Cohen's about 65%), so quote a range.

### Phases
- **Beta phase:** commercial cones at room temperature; solid, compactable and elongatable, brittle when aged, non-adhesive; does not shrink.
- **Alpha phase:** produced by heating, **42-49 °C** (three sources agree: Weine, Sikri, Torabinejad et al; Azim and Aksel's 70 °C is an outlier). Tacky, flowable under pressure; shrinks as it cools, so compaction must continue during cooling.
- **Gamma/amorphous:** a further phase around 56-62 °C (Weine) or 53-59 °C (Torabinejad et al); properties poorly known.
Alpha-phase gutta-percha is used in thermoplastic techniques, beta-phase in lateral condensation.

### Forms
- Standardised ISO cones, 0.02 taper, sizes 15 or 25 to 140, matching instruments; used for lateral condensation.
- Greater-taper (0.04, 0.06) and variable-taper cones matched to rotary systems.
- Non-standardised cones (extra-fine to extra-large), used for vertical compaction.
- Pellets and cartridges for injection; carrier-coated obturators; medicated points; gutta-percha powder in GuttaFlow.

### Advantages
Compactable once softened; adapts to canal walls; dimensionally stable once set (it shrinks only when softened by heat or solvent); very low solubility; well tolerated, among the most inert dental materials; weakly bacteriostatic; radiopaque; semisolid on insertion; easily disinfected; removable with heat or solvents (chloroform, eucalyptol).

### Disadvantages
Lacks rigidity, so small sizes buckle; no adhesion to dentin, so a sealer is mandatory; displaced under pressure with risk of overextension; shrinks on cooling; becomes brittle when stored in heat and humidity. At room temperature it cannot be made to flow, and compaction forces pass to the canal wall and may fracture the root.

### Medicated and modified gutta-percha
Points carrying calcium hydroxide, iodoform or chlorhexidine are not encouraged: the effectiveness has not been shown and the drug must leach out, damaging the seal. GuttaCore has a cross-linked gutta-percha carrier.

### Conclusion
Gutta-percha remains the core material of choice because of its plasticity and inertness; its lack of adhesion and cooling shrinkage are managed by the sealer and by compaction during cooling.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Trans-polyisoprene; ZnO about 65% filler; barium sulfate radiopacity.
- Beta to alpha at 42-49 °C; alpha shrinks on cooling.
- Standardised cones for lateral; non-standardised for vertical.
- Cannot be heat sterilised.
