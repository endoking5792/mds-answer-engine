## STUDY MODE

### Parameters
Length and density: the filling should be dense, with no voids, from the orifice to the dentinocemental junction, sealing all portals of entry and exit (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Berman et al add taper, level of gutta-percha and the coronal seal (Berman et al, 2021).

### Length
0-2 mm from the radiographic apex gives the best outcome; success falls 12-14% for each millimetre of working length lost; fillings short of 2 mm leave unfilled infected areas, and fillings beyond the apex irritate the ligament (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Dentinocemental junction is about 0.5 mm (0.2-2.5 mm) from the radiographic apex (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### Density and voids
Absence of voids and adequate density are associated with better outcomes; poor density is a common, often unnoticed problem (Azim and Aksel, 2022; Problem Solving in Endodontics, Ch.12).

### Limits of radiographic assessment
Radiographic evaluation does not correlate well with leakage; a good two-dimensional appearance may not mean a seal; variation in radiographic interpretation, overlying bone and lack of uniformity limit reliability (Berman et al, 2021). A seal cannot be seen on a radiograph, and a radiograph shows only two dimensions of a three-dimensional object (Problem Solving in Endodontics, Ch.12). Radiopaque sealer extrusion does not prove a good seal (Problem Solving in Endodontics, Ch.12).

### Coronal seal
Part of the assessment: poor coronal restoration and poor root filling have similar odds of impairing healing (Berman et al, 2021).

### Outcome as the real test
Healing of apical periodontitis, absence of symptoms and radiographic repair; follow-up (Endodontic Prognosis; Azim and Aksel, 2022).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 131; about 6 min at 22 words/min)**

### Introduction
Obturation quality is judged by length, density and the coronal seal, and ultimately by healing.

### Criteria
1. **Length:** 0-2 mm from the radiographic apex; each mm lost lowers success 12-14%.
2. **Density:** compact, homogeneous filling with no voids from orifice to apex.
3. **Taper:** continuous taper matching the preparation.
4. **Coronal seal:** orifice sealed and permanent restoration.
5. **Absence of overextension** or excess sealer.

### Radiographic assessment
Use at least two angulations. Radiographs show only two dimensions, cannot display a seal and correlate poorly with leakage; extruded sealer does not mean a perfect seal.

### Outcome measures
Absence of symptoms, radiographic healing of the lesion on follow-up, and a sound coronal restoration.

### Conclusion
Radiographic quality is necessary but not sufficient; a good-looking filling can leak, so follow-up is the final test.
