## STUDY MODE

### Sense 1: interappointment temporary double seal (Grossman's)
When treatment needs more than one visit, a temporary filling must seal the access cavity so saliva, bacteria and food cannot enter the canal and the intracanal medicament cannot leak (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Double seal:** a cotton pellet in the pulp chamber is sealed by an **inner seal of temporary stopping** and an **outer seal of Cavit or IRM** cement.
- **Single seal:** medicated cotton pellet in the chamber covered by a dry cotton pellet and sealed with Cavit or IRM.
- **Materials:** Cavit-G (zinc oxide, calcium sulfate, zinc sulfate, glycoacetate, polyvinyl acetate resin, triethanolamine; hygroscopic expansion gives a superior seal; minimum thickness 3.5 mm); IRM (resin-reinforced zinc oxide-eugenol; seal inferior to Cavit); TERM (light-cured UDMA composite, good seal even at 1-2 mm) (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- All interim materials leak invariably if left more than 3 weeks; ideal thickness 3-5 mm; renew medicament dressings within 1-2 weeks (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Significance:** prevents recontamination and loss of medicament between visits; the thickness and placement matter most (Torabinejad et al, Ch.15).

### Sense 2: apical plus coronal seal of the finished root filling [Likely]
In an obturation context "double seal" is commonly taken to mean a seal at both ends: the apical seal (core, sealer and apical plug) and the coronal seal (a barrier over the canal orifice and a permanent restoration). Textbook support for the *term* in this sense was not found in the books read, but the content is established: coronal leakage can occur quickly through seemingly well-obturated canals; Cavit or Super EBA, 3.5 mm thick, reduced bacterial leakage by 85% and 65% against unsealed controls at 45 days; lining the pulp chamber floor with bonded material gave no bacterial leakage at 60 days (Berman et al, 2021). See Q12.

### Sense 3: root-end surgery (Gulabivala and Ng)
A "double seal" is the physical barrier of the root-end filling material together with a biological seal formed by regeneration of the periodontal apparatus over the resected root face; the ideal outcome in apical surgery, achievable with the root-end materials discussed (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Harty's also uses "double seal" for a bioceramic cap over the blood clot with a conventional restoration above it in regenerative endodontics (Chong, 2017; year to confirm).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 130; about 6 min at 22 words/min)**

### Definition
A double seal is a two-layer barrier that prevents leakage into the canal.

### Interappointment double seal
Cotton pellet in the chamber, inner seal of temporary stopping, outer seal of Cavit (minimum 3.5 mm) or IRM. Single seal uses a dry cotton pellet and one cement.

### Significance
- Prevents salivary and bacterial contamination of the canal.
- Stops leakage of the intracanal medicament.
- Interim materials leak after 3 weeks, so visits must be planned within that time.

### Other meanings
- Apical seal plus coronal seal of the completed root filling: both are needed since coronal leakage can reinfect a well-filled canal.
- In root-end surgery: root-end filling plus biological seal from periodontal regeneration.

### Conclusion
Double sealing, whether between visits or after obturation, protects the disinfected canal from reinfection.
