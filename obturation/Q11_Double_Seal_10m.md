## STUDY MODE

### Sense 1: interappointment temporary double seal (Grossman's)
When treatment needs more than one visit, a temporary filling must seal the access cavity so saliva, bacteria and food cannot enter the canal and the intracanal medicament cannot leak (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Double seal:** a cotton pellet in the pulp chamber is sealed by an **inner seal of temporary stopping** and an **outer seal of Cavit or IRM** cement.
- **Single seal:** medicated cotton pellet in the chamber covered by a dry cotton pellet and sealed with Cavit or IRM.
- **Materials:** Cavit-G (zinc oxide, calcium sulfate, zinc sulfate, glycoacetate, polyvinyl acetate resin, triethanolamine; hygroscopic expansion gives a superior seal; minimum thickness 3.5 mm); IRM (resin-reinforced zinc oxide-eugenol; seal inferior to Cavit); TERM (light-cured UDMA composite, good seal even at 1-2 mm) (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- All interim materials leak invariably if left more than 3 weeks; ideal thickness 3-5 mm; renew medicament dressings within 1-2 weeks (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Significance:** prevents recontamination and loss of medicament between visits; the thickness and placement matter most (Torabinejad et al, Ch.15).

### Sense 2: apical plus coronal seal of the finished root filling [Likely]
In an obturation context "double seal" is commonly taken to mean a seal at both ends: the apical seal (core, sealer and apical plug) and the coronal seal (a barrier over the canal orifice and a permanent restoration). Textbook support for the *term* in this sense was not found in the books read, but the content is established: coronal leakage can occur quickly through seemingly well-obturated canals; Cavit or Super EBA, 3.5 mm thick, reduced bacterial leakage by 85% and 65% against unsealed controls at 45 days; lining the pulp chamber floor with bonded material gave no bacterial leakage at 60 days (Berman et al, 2021). See Q12.

### Sense 3: root-end surgery (Gulabivala and Ng)
A "double seal" is the physical barrier of the root-end filling material together with a biological seal formed by regeneration of the periodontal apparatus over the resected root face; the ideal outcome in apical surgery, achievable with the root-end materials discussed (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Harty's also uses "double seal" for a bioceramic cap over the blood clot with a conventional restoration above it in regenerative endodontics (Chong, 2017; year to confirm).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 389; about 18 min at 22 words/min)**

### Definition
A double seal is a two-layer temporary restoration placed in the access cavity between appointments to prevent leakage of saliva and bacteria into the canal and loss of the intracanal dressing.

### Why a temporary seal is needed
Multiple visits are required when a periradicular abscess drains through the canal or when disinfection is incomplete. The temporary filling must seal the access cavity so saliva with its bacterial flora, food and foreign material cannot contaminate the canal and the medicament cannot leak. Leakage can undo the most careful treatment.

### Double seal and single seal
| Seal | Construction |
|---|---|
| **Double seal** | A cotton pellet in the pulp chamber is covered by an **inner seal of temporary stopping** and an **outer seal of Cavit or IRM** cement |
| **Single seal** | A medicated cotton pellet is covered by a dry cotton pellet and sealed with Cavit or IRM |

### Materials
- **Cavit-G:** premixed zinc oxide, calcium sulfate, zinc sulfate, glycoacetate, polyvinyl acetate resin and triethanolamine. The hydrophilic calcium sulfate absorbs moisture and expands, giving a superior seal. Minimum thickness 3.5 mm.
- **IRM:** resin-reinforced zinc oxide-eugenol powder and liquid; a recommended filling for areas under occlusal load, although its seal is inferior to Cavit.
- **TERM:** light-cured UDMA composite interim filling; a good seal even at 1-2 mm.
- Zinc oxide-eugenol alone is not ideal because it leaks.

### Significance
- Prevents recontamination of the disinfected canal between visits.
- Keeps the intracanal medicament in place; dressings should be renewed within one to two weeks because exudate dilutes them and bacteria decompose them.
- All interim materials leak invariably if left more than three weeks, so the root canal treatment should be completed or the filling replaced within that time.
- Ideal thickness of the temporary filling: 3-5 mm. Thickness and placement matter most.

### Other meanings of the term
In obturation, the idea of a double seal can also be read as the apical seal plus the coronal seal of the finished root filling: both are needed because coronal leakage can reinfect a well-filled canal. In root-end surgery, a double seal is the root-end filling plus a biological seal from periodontal regeneration over the resected root face.

### Conclusion
A double seal of temporary stopping under Cavit or IRM, at least 3.5 mm thick, is the standard interappointment restoration and should not stay in place beyond three weeks.
