## STUDY MODE

### Definitions
**Overextension:** filling material beyond the apex with an inadequately filled canal. **Overfilling:** adequately filled canal with excess material beyond the apex. **Underfilling:** short of the apex (Sikri, 2019; Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### Effects
- Histology: severe inflammation despite absence of pain, "subclinical" (Ricucci and Langeland, in Azim and Aksel, 2022); chronic inflammation, encapsulation of the sealer against the root, apparent radiographic healing that may hide an unresolved reaction (Problem Solving in Endodontics, Ch.12).
- Healing delay: more than 10 years in some cases (Molven); about 14 months delay with extruded gutta-percha (Azim et al); 96% radiographic repair of overextended cases (Augsburger and Peters), mostly extruded sealer (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
- Sealer extrusion about 50% of cases without affecting outcome; type of extruded sealer made no difference at 4 years (Ricucci et al); sealer may persist (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
- Grossman's: extruded material can reduce healing probability by as much as 25% (Gopikrishna, 2021). Harty's: evidence "equivocal"; most excess sealer is absorbed with time, large volumes must be avoided (Chong).
- Bioceramics in Endodontics: poor outcomes in overfilled teeth without apical periodontitis were not associated with overfilling; symptoms relate to coexisting infection rather than the material's cytotoxicity (Chopra, Ch.12, citing Ricucci).
- Paraformaldehyde sealers: neurotoxicity, osteomyelitis and paresthesia after extrusion (Berman et al, 2021).

### Prevention (Sikri, 2019)
Meticulous working length control; avoid over-preparing the apical constriction; caution with rotary instruments; re-check if cone and working length disagree; judicious plasticised gutta-percha; place injection needle 3-6 mm from the apex (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Sikri, 2019).

### Management (Problem Solving in Endodontics, Ch.12)
- The mere presence of filling outside the canal is not a major cause for alarm if the canal is three-dimensionally obturated; inform the patient; review periodically.
- Lateral compaction overextension: tease the cone back before the sealer hardens; if hardened, soften apical gutta-percha with solvent, insert a Hedström file, let it harden and withdraw.
- Vertical compaction or injectable overextension: retraction is impossible; routine immediate surgery is not justified; surgery only if signs or symptoms of periradicular inflammation persist.
- Core-carrier overextension: remove the core; small attached gutta-percha may come with it.
Sikri adds that retrieval of overextended gutta-percha is difficult (Sikri, 2019).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 377; about 17 min at 22 words/min)**

### Definitions
**Overextension:** filling material extends beyond the apex while the canal is inadequately filled. **Overfilling:** an adequately filled canal with surplus material beyond the apex. **Underfilling:** material short of the apex.

### Effects of extruded material
- **Histology:** severe inflammation despite no clinical pain ("subclinical inflammation") when sealer or gutta-percha is extruded; chronic inflammation, encapsulation of sealer against the root, and apparent radiographic healing that may hide an unresolved reaction.
- **Healing:** delayed in some cases (more than 10 years in one report; about 14 months delay with extruded gutta-percha), yet 96% of overextended cases showed radiographic repair, mostly where sealer was the extruded material.
- **Frequency:** sealer extrusion occurs in about 50% of cases without affecting outcome; the sealer type (zinc oxide-eugenol, resin or calcium hydroxide) made no difference, and some sealer persisted at 4 years.
- **Conflict:** Grossman's states extruded material can reduce healing probability by as much as 25%; Harty's calls the evidence equivocal; Bioceramics in Endodontics notes that symptoms in overfilled teeth relate to coexisting infection rather than to the material.
- **Paraformaldehyde sealers:** neurotoxicity, osteomyelitis and paresthesia when forced through the foramen.

### Causes
Over-preparation or loss of the apical constriction, wrong working length, open apex or apical resorption, injection needle too near the apex, excessive compaction, and large sealer volume.

### Prevention
Accurate working length with an apex locator; preserve the constriction; confirm the cone fits to length; place injection needles 3-6 mm from the preparation; use an apical plug or custom cone when there is no stop; use a small volume of sealer.

### Management
1. If the canal is three-dimensionally filled, extrusion is not a major cause for alarm: inform the patient and review periodically.
2. **Lateral compaction:** tease the cone back before the sealer sets; if set, soften apical gutta-percha with solvent, insert a Hedström file into the softened mass, let it harden and withdraw it parallel to the canal axis.
3. **Warm vertical compaction or injected gutta-percha:** retraction is impossible; routine immediate surgery is not justified; periradicular surgery is indicated only if signs or symptoms of inflammation persist.
4. **Carrier overextension:** remove the core; small attached gutta-percha may come with it.

### Conclusion
Extrusion is best prevented. When it occurs, observation is usually correct; surgery is reserved for persistent symptoms or lesions.
