## STUDY MODE

### Definitions
**Overextension:** filling material beyond the apex with an inadequately filled canal. **Overfilling:** adequately filled canal with excess material beyond the apex. **Underfilling:** short of the apex (Sikri, 2019; Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### Effects
- Histology: severe inflammation despite absence of pain, "subclinical" (Ricucci and Langeland, in Azim and Aksel, 2022); chronic inflammation, encapsulation of the sealer against the root, apparent radiographic healing that may hide an unresolved reaction (Problem Solving in Endodontics, Ch.12).
- Healing delay: more than 10 years in some cases (Molven); about 14 months delay with extruded gutta-percha (Azim et al); 96% radiographic repair of overextended cases (Augsburger and Peters), mostly extruded sealer (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
- Sealer extrusion about 50% of cases without affecting outcome; type of extruded sealer made no difference at 4 years (Ricucci et al); sealer may persist (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
- Grossman's: extruded material can reduce healing probability by as much as 25% (Gopikrishna, 2021). Harty's: evidence "equivocal"; most excess sealer is absorbed with time, large volumes must be avoided (Chong).
- Bioceramics in Endodontics: poor outcomes in overfilled teeth without apical periodontitis were not associated with overfilling; symptoms relate to coexisting infection rather than the material's cytotoxicity (Chopra, Ch.12, citing Ricucci).
- Paraformaldehyde sealers: neurotoxicity, osteomyelitis and paresthesia after extrusion (Berman et al, 2021).

### Prevention (Sikri, 2019)
Meticulous working length control; avoid over-preparing the apical constriction; caution with rotary instruments; re-check if cone and working length disagree; judicious plasticised gutta-percha; place injection needle 3-6 mm from the apex (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Sikri, 2019).

### Management (Problem Solving in Endodontics, Ch.12)
- The mere presence of filling outside the canal is not a major cause for alarm if the canal is three-dimensionally obturated; inform the patient; review periodically.
- Lateral compaction overextension: tease the cone back before the sealer hardens; if hardened, soften apical gutta-percha with solvent, insert a Hedström file, let it harden and withdraw.
- Vertical compaction or injectable overextension: retraction is impossible; routine immediate surgery is not justified; surgery only if signs or symptoms of periradicular inflammation persist.
- Core-carrier overextension: remove the core; small attached gutta-percha may come with it.
Sikri adds that retrieval of overextended gutta-percha is difficult (Sikri, 2019).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 174; about 8 min at 22 words/min)**

### Definitions
Overextension: material beyond the apex with the canal inadequately filled. Overfilling: canal filled with surplus material beyond the apex.

### Effects
Chronic subclinical inflammation, encapsulation, delayed healing; however most extruded sealer is absorbed and many cases show repair. Evidence on its effect is conflicting (Grossman's: as much as 25% reduction; Harty's: equivocal; Azim and Aksel: 96% repair).

### Causes
Over-preparation of the constriction, lost working length, open apex, resorption, forceful compaction, injection too close to the apex.

### Prevention
Accurate working length with an apex locator, preserve the constriction, fit the cone to length, place injection needles 3-6 mm from the apex, consider an apical barrier or custom cone.

### Management
If the canal is well filled: reassure and review. Lateral compaction: tease the cone back before the sealer sets, or soften with solvent and retrieve with a Hedström file. Warm vertical or injected: cannot be withdrawn; surgery only if symptoms persist. Carrier: remove the core. Avoid paraformaldehyde sealers.

### Conclusion
Extrusion is avoidable; once present, observation is usually correct and surgery is reserved for persistent symptoms.
