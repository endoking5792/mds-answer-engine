## STUDY MODE

### Definition
Trifecta combines the two earlier syringe and carrier approaches, **Successfil** and **Ultrafil** (Sikri, 2019).

### Components
- **Successfil:** solid titanium or radiopaque plastic carrier coated with alpha-phase gutta-percha, supplied in syringes of high-viscosity gutta-percha that sets in about 2 min (Sikri, 2019).
- **Ultrafil:** alpha-phase gutta-percha in cannulas with a 22 gauge needle, softened in a heater at about 70-90 °C; regular (white), firm-set (blue) and endoset (green) viscosity; working time about 60-70 s (Sikri, 2019).

### Technique
1-2 mm of alpha-phase Successfil gutta-percha is coated on the tip of a carrier, placed to the required depth, **rotated slowly counter-clockwise and withdrawn**, leaving a small mass of gutta-percha apically. This is compacted. The coronal part of the canal is **back-filled with Ultrafil** gutta-percha (Sikri, 2019).

### Rationale
Apical control comes from the small, carrier-placed apical plug (as in sectional or apical-plug obturation), while the injectable backfill fills the wide coronal canal (Sikri, 2019; Berman et al, 2021 on injection systems).

### Advantages and limits
Combines apical control with dense backfill; both parts use heated gutta-percha, so sealer is needed and cooling shrinkage must be offset by compaction (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). Injection techniques risk extrusion if the needle is placed too close to the apex; keep the needle 4-6 mm from the end of the preparation (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). Little published outcome evidence in the books read. It does not appear in the recent textbooks (Cohen's, Grossman's, Ingle's, Azim and Aksel).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 110; about 5 min at 22 words/min)**

### Definition
Trifecta is a hybrid obturation technique that combines carrier-based apical placement (Successfil) with an injectable gutta-percha backfill (Ultrafil).

### Technique
1. Prepare, dry and apply sealer.
2. Coat 1-2 mm of alpha-phase gutta-percha on the tip of a Successfil carrier.
3. Place to the required depth, rotate counter-clockwise and withdraw; compact the apical mass.
4. Back-fill the coronal canal with Ultrafil.
5. Radiograph.

### Advantages
Apical control as with a carrier; dense, fast backfill.

### Disadvantages
Two systems and equipment; extrusion risk with injection; little outcome evidence; rarely used now.

### Conclusion
Trifecta shows the hybrid principle (controlled apical plug plus warm backfill) that modern down-pack and back-fill systems use more simply.
