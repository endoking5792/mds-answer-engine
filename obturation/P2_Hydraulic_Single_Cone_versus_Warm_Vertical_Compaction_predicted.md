## STUDY MODE

### Single cone with hydraulic sealer
A cone matched to the master apical file (MAF) and a calcium silicate sealer. Calcium silicate sealers do not shrink and expand by absorbing tissue fluid, so the technique resurfaced with them; it is easy, less technique-sensitive and has a high success rate in treatment and retreatment, but it is "sealer-dependent" and needs a non-shrinking sealer (Azim and Aksel, 2022). In a canal of constant taper the cone acts as a piston that pushes sealer laterally; oval canals or a poorly fitting cone defeat this (Chopra, Bioceramics, Ch.12). Grossman's: used with calcium silicate sealers but also zinc oxide-eugenol and epoxy (Gopikrishna, 2021).

### Warm vertical compaction / continuous wave
Heat plugger about 200 °C, down-pack in the apical 3-4 mm, thermoplastic back-fill. Denser, more homogeneous fill; most commonly used among specialists (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Needs a tapered preparation and skill; risks of root fracture and extrusion (Sikri, 2019). Heat plugger kept 3-5 mm from the apex leaves apical gutta-percha incompletely plasticised, lowering extrusion risk (Tennert et al, in Chopra, Bioceramics, Ch.12).

### Coneless / BioConeless
When no cone fits (apical deltas, abrupt curves, stripping) bioceramic sealer plus warm back-fill; foramen under about 0.7 mm; under- and overfilling risks (Chopra, Bioceramics, Ch.12). Heat-tolerant sealer (BC HiFlow) suits warm techniques; BioRoot RCS does not (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### Comparison
| Feature | Single cone + bioceramic | Warm vertical / continuous wave |
|---|---|---|
| Skill and time | Low | High |
| Fill of oval, irregular canals | Relies on sealer | Better adaptation |
| Sealer volume | Large | Small |
| Extrusion | Sealer may extrude | Gutta-percha and sealer |
| Retreatability | Debated (Q1 of `endodontic_sealers/`) | Good |
| Evidence | High success reported; no human comparative trials in Cohen's | Toronto data suggest advantage over lateral compaction |

### Evidence
Cohen's: little evidence of superiority; Toronto data favour warm vertical over lateral but not definitive (Berman et al, 2021). Azim and Aksel: clinical studies show no significant difference between techniques. Endodontic Prognosis: bioceramic materials lack clinical data for comparison.

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 201; about 9 min at 22 words/min)**

### Introduction
Hydraulic single-cone and warm vertical compaction represent the two ends of current practice: minimal gutta-percha and sealer-based sealing versus heat-compacted gutta-percha with minimal sealer.

### Single cone with calcium silicate sealer
Cone matched to the master apical file with tug-back; the hydrophilic non-shrinking sealer expands with tissue fluid and fills the space. Advantages: simple, quick, less technique-sensitive, high reported success. Disadvantages: sealer-dependent; poor in oval or irregular canals; larger sealer volume; retreatment debated.

### Warm vertical compaction / continuous wave
Heated plugger at about 200 °C down-packs gutta-percha to the apical 3-4 mm, then the canal is back-filled with injected gutta-percha. Advantages: dense, homogeneous, adapts to irregularities; most used by specialists. Disadvantages: skill, equipment, root fracture, extrusion, heat.

### Selection
- Round, tapered canal, good tug-back: single cone with bioceramic sealer.
- Oval, C-shaped, resorbed or curved canals: warm vertical compaction or injectable.
- No cone fits (deltas, large foramen under 0.7 mm): coneless technique with warm back-fill.
- Warm techniques require a heat-tolerant sealer such as BC HiFlow.

### Evidence
No technique is proven superior in clinical studies; bioceramic sealers lack human comparative trials.

### Conclusion
Choose by canal anatomy, sealer and skill: single cone for simple canals, warm compaction for complex ones.
