## STUDY MODE

### What "newer" means
Techniques introduced since the classical cold lateral and Schilder methods: engine- and heat-assisted warm techniques, thermoplastic injection, carrier-based systems, hydraulic bioceramic techniques, bonded systems, and apical-barrier approaches. Many have been in use for decades; the newest are the bioceramic hydraulic techniques.

### 1. Warm vertical compaction with continuous wave and back-fill
Buchanan's continuous wave (System B, Elements, Calamus Dual 3D, Touch 'n Heat): tapered pluggers matched to tapered cones, heat about 200 °C, apical pressure, then thermoplastic injection back-fill. Most commonly used among specialists because of denser, more homogeneous three-dimensional filling (Azim and Aksel, 2022; Gopikrishna, 2021). Calamus Pack/Flow: electric heat plugger plus cartridge delivery at about 38-44 °C (Sikri, 2019).

### 2. Warm lateral compaction with vibration
Endotec/Endotec II (heated spreader), DownPak/EndoTwinn (heat plus ultrasonic vibration; also usable with Resilon) (Sikri, 2019). Heat and vibration together give denser fills than heat alone (Sikri, 2019).

### 3. Injectable thermoplasticised gutta-percha
Obtura II/III Max (160-200 °C, 20-23 gauge needle), Ultrafil 3D (low-fusing gutta-percha at about 70-90 °C), PAC-160, HotShot (Berman et al, 2021; Sikri, 2019). Best adaptation to the prepared canal walls compared with Thermafil and cold lateral condensation in one study (Weller et al, in Rotstein and Ingle, 2019), but poor and unpredictable apical control.

### 4. Carrier-based systems
Thermafil, Densfil, Successfil, ProTaper/GT obturators; **GuttaCore**: cross-linked gutta-percha carrier, removable, ProTaper Next/Gold matched sizes (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). RealSeal One: polysulfone carrier with Resilon coating (Sikri, 2019).

### 5. Hydraulic / bioceramic techniques
- **Single cone with calcium silicate sealer:** sealers do not shrink and expand by absorbing tissue fluid; the technique is sealer-dependent, simple and less technique-sensitive; high reported success in treatment and retreatment (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
- **Coneless / BioConeless:** bioceramic sealer filling the canal, with warm gutta-percha back-fill; useful when no cone fits (apical deltas, abrupt curves, stripping); foramen cut-off about 0.7 mm; risks of under- or overfilling (Chopra, Bioceramics, Ch.12).
- **Synchronised hydraulic condensation:** bioceramic sealer with proprietary coated cone (Common Complications).
- **Heat-tolerant bioceramic sealer (BC HiFlow)** for warm techniques; BioRoot RCS not for warm techniques (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### 6. Bonded systems
Resilon/RealSeal (withdrawn 2014); resin-coated EndoREZ points; glass-ionomer-coated Activ GP; bioceramic-nanoparticle-coated cones (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Monoblock concept (Q8 of `endodontic_sealers/`).

### 7. Cold flowable and hydrophilic systems
GuttaFlow/GuttaFlow 2 (silicone matrix, microsilver, 0.2% expansion, working time 10-15 min; FAST version 4-5 min) (Sikri, 2019); EZ Fill (bi-directional spiral with epoxy sealer; room temperature, no cooling shrinkage) (Sikri, 2019); SmartSeal (Propoint swelling points) (Sikri, 2019).

### 8. Apical-barrier and apical-plug systems
MTA or bioceramic putty plug 3-6 mm; SimpliFill (5 mm apical gutta-percha plug on a carrier); FiberFill (fibre post with gutta-percha tip); dentin chips; custom cone (Sikri, 2019; Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### 9. Adjuncts
Ultrasonic activation of sealer improved distribution (Berman et al, 2021); thermomechanical NiTi condensers (MicroSeal) (Q9). Future: nanodiamond-embedded gutta-percha in trials (Berman et al, 2021).

### Comparison
| Technique | Strength | Weakness | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous wave + back-fill | Dense, 3-D | Skill, extrusion | Most used; outcome equal |
| Injectable | Resorptions, C-shaped | Apical control | Outcome equal |
| Carrier-based | Fast | Stripping; retreatment | Minimal gaps in some reports; equal healing |
| Single cone + bioceramic | Simple | Sealer-dependent; retreatability debated | High success reported; no human comparative trials in Cohen's 12e |
| Coneless | Fills irregular anatomy | Under- or overfilling | Case-based |
| Resilon | Bonded | Withdrawn 2014 | 56% vs 88% at 6 years |

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim 1 — newer techniques produce denser or simpler fills but not demonstrably better healing.** Cohen's: little evidence supports one method over another; Azim and Aksel: no significant difference among gutta-percha-and-sealer techniques; Endodontic Prognosis: same clinical outcome across older sealer classes. The only newer system followed long term, Resilon, did worse. **Claim 2 — hydraulic bioceramic techniques are rising on biological rationale, not trial evidence.** Calcium silicate sealers lack human clinical comparison in Cohen's 12e and Endodontic Prognosis, while Azim and Aksel report high success of single cone in clinical studies. INTERPRETATION: adopt a technique for anatomy and ease, and judge claims of superior sealing cautiously. LIMITATION: many figures come from in vitro testing and manufacturer data. [Likely]

### Introduction
Cold lateral compaction, the classical technique, does not fill canal irregularities well, and gutta-percha does not bond to dentin or sealer. "Newer" obturation techniques try to improve adaptation by heat, carriers, hydraulics or bonding, to simplify delivery, or to add bioactivity. They are described below with their principle, steps, merits, demerits and evidence, followed by a comparison and a selection guide.

### 1. Warm vertical compaction with continuous wave and back-fill
- *Principle:* a tapered cone and tapered pluggers (.06, .08, .10, .12) allow hydraulic force during warm compaction (Buchanan's modification of Schilder's technique).
- *Devices:* System B, Elements (System B plus extruder), Touch 'n Heat, Calamus Dual 3D (Pack heat plugger and Flow cartridge unit).
- *Steps:* fit a tapered master cone with sealer; heat the plugger to about 200 °C and advance it to 3-4 mm from the binding point; stop heat for 2-3 s; sear off the coronal cone; hold apical pressure for 5-10 s while the mass cools; a 1-second burst of heat releases the plugger; back-fill the remaining canal with injected thermoplasticised gutta-percha.
- *Merits:* dense, homogeneous, three-dimensional fill including lateral canals; most commonly used among specialists.
- *Demerits:* skill and equipment, needs a tapered preparation, risk of root fracture and extrusion, heat to the periodontium.

### 2. Warm lateral compaction with vibration
Endotec and Endotec II (battery-heated spreader up to 350 °C; the ZAP and TAP method for C-shaped canals), DownPak/EndoTwinn (heat plus ultrasonic vibration; usable with Resilon) and Thermapact. These give denser fills than cold lateral compaction, with more gutta-percha and less sealer, although the heated tips do not compact.

### 3. Injectable thermoplasticised gutta-percha
Obtura II and III Max heat pellets to about 160-200 °C and inject through a 20-23 gauge needle placed 3-6 mm from the apical preparation; Ultrafil 3D uses low-fusing gutta-percha at about 70-90 °C; PAC-160 holds 160 °C; HotShot and Elements are other units. The mass is compacted as it cools. It fills internal resorptions, C-shaped canals, webbings and lateral canals, and adapted better to canal walls than Thermafil or cold lateral compaction in one study. The drawbacks are poor and unpredictable apical control, extrusion, voids if pressure is not applied, and heat.

### 4. Carrier-based systems
Thermafil, Densfil, Successfil and the ProTaper or GT obturators carry alpha-phase gutta-percha on a plastic (formerly metal) carrier heated in an oven. **GuttaCore** replaces the carrier with cross-linked gutta-percha that bonds to the surrounding gutta-percha, removing the carrier-gap and easing post space and retreatment, but with limited studies. RealSeal One used a polysulfone carrier coated with Resilon. *Merits:* quick, flexible, fills fins and resorptive defects, low condensation force. *Demerits:* gutta-percha may strip off the carrier, extrusion, post-space and retreatment difficulty.

### 5. Hydraulic bioceramic techniques
- **Single cone with calcium silicate sealer:** sealers do not shrink and expand slightly with tissue fluid; the cone acts as a piston in a canal of constant taper. The technique is simple and less technique-sensitive with a high success rate reported in treatment and retreatment, but it is "sealer-dependent" and poor when the cone does not fit or the canal is oval.
- **Coneless and BioConeless:** bioceramic sealer is placed in the canal and warm gutta-percha is injected as a back-fill; useful when no cone fits, in apical deltas, abrupt curves and after stripping; the foramen should be under about 0.7 mm; under- and overfilling are the main concerns, and the canal can be retreated before the sealer sets.
- **Synchronised hydraulic condensation:** a bioceramic sealer with its own coated cones.
- **Heat-tolerant BC HiFlow:** for warm techniques. BioRoot RCS has high viscosity and should not be used with warm techniques.

### 6. Bonded systems and the monoblock
Resilon with Epiphany (polycaprolactone core, self-etch primer, dual-cure sealer) aimed to bond dentin, sealer and core. It was withdrawn in 2014: polycaprolactone degrades, the C-factor in a canal is about 1,000 against 5 in a Class I cavity, and one study found 56% success at six years against 88% for gutta-percha. Coated cones continue the idea: EndoREZ resin-coated points, glass-ionomer-coated Activ GP, and bioceramic-nanoparticle-coated cones.

### 7. Cold flowable and expanding systems
GuttaFlow (gutta-percha powder in polydimethylsiloxane, working time 10-15 min, set 25-30 min, expansion about 0.2% in GuttaFlow 2 with microsilver); EZ Fill (bi-directional spiral with epoxy sealer, room temperature, so no cooling shrinkage); SmartSeal (Propoint hydrophilic point that swells laterally within four hours, with Smartpaste or Smartpaste Bio).

### 8. Apical-barrier and apical-plug systems
MTA or bioceramic putty plug of 3-6 mm for open apices, resorbed foramina and apical transportation; SimpliFill (5 mm apical gutta-percha plug on a removable carrier); FiberFill (fibre post with gutta-percha tip and dual-cure sealer); dentin chips; custom cone.

### 9. Adjuncts and future technology
Ultrasonic activation gave better sealer distribution when used circumferentially; NiTi thermomechanical condensers (MicroSeal); nanodiamond-embedded gutta-percha is in clinical trials with no adverse events reported.

### Critical appraisal
- **Bench versus clinic:** most advantages are laboratory findings (adaptation, voids, lateral canal filling). Outcome studies show no significant differences.
- **Risk transfer:** each gain brings a new risk: warm techniques add heat and fracture risk; injection adds extrusion; carriers add stripping; hydraulic sealers add dependence on the sealer and a retreatment question.
- **Extrusion is not always harmful:** sealer extrusion in about 50% of cases did not change outcome; poor results in overfilled teeth without apical periodontitis were not linked to overfilling but to coexisting infection.
- **Industry claims:** manufacturers' data on set time, expansion and temperature are not independent; textbooks give different set times for the same class of sealer (165 min to 24 h).

### Comparison
| Technique | Strength | Weakness | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous wave + back-fill | Dense, three-dimensional | Skill; fracture; extrusion | Most used; outcome equal |
| Injectable | Complex anatomy | Apical control | Outcome equal |
| Carrier-based | Fast | Stripping; retreatment | Minimal gaps in some reports; equal healing |
| Single cone + bioceramic | Simple | Sealer-dependent | High success reported; no comparative human trials in Cohen's 12e |
| Coneless | Irregular anatomy | Under- or overfilling | Case-based |
| Resilon | Bonded | Failed | 56% vs 88% at 6 years |

### Selection
Curved, oval, C-shaped or resorbed canals favour warm techniques or coneless filling; round, well-tapered canals suit a single cone with bioceramic sealer; open apices need an MTA plug; where easy retreatment matters, gutta-percha with epoxy or ZOE sealer is preferred.

### Evidence
Cohen's states that little evidence supports one method over another; the Toronto studies suggest warm vertical compaction may be better than lateral compaction but not definitively. Clinical studies show no significant outcome difference among techniques using gutta-percha and sealer. Bioceramic sealers lack human comparative trials; Resilon, the one bonded system followed long term, did worse.

### Conclusion
Newer techniques give denser or simpler fills in complex anatomy but have not yet improved healing. The technique should be chosen for the anatomy and the clinician's skill, with disinfection, length control and an early coronal seal given priority.
