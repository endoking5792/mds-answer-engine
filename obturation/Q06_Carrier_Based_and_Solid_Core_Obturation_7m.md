## STUDY MODE

### Principle
A central carrier coated with alpha-phase gutta-percha is heated and delivered to the canal, so the carrier gives a rigid vehicle for plasticised gutta-percha (Berman et al, 2021).

### History
Developed by W.B. (Ben) Johnson in 1978 as Thermafil; the first carriers were stainless steel files, replaced by grooved plastic carriers to ease retreatment, post-space preparation and root-end surgery (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Gopikrishna, 2021). Later: GT, GTX and ProTaper obturators matched to rotary files; Densfil (plastic or titanium carrier, DensHeat oven); Successfil (titanium or plastic carrier, syringe-delivered gutta-percha); GuttaCore (cross-linked gutta-percha carrier) (Sikri, 2019; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).

### Technique (Thermafil)
1. Size selection with a verifier (plastic carrier that stops about 1 mm short of working length); not possible with GuttaCore (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).
2. Dry the canal and apply a thin coat of sealer; close other canal orifices with damp cotton so gutta-percha does not block them (Sikri, 2019).
3. Set the stopper at working length and heat in the oven. **Heating time differs:** about 10 s in the ThermaPrep Plus oven (Gopikrishna, 2021; "a few seconds" in Rotstein and Ingle, 2019) vs 115 °C for 3-7 min depending on size (Sikri, 2019). Use when the coating looks shiny, expanded and sticky.
4. Insert to working length with firm, uniform apical pressure without rotating; verify radiographically; allow 2-4 min to cool, then sever the carrier at the orifice with a bur while holding firm pressure (Gopikrishna, 2021; Sikri, 2019).
5. Thermafil gutta-percha re-solidifies about 90 s after heating (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).

### Advantages
Quick and easy; flexible carrier can follow curvature; fills fins, lateral canals and resorptive defects; useful in open apices; lower condensation forces than lateral or vertical compaction (Sikri, 2019; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). Lateral-canal filling equal to vertical compaction and System B and better than lateral condensation (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).

### Disadvantages
Gutta-percha may strip from the carrier in constricted canals and the radiopaque carrier hides this, compromising the apical seal; extrusion; post space difficult; carrier removal in retreatment (metal worse than plastic) (Azim and Aksel, 2022; Berman et al, 2021). GuttaCore avoids the carrier-gap and eases removal but has limited studies (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### Evidence
Carrier-based systems produce minimal gaps and voids in some reports, and healing rates equal to lateral compaction in others (Berman et al, 2021).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 320; about 15 min at 22 words/min)**

### Definition
Carrier-based obturation uses a central carrier coated with alpha-phase gutta-percha. The coated carrier is heated and delivered to working length, so the rigid carrier is the vehicle for the plasticised gutta-percha.

### History and systems
Developed by W.B. (Ben) Johnson in 1978 as **Thermafil**. The first obturators had stainless steel file carriers, which complicated retreatment, post space and root-end surgery; grooved plastic carriers replaced them. Later systems: **Successfil** (titanium or plastic carrier, syringe-loaded gutta-percha that sets in about 2 min), **Densfil** (plastic or titanium carrier, DensHeat oven), GT, GTX, ProTaper and WaveOne obturators matched to rotary files, and **GuttaCore** (cross-linked gutta-percha carrier).

### Technique (Thermafil)
1. Select the obturator with a size verifier (the plastic carrier that stops about 1 mm short of working length); this is not possible with GuttaCore.
2. Dry the canal and apply a thin coat of sealer; close the other canal orifices with damp cotton so gutta-percha does not block them.
3. Set the stopper to working length and heat in the oven until the gutta-percha is shiny, expanded and sticky (about 10 s in Grossman's; 115 °C for 3-7 min in Sikri).
4. Insert to working length with firm, uniform apical pressure, **without rotation**; confirm radiographically.
5. Let it cool for 2-4 min, then sever the carrier at the canal orifice with a bur while holding the handle firmly.

### Advantages
Quick and easy; flexible carriers follow curvature; fills fins, lateral canals and resorptive defects; useful in open apices; lower condensation forces on the root than lateral or vertical compaction; lateral-canal filling equal to vertical compaction and System B and better than lateral condensation.

### Disadvantages
Gutta-percha may strip from the carrier in constricted canals and the radiopaque carrier hides this, weakening the apical seal; extrusion; post space is difficult; metal carriers are hard to retrieve.

### Conclusion
A fast technique with outcomes similar to lateral compaction; plastic and cross-linked carriers have reduced its main drawbacks.
