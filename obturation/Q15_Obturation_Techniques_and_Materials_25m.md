## STUDY MODE

### Definition and aims
Obturation is the three-dimensional filling of the entire root canal system as close to the cementodentinal junction as possible, with minimal biocompatible sealer and a core material (Sikri, 2019). It prevents reinfection, entombs residual bacteria, stops periapical fluid seeping into the canal and provides a barrier between mouth and periapex (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### When to obturate
Asymptomatic tooth, dry canal, no sinus tract or foul odour, intact temporary filling; a negative culture preferably (Sikri, 2019). Vital teeth can usually be completed in one visit; necrotic or retreatment cases with apical periodontitis carry high bacterial loads and may need more visits (Azim and Aksel, 2022). If the canal cannot be dried, use an intracanal medicament for at least one week (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### Extent
Obturate to the dentinocemental junction, about 0.5 mm (0.2-2.5 mm) from the radiographic apex; 0-2 mm from the apex gives the best outcome; success falls 12-14% per millimetre lost (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Overextension means material beyond the apex with an inadequately filled canal; overfilling means a well-filled canal with surplus material beyond the apex (Sikri, 2019; Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### Materials
- **Core:** gutta-percha (standard); silver cones (obsolete); Resilon (withdrawn 2014); coated cones; GuttaCore; pastes (N2 banned); MTA/bioceramic for apical plugs (Q1, Q2).
- **Sealers** (Grossman's criteria): zinc oxide-eugenol, calcium hydroxide, epoxy resin, methacrylate resin, glass ionomer, silicone, calcium silicate (bioceramic); medicated paraformaldehyde sealers are contraindicated (see `endodontic_sealers/`).

### Pre-obturation steps (Azim and Aksel, 2022; Sikri, 2019)
1. Select the master cone matching the master apical file, with tug-back, radiographic verification and length 0.5-2 mm from the radiographic apex.
2. Dry with paper points (calcium silicate sealers need moisture; alcohol dehydration may delay their set).
3. Disinfect the cone: 5.25% NaOCl, 1 min (Q3).
4. Place sealer with the cone, a file or a lentulo/injection tip.

### Techniques
| Technique | Principle | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold lateral compaction** | Master cone and spreader to 1-2 mm of working length; accessory cones | Length control, simple, wide sealer choice | Fills irregularities less well; voids; spreader force can fracture roots |
| **Single cone** | Cone matched to final file plus non-shrinking sealer | Simple, less sensitive; resurgent with calcium silicate sealers | "Sealer-dependent" |
| **Warm vertical / continuous wave** | Heat plugger about 200 °C down-pack, then back-fill | Denser, homogeneous; most used by specialists | Skill, fracture risk, extrusion |
| **Injectable** (Obtura, Calamus, Ultrafil) | Heated gutta-percha injected, compacted | Adapts to resorptions, C-shaped canals | Extrusion; poor apical control |
| **Carrier-based** (Thermafil, GuttaCore) | Gutta-percha on heated carrier | Fast, easy | Stripping, post space, retreatment |
| **Thermomechanical** (McSpadden) | Friction heat | Simple | Instrument fracture, not for curved canals |
| **Solvent / custom cone** | Chloroform-softened cone | Open apex | Shrinkage |
| **Apical plug** (MTA) | MTA 3-6 mm then back-fill | Open apex, resorption | Technique-sensitive; discolouration |

### Recent advances
Calcium silicate sealers and hydraulic techniques (single cone, coneless/BioConeless); coated cones (resin, glass ionomer, bioceramic nanoparticle); GuttaCore, GuttaFlow, SmartSeal; heat-tolerant bioceramic sealer (BC HiFlow); nanodiamond gutta-percha (trial stage); Resilon abandoned (Azim and Aksel, 2022; Berman et al, 2021; Sikri, 2019).

### Monoblock
Core, sealer and dentin as one bonded unit (Tay): primary, secondary and tertiary monoblocks; no material currently achieves a reinforcing monoblock (see `endodontic_sealers/` Q8).

### Evaluation
Quality is judged by length and density with no voids; radiographic appearance does not correlate well with leakage (Berman et al, 2021).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim — no obturation technique or material has been shown to give better outcomes.** Cohen's: "little evidence exists" supporting one method over another; Toronto data hint at warm vertical compaction, but not definitive (Berman et al, 2021). Azim and Aksel: clinical studies show no significant difference among gutta-percha-and-sealer techniques. Endodontic Prognosis: ZOE, epoxy and other classes give the same clinical outcome. INTERPRETATION: technique choice should follow canal anatomy and operator skill; length control, density and coronal restoration matter more. LIMITATION: studies differ in design; new materials lack long-term clinical data. [Likely]

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 712; about 32 min at 22 words/min)**

### Introduction
Obturation is the three-dimensional filling of the cleaned and shaped root canal system to the dentinocemental junction, using a core material and a minimal amount of sealer. Its aims are to prevent reinfection, entomb residual bacteria, stop periapical fluid seeping into the canal, form a barrier between the mouth and periapex, and give a favourable biological environment for healing. Success also needs good cleaning and shaping and a fluid-tight coronal restoration.

### Timing and extent
Obturate when the tooth is asymptomatic and the canal is dry, with no sinus tract or foul odour and an intact temporary filling. Fill to the dentinocemental junction, about 0.5 mm from the radiographic apex; 0-2 mm from the radiographic apex gives the best outcome and success falls by 12-14% for each millimetre of working length lost. Overextension is material beyond the apex with an inadequately filled canal; overfilling is a well-filled canal with surplus beyond the apex.

### Ideal requirements (Grossman)
Easily manipulated with adequate working time; dimensionally stable; seals laterally and apically; non-irritating; moisture-impervious; unaffected by tissue fluids; bacteriostatic; radiopaque; non-staining; sterile; easily removed.

### Materials
**Core:** gutta-percha (trans-polyisoprene, zinc oxide 59-76%, beta phase at room temperature, alpha when heated; compactable, inert, radiopaque, removable; no adhesion). Silver cones are obsolete (corrosion, poor seal). Resilon was withdrawn in 2014. Coated cones, GuttaCore, GuttaFlow and SmartSeal are newer. N2 paste is banned.
**Sealers:** zinc oxide-eugenol, calcium hydroxide, epoxy resin (AH Plus), methacrylate resin, glass ionomer, silicone and calcium silicate (bioceramic). Paraformaldehyde sealers are contraindicated.

### Pre-obturation steps
1. Select the master cone to match the master apical file; confirm tug-back and length radiographically.
2. Dry the canal with paper points (bioceramic sealers need moisture).
3. Disinfect the cone in 5.25% sodium hypochlorite for one minute.
4. Mix and place sealer with the cone, file or injection tip.

### Techniques
**1. Cold lateral compaction.** Master cone and sealer to length; a finger spreader to 1-2 mm short of the working length for about 20 s; accessory cones placed in the space; repeat until filled; sear and compact at the orifice. Merits: simple, good length control, any sealer. Demerits: irregularities poorly filled, voids, root fracture from spreader force.
**2. Single cone.** Cone matched to the final file with a non-shrinking sealer; simple, but sealer-dependent and poor in oval canals.
**3. Warm vertical compaction / continuous wave.** Heat plugger at about 200 °C advanced to 3-4 mm from the binding point; apical pressure 5-10 s; back-fill with injected gutta-percha. Denser and more homogeneous; most used by specialists; risk of root fracture, extrusion and heat.
**4. Warm lateral compaction.** Heated spreader (Endotec, DownPak): more gutta-percha, less sealer.
**5. Injectable thermoplasticised gutta-percha.** Obtura (160-200 °C), Ultrafil (70-90 °C), Calamus; needle 3-6 mm from the apical preparation. Fills resorptions and C-shaped canals; extrusion risk.
**6. Carrier-based.** Thermafil, GuttaCore: oven-heated carrier inserted without rotation, severed at the orifice. Quick, flexible; gutta-percha stripping, post-space and retreatment difficulty.
**7. Thermomechanical compaction.** McSpadden compactor at 8,000-20,000 rpm; frictional heat; instrument fracture and extrusion; not for curved canals.
**8. Chemically softened and custom cone.** Chloroform-dipped tip; shrinkage on evaporation; custom cones for open apices.
**9. Apical plug.** MTA 3-6 mm for open apices.

### Recent advances
Calcium silicate sealers with single cone, coneless and BioConeless techniques; synchronised hydraulic condensation; heat-tolerant BC HiFlow; coated cones (resin, glass ionomer, bioceramic nanoparticles); GuttaCore; GuttaFlow; SmartSeal; nanodiamond gutta-percha (trials). Resilon was abandoned.

### Monoblock
Core, sealer and dentin acting as one bonded unit (Tay): primary, secondary and tertiary monoblocks by number of interfaces. Polymerisation shrinkage and the low modulus of filling materials (dentin about 14,000 MPa) mean a reinforcing monoblock has not been achieved.

### Complications
Overfilling or overextension, voids, vertical root fracture, thermal injury (root surface rise above 10 °C is believed harmful), carrier stripping, and paraformaldehyde toxicity.

### Evaluation and coronal seal
Judge by length and density; radiographs do not correlate well with leakage. Restore promptly; 3.5 mm Cavit or Super EBA reduced bacterial leakage.

### Evidence
Little evidence supports one method over another; Toronto data hint at warm vertical compaction. Clinical studies show no significant difference between techniques.

### Conclusion
Gutta-percha with sealer remains the standard. Choose cold lateral compaction or single cone for simple canals and warm techniques for curved, oval, C-shaped or resorbed canals. Disinfection, length control and an early coronal seal matter more than the technique.
