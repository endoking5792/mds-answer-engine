## STUDY MODE

### Classification of obturation techniques
Sikri's classification of gutta-percha techniques (Sikri, 2019):
1. **Cold:** lateral compaction and modifications (flexible spreaders for curved canals; inverted-cone and roll-cone for open apices); single-cone.
2. **Chemically softened:** chloroform, eucalyptol or halothane dip (Callahan-Johnston; chloropercha, eucapercha).
3. **Heat-softened:**
   - *Intraradicular* (heated in the canal): warm vertical (Schilder; Touch 'n Heat; System B; Elements); warm lateral (Endotec, DownPak/EndoTwinn, Thermapact); thermocompaction (McSpadden and others); ultrasonic plasticising.
   - *Extraradicular* (heated outside, delivered in): injectable (Obtura II/III, Calamus, Ultrafil, PAC-160); solid-core carriers (Thermafil, Successfil, Densfil, Trifecta, GuttaCore).
Other books list the main techniques as cold lateral compaction, warm vertical compaction (with its continuous-wave modification), carrier-based, single cone (and custom cone, apical plug) (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### What "thermoplasticised" means
Beta-phase gutta-percha is heated to the alpha phase, becomes tacky and flowable and can adapt to canal irregularities; it shrinks as it cools, so compaction must continue during cooling (Berman et al, 2021; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). Temperatures are stated differently by source (see Q2).

### 1. Warm vertical compaction and continuous wave (intraradicular)
- **Schilder (1967):** three pluggers of graded size; repeated heat waves and apical compaction in 3-4 mm increments; apical 4-5 mm sealed; back-packing with 5 mm segments (Sikri, 2019). Dense, homogeneous fill, but difficult, time consuming, needs more taper, risk of vertical root fracture and extrusion, less length control than lateral compaction (Sikri, 2019).
- **Continuous wave (Buchanan):** tapered NiTi shaping, tapered pluggers (.06, .08, .10, .12) matched to tapered cones; heat plugger about 200 °C advanced to 3-4 mm from the binding point, heat off 2-3 s, searing of the coronal cone, apical pressure 5-10 s, followed by thermoplastic back-fill (Gopikrishna, 2021; Azim and Aksel, 2022).
- **System B:** digital temperature display; tip heated to 200 °C, heat wave 250-300 °C (Sikri, 2019). **Touch 'n Heat:** heat carrier; keep heat 4-5 mm from the terminus (Sikri, 2019). **Elements:** System B plus extruder in one unit (Sikri, 2019).
- Warm vertical compaction with its modifications is the most commonly used technique among specialists because it gives a denser, more homogeneous fill (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

### 2. Warm lateral compaction
Endotec (heated spreader up to 350 °C, ZAP and TAP method for C-shaped canals), DownPak/EndoTwinn (heat plus ultrasonic vibration), Thermapact (42 °C for warm lateral, about 59 °C for warm vertical per Sikri, 2019). More gutta-percha and less sealer than cold lateral compaction; the tips heat but do not compact (Sikri, 2019).

### 3. Injectable (extraradicular) thermoplasticised gutta-percha
- **Obtura II/III:** unit adjustable about 160-200 °C (Sikri, 2019); Cohen's 160 °C is the commonly cited default setting within that range, not a conflicting figure (Berman et al, 2021). The extruded gutta-percha itself is cooler at the needle tip, about 62-65 °C (Sikri, 2019). 20 or 23 gauge needle placed 3-5 mm from the apical preparation (Sikri, 2019); Ingle's advises 4-6 mm from the end of the preparation; passive injection avoiding apical pressure, compaction during cooling (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).
- **Ultrafil (3D):** low-temperature alpha-phase gutta-percha in cannulas, heated about 70-90 °C (Sikri, 2019; 90 °C in Berman et al, 2021); three viscosities (regular, firm set, endoset); working time about 60-70 s; cannot be compacted well and may shrink (Sikri, 2019).
- **Calamus Dual 3D:** Pack handpiece (heat plugger for down-pack) and Flow handpiece (cartridge and bendable cannula for back-fill); gutta-percha dispensed at about 38-44 °C and flows for 45-60 s (Sikri, 2019).
- **PAC-160:** temperature maintained at 160 °C (Sikri, 2019). Also HotShot, Elements (Berman et al, 2021).

### 4. Carrier-based and thermomechanical
See Q6 and Q9.

### Indications
Curved or irregular canals, internal resorption, C-shaped canals, webbings, lateral and accessory canals, back-filling, unnegotiable canals filled coronally before root-end surgery (Sikri, 2019; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Berman et al, 2021).

### Limitations and risks
- **Apical control:** extrusion of gutta-percha and sealer; injected material lacks precise control (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). Heat can damage the periodontium (Sikri, 2019).
- **Voids:** unless vertical pressure follows injection, the interface seal weakens and voids form (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Heat to the root surface:** System B and Touch 'n Heat raised external temperature less than 10 °C in maxillary incisors and premolars; Touch 'n Heat exceeded 10 °C in mandibular incisors; flame-heated carriers exceeded 10 °C in all teeth; the critical level for irreversible bone damage is believed to exceed 10 °C (Berman et al, 2021).
- **Root fracture:** compaction force, especially vertical (Sikri, 2019).
- **Shrinkage** on cooling.
- **Cost and learning curve.**

### Evidence
Little evidence supports any method over another on outcome (Berman et al, 2021); the Toronto studies suggested warm vertical compaction may be superior to lateral compaction but definitive evidence is lacking (Berman et al, 2021). Clinical studies show no significant difference among gutta-percha-and-sealer techniques (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Thermoplastic injection adapts better than cold lateral compaction in vitro (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).

---

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim — thermoplasticised gutta-percha fills the canal more densely than cold lateral compaction, but this has not been shown to improve clinical outcome.** In vitro adaptation and filling of lateral canals favour warm techniques (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019); the Toronto cohort hinted at better outcomes with warm vertical compaction (Berman et al, 2021), yet Azim and Aksel (2022) and Berman et al (2021) conclude outcome studies show no significant difference. INTERPRETATION: choose by anatomy (curved, oval, resorptive canals favour warm techniques) and operator skill; extrusion control matters more than the technique's name. LIMITATION: most evidence is bench-top; heating figures differ between sources. [Likely]

### Introduction
Obturation techniques are classified by how gutta-percha is softened: not at all (cold), by solvent, or by heat. Thermoplasticised techniques heat beta-phase gutta-percha into the flowable alpha phase so it adapts to canal irregularities, and then compact it while it cools to offset shrinkage.

### Classification of obturation techniques
1. **Cold gutta-percha:** lateral compaction and modifications (flexible spreaders for curved canals; inverted-cone and roll-cone for open apices).
2. **Chemically softened:** chloroform, eucalyptol or halothane (chloropercha, eucapercha).
3. **Heat softened**
 - *Intraradicular* (heated in the canal): warm vertical compaction (Schilder; Touch 'n Heat; System B; Elements); warm lateral compaction (Endotec, DownPak, Thermapact); thermocompaction (McSpadden); ultrasonic plasticising.
 - *Extraradicular* (heated outside the canal): injectable (Obtura, Calamus, Ultrafil, PAC-160); solid-core carriers (Thermafil, Successfil, Densfil, Trifecta, GuttaCore).

### Principle of thermoplasticisation
Heating converts beta gutta-percha to the tacky, flowable alpha phase (transition temperatures differ between books, about 42-49 °C onwards). The mass expands when heated and shrinks as it cools, so heat must always be followed by compaction under pressure, and a sealer is needed for the interface.

### Warm vertical compaction and continuous wave
Schilder's technique used three pluggers and repeated heat waves, packing in 3-4 mm increments until the apical 4-5 mm is sealed, and back-packing with 5 mm segments. Buchanan's continuous wave simplified it: a tapered cone and tapered pluggers, a System B plugger at about 200 °C advanced to 3-4 mm from the binding point, apical pressure held for 5-10 s, and back-fill with injected gutta-percha. It gives a dense, homogeneous fill and is the most used technique among specialists, but it is difficult and time consuming, needs a tapered preparation, and carries the risk of vertical root fracture, extrusion and heat.

### Injectable gutta-percha
Obtura II and III are adjustable about 160-200 °C (160 °C a common setting) and inject through a 20 or 23 gauge needle placed 3-6 mm from the apical preparation; injection is passive and the mass is compacted as it cools. Ultrafil uses low-fusing gutta-percha at about 70-90 °C in three viscosities; Calamus Dual 3D has a Pack handpiece for down-packing and a Flow handpiece for back-fill.

### Warm lateral compaction
Endotec (heated spreader up to 350 °C, ZAP and TAP method for C-shaped canals), DownPak/EndoTwinn (heat plus ultrasonic vibration) and Thermapact give more gutta-percha and less sealer than cold lateral compaction.

### Carrier-based systems
Alpha-phase gutta-percha on a carrier is heated in an oven and inserted with firm pressure without rotation, then the carrier is severed at the orifice. They are quick and flexible, but gutta-percha can strip from the carrier, and post space and retreatment are harder.

### Thermomechanical compaction
Friction from a rotating compactor (McSpadden, 8,000-20,000 rpm; NiTi MicroSeal at 1,000-4,000 rpm) softens gutta-percha and drives it apically and laterally.

### Technique comparison
| Technique | Heat source | Apical control | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuous wave | Plugger about 200 °C | Good (3-4 mm from binding point) | Root fracture, heat |
| Injectable | Gun 160-200 °C (Ultrafil 70-90 °C) | Poor and unpredictable | Extrusion, voids |
| Carrier-based | Oven | Good length control by the carrier | Stripping, post space |
| Warm lateral | Heated spreader | Good | Tips do not compact |
| Thermomechanical | Friction | Poor | Fracture, heat |

### Indications
Curved or irregular canals, internal resorption, C-shaped canals, webbings, lateral and accessory canals, back-filling, and unnegotiable canals filled coronally before surgery.

### Disadvantages and precautions
- Extrusion and poor apical control with injection techniques; use a needle 3-6 mm short and an apical stop.
- Heat injury: external root temperature should not rise more than about 10 °C; keep heated pluggers 4-5 mm from the apex and avoid flame-heated carriers.
- Cooling shrinkage and voids unless compacted.
- Vertical root fracture from compaction force.
- Cost, equipment and learning curve.

### Evidence
Cohen's: little evidence supports one method over another, and the Toronto studies suggest warm vertical compaction may be better than lateral compaction but not definitively. Azim and Aksel: no significant outcome difference among gutta-percha-and-sealer techniques. In vitro, injectable and carrier techniques adapt better to canal walls than cold lateral compaction.

### Conclusion
Thermoplasticised techniques give a denser fill in complex anatomy but depend on apical control, heat limits and compaction during cooling; clinical outcomes are similar to cold lateral compaction.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Beta to alpha with heat; shrinks on cooling, so compact while cooling.
- System B 200 °C; Obtura 160-200 °C; Ultrafil 70-90 °C.
- Down-pack then back-fill (continuous wave).
- Indications: curved, resorptive, C-shaped canals.
- Root-surface heat threshold believed to be above 10 °C.
