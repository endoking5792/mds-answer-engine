## STUDY MODE
**Definition.** Obturation is "the three-dimensional filling of the entire root canal system as close to the cementodentinal junction as possible", with minimal biocompatible sealer used with a core material to establish an adequate seal (Sikri, 2019).

**Objectives**
1. Prevent recontamination of the canal by microorganisms left after preparation or entering from the oral cavity or lateral communications (Sikri, 2019).
2. Seal or "entomb" remaining bacteria in the canal (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
3. Stop periradicular fluids and exudate seeping into the canal and feeding residual bacteria (Azim and Aksel, 2022; Sikri, 2019).
4. Create a barrier between the oral cavity and the periapical tissues (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
5. Create a favourable biological environment for healing (Sikri, 2019).
6. Fill the space with a dense, void-free filling from orifice to the dentinocemental junction to seal portals of entry and exit (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

Success depends on cleaning and shaping, three-dimensional sealing and a fluid-tight coronal restoration (Sikri, 2019). Materials and techniques do not give an impervious seal; "all materials leak to some extent" (Berman et al, 2021).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 168; about 8 min at 22 words/min)**

### Definition
Root canal obturation is the three-dimensional filling of the entire root canal system as close to the cementodentinal junction as possible, using a core material and a minimal amount of biocompatible sealer to establish an adequate seal.

### Objectives
1. **Prevent recontamination** of the canal by microorganisms left after preparation, or entering from the oral cavity or lateral communications.
2. **Entomb** the bacteria that remain in the canal and dentinal tubules.
3. **Stop fluid seepage:** prevent periradicular tissue fluid and exudate entering the canal and feeding residual bacteria.
4. **Provide a barrier** between the oral cavity and the periapical tissues.
5. **Create a favourable biological environment** for periapical healing.
6. **Achieve a dense, void-free filling** from the orifice to the dentinocemental junction (0-2 mm from the radiographic apex), sealing all portals of entry and exit.

### Related points
Success of treatment depends on cleaning and shaping, a three-dimensional seal and a fluid-tight coronal restoration. All materials and techniques leak to some extent, so obturation reduces rather than abolishes leakage.
