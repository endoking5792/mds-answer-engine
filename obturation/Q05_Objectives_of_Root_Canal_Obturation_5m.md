## STUDY MODE
**Definition.** Obturation is "the three-dimensional filling of the entire root canal system as close to the cementodentinal junction as possible", with minimal biocompatible sealer used with a core material to establish an adequate seal (Sikri, 2019).

**Objectives**
1. Prevent recontamination of the canal by microorganisms left after preparation or entering from the oral cavity or lateral communications (Sikri, 2019).
2. Seal or "entomb" remaining bacteria in the canal (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
3. Stop periradicular fluids and exudate seeping into the canal and feeding residual bacteria (Azim and Aksel, 2022; Sikri, 2019).
4. Create a barrier between the oral cavity and the periapical tissues (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
5. Create a favourable biological environment for healing (Sikri, 2019).
6. Fill the space with a dense, void-free filling from orifice to the dentinocemental junction to seal portals of entry and exit (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

Success depends on cleaning and shaping, three-dimensional sealing and a fluid-tight coronal restoration (Sikri, 2019). Materials and techniques do not give an impervious seal; "all materials leak to some extent" (Berman et al, 2021).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 78; about 4 min at 22 words/min)**

Obturation is the three-dimensional filling of the cleaned and shaped canal as close to the cementodentinal junction as possible. Objectives:
- prevent reinfection from the mouth or from residual bacteria;
- entomb residual bacteria and remove their nutrient supply by preventing periapical fluid seepage;
- provide a barrier between oral cavity and periapex;
- create a favourable environment for periapical healing;
- achieve a dense, void-free filling to the dentinocemental junction (0-2 mm from the radiographic apex).
