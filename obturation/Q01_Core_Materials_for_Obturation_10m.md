## STUDY MODE

### Definition
Core materials occupy the major volume of the prepared canal and are used together with a sealer (Azim and Aksel, 2022). "None of the materials satisfied the requirements of an ideal root canal filling material" (Sikri, 2019). Grossman's remark, quoted in Ingle's: few body cavities have been plugged with as many different materials as the root canal (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).

### Requirements of an ideal obturating material
- **Grossman (Cohen's Box 9.2, eleven):** easily manipulated with ample working time; dimensionally stable; seals laterally and apically; non-irritating; impervious to moisture and non-porous; unaffected by tissue fluids (no corrosion); inhibits bacterial growth; radiopaque; no discolouration; sterile; easily removed if necessary (Berman et al, 2021).
- **Sikri (ten):** easily introduced; seals laterally and apically; dimensionally stable; impervious to moisture; bacteriostatic; radiopaque; non-staining; non-irritating; easily and quickly sterilised; easily removed (Sikri, 2019).
- **West's seventeen** (Ingle's) also include: non-resorbable, semisolid on insertion and solid afterwards, non-conductor of thermal change, slightly expandable, sticks to canal walls (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).

### Classification
Ingle's: **pastes and cements; solid materials; semi-solid materials** (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). Sikri (Flowchart 20.1): (1) gutta-percha (cold, chemically softened, heat-softened intra- or extraradicular); (2) silver cones; (3) miscellaneous: GuttaFlow, GuttaCore, Resilon/RealSeal, EZ Fill, SmartSeal, coated cones; (4) apical-third obturation: warm gutta-percha, dentin chips, MTA, carrier-based apical plugs; (5) pastes (N2, MTA, calcium phosphate cement, CEM cement, Biodentine); (6) retrograde filling materials (Sikri, 2019).

### The materials
| Material | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Gutta-percha** (semi-solid) | Material of choice for most cases; plastic, inert, radiopaque, removable; no adhesion, so a sealer is always required (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Berman et al, 2021). See Q2. |
| **Silver cones** (solid) | Introduced by Jasper, 1933. Rigid, easy length control, but do not fill irregular or elliptical canals, corrode on contact with tissue fluid to cytotoxic by-products, and are hard to remove. "Obsolete... no indication" (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019); "below the standard of care" (Berman et al, 2021). Silver's oligodynamic effect was believed but not true (Berman et al, 2021). |
| **Resilon** | Polycaprolactone-based resin with bioactive glass; withdrawn 2014 for poor long-term stability and success (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Q7 of `endodontic_sealers/`. |
| **Coated cones** | Gutta-percha coated with resin (EndoREZ points), glass ionomer (Activ GP) or bioceramic nanoparticles, to bond to the sealer (Azim and Aksel, 2022). |
| **GuttaCore** | Carrier-based; cross-linked gutta-percha core with alpha gutta-percha surface; removable (Sikri, 2019; Azim and Aksel, 2022). Limited studies. |
| **GuttaFlow** | Cold flowable gutta-percha powder (under 30 μm) in polydimethylsiloxane matrix; working time 10-15 min (Sikri, 2019). |
| **SmartSeal (Propoint, Smartpaste)** | Hydrophilic nylon-polymer point swells laterally in the first 4 h; limited documentation (Sikri, 2019). |
| **Pastes** | Adapt to anatomy but extrude, porous, hard to remove; paraformaldehyde pastes (N2) severely toxic (Berman et al, 2021; Sikri, 2019). |
| **MTA / bioceramics** | Used as apical plug or whole-canal fill for open apices, resorption, retreatment; MTA can discolour (bismuth oxide) (Azim and Aksel, 2022; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). |
| **Dentin chips** | Biologic apical plug, 1-2 mm; infected chips may impede repair (Sikri, 2019). |

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 398; about 18 min at 22 words/min)**

### Introduction
Root canal obturation uses a **core material**, which occupies the major volume of the prepared canal, together with a **sealer**, which fills the space between core and wall. Many substances have been tried and none meets every requirement of an ideal filling.

### Requirements of an ideal obturating material
Grossman's list (Cohen's Box 9.2): easily manipulated with ample working time; dimensionally stable with no shrinkage; seals the canal laterally and apically and conforms to its anatomy; non-irritating to periapical tissues; impervious to moisture and non-porous; unaffected by tissue fluids, with no corrosion; inhibits bacterial growth; radiopaque; does not discolour tooth structure; sterile; easily removed if necessary.

### Classification
Ingle's divides materials into **pastes and cements, solid materials and semi-solid materials**. Sikri's classification is: (1) gutta-percha (cold, chemically softened, heat softened, intraradicular or extraradicular); (2) silver cones; (3) miscellaneous (GuttaFlow, GuttaCore, Resilon, EZ Fill, SmartSeal, coated cones); (4) apical-third obturation (warm gutta-percha, dentin chips, MTA, carrier-based plugs); (5) pastes (N2, MTA, calcium phosphate cement, CEM cement, Biodentine).

### The individual materials
| Material | Description | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gutta-percha** | Trans-polyisoprene; zinc oxide filler; beta phase solid, alpha phase tacky when heated | Compactable once softened, inert, well tolerated, radiopaque, stable, removable with heat or solvent, easily disinfected | No adhesion, so sealer is always needed; lacks rigidity in small sizes; shrinks on cooling; displaced by pressure |
| **Silver cones** | Rigid cones, Jasper 1933, matched to file sizes | Easy placement and length control | Do not seal irregular or oval canals; corrode in tissue fluid to cytotoxic products; hard to remove. Obsolete |
| **Resilon** | Polycaprolactone core with bioactive glass; used with Epiphany sealer and primer | Attempted a monoblock; thermoplastic | Enzymatic degradation, high C-factor; 56% vs 88% success at 6 years; withdrawn 2014 |
| **Coated cones** | Gutta-percha coated with resin (EndoREZ), glass ionomer (Activ GP) or bioceramic nanoparticles | Bond to the sealer | Coating may fail; no outcome benefit shown |
| **GuttaCore** | Carrier-based, cross-linked gutta-percha core | Removable; no plastic carrier | Limited studies |
| **Pastes** | ZOE, medicated, N2 (Sargenti) | Adapt to anatomy | Extrusion, porosity, cannot be controlled or removed; paraformaldehyde pastes cause severe toxicity, paresthesia, resorption |
| **MTA / bioceramic putty / dentin chips** | Apical plugs | Biocompatible, hydrophilic seal in open apices | Technique-sensitive; MTA discolours |

### Conclusion
No core material meets all of Grossman's requirements. Gutta-percha comes closest because it is compactable, inert and removable, but its lack of adhesion makes the sealer essential. Silver cones and pastes are obsolete and Resilon has been withdrawn.
