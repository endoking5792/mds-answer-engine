## STUDY MODE

### S1. How to read "1971 to 2006" [orientation]
The window opens at the discovery and closes just before nano-ionomers (2007) and the modern bioactive era. A good answer tells the story as a chain: a problem, a fix, a new problem. Four eras fit: (1) origins and launch, (2) refinement of the chemistry, (3) modification with resin and metal, (4) high-viscosity and minimal-intervention use.

### S2. Origins [basic science]
Silicate cement released fluoride and was translucent, but dissolved quickly [SRC:C110]. Zinc polycarboxylate (1960s) was the first cement with real adhesion to tooth but was weak [SRC:A21]. Replacing zinc oxide with the ion-leachable glass of silicate cement gave GIC [SRC:B3]. First reports date to 1969 [SRC:E195] or 1972 [SRC:B156]; Craig's 14th edition says "the 1960s" in one place and "the 1970s" in another [SRC:B479] [SRC:B461]. The Wilson and Kent work is generally dated to about 1971 [RECALL].

### S3. 1972-1976: first products and clinical use
The first material, ASPA (aluminosilicate polyacrylate), was reported by Wilson and Kent in 1972 and recommended for cervical abrasion lesions, though it lacked aesthetics and translucency [SRC:A13]; first clinical use was reported in 1975 [SRC:B156]; original auto-cure materials were introduced in 1976 [SRC:A14], and Marzouk describes ASPA as the newest tooth-coloured restoration, introduced in the mid-seventies [SRC:C2200]. Early restorations often failed because they were left unsealed, so they took up water or dried and cracked [SRC:A428].

### S4. Late 1970s to 1980s: refining the chemistry
- Gelling of the 50% polyacrylic acid liquid was solved with itaconic and maleic copolymers [SRC:B5] [SRC:A19].
- Tartaric acid sharpened the set and allowed lower-fluoride, more translucent glasses [SRC:A20].
- Water-hardening cements, with freeze-dried polyacid in the powder, appeared in 1981 [SRC:B228].
- Strength was improved with metals: the amalgam-alloy admix ("miracle mix") is credited to Simmons in 1983 [SRC:A25], and glass cermet to McLean in 1985 [SRC:B188].
- Wilson and McLean published their classification (Type I luting, II restorative, III lining and base) in 1988 [SRC:E193] [SRC:A18].

### S5. Late 1980s to 1990s: resin modification and high viscosity [classification]
- **Resin-modified GIC** arose in the 1980s, adding water-soluble polymer or polymerisable resin (HEMA) so the cement set by light and resisted water early [SRC:E197]; Craig dates its introduction to the late 1980s [SRC:E201] and Powers to the early 1990s [SRC:B428].
- **Tri-cure** added a chemical dark cure [SRC:B190].
- **High-viscosity GIC** (Fuji VIII and IX) arrived in the 1990s for minimal-preparation and ART restorations [SRC:B197].
- **Compomer** (polyacid-modified composite) from about 1995 is resin-based, not a true GIC [SRC:B428] [SRC:A208].

### S6. 2000-2006: consolidation
Interest shifted to antibacterial and remineralising additives and to using ion exchange to deliver other ions; results were equivocal [SRC:A444]. A 2006 European conference reported that several improvements were expected [SRC:E448]. Nano-ionomers followed in 2007, just outside the window [SRC:B469].

### S7. The lesson of the timeline
Each stage fixed one weakness but never all: handling and strength improved, yet brittleness and water sensitivity remained, which is why newer research tries to raise fracture resistance without losing ion exchange [SRC:A445].

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 34 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Glass ionomer cement (glass polyalkenoate cement) arose in the early 1970s as a hybrid of silicate and zinc polycarboxylate cements and has been refined for more than three decades [SRC:B3] [SRC:B1]. Its history is a series of fixes to handling, strength and aesthetics.

### 2. Background: before 1971
| Cement | Gift | Flaw |
|---|---|---|
| Silicate | fluoride release, translucency [SRC:C110] | high solubility, poor strength |
| Zinc polycarboxylate (1960s) | first adhesion to tooth [SRC:A21] | low tensile strength, opaque [SRC:B2] |
| Glass ionomer | adhesion plus fluoride plus translucency [SRC:B219] | brittle, water sensitive |

### 3. Discovery and launch (1969-1976)
- Dated 1969 [SRC:E195], 1972 [SRC:B156] or "the 1960s" [SRC:B479]; Indian textbooks give 1972 [SRC:G3].
- Named ASPA; first clinical use reported 1975 [SRC:B156]; auto-cure materials introduced 1976 [SRC:A14].
- First use: cervical abrasion lesions; poor aesthetics [SRC:A13].
- Early failure: unsealed cement took up water or dried out and cracked [SRC:A428].

### 3A. The first product and its names
- **First commercial material:** ASPA, aluminosilicate polyacrylic acid, a hybrid of silicate powder and polycarboxylate liquid; early materials were very technique sensitive [SRC:C1201].
- **Fuji ASPA (as reported by Bhat):** polyacrylic acid liquid with a citric acid conditioner, powders in three shades and two colour pigments [SRC:B156].
- **Alternative names:** ASPA cement, glass polyalkenoate, glass ionomer cement, Ionofill, Chemfill [SRC:B157].
- **Why it spread:** it released fluoride like silicate cement, and adhesion to enamel and dentine made it replace silicates quickly after 1976 [SRC:A15].

### 3B. The fluoride story behind the interest
In the 1950s silicate cement was noticed to release fluoride, and in 1957 fluoride from silicate cements was shown to reduce adjacent enamel solubility [SRC:C133] [SRC:A15]. This earlier finding is why a fluoride-releasing, adhesive successor attracted so much interest.

### 4. Refinement of chemistry (late 1970s to 1980s)
- **Copolymers:** itaconic and maleic acid stop the liquid gelling [SRC:B5] [SRC:A19].
- **Tartaric acid:** sharper set, lower-fluoride, translucent glass [SRC:A20].
- **Water-hardening cement:** 1981 [SRC:B228].
- **Metal-reinforced:** miracle mix (1983) and glass cermet (1985) [SRC:A25] [SRC:B188].
- **Classification:** Wilson and McLean, 1988 [SRC:E193].

### 5. Resin modification (late 1980s to 1990s)
- **RMGIC:** HEMA added to the liquid; light set first, early water resistance, longer working time with a quick true set [SRC:E197] [SRC:E201].
- **Tri-cure:** adds redox dark cure for bulk placement [SRC:B190].
- **Date disagreement:** late 1980s (Craig) against early 1990s (Powers) [SRC:E201] [SRC:B428].

### 6. High-viscosity and minimal intervention (1990s)
- **High-viscosity GIC** (Fuji VIII, IX): packable, quicker to finish, better wear resistance; used in ART and posterior primary teeth [SRC:B197] [SRC:B198].
- **Compomer (1995):** resin-based, no ion-exchange bond [SRC:B428] [SRC:A209].
- **GIC liners and bases:** from the 1990s, polycarboxylate and glass-ionomer liners and bases replaced zinc oxide-eugenol and zinc phosphate [SRC:C2197].

### 7. 2000-2006
- Antibacterial additives (chlorhexidine, chitosan) and remineralising agents (CPP-ACP): results equivocal [SRC:A444].
- More use of ion exchange to release other ions [SRC:C270].
- A 2006 conference anticipated further improvements [SRC:E448].
- Nano-ionomer followed in 2007 [SRC:B469].

### 8. Uses that grew over the period
Restoration of cervical lesions, luting, lining and base, fissure sealant, ART and primary teeth, and later bone replacement in surgery [SRC:A13] [SRC:A22].

### 9A. Summary table: what each stage gave and what it left unsolved
| Stage | Gain | Still unsolved |
|---|---|---|
| ASPA (1972-76) | adhesion plus fluoride, translucent [SRC:B219] | unsealed water uptake and cracking [SRC:A428] |
| Copolymers, tartaric acid (late 1970s) | stable liquid, sharper set, translucency [SRC:A19] [SRC:A20] | brittleness |
| Water-hardening (1981) | easier handling, longer shelf life | moisture sensitivity [SRC:B228] |
| Metal-reinforced (1983-85) | abrasion resistance [SRC:A25] [SRC:B188] | colour, weak interface [SRC:G31] |
| RMGIC (late 1980s-1990s) | early water resistance, command set [SRC:E197] | HEMA, shrinkage, water uptake [SRC:B364] |
| High-viscosity (1990s) | packable, ART, wear resistance [SRC:B198] | strength little changed [SRC:B262] |
| Additives (2000-06) | antibacterial and remineralising aims [SRC:A444] | equivocal results |

### 9. Conclusion
Over 1971-2006 GIC moved from a brittle, unsealed, opaque filling to a family of luting, restorative, resin-modified and high-viscosity materials. Brittleness and water sensitivity remained the unsolved problems [SRC:A445].

### 10. Points an examiner expects in this answer
- State the parentage (silicate plus polycarboxylate) and the name ASPA before any dates [SRC:C1201].
- Give each date with the development it belongs to, not as a bare list.
- Separate true glass ionomers (conventional, metal-reinforced, resin-modified, high-viscosity) from resin-based look-alikes such as compomer [SRC:A208].
- Close by naming the unsolved problems: brittleness, water sensitivity and the debated fluoride benefit [SRC:A445] [SRC:G25].
