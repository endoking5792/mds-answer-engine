## STUDY MODE

### S1. Idea [basic science]
A tri-cure cement sets by three processes: the acid-base reaction, light polymerisation and a chemical redox "dark cure" [SRC:G16]. The third mechanism removes the main weakness of a light-cure material, which is that light cures only to a limited depth [SRC:A88].

### S2. Composition and setting
Bhat's tri-cure powder is an ion-leachable fluoride glass with a BisGMA resin matrix, organosilane coupling agent and light and chemical initiators (benzoyl peroxide and camphoroquinone with an amine accelerator) [SRC:B191]. The liquid is 30-50% aqueous polyacrylic acid with carboxyl groups modified with methyl methacrylate and HEMA [SRC:B191]. Sikri says the redox reaction between initiator and catalyst runs after mixing so that, with time, the whole restoration cures with no free resin left [SRC:G16].

### S3. Properties and uses [treatment relevance]
Tri-cure materials permit bulk placement with optimum cure even where light does not reach, so no incremental placement is needed (example: Vitremer) [SRC:B190]. Reported properties: fluoride release, mild pulp response, compressive strength 105 MPa, tensile 20 MPa, hardness 40 KHN, chemical adhesion, but greater polymerisation shrinkage, and water uptake with reduced translucency [SRC:B192]. Uses: liner under composite, core build-up, fissure sealant, cement base, orthodontic band cementation [SRC:B192].

### S4. Limits
Greater shrinkage and dehydration sensitivity are the costs of the added resin [SRC:B192]. Resin-modified materials in general take up water over 5-7 days [SRC:A92].

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Tri-cure GIC is a resin-modified glass ionomer that hardens by three mechanisms, so that it sets completely even where light cannot reach [SRC:G16] [SRC:B190].

### 2. The three setting mechanisms
1. **Acid-base reaction** between glass powder and polyacrylic acid (the conventional GIC reaction) [SRC:G16].
2. **Light cure:** polymerisation of methacrylate groups under visible light (camphoroquinone) [SRC:G16] [SRC:B191].
3. **Chemical (redox) dark cure:** reaction between initiator and catalyst once powder and liquid meet; benzoyl peroxide with an amine accelerator [SRC:G16] [SRC:B191].

### 3. Composition
- **Powder:** fluoride glass, BisGMA resin matrix, organosilane coupling agent, light and chemical initiators [SRC:B191].
- **Liquid:** 30-50% aqueous polyacrylic acid with carboxyl groups modified by methyl methacrylate and HEMA, plus activators [SRC:B191].
- **Presentation:** often capsules [SRC:B191].

### 4. Properties
| Property | Value or effect |
|---|---|
| Compressive strength | about 105 MPa [SRC:B192] |
| Tensile strength | about 20 MPa [SRC:B192] |
| Hardness | about 40 KHN [SRC:B192] |
| Fluoride | released; mild pulp response [SRC:B192] |
| Adhesion | chemical [SRC:B192] |
| Shrinkage | greater polymerisation shrinkage [SRC:B192] |
| Water | takes up water, dimensional change, reduced translucency [SRC:B192] |

### 4A. Comparison with the other forms
| Feature | Conventional GIC | Dual-cure (resin-modified) | Tri-cure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanisms | acid-base only [SRC:G14] | acid-base plus light [SRC:G16] | acid-base plus light plus redox [SRC:G16] |
| Command set | no | yes [SRC:G33] | yes [SRC:B190] |
| Depth of cure | full, slow | limited by light penetration [SRC:A88] | full, by chemical cure [SRC:B190] |
| Placement | bulk, sealed at once | increments | bulk possible [SRC:B190] |

### 4B. Depth of cure in practice
In a deep cavity (about 8 mm at its deepest) a dual-cure cement was light-activated for 40 seconds from the occlusal surface, with light penetration expected to about 4 mm and the remaining cement setting by its own chemistry [SRC:A361]. A tri-cure material adds a chemical resin cure for exactly this situation [SRC:G16]. Light activation hardens the resin to the depth of penetration, and the auto-cure component then completes the set [SRC:A88].

### 5. Advantages
Bulk placement with full cure even without light penetration; no incremental technique; no free resin left with time [SRC:B190] [SRC:G16].

### 6. Disadvantages
Greater polymerisation shrinkage; water uptake and loss of translucency; sensitivity to dehydration [SRC:B192].

### 7. Uses
Liner under composite, core build-up, fissure sealant, cement base, orthodontic band cementation [SRC:B192]; deep restorations where light penetration may be insufficient [SRC:G16].

### 8. Conclusion
Tri-cure adds a chemical cure to resin-modified GIC, giving a dependable set in depth at the cost of more shrinkage and water uptake.
