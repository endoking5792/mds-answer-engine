## STUDY MODE

### S1. Definition [orientation]
Cast metals are used to make inlays, onlays, crowns, bridges, endodontic posts and removable partial denture frameworks (Rudolf et al, 2024).

### S2. Classification [framework]
- By noble metal content: high noble, noble and predominately base metal (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); Craig 14th Table 10.3 adds a titanium and titanium alloys row (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); Phillips names a 2003 ADA revision with the same category (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- By strength: ANSI/ADA No. 5 Types I-IV by yield strength and elongation (Sakaguchi et al, 2019), with limits that differ between books (see Exam Mode and Q1); ISO 22674 gives six types by mechanical properties without reference to composition (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- By principal elements, for example Pd-Ag, Co-Cr or Ni-Cr-Be (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- By application: all-metal, metal-ceramic and removable partial denture (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### S3. Composition [composition]
Gold alloys: copper in enough amount makes the alloy heat-treatable and zinc is an oxygen scavenger (Anusavice et al, 2013). Base-metal alloys rely on chromium for corrosion resistance (Anusavice et al, 2013); chromium, cobalt and nickel together make 82 to 92 wt% (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### S4. Properties [properties]
Density runs from 4.5 g/cm3 for titanium alloys to more than 18 for some gold-rich alloys (Powers and Wataha, 2013). Base-metal alloys have about half the density of gold-based alloys (Anusavice et al, 2013). Hardened gold for removable prostheses has yield 480 to 510 MPa and elongation 5 to 7%, while Vitallium has 644 MPa and 1.5% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### S5. Requirements [criteria]
Craig 11th lists, as minimal characteristics of metals and alloys used as substitutes for gold alloys: no toxic or allergic effect, corrosion resistance, satisfactory physical and mechanical properties, a technique feasible for the average dentist, and plentiful inexpensive materials (Craig and Powers, 2002); Craig 14th introduces its list the same way, for substitutes for noble alloys (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Bhat adds excellent castability with low viscosity (Bhat et al, 2019). Rudolf et al add ease of melting, little solidification shrinkage and minimal reactivity with the mould (Rudolf et al, 2024).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 18 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition
Casting alloys are metals cast by the lost-wax method into inlays, onlays, crowns, bridges, posts and removable partial denture frameworks (Rudolf et al, 2024).

### 2. Classification
| Basis | Classes |
|---|---|
| Noble metal content (ADA) | High noble: noble metals 60 wt% or more, gold 40% or more; noble: 25% or more; predominately base metal: below 25%; Table 10.3 adds titanium and titanium alloys, Ti 85% or more (Sakaguchi et al, 2019) |
| Revision named by Phillips | 2003: high noble, titanium and titanium alloys, noble, predominantly base metal (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Strength (ANSI/ADA No. 5) | Types I-IV yield (MPa): Craig 14th below 140, 140-200, 201-340, above 340 (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); Phillips ISO draft 80, 180, 270, 360 (Anusavice et al, 2013); Bhat 80, 180, 240 or 270, 300 or 360 (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Principal elements | Pd-Ag, Co-Cr, Ni-Cr-Be (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Application | All-metal, metal-ceramic, removable partial denture (Anusavice et al, 2013) |

### 3. Composition
- Gold alloys contain copper, which makes them heat-treatable, and zinc as oxygen scavenger (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Base-metal alloys rely on chromium for corrosion resistance (Anusavice et al, 2013); chromium, cobalt and nickel make 82 to 92 wt% (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### 4. Properties
- Density 4.5 g/cm3 (titanium alloys) to above 18 (gold-rich) (Powers and Wataha, 2013).
- Hardened gold RPD alloy: yield 480-510 MPa, elongation 5-7%; Vitallium: 644 MPa, 1.5% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### 5. Requirements
Craig lists these as minimal characteristics of metals and alloys used as substitutes for gold alloys (Craig and Powers, 2002; Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
1. No harmful toxic or allergic effect (Craig and Powers, 2002).
2. Resistance to corrosion in oral fluids (Craig and Powers, 2002).
3. Satisfactory physical and mechanical properties (Craig and Powers, 2002).
4. Technique feasible for the average dentist and skilled technician (Craig and Powers, 2002).
5. Plentiful and relatively inexpensive (Craig and Powers, 2002).
6. Excellent castability, low viscosity (Bhat et al, 2019).
7. Little solidification shrinkage, minimal reaction with the mould (Rudolf et al, 2024).

### 6. Conclusion
Gold Types 2 and 3 are the benchmark against which other casting alloys are measured (Rudolf et al, 2024).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Three ADA groups: 60% noble with 40% gold, 25% noble, below 25% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Base metals depend on chromium (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Gold Types 2 and 3 are the benchmark (Rudolf et al, 2024).
