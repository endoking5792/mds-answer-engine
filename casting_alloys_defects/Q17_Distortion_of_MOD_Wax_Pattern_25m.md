## STUDY MODE

### S1. Why wax distorts [mechanism]
Anusavice et al state that distortion of wax patterns is the most serious problem in forming and removing the pattern (Anusavice et al, 2013). Wax has a high coefficient of thermal expansion, about 350 x 10^-6 per kelvin for inlay wax, and expands and contracts more per degree than any other dental material (Anusavice et al, 2013). Craig and Powers add a low softening temperature, which allows stress release or flow, and say distortion is a continual hazard (Craig and Powers, 2002). Marzouk gives a coefficient of up to 400 parts per million per degree (Marzouk, year not printed). Bhat et al give 300 to 700 parts per million per degree (Bhat et al, 2019). Waxes are poor thermal conductors, so the surface sets before the bulk and internal stresses form (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### S2. Causes [factors]
Anusavice et al list occluded air, physical deformation during molding, carving or removal, release of trapped stresses, excessive storage time and extreme temperature changes in storage (Anusavice et al, 2013). Wax also shows elastic memory, tending to return partly to its original shape (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat et al add that the wax may not be heated uniformly, may not be held under uniform pressure while cooling, or may have melted wax added to a deficient area (Bhat et al, 2019). Craig and Powers add a cool carving instrument, and a cool metal die when molten increments are used (Craig and Powers, 2002). Sikri adds wax added after initial cooling (Sikri, 2020). Marzouk adds volatile ingredients and air bubbles in incremental build-up (Marzouk, year not printed).

### S3. Storage and time [factors]
Craig and Powers: distortion increases with temperature and time of storage (Craig and Powers, 2002). Powers and Wataha: off the die, distortion may occur in 1 to 2 minutes (Powers and Wataha, 2013).

### S4. Removal and investing [factors]
Anusavice et al remove an MOD pattern with a staple-like wire over the proximal walls and floss, withdrawn parallel to the axial walls (Anusavice et al, 2013). Sikri uses a copper staple or brass wire for MOD patterns (Sikri, 2020). Investment expansion can itself distort a pattern: the proximal walls move outward and the gingival margins are forced apart, and distortion increases as the thickness of the pattern decreases (Anusavice et al, 2013). Craig and Powers state that once the investment has set there is no danger of further pattern distortion (Craig and Powers, 2002).

Marzouk starts with stress-free or annealed wax and builds the pattern in layers (Marzouk, year not printed).

### S5. Management [prevention]
- Temperature: heat evenly; Craig and Powers and Marzouk carve with warm instruments (Craig and Powers, 2002; Marzouk, year not printed), whereas Bhat et al advise a cold instrument for the direct pattern (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Time: invest at once (Anusavice et al, 2013; Bhat et al, 2019); Anusavice et al also say the pattern should stay on the die for several hours (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Sprue: Y-sprue for MOD inlays (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### S6. Shrinkage compensation of wax [wording 3]
Bhat et al state that wax patterns were once expanded in warm water, which distorted them, so the method is not practised (Bhat et al, 2019). Craig and Powers call wax expansion before investing impractical because of residual stress (Craig and Powers, 2002). Marzouk describes stressing the pattern evenly so that it is released in an expansive fashion to compensate for some shrinkage (Marzouk, year not printed). The glossary of Sikri (2017) defines wax expansion as a method of expanding a pattern to compensate for gold shrinkage (Sikri, 2017).

### S7. Critical evidence note [one claim]
The claim examined is that the pattern should be invested immediately. Anusavice et al, Bhat et al and Marzouk advise immediate investing (Anusavice et al, 2013; Bhat et al, 2019; Marzouk, year not printed). Craig and Powers allow 20 to 30 minutes in the investing section and refrigeration if the pattern must stand more than 30 minutes (Craig and Powers, 2002). Powers and Wataha give less than 30 minutes in one place and certainly less than 10 minutes in another (Powers and Wataha, 2013). Marzouk accepts refrigeration if investing cannot follow within 5 minutes, while Anusavice et al also state that the pattern should be kept on the die for several hours (Marzouk, year not printed; Anusavice et al, 2013). The books therefore give different time limits and are not reconciled here.

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 42 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Distortion of any wax pattern, including the MOD inlay pattern, is the most serious problem in forming and removing it (Anusavice et al, 2013). A distorted pattern gives a casting that does not fit (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### 2. Why wax distorts
- High thermal expansion: about 350 x 10^-6 per kelvin (Anusavice et al, 2013), 300 to 700 parts per million per degree (Bhat et al, 2019), up to 400 parts per million per degree (Marzouk, year not printed).
- Poor conductivity: the surface solidifies first and internal stresses form (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Elastic memory: wax tends to return partly to its original shape (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Air bubbles trapped in an incremental build-up expand on heating, and volatile ingredients may vaporise if overheated, leaving voids (Marzouk, year not printed).
- Low softening temperature: stress release or flow occurs easily (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### 3. Causes
Off the die, a pattern may distort within 1 to 2 minutes (Powers and Wataha, 2013).

| Group | Cause |
|---|---|
| Temperature | Uneven heating of the wax (Bhat et al, 2019); heating over a Bunsen flame gives non-uniform consistency (Craig and Powers, 2002); a cold metal die with molten increments gives uneven setting and pull-away, while stone die temperature is of little concern (Craig and Powers, 2002) |
| Manipulation | Cool carving instrument (Craig and Powers, 2002); wax added after cooling (Sikri, 2020); not held under uniform pressure (Bhat et al, 2019); occluded air (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Stress | Release of trapped stresses and elastic memory (Anusavice et al, 2013); residual stress (Powers and Wataha, 2013) |
| Storage | Long storage and temperature change (Anusavice et al, 2013; Craig and Powers, 2002) |
| Removal | Careless removal, touching with fingers (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Investing | Non-uniform investment expansion forces gingival margins apart; thinner patterns distort more (Anusavice et al, 2013) |

### 4. Management
**Preparing.**
- Soften uniformly; Craig and Powers use about 50 to 52 °C in a dry-heat oven, Marzouk uses a 55 °C oven for 15 minutes, and Sikri uses a wax annealer at 65 °C (Craig and Powers, 2002; Marzouk, year not printed; Sikri, 2020).
- Softening medium: Anusavice et al prefer dry heat (Anusavice et al, 2013); Sikri allows hot water or dry heat (Sikri, 2020); Craig and Powers allow annealing in water but not long storage (Craig and Powers, 2002); McCabe and Walls note that warm water softens more regularly (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Carving instrument: Craig and Powers, Marzouk, and Powers and Wataha advise warm instruments (Craig and Powers, 2002; Marzouk, year not printed; Powers and Wataha, 2013); Bhat et al advise a cold instrument for the direct pattern (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Add wax in small layers and use a warmed die (Craig and Powers, 2002); do not add wax after the pattern has cooled (Sikri, 2020).
- In the direct method allow ample cooling time (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Cut the pattern without pulling at the margins and remove it with a lubricant on the die (Bhat et al, 2019).

**Removing the MOD pattern.**
- Insert a staple-like wire over the proximal walls, loop floss through it and withdraw parallel to the axial walls, without touching the pattern (Anusavice et al, 2013).

**Spruing.** Use a solid wax sprue or a hollow metal sprue filled with sticky wax to reduce distortion (Craig and Powers, 2002). Use a Y-sprue on MOD inlays (Craig and Powers, 2002). Attach the sprue while the pattern is still on the die (Powers and Wataha, 2013). Keep the sprue away from fine margins (Craig and Powers, 2002).

**Storage and investing.**
| Book | Advice |
|---|---|
| Anusavice et al, 2013 | Invest immediately after removal; also keep the pattern on the die for several hours; the two statements are not reconciled (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Craig and Powers, 2002 | Not more than 20 to 30 minutes; if more than 30 minutes, refrigerate and readapt the margins (Craig and Powers, 2002) |
| Powers and Wataha, 2013 | Less than 30 minutes; elsewhere certainly less than 10 minutes (Powers and Wataha, 2013) |
| Marzouk, year not printed | Immediately; refrigerate if not within 5 minutes (Marzouk, year not printed) |
| Bhat et al, 2019 | Immediately, or cold storage for a short delay (Bhat et al, 2019) |

Craig and Powers state that once the investment has set no further pattern distortion occurs (Craig and Powers, 2002). Anusavice et al say the same in the wax section, but also say that some distortion occurs as the investment hardens (Anusavice et al, 2013). The books are not reconciled.

### 5. Investment-related distortion
Anusavice et al state that a lower setting expansion gives less distortion, a liner short of the ring ends gives more uniform expansion, and the problem is generally not serious (Anusavice et al, 2013). Lower expansion leaves less compensation for shrinkage, since total expansion must still be sufficient (Craig and Powers, 2002); see Q16.

### 6. Shrinkage compensation of wax patterns
Wax contracts about 0.35% on cooling from 37 to 25 °C (Anusavice et al, 2013). Expanding the wax before investing is not practised or impractical (Bhat et al, 2019; Craig and Powers, 2002). Marzouk instead describes even stressing so that the pattern releases in an expansive fashion (Marzouk, year not printed). The mold is also enlarged, partly by the 37 °C bath in the low-heat technique (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 7. Conclusion
MOD distortion arises from stress and temperature in the wax, and is managed by even heating, careful removal and prompt investing (Anusavice et al, 2013; Craig and Powers, 2002).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Wax distortion is the most serious wax-pattern problem (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- MOD pattern removal: staple-like wire and floss, parallel to axial walls (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Y-sprue is used on MOD inlays (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Investing time limits differ between books (Craig and Powers, 2002; Marzouk, year not printed).
- Warm-water expansion of wax is not practised (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Carving instrument: warm in Craig and Powers, cold for the direct pattern in Bhat et al (Craig and Powers, 2002; Bhat et al, 2019).
