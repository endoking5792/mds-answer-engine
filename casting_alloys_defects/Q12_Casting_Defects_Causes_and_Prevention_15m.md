## STUDY MODE

### S1. Principle [overview]
Defects in castings can in almost all instances be avoided by strict observance of the fundamental rules of the procedure (Anusavice et al, 2013). Porosity cannot be prevented entirely but can be minimised by proper technique (Anusavice et al, 2013). Marzouk calls wax-pattern fabrication the cause of most casting failures (Marzouk, year not printed).

### S2. Wax pattern and investing [prevention]
Distortion is prevented by proper handling of the wax pattern and by investing gently and uniformly (Sikri, 2020). Garg and Garg say the pattern should be invested immediately after fabrication (Garg and Garg, 2011). Air bubbles and nodules are prevented by vacuum investing, vibration before and after mixing, and a thin wetting agent that is air-dried (Sikri, 2020); Phillips calls a wetting agent helpful but by no means a certain remedy (Anusavice et al, 2013). Water-film ridges are prevented by avoiding movement of the pattern after investing (Sikri, 2020). The liquid/powder ratio must be accurate, since both excess and too little cause rough castings (Sikri, 2020).

### S3. Burnout and heating [prevention]
Sikri says the ring should be heated gradually, taking 60 minutes from room temperature to 700 degrees C for gypsum-bonded investment, because rapid heating flakes the investment (Sikri, 2020). Phillips gives a final burnout of 700 to 1030 degrees C for phosphate-bonded investment, depending on the alloy (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat lists rapid temperature rise, prolonged heating and overheating as reasons for investment fracture and fins (Bhat et al, 2019). Marzouk states that gradual heating gives even expansion of the mold walls (Marzouk, year not printed). Incomplete wax removal is prevented by adequate burnout time and temperature (Sikri, 2020).

### S4. Sprue, temperature and pressure [prevention]
Localized shrinkage is avoided by flaring the sprue attachment, avoiding a hot spot and an excessively long sprue, using a reservoir and lowering the casting temperature by about 30 degrees C (Sikri, 2020). Incomplete castings need a larger sprue, a hot enough alloy, a mold soaked at burnout temperature and enough casting force (Sikri, 2020). Phillips adds that pressure should be applied for at least 4 seconds (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### S5. Gas and back-pressure porosity [prevention]
Gas porosity is reduced by using the reducing zone of the flame and premelting used alloy on graphite (Anusavice et al, 2013). Back-pressure porosity is minimised by proper burnout, adequate mold and casting temperature, enough pressure, correct liquid/powder ratio and investment of not more than 6 mm beyond the pattern (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat gives less than 6 mm (Bhat et al, 2019) and Singh gives 6 to 8 mm from the end of the ring (Singh, 2020).

### S6. Contamination [prevention]
Gypsum-bonded investment should not be heated above 700 degrees C, and steel tongs should not be used in pickling (Sikri, 2020). A new amalgam should not be placed next to a high-noble restoration, and mixed alloys should not be melted together (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### S7. Where books differ [comparison]
For suckback, Craig 11th advises a Y-shaped sprue with widely separated arms and, if a Y-sprue is already used, wider legs or an extra 1 to 2 g of alloy; it raises the mold temperature from 500 to 650 or 700 degrees C, with figures from one gold alloy (solidification 940, cast at 1040 degrees C), and says raising the alloy temperature does not help (Craig and Powers, 2002). Phillips advises flaring the sprue attachment and lowering the casting temperature by about 30 degrees C (Anusavice et al, 2013). For the heating of the alloy, Sikri prints at least 150 degrees F (57 degrees C) above fusion temperature, and Garg and Garg print 570 degrees C, tied to heating above fusion temperature; no value is chosen (Sikri, 2020; Garg and Garg, 2011).

### 1. Introduction
Casting defects can in almost all instances be avoided by strict observance of procedure (Anusavice et al, 2013). Porosity cannot be prevented entirely but can be minimised (Anusavice et al, 2013). Marzouk calls wax-pattern fabrication the cause of most casting failures (Marzouk, year not printed).

### 2. Causes and prevention by defect
| Defect | Cause | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Distortion | Mishandled wax; uneven wall movement as investment sets (Garg and Garg, 2011) | Proper manipulation, immediate investing, gentle uniform investing (Garg and Garg, 2011; Sikri, 2020) |
| Nodules, roughness | Air bubbles; water films (Sikri, 2020) | Vacuum investing, vibration, air-dried wetting agent (not a certain remedy, Anusavice et al, 2013); do not move the pattern (Sikri, 2020) |
| Rough surface | Excess or too little water (Sikri, 2020) | Accurate liquid/powder ratio (Sikri, 2020) |
| Fins | Rapid or prolonged heating (Bhat et al, 2019) | Heat 60 minutes to 700 degrees C if gypsum-bonded (Sikri, 2020) |
| Incomplete casting | Small sprue, cold alloy or mold, low force (Sikri, 2020) | Larger sprue, soak mold, cast within one minute (Sikri, 2020) |
| Rounded margins | Wax residue, underheated alloy (Sikri, 2020) | Adequate burnout, enough pressure for 4 seconds (Sikri, 2020; Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Localized shrinkage | Sprue freezes first, hot spot (Sikri, 2020) | Flared sprue, reservoir, casting temperature lowered about 30 degrees C (Sikri, 2020) |
| Microporosity | Low casting temperature, rapid solidification (Garg and Garg, 2011) | Raise casting temperature (Garg and Garg, 2011) |
| Pinhole, gas inclusion | Poor torch flame, non-reducing zone (Garg and Garg, 2011) | Reducing zone; premelt alloy on graphite (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Back pressure | Dense investment, low temperature or pressure (Garg and Garg, 2011) | Porous investment, proper burnout, investment 6 mm or less (Anusavice et al, 2013); 6 to 8 mm (Singh, 2020) |
| Black casting | Investment above 700 degrees C, sulfur in flame (Sikri, 2020); Craig 11th adds wax left, mold too long in oven, oxidizing flame, no deoxidizer (Craig and Powers, 2002) | Avoid overheating; change heat source (Sikri, 2020) |
| Pontic porosity | Pontic retains heat (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Small chill-set sprues (Anusavice et al, 2013) |

### 3. Books that differ (side by side)
- Suckback: Craig 11th, widely separated Y-sprue arms, wider legs or extra 1 to 2 g alloy if a Y-sprue is already used, mold temperature raised from 500 to 650 or 700 degrees C (one gold alloy study), and raising alloy temperature does not help (Craig and Powers, 2002); Phillips, flared sprue and casting temperature lowered about 30 degrees C (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Alloy heating for incomplete casting: Sikri, 150 degrees F (57 degrees C) above fusion temperature (Sikri, 2020); Garg and Garg, 570 degrees C above fusion temperature, as printed; no value chosen (Garg and Garg, 2011).

### 4. General measures
Bhat advises a reservoir about 1 mm from the pattern and a sprue thicker than the thickest part of the pattern to feed the last solidifying metal (Bhat et al, 2019). Phillips advises that the sprue direction should avoid a direct 90 degree impact on a weak mold surface (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 5. Conclusion
Phillips attributes casting defects to carelessness or ignorance of the operator (Anusavice et al, 2013). This answer groups prevention by stage (own organisation): wax, investing, burnout, sprue, melting.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Heat 60 minutes to 700 degrees C (gypsum-bonded) to prevent fins (Sikri, 2020).
- Flared sprue, reservoir and about 30 degrees C lower casting temperature prevent localized shrinkage (Sikri, 2020).
- Pressure for at least 4 seconds (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Investment beyond the pattern: 6 mm or less (Anusavice et al, 2013); 6 to 8 mm (Singh, 2020).
- Vacuum investing prevents nodules (Anusavice et al, 2013).
