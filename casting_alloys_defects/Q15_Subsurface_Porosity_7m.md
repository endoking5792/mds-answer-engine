## STUDY MODE

### S1. Place in the classification [classification]
Anusavice et al (2013) list subsurface porosity as the third of four trapped-gas porosities, after pinhole porosity and gas inclusions and before back-pressure porosity (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat et al (2019) call it "sub-surface porosities" under air inclusion porosities (Bhat et al, 2019). Sikri lists it as item c among the porosity types (Sikri, 2020).

### S2. Appearance and cause [definition and mechanism]
Anusavice et al (2013) state that the reasons for these voids are not well understood and that they may arise from simultaneous nucleation of solid grains and gas bubbles when the alloy first freezes at the mold walls (Anusavice et al, 2013). Sikri repeats the same suggestion and states that the exact reason has not been established (Sikri, 2020). Bhat et al describe regular small bubble voids below the surface, from trapped or absorbed gases, attributed to simultaneous nucleation and release of gas bubbles (Bhat et al, 2019). Singh gives casting in a cold mold, or simultaneous nucleation of grains and gas bubbles at the mold wall (Singh, 2020). Marzouk states that a substantial difference between melt and mold temperature will precipitate subsurface or generalised porosity (Marzouk, year not printed).

### S3. Effect of casting variables [factors]
Table 10-4 of Anusavice et al (2013) lists subsurface porosity as increased with greater sprue thickness, decreased with greater sprue length, and increased with higher melt temperature and higher mold temperature (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat et al give the remedy as a higher liquid temperature (Bhat et al, 2019). The books therefore differ on temperature: higher melt and mold temperature increase it in Table 10-4 (Anusavice et al, 2013), a cold mold is a cause for Singh (Singh, 2020), and Bhat et al advise a higher liquid temperature (Bhat et al, 2019).

### S4. Control [prevention]
Anusavice et al: control the rate at which molten metal enters the mold (Anusavice et al, 2013). Sikri and Singh give the same control (Sikri, 2020; Singh, 2020).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 12 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition
Subsurface porosity is a group of small voids lying just below the surface of a casting. Anusavice et al class it with trapped-gas porosities, together with pinhole porosity, gas inclusions and back-pressure porosity (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat et al class it as an air inclusion porosity (Bhat et al, 2019).

### 2. Cause
- The reasons are not well understood; it may be caused by solid grains and gas bubbles forming together when the alloy first freezes at the mold wall (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Sikri says the exact reason has not been established and gives the same possibility (Sikri, 2020).
- Bhat et al: trapped or absorbed gases released below the surface as small regular voids (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Singh: a cold mold, or grains and gas bubbles forming together (Singh, 2020).
- Marzouk: a large difference between melt and mold temperature (Marzouk, year not printed).

### 3. Effect of variables (Anusavice et al, 2013)
| Variable increased | Subsurface porosity |
|---|---|
| Sprue thickness | Increased (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Sprue length | Decreased (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Melt temperature | Increased (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Mold temperature | Increased (Anusavice et al, 2013) |

The books differ on temperature: Bhat et al advise a higher liquid temperature (Bhat et al, 2019), whereas Singh names a cold mold as a cause (Singh, 2020).

### 4. Control
Control the rate at which molten metal enters the mold (Anusavice et al, 2013; Sikri, 2020; Singh, 2020).

### 5. Significance
Like all porosities it lowers strength (Bhat et al, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Phillips: cause "not well understood"; simultaneous nucleation of grains and gas bubbles at the mold wall (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Control the rate of metal entry (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Phillips places it under trapped gases; its Table 10-4 sits with solidification porosity (Anusavice et al, 2013).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Stating a firm cause; two books say the cause is not established (Anusavice et al, 2013; Sikri, 2020).
2. Confusing it with pinhole porosity, which is gas expelled from the molten metal on solidification (Anusavice et al, 2013).
