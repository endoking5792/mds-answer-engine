## STUDY MODE

### S1. Meaning and importance [definition]
Porosity is one of the commonest casting defects and occurs both inside the casting and on its surface (Anusavice et al, 2013). Internal porosity weakens the casting; porosity that reaches the surface may cause discoloration, plaque accumulation, secondary caries or periodontal disease (Anusavice et al, 2013). Porosity cannot be prevented entirely but can be minimised by proper technique (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### S2. Classification [classification]
Anusavice et al (2013) list solidification defects (localized shrinkage, microporosity, suck-back) and trapped gases (pinhole, gas inclusions, subsurface, back-pressure) (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat et al (2019) list solidification shrinkage porosities (microporosity, localized shrinkage, hot spot or suck-back), air inclusion porosities (pinhole, large air bubbles, sub-surface) and back-pressure porosity under incomplete castings (Bhat et al, 2019). McCabe and Walls (2008) list porosity in casting as one of four faults and describe local shrinkage near the sprue base and gaseous porosity (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### S3. Solidification porosities [causes and prevention]
- Localized shrinkage: the sprue freezes before the casting, leaving a void in the last part to solidify, usually near the sprue junction; the linear contraction of noble alloys from liquid to solid is at least 1.25% (Anusavice et al, 2013). Sikri lists flaring the sprue, a reservoir, avoiding a long sprue and lowering the casting temperature by about 30 °C (Sikri, 2020). Table 10-4 of Anusavice et al shows a higher melt temperature and a higher mold temperature each decreasing localized shrinkage (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat et al advise a sprue thicker than the thickest part of the pattern, with a reservoir (Bhat et al, 2019). The books differ on temperature and none is preferred. McCabe and Walls thicken the sprue near the casting as a reservoir so the shrinkage porosity falls in the reservoir (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Suck-back: a hot spot where metal strikes the mold wall, often at a poorly rounded line angle; Anusavice et al remove it by flaring the sprue attachment and lowering the casting temperature by about 30 °C (Anusavice et al, 2013). Craig and Powers define suckback as a portion of the sprue alloy freezing before the alloy in the casting (Craig and Powers, 2002). They use a Y-sprue, an extra 1 to 2 g of alloy only if a Y-sprue has already been employed, or a mold temperature of 650 to 700 °C, and state that a hotter alloy or an extra turn does not help (Craig and Powers, 2002). Bhat et al advise a lower liquid temperature and a thinner sprue (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Microporosity: rapid solidification when mold or casting temperature is too low; seen only on sectioning; generally not serious (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat et al give a higher casting temperature as the remedy (Bhat et al, 2019).

### S4. Gas porosities [causes and prevention]
- Pinhole: copper and silver dissolve oxygen, platinum and palladium take up hydrogen and oxygen, and the gas is expelled on freezing; Anusavice et al give the cause but no remedy for pinhole porosity (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat et al advise not keeping the liquid long before casting and not casting close to the liquidus (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Gas inclusion: the larger spherical voids follow a poorly adjusted torch or the oxidising zone; premelting used alloy on graphite and using the reducing zone minimise them (Anusavice et al, 2013). McCabe and Walls advise avoiding overheating or casting under inert gas or vacuum (McCabe and Walls, 2008). Marzouk states that palladium has a high affinity for hydrogen at the melting stage and silver a similar affinity for oxygen (Marzouk, year not printed).
- Subsurface: cause not well understood; possibly simultaneous nucleation of grains and gas bubbles; control the rate of entry of metal (Anusavice et al, 2013). Detail in Q15.
- Back-pressure: air that cannot escape through the investment forms concave depressions on the inner surface (Anusavice et al, 2013). Dense investments, vacuum investing and carbon clogging of the mold increase it (Anusavice et al, 2013). Prevention: full burnout, adequate mold and casting temperature, adequate pressure, correct liquid/powder ratio and not more than 6 mm of investment behind the pattern (Anusavice et al, 2013).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition and importance
Casting porosity is voids in or on a casting that weaken it; surface porosity causes discoloration, plaque retention and secondary caries; it can be minimised but not prevented (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 2. Classification
- I. Solidification defects: localized shrinkage, microporosity, suck-back (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- II. Trapped gases: pinhole, gas inclusions, subsurface, back-pressure (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 3. Types, causes and prevention
| Type | Cause | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Localized shrinkage | Sprue freezes first; void near sprue junction (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Flared sprue, reservoir, casting temperature about 30 °C lower (Sikri, 2020); Table 10-4: hotter melt decreases it (Anusavice et al, 2013); thicker sprue (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Suck-back | Hot spot at mold wall (Anusavice et al, 2013); sprue alloy freezing before the casting (Craig and Powers, 2002) | Flare sprue, casting temperature about 30 °C lower (Anusavice et al, 2013); Y-sprue, mold 650 to 700 °C, extra 1 to 2 g only if a Y-sprue is already used (Craig and Powers, 2002) |
| Microporosity | Rapid freezing, low mold or casting temperature (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Higher casting temperature (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Pinhole | Dissolved gas expelled on freezing (Anusavice et al, 2013) | No remedy given by Phillips (Anusavice et al, 2013); do not hold liquid long (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Gas inclusion | Poorly adjusted torch, oxidising zone (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Reducing zone, premelt on graphite (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Subsurface | Not well understood; possibly grains and gas bubbles forming together (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Control the rate of metal entry (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Back-pressure | Air cannot escape; dense investment, carbon-clogged mold (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Full burnout, adequate temperature and pressure, 6 mm or less of investment behind the pattern (Anusavice et al, 2013) |

### 4. Naming in other books
Bhat et al join hot spot with suck-back and add large air bubbles (Bhat et al, 2019). Craig and Powers name suck-back only (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### 5. Conclusion
Shrinkage porosity is a feeding problem; the others are gas problems (Anusavice et al, 2013).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Phillips lists 3 solidification types and 4 trapped-gas types (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Noble alloys contract linearly at least 1.25% from liquid to solid (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Suck-back: flared sprue and casting temperature lower by about 30 °C (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Back-pressure: not more than 6 mm of investment behind the pattern (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Raising alloy temperature does not cure suck-back in Craig 11th ed (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Table 10-4: higher melt temperature decreases localized shrinkage and microporosity but increases subsurface porosity (Anusavice et al, 2013).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Listing only gas porosity and missing the shrinkage types (Anusavice et al, 2013).
2. Saying a hotter alloy cures suck-back; Craig 11th ed says it may increase it (Craig and Powers, 2002).
4. Giving premelting and the reducing zone as the cure for pinhole porosity; Phillips gives them for the larger gas inclusions (Anusavice et al, 2013).
3. Giving a prevention for subsurface porosity as a settled cause; the cause is not well understood (Anusavice et al, 2013).
