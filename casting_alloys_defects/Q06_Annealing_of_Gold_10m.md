## STUDY MODE

### S1. The two senses [definition]
In gold work the word is used for (A) direct filling gold before it is placed, and (B) cast gold alloys, where Bhat calls the softening treatment an annealing heat treatment (Bhat et al, 2019).

### S2. Sense A: direct gold [procedure]
Phillips: the dentist heats direct gold to remove the protective coating, wax or surface contaminants, over an alcohol flame or on an electric annealing plate; the step is commonly called annealing, heat treatment or degassing, but desorption is the more appropriate term (Anusavice et al, 2013). Powdered gold pellets take 15 to 20 seconds, foil and electrolytic pellets only 1 or 2 seconds; underheating compromises cohesion, overheating causes sintering, contamination and embrittlement (Anusavice et al, 2013).
Bhat: the manufacturer treats the gold with ammonia gas; heating drives it off and improves welding; the temperature range is 350 to 700 C, just red hot, with a pure alcohol reducing flame (Bhat et al, 2019).
Sikri: annealing means removal of surface contaminants; the preferred terms are degassing or desorption; the alcohol flame is held until dull cherry red (Sikri, 2020).
McCabe and Walls: foil is heated to about 250 C in an electric furnace or flame to remove adsorbed grease or gases before welding (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### S3. Sense B: cast gold [softening]
Phillips: place the casting in a furnace for 10 minutes at 700 C and quench in water; tensile strength, proportional limit and hardness fall and ductility rises; this is the solution heat treatment (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat: about 700 C, just below the solidus, about 10 minutes, then quench (Bhat et al, 2019). McCabe and Walls: castings are first softened by cooling rapidly from above 600 C; when platinum or palladium are present, 700 C for 10 minutes then quench removes coring (McCabe and Walls, 2008). Craig 11th: in the worked example of an Au-Cu-Ag-Pd-I alloy, it is often heated to 700 C, above the 424 C transition of the ordered phase, then quenched (Craig and Powers, 2002). Van Noort: a homogenizing anneal at approximately 700 C (van Noort, 2013).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

The question can mean two things; both are answered.

### A. Annealing (desorption) of direct gold foil
1. Aim: remove wax, ammonia film or surface contaminants so that gold cold-welds (Anusavice et al, 2013; Bhat et al, 2019).
2. Name: Phillips calls "annealing" common and "desorption" more appropriate (Anusavice et al, 2013); Bhat says formerly, wrongly, termed annealing (Bhat et al, 2019); Sikri prefers degassing or desorption (Sikri, 2020).
3. Method: alcohol flame or electric annealing plate (Anusavice et al, 2013).

| Book | Heating stated |
|---|---|
| Anusavice et al, 2013 | powdered pellets 15 to 20 s; foil and electrolytic pellets 1 to 2 s |
| Bhat et al, 2019 | 350 to 700 C, just red hot |
| Sikri, 2020 | dull cherry red; 600 to 1300 F; electric annealer 800 F, 10 min (may be inadequate) |
| McCabe and Walls, 2008 | about 250 C |

4. Faults: underheating loses cohesion; overheating gives sintering, contamination and brittleness (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### B. Softening (solution) heat treatment of cast gold alloys
1. Aim: soften and make ductile a casting before it is ground or reshaped (Anusavice et al, 2013) and burnished at the margins (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
2. Procedure: quench in water after heating (below).

| Book | Softening |
|---|---|
| Anusavice et al, 2013 | 10 minutes at 700 C, quench in water |
| van Noort, 2013 | homogenizing anneal at about 700 C |
| Craig and Powers, 2002 | Au-Cu-Ag-Pd-I example: often 700 C, above 424 C, quenched |
| Bhat et al, 2019 | about 700 C for about 10 min, quench |
| McCabe and Walls, 2008 | 700 C, 10 minutes, quench, if Pt or Pd present |

3. Mechanism: quenching keeps the disordered solid solution and prevents Au-Cu ordering (Anusavice et al, 2013; Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
4. Effect: strength, proportional limit and hardness fall, ductility rises (Anusavice et al, 2013).
5. Limit: a casting cooled slowly is hard; heating to 400 C does not soften it because 424 C must be exceeded (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### Conclusion
Annealing of foil is desorption of contaminants; annealing of cast gold is the quenching treatment that gives the soft, ductile state (Anusavice et al, 2013).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Desorption is the preferred term for "annealing" of foil (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Underheating loses cohesion; overheating embrittles (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Softening of cast gold: 700 C, 10 minutes, quench (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Hardening needs ordering; softening prevents it (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
