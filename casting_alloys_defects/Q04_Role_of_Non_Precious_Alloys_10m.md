## STUDY MODE

### S1. Meaning and reason for use [definition]
Predominately base metal alloys have a noble metal content below 25% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Noble metal prices began rising in the 1970s and alternatives were sought (Rudolf et al, 2024).

### S2. Types and uses [classification]
Titanium is treated as a separate category by Phillips and Craig 14th (Anusavice et al, 2013; Sakaguchi et al, 2019). The main base-metal groups are nickel-chromium, with or without beryllium, and cobalt-chromium; most Ni-Cr alloys are for small castings such as crowns and fixed prostheses and Co-Cr alloys for removable partial dentures (Anusavice et al, 2013). Cast Co-Cr and Ni-Cr have replaced Type IV gold almost completely for partial denture frameworks (Craig and Powers, 2002). Ni-Cr and Co-Cr are also used for porcelain-fused-to-metal restorations (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### S3. Composition and properties [properties]
Chromium, cobalt and nickel together make 82 to 92 wt% (Craig and Powers, 2002). Base metals rely on chromium for corrosion resistance (Anusavice et al, 2013). ANSI/ADA specification No. 14 (ISO 6871) for removable-denture Co-Cr and Ni-Cr alloys asks for Cr+Co+Ni of 85% or more, elongation 1.5%, yield strength 500 MPa and elastic modulus 170 GPa as minima (Craig and Powers, 2002; Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Vitallium shows yield 644 MPa and elongation 1.5%, hardened gold 480 to 510 MPa and 5 to 7% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### S4. Advantages and limits [evaluation]
Base-metal alloys are the least expensive casting alloys but the most difficult to cast and polish (Powers and Wataha, 2013). Craig 11th says crown and bridge Ni-Cr and Co-Cr alloys have higher solidification shrinkage, so a satisfactory fit is harder (Craig and Powers, 2002); Phillips lists 2.30% against 1.37 to 1.56% for gold (Anusavice et al, 2013). Powers and Wataha call them controversial because of relatively high corrosion and questionable biocompatibility, and put nickel allergy at 8% to 15% of the population (Powers and Wataha, 2013). Phillips lists lighter weight, greater stiffness and strength and reduced cost (Anusavice et al, 2013); Craig 14th gives density of 7 to 8 g/cm3 (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### S5. Hazards [safety]
Phillips: nickel is a moderate allergen (in the 24 to 44 age group with a nickel fixed prosthesis 9.7% of females and 0.8% of males reacted; all ages 4.5% and 1.5%; no correlation with intraoral nickel restorations) and beryllium has high vapour pressure at casting temperature (Anusavice et al, 2013). Craig 14th links beryllium to dermatitis, chronic lung disease, lung carcinoma and osteosarcoma (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 18 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition
Non-precious (predominately base metal) alloys have a noble metal content below 25 wt% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Rising noble metal prices from the 1970s led to their use (Rudolf et al, 2024).

### 2. Types
Ni-Cr, with or without beryllium, and Co-Cr (Anusavice et al, 2013). Titanium is a separate category in Phillips and Craig 14th (Anusavice et al, 2013; Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### 3. Role
- Partial denture frameworks: Co-Cr and Ni-Cr replaced Type IV gold almost completely (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Crowns and bridges: most Ni-Cr alloys are for small castings (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Metal-ceramic: Ni-Cr and Co-Cr are used (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### 4. Composition and properties
- Chromium, cobalt and nickel make 82 to 92 wt% (Craig and Powers, 2002); chromium gives corrosion resistance (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- ANSI/ADA No. 14 (ISO 6871), removable-denture Co-Cr and Ni-Cr: Cr+Co+Ni 85% or more; minima elongation 1.5%, yield 500 MPa, modulus 170 GPa (Craig and Powers, 2002; Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Density 7 to 8 g/cm3, about half that of gold (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); Phillips adds lighter, stiffer, stronger, cheaper (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 5. Advantages and limitations
- Least expensive; most difficult to cast and polish; controversial for relatively high corrosion and questionable biocompatibility (Powers and Wataha, 2013).
- Crown and bridge Ni-Cr and Co-Cr: higher solidification shrinkage (Craig and Powers, 2002); Phillips lists 2.30% against 1.37 to 1.56% for gold (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 6. Hazards
- Nickel: moderate allergen; ages 24 to 44 with nickel fixed prosthesis, 9.7% of females and 0.8% of males reacted, all ages 4.5% and 1.5%, no correlation with intraoral nickel restorations (Anusavice et al, 2013); 5% to 8% of females (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); 8% to 15% of the population (Powers and Wataha, 2013); more contact dermatitis than all other metals combined (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Beryllium: dermatitis, lung disease, lung carcinoma, ISO limit 0.02 wt% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); animal carcinogen (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### 7. Titanium
Melting point 1668 °C, special casting machine (Anusavice et al, 2013); use mainly restricted to preformed implants (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### 8. Conclusion
Base metals are least expensive and strong but hardest to cast (Powers and Wataha, 2013).
## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Base metal: below 25 wt% noble metal (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- ANSI/ADA No. 14 minima for removable-denture alloys: 1.5%, 500 MPa, 170 GPa (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Nickel allergen; beryllium limit 0.02 wt% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
