## STUDY MODE

### S1. Meaning [orientation]
Phillips: the noble metals are a group of seven metals resistant to corrosion and tarnish in the mouth, and silver is not considered noble (Anusavice et al, 2013). High-noble alloys have 60 wt% noble metal and 40% gold or more; noble alloys 25% noble metal or more (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### S2. Physical properties [physical]
Density is above 13 g/cm3 for high-noble alloys, which makes them easy to cast, and 10 to 12 for noble alloys (Powers and Wataha, 2013). Gold-based alloys are yellow; Pd-Cu-Ga and Ag-Pd are white (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Casting shrinkage of the gold alloys listed by Phillips is 1.37 to 1.56% (Anusavice et al, 2013). Phillips Table 16-6 lists a type 1 high-noble alloy at 16.6 g/cm3 with yield 126 MPa (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### S3. Mechanical properties [mechanical]
Yield strength in the hard condition ranges from 320 to 1145 MPa; Pd-Cu-Ga is strongest (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Hardness runs from 155 kg/mm2 (Ag-Pd) to 425 (Pd-Cu-Ga), typically about 200; the hardness of most noble casting alloys is below enamel at 343, but Pd-Cu-Ga at 425 is above it (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Soft-condition elongation is 8% to 30% against 1% to 2% for base metals in Craig 14th (Sakaguchi et al, 2019), whereas Phillips Table 16-7 lists 7% to 29% for base-metal crown, FDP and metal-ceramic alloys (Anusavice et al, 2013) and Craig 14th Table 10.13 gives 1.5% and 2.4% for cast removable-denture alloys (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). In a typical gold-based casting alloy an ordered solution may raise yield strength by 50%, tensile strength by 25% and hardness by at least 10% while elongation falls (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### S4. Chemical and biological properties [chemical]
Ag-Pd alloys have greater potential for tarnish and corrosion, yet corrosion is negligible if palladium exceeds 25% (Anusavice et al, 2013). Multiple-phase alloys release more atoms than single-phase alloys, and alloys with high noble metal content release less (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### 1. Meaning
Noble alloys resist corrosion and tarnish in the mouth; silver is not counted as noble (Anusavice et al, 2013). High noble: 60 wt% noble metals with 40% gold or more; noble: 25% or more (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### 3. Physical
- High-noble density above 13 g/cm3, easy to cast; noble 10 to 12 (Powers and Wataha, 2013).
- Casting shrinkage of gold alloys 1.37 to 1.56% (Anusavice et al, 2013); Phillips Table 16-6 type 1 high noble 16.6 g/cm3, yield 126 MPa (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 4. Mechanical
- Hard-condition yield 320 to 1145 MPa, strongest Pd-Cu-Ga (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Hardness of most noble alloys about 200 kg/mm2, below enamel (343), but Pd-Cu-Ga (425) is above; a harder alloy may wear opposing enamel (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Typical gold-based alloy: ordered solution may raise yield 50%, tensile 25%, hardness at least 10%; elongation falls (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Soft elongation 8% to 30%; base metals 1% to 2% (Craig 14th), RPD alloys 1.5% and 2.4% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); Phillips Table 16-7 base metals for crowns, FDPs and metal-ceramic 7% to 29% (Anusavice et al, 2013); high elongation allows burnishing (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### 5. Chemical and biological
- Ag-Pd: more tarnish; corrosion negligible if palladium exceeds 25% (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Contact allergy to gold is not as rare as may be imagined (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### 6. Conclusion
Noble alloys combine corrosion resistance with ductility and, in most, a hardness below enamel (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Hardness of most noble alloys about 200 kg/mm2, enamel 343 (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Typical gold-based alloy: ordered solution may raise yield 50%, tensile 25%, hardness 10% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Pd-Cu-Ga yield 1145 MPa (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Ag-Pd corrosion negligible if Pd above 25% (Anusavice et al, 2013).
