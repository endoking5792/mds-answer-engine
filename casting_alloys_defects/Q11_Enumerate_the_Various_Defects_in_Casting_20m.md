## STUDY MODE

### S1. What a casting defect is [definition]
A precision casting should have the exact shape, size, correct fit, smooth surface and no internal or external flaw (Bhat et al, 2019). Phillips says defects can be avoided in almost all instances by strict observance of the fundamental rules of the procedure (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### S2. How the books group the defects [classification]
Phillips uses four headings: distortion, surface roughness and irregularities, porosity, and incomplete or missing detail (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat uses seven: distortion, discolouration, surface roughness with nodules, solidification shrinkage porosities, air inclusion porosities, incomplete castings and fins (Bhat et al, 2019). McCabe and Walls use four: finning and bubbling, incomplete casting, porosity, and oversized or undersized casting (McCabe and Walls, 2008). Singh uses six: under or oversized, distortion, surface roughness and irregularities, incomplete, contaminated and porosities (Singh, 2020). Sikri tabulates causes and solutions under incomplete castings, rounded margins, pits, distortion, surface roughness and irregularities, and discoloration (Sikri, 2020).

### S3. Distortion and size [defect group]
Marked casting distortion is probably related to wax-pattern distortion, and uneven investment expansion can also move the pattern walls (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat attributes a change of size to under or over compensation of casting shrinkage, and distortion of shape to careless removal, delay in investing and too much vibration (Bhat et al, 2019). McCabe and Walls describe an undersized or oversized casting as the net balance of expansions and contractions (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### S4. Surface defects [defect group]
Roughness rises with the liquid/powder ratio, and nodules come from air bubbles on the pattern (Anusavice et al, 2013). Fins form when the investment cracks and metal flows into the crack (Singh, 2020). Sulfur contamination gives a brittle black or gray layer on gold alloys (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### S5. Incomplete casting [defect group]
Incomplete casting follows insufficient venting or high viscosity of the metal (Anusavice et al, 2013). Rounded margins follow insufficient casting pressure or underheated alloy (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### S6. Porosity [defect group]
Phillips lists solidification defects (localized shrinkage, microporosity, suck-back) and trapped gases (pinhole, gas inclusions, subsurface, back pressure) (Anusavice et al, 2013). Porosity cannot be prevented entirely but can be minimised (Anusavice et al, 2013). Garg and Garg list solidification shrinkage, gaseous, back-pressure and subsurface porosity (Garg and Garg, 2011).

### S7. Critical evidence: books that differ [critical evidence]
Suck-back is placed differently. Phillips calls it another type of localized shrinkage porosity caused by a hot spot (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat describes a void opposite the sprue from a hot spot (Bhat et al, 2019). Singh ties it to a sprue attached at a right angle (Singh, 2020). Craig 11th gives the sequence in which sprue alloy freezes before alloy at the pulpal floor, and also the hot spot of investment heated under a single sprue (Craig and Powers, 2002). The books differ in classification and remedy; no ruling is made here. Subsurface porosity is also handled differently: Bhat gives "temperature of the liquid should be higher" (Bhat et al, 2019), while Phillips, Sikri and Singh say control the rate at which metal enters the mold, and Garg and Garg say control the flow (Anusavice et al, 2013; Sikri, 2020; Singh, 2020; Garg and Garg, 2011).

### 1. Introduction
A good casting has exact shape, size and fit, a smooth surface and no internal or external flaw (Bhat et al, 2019).

### 2. Classification (each book as printed)
| Book | Groups |
|---|---|
| Phillips 12th | Distortion; surface roughness and irregularities; porosity; incomplete or missing detail (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Bhat | Distortion; discolouration; surface roughness and nodules; solidification shrinkage porosities; air inclusion porosities; incomplete castings; fins (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| McCabe and Walls | Finning and bubbling; incomplete casting; porosity; oversized or undersized casting (McCabe and Walls, 2008) |
| Singh | Under or oversized; distortion; roughness and irregularities; incomplete; contaminated; porosities (Singh, 2020) |
| Sikri | Table 16.5 headings: incomplete castings; rounded margins; pits; distortion; surface roughness and irregularities; discoloration (Sikri, 2020) |
| Craig 11th | No formal list; describes suckback porosity, black castings, rounded margins and air-bubble positives (Craig and Powers, 2002) |

### 3. Defects enumerated with causes
1. **Distortion:** wax-pattern distortion, or uneven investment expansion that forces the gingival margins apart while the occlusal wax bar resists (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat adds careless removal from the die, delay in investing and too much vibration (Bhat et al, 2019).
2. **Oversize or undersize:** under or over compensation of casting shrinkage (Bhat et al, 2019); McCabe and Walls give the balance of alloy shrinkage against setting expansion, thermal expansion and inversion (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
3. **Surface roughness:** high liquid/powder ratio, coarse silica and foreign bodies in the mold (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat adds a rough pattern, inadequate vibration and insufficient wetting (Bhat et al, 2019).
4. **Nodules (positives):** air bubbles on the pattern; those on margins or inside are difficult to remove and may need recasting (Anusavice et al, 2013; Craig and Powers, 2002).
5. **Fins:** cracked investment, from weak investment, rapid heating or high casting force (Bhat et al, 2019).
6. **Discolouration:** sulfur contamination gives a brittle black or gray layer not cleaned by pickling (Anusavice et al, 2013).
7. **Black casting:** Craig 11th gives wax not eliminated, mold left too long in the oven, oxidizing flame or no deoxidizer; oxide-black pickles off, carbon-black does not (Craig and Powers, 2002); Singh gives wax residue or overheated investment (Singh, 2020).
8. **Incomplete casting, rounded margins:** poor venting, viscous metal, low pressure, wax residue (Anusavice et al, 2013).
9. **Porosity:** see the table (Anusavice et al, 2013).
10. **Alloy mix-up or mercury contact:** low corrosion resistance and loss of ductility (Anusavice et al, 2013).
11. **Pontic porosity:** heat retained by the bulky pontic in the heat center of the ring (Anusavice et al, 2013).
### 4. Porosity types and causes
| Type | Cause |
|---|---|
| Localized shrinkage | Sprue freezes before the casting is complete (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Microporosity | Too rapid solidification, low mold or casting temperature (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Suck-back | Hot spot where metal impinges on the mold wall (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Pinhole | Dissolved gas expelled on solidification (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Gas inclusion | Larger gas voids, poor torch flame (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Subsurface | Not well understood, grains and gas bubbles nucleate together (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Back pressure | Air cannot escape through investment pores (Anusavice et al, 2013) |

### 5. Where books differ (side by side)
- Suck-back: Phillips, a localized shrinkage type from a hot spot (Anusavice et al, 2013); Bhat, a void opposite the sprue (Bhat et al, 2019); Singh, a sprue attached at a right angle (Singh, 2020); Craig 11th, sprue alloy freezing first, with a pulpal-floor hot spot (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Subsurface porosity: Bhat gives "temperature of the liquid should be higher" (Bhat et al, 2019); Phillips, Sikri and Singh control the rate of metal entry (Anusavice et al, 2013; Sikri, 2020; Singh, 2020); Garg and Garg control the flow (Garg and Garg, 2011).
- Pinhole prevention: Singh says nothing much can be done (Singh, 2020); Bhat says do not hold the melt long, nor too near liquidus (Bhat et al, 2019); McCabe and Walls advise avoiding overheating, or inert gas or vacuum (McCabe and Walls, 2008); Phillips ties pinhole gas to abuse of the metal and gives premelting for larger gas voids (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 6. Conclusion
Phillips groups defects as distortion, surface, incomplete and porosity (Anusavice et al, 2013), while Bhat, McCabe and Walls and Singh group them differently (Bhat et al, 2019; McCabe and Walls, 2008; Singh, 2020). Porosity cannot be prevented entirely but can be minimised by proper technique (Anusavice et al, 2013).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Phillips: four headings (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Bhat: seven classes (Bhat et al, 2019).
- McCabe and Walls: four types (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Nodules come from air bubbles (Anusavice et al, 2013); fins from cracked investment (Singh, 2020; Bhat et al, 2019; McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Porosity is minimised, not prevented entirely (Anusavice et al, 2013).
