## STUDY MODE

### S1. Meaning of the term [definition]
Gallium is a greyish metal of low density (5.9 g/cc) with a melting point of 29.8 C; tin and indium depress the melting point so that it stays liquid at room temperature, and it can be triturated with Ag-Sn-Cu alloy powder like mercury (Bhat et al, 2019). Phillips says the aim was to eliminate mercury from direct metallic restorative materials, and that gallium alloyed with indium and tin is liquid at room temperature (Anusavice et al, 2013). Sikri describes the mercury-free alloy as the answer to the toxic effects and hygiene problems of mercury (Sikri, 2020).

### S2. Composition [composition]
| Book | Powder | Liquid |
|---|---|---|
| Bhat et al, 2019 | Ag 50%, Sn 25%, Cu 15%, Pd 9.0%, trace 0.5% | Ga 65%, In 18.5%, Sn 16%, trace 0.35 |
| Sikri, 2020 | Ag 60%, Sn 25%, Cu 13%, Pd 2.0% | Ga 62%, In 25%, Sn 13%, Bi traces |

### S3. Setting and properties [properties]
Bhat: on trituration CuGa2, PdGa5, Ag9Ga3 and beta-tin form and bind the unreacted particles; compressive strength 343 MPa at 1 hour and 383 MPa at 24 hours; tensile strength 57 MPa; creep 0.17%; setting expansion is more (Bhat et al, 2019). Sikri: strength, dimensional stability and corrosion resistance equal to or greater than silver amalgam; creep as low as 0.09%; sets early, so polishing the same day (Sikri, 2020). Van Noort: properties similar to mercury amalgam (van Noort, 2013).

### S4. Limitations [problems]
Bhat: paste-like and difficult to fill, very dry field needed, lower corrosion resistance, adverse pulp effects, more cytotoxic than traditional alloys (Bhat et al, 2019). Sikri: setting expansion later proved deleterious and fractured weakened cusps in larger restorations; sticky, slow condensation, difficult matrix removal (Sikri, 2020). Van Noort: excessive setting expansion and poor handling (van Noort, 2013). Craig: use not common; much higher corrosion rates than standard amalgams, causing roughness and discoloration; foreign body reaction on implantation (Craig and Powers, 2002); Craig also reports severe reactions to gallium-based alloys in animal implantation studies (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### S5. Gallium in cast alloys [other use]
Gallium is used mainly in Pd-based metal-ceramic alloys, where it strengthens the alloy and decreases the melting range (Anusavice et al, 2013). Craig: not used in pure form; a component of some gold and palladium alloys, and its oxides are important for ceramic bonding (Craig and Powers, 2002).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition
Gallium alloys are mercury-free direct restorative alloys in which liquid gallium (melting point 29.8 C, lowered by tin and indium) replaces mercury and is triturated with silver-tin-copper powder (Bhat et al, 2019). The aim is to remove mercury from direct metallic restorations (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 2. Composition (as printed by two books)
| | Powder | Liquid |
|---|---|---|
| Bhat et al, 2019 | Ag 50, Sn 25, Cu 15, Pd 9.0% | Ga 65, In 18.5, Sn 16% |
| Sikri, 2020 | Ag 60, Sn 25, Cu 13, Pd 2.0% | Ga 62, In 25, Sn 13%, Bi traces |

### 3. Setting and properties
- Phases formed: CuGa2, PdGa5, Ag9Ga3, beta-tin (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Bhat: compressive strength 343 MPa (1 h), 383 MPa (24 h); tensile 57 MPa; creep 0.17% (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Sikri: creep as low as 0.09%; sets early; expands after mixing (Sikri, 2020).
- Van Noort: properties similar to amalgam (van Noort, 2013).
- Phillips: like mercury, gallium can be triturated with alloys for high-copper amalgam (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Craig 11th: these restorations release significant amounts of gallium in vitro, the importance of which is not known (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### 4. Advantages
No mercury hazard and less microleakage (Bhat et al, 2019); early setting, same-day polishing (Sikri, 2020).

### 5. Limitations
- Setting expansion: excessive (van Noort, 2013); fractured weakened cusps in large restorations (Sikri, 2020).
- Handling: sticky, difficult condensation and matrix removal (Sikri, 2020); paste-like, dry field needed (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Corrosion, shown side by side: Sikri says equal to or greater than silver amalgam (Sikri, 2020); Bhat lists lower corrosion resistance, pulp effects and more cytotoxicity (Bhat et al, 2019); Craig reports much higher corrosion rates and roughness, but cell-culture cytotoxicity no greater than high-copper amalgam (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### 6. Gallium in cast alloys
Added to Pd-based metal-ceramic alloys to strengthen and lower the melting range (Anusavice et al, 2013); its oxides help ceramic bonding (Craig and Powers, 2002).

### 7. Conclusion
Gallium alloy was a mercury-free alternative to amalgam, held back by expansion and handling (van Noort, 2013) and, per Craig, higher corrosion rates (Craig and Powers, 2002).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Ga melting point 29.8 C; tin and indium keep it liquid (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Phases CuGa2, PdGa5, Ag9Ga3 (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Main faults: setting expansion and handling (van Noort, 2013).
- Books differ on creep: 0.17% (Bhat et al, 2019), 0.09% (Sikri, 2020).
