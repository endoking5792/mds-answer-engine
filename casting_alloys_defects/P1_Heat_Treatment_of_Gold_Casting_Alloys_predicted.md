## STUDY MODE

### S1. Why gold alloys can be heat treated [principle]
Au-Cu alloys can exist as a disordered solid solution or an ordered structure (Anusavice et al, 2013). Craig: the ordered solution forms on slow cooling and is avoided by rapid cooling; the change is reversible (Craig and Powers, 2002). Rapid cooling gives a softer, weaker alloy with greater elongation (Craig and Powers, 2002). Copper is needed: Types I and II have too little for age hardening (Anusavice et al, 2013); van Noort puts the minimum at 11% copper (van Noort, 2013).

### S2. Softening (solution) treatment [procedure]
Phillips: 10 minutes at 700 C, then quench in water; the intermediate phases become a disordered solid solution, and tensile strength, proportional limit and hardness fall while ductility rises (Anusavice et al, 2013). It is indicated for structures that are ground or reshaped (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### S3. Age hardening [procedure]
Phillips: furnace set between 200 and 450 C, held 15 to 30 minutes, then quenched (Anusavice et al, 2013). It is indicated for partial dentures and other structures needing rigidity and reduces ductility (Anusavice et al, 2013). Ordering raised yield strength by 50% and tensile strength by 25%, with elongation falling from 30% to about 12%, in Craig's typical alloy (Craig and Powers, 2002). Phillips notes the step is not commonly performed because of the extra time (Anusavice et al, 2013).

## EXAM MODE

### 1. Introduction
Gold-copper casting alloys can be softened or hardened by heat treatment because the Au-Cu structure can be disordered or ordered (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 2. Mechanism
- Slow cooling forms the ordered solution: harder, stronger (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Rapid cooling traps the disordered solid solution: softer, more elongation (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Copper must be enough: absent in Types I and II (Anusavice et al, 2013); at least 11% (van Noort, 2013).

### 3. Softening (solution) heat treatment
- Heat the casting 10 minutes at 700 C, quench in water (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Effect: hardness, proportional limit and strength fall; ductility rises (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Use: before grinding, adjusting or reshaping; also before age hardening (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### 4. Age (hardening) heat treatment
- Furnace 200 to 450 C, 15 to 30 minutes, quench (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Ordering in Craig's typical alloy: yield strength up 50%, tensile strength up 25%, elongation 30% to about 12% (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Use: partial dentures, saddles, bridges; reduces ductility (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Overlong treatment makes the casting brittle (Bhat et al, 2019).

### 5. Books compared
| Book | Softening | Hardening |
|---|---|---|
| Anusavice et al, 2013 | 700 C, 10 min | 200 to 450 C, 15 to 30 min |
| van Noort, 2013 | about 700 C | 400 C, about 30 min |
| Bhat et al, 2019 | about 700 C, 10 min | 350 to 400 C, 10 to 20 min |
| McCabe and Walls, 2008 | 700 C, 10 min, if Pt or Pd present | above 450 C, cool to about 200 C |

Ordering temperatures differ by book: Phillips 410, 390 and 385 C (Anusavice et al, 2013); Bhat 410 and 375 C (Bhat et al, 2019); Craig 424 C (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Self-hardening alloys cool slowly on the bench, are not quenched and are less controlled (van Noort, 2013).
- Hardening is hardly ever done for Type 3 (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Follow the maker's instructions (Bhat et al, 2019).

### 7. Conclusion
Quench to soften and adjust; age at a lower temperature to harden (Anusavice et al, 2013).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Softening = solution treatment; hardening = age hardening (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Types I and II do not age harden (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Quench keeps the disordered, soft state (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Overlong treatment embrittles (Bhat et al, 2019).
