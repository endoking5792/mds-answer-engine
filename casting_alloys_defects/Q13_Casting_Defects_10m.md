## STUDY MODE

### S1. Groups [classification]
Phillips classes defects under four headings: distortion, surface roughness and irregularities, porosity, and incomplete or missing detail (Anusavice et al, 2013). Bhat has seven classes, adding discolouration and fins (Bhat et al, 2019). McCabe and Walls have four types: finning and bubbling, incomplete casting, porosity, and oversized or undersized casting (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### S2. Distortion and surface defects [causes]
Marked casting distortion is probably related to wax-pattern distortion (Anusavice et al, 2013). Roughness rises as the liquid/powder ratio rises, and nodules come from air bubbles on the pattern (Anusavice et al, 2013). Fins arise when investment cracks, for example from rapid heating (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### S3. Porosity [causes]
Phillips lists localized shrinkage, microporosity and suck-back as solidification defects, and pinhole, gas inclusions, subsurface and back-pressure porosity as trapped gases (Anusavice et al, 2013). Localized shrinkage follows freezing of the sprue before the casting is complete (Anusavice et al, 2013). Back pressure follows air that cannot escape through the investment (Anusavice et al, 2013). Pinhole porosity follows gas dissolved in the molten metal and expelled on solidification (Anusavice et al, 2013). Singh says nothing much can be done to prevent it (Singh, 2020), Bhat says do not hold the melt long nor too near liquidus (Bhat et al, 2019), and McCabe and Walls advise avoiding overheating (McCabe and Walls, 2008); Phillips gives premelting for larger gas voids (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### S4. Incomplete casting [causes]
Insufficient venting and high viscosity of the metal are the causes Phillips names (Anusavice et al, 2013). Incomplete wax elimination gives rounded, shiny margins (Anusavice et al, 2013).

### S5. Prevention [methods]
Vacuum investing avoids nodules (Anusavice et al, 2013). A flared sprue, a reservoir and a casting temperature lowered by about 30 degrees C reduce shrinkage porosity (Sikri, 2020). Gradual heating over 60 minutes to 700 degrees C avoids fins in gypsum-bonded investment (Sikri, 2020).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 17 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition
Casting defects are flaws in shape, size, surface or internal structure that make a casting misfit or weak (Bhat et al, 2019).

### 2. Classification
| Book | Groups |
|---|---|
| Phillips 12th | Distortion; surface roughness and irregularities; porosity; incomplete or missing detail (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Bhat | Distortion; discolouration; surface roughness and nodules; shrinkage porosities; air inclusion porosities; incomplete castings; fins (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| McCabe and Walls | Finning and bubbling; incomplete casting; porosity; oversized or undersized casting (McCabe and Walls, 2008) |

### 3. Causes and prevention
| Defect | Cause | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Distortion | Wax distortion, uneven investment expansion (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Proper wax handling (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Nodules | Air bubbles on pattern (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Vacuum investing; wetting agent not a certain remedy (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Roughness | High liquid/powder ratio, coarse silica (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Measure water and investment accurately (Anusavice et al, 2013) |
| Fins | Investment cracks (Bhat et al, 2019) | Gradual burnout (Sikri, 2020) |
| Localized shrinkage | Sprue freezes first (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Flared sprue, reservoir (Sikri, 2020) |
| Back pressure | Air cannot escape (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Proper burnout, adequate pressure, investment 6 mm or less (Anusavice et al, 2013); 6 to 8 mm (Singh, 2020) |
| Incomplete casting | Poor venting, viscous metal (Anusavice et al, 2013) | Enough pressure for 4 seconds, alloy above liquidus (Anusavice et al, 2013) |

### 4. Note
Suck-back is classified differently: Phillips calls it localized shrinkage from a hot spot (Anusavice et al, 2013); Craig 11th gives sprue alloy freezing first and a pulpal-floor hot spot (Craig and Powers, 2002).

Pinhole prevention also differs: Singh says nothing much can be done (Singh, 2020); Bhat says do not hold the melt long (Bhat et al, 2019); McCabe and Walls say avoid overheating (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### 5. Conclusion
Phillips attributes defects to carelessness or ignorance of the operator, and says porosity can be minimised but not prevented entirely (Anusavice et al, 2013).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Four Phillips headings (Anusavice et al, 2013).
- Seven Bhat classes (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Nodules mean air bubbles (Anusavice et al, 2013); fins mean cracked investment (Singh, 2020; Bhat et al, 2019; McCabe and Walls, 2008).
