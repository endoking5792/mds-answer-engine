## STUDY MODE

### S1. Margin design: no bevel
GIC is brittle and needs bulk for strength, so Summitt recommends against a cavosurface bevel and uses a 90-degree butt joint about 1 mm deep as a reasonable minimum (Hilton et al, 2013); the margin is as close to 90 degrees as possible and extends only to remove caries and fragile enamel (Hilton et al, 2013). McCabe agrees: knife-edge margins are unsuitable, and a butt joint 0.75 mm or more deep is used (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### S2. Where bevelling is done
Mount bevels the enamel margin only when GIC is cut back for a composite laminate, because the bevel serves the composite (Mount et al, 2016). This does not contradict the rule: the bevel is on enamel for composite, not on GIC.

### S3. Surface treatment
Enamel margins are polished with a 25-micron diamond bur to improve adhesion (Mount et al, 2016). The cavosurface surface is then cleaned with pumice and conditioned with polyacrylic acid before placement (Mount, 1994; Sikri, 2020).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 8 minutes.)*

### 1. Margin design
- **No bevel:** GIC is brittle and needs bulk; edge strength is low (Hilton et al, 2013).
- **Butt joint:** margin near 90 degrees, at least about 0.75-1 mm deep, no knife edge (Hilton et al, 2013; McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- **Extent:** only as far as needed to remove caries and fragile enamel; no sharp internal line angles (Hilton et al, 2013).
- **Retention:** not required, since the cement bonds; grooves only if the lesion is wide (Sikri, 2020).

### 2. Surface treatment
- Polish enamel margins with a fine (25 micron) diamond bur (Mount et al, 2016).
- Clean with plain pumice slurry (Mount, 1994).
- Condition with polyacrylic acid for 10-20 seconds; wash; dry lightly; never acid-etch (Sikri, 2020; Mount et al, 2016).

### 2A. Why these rules
The edge strength of GIC is low, so a thin bevelled edge would chip; bulk at the margin gives strength, and chemical adhesion makes undercuts and bevel-based retention unnecessary (Hilton et al, 2013; Mount et al, 2016). Weakened, unsupported enamel is better supported by GIC than bonded with composite (Mount et al, 2016).

### 3. Exception
With a sandwich restoration, cut the GIC back and bevel the enamel margin for composite (Mount et al, 2016).

### 4. Margin protection
Seal the finished margin at once with resin bonding agent (Sikri, 2020).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- GIC margins: butt joint, no bevel (Hilton et al, 2013).
- Minimum depth about 0.75-1 mm (McCabe and Walls, 2008; Hilton et al, 2013).
- Bevel enamel only for a composite laminate (Mount et al, 2016).
- Pumice, then polyacrylic acid; seal at once (Mount, 1994; Sikri, 2020).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Bevelling the margin of a GIC restoration.
2. Leaving a knife-edge or feather margin of cement.
3. Forgetting that conditioning is polyacrylic acid, not phosphoric acid.
