## 1. Basis: oxidation by free radicals

### Explanation

The mechanism "is considered to be oxidation of organic pigments, although the chemistry is not well understood" (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; "Contemporary bleaching techniques rely on oxidative processes that actively break down large pigmenting molecules" (Setzer, 2021) [SRC]. Grossman's frames it as a redox reaction in which "Free reactive radicals react with the unsaturated bonds" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. "Hydrogen peroxide diffuses through the organic matrix of the enamel and dentin because of its low molecular weight" and reaches the pulp "in a matter of 5 to 15 minutes" (Haywood, da Costa and Berry, 2013) [SRC]. "Ionization of H2O2 produces different types of free radicals" — hydroxyl and perhydroxyl ions (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]; Luk and Anagnostaki describe "the unstable oxygen or perhydroxyl radicals are diffusing through the organic matrix of the enamel and dentine" (Luk and Anagnostaki, 2023) [SRC]. Heat speeds it: "An increase of 10°C doubles the rate of chemical reaction" (Haywood, da Costa and Berry, 2013) [SRC]; alkaline pH favours it — "The optimum pH for hydrogen peroxide ranges from 9.5 to 10.8" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Sodium perborate and carbamide peroxide act through the hydrogen peroxide they release: sodium perborate decomposes "to sodium metaborate, hydrogen peroxide, and nascent oxygen" (Setzer, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Principle:** bleaching is an oxidation-reduction reaction. The bleaching agent (oxidizer) gives up free radicals; the stain molecule (reducer) accepts them (Gopikrishna, 2021).

1. **Penetration:** H2O2 has a low molecular weight, so it diffuses through the organic matrix of enamel and dentin, reaching the pulp in 5-15 min (Haywood, da Costa and Berry, 2013).
2. **Free radical formation:** H2O2 → nascent oxygen (O•), hydroxyl (OH•) and perhydroxyl (HO2•) radicals; perhydroxyl is the most reactive and forms in alkaline pH (optimum 9.5-10.8) (Sikri, 2020; Gopikrishna, 2021). Heat speeds the reaction (doubles per 10°C).

## 2. Chromophore breakdown and the saturation point

### Explanation

"Bleaching agent opens the more highly pigmented carbon ring (yellow color) and converts it to carbon chains"; "When the carbon double bonds are converted to hydroxyl groups, the amount of light absorbed is reduced" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Cohen's: double bonds of high-molecular-weight pigments "are broken down by the chemical reactions with the free radicals", changing the reflected wavelength; afterwards "a shade rebound effect may be observed, resulting from a reformation of double bonds of the molecules" (Setzer, 2021) [SRC]. "Saturation point is that point at which only hydrophilic colorless tooth structure exists"; "Bleaching beyond this point results in the breakdown of the organic matrix resulting in weakening of the enamel and surface porosities" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; "At this point, bleaching should be stopped immediately, or the next step would be a rapid loss of enamel" (Luk and Anagnostaki, 2023) [SRC]. The same authors note the stain-breakdown "hypothesis might be weak since" chromophores in enamel are scarcely detectable and enamel grows more opaque; they propose "the peroxide whitens teeth by oxidizing its transparent organic matrix into an opaque whiter material" (Luk and Anagnostaki, 2023) [SRC]. Ingle's adds "other possible mechanisms include cleansing of tooth surface, temporary dehydration of enamel during the bleaching process" (Rotstein, 2019) [SRC]. The chromophore account is the textbook standard but remains a hypothesis [Likely].

#### ✎ Exam core

3. **Chromophore breakdown:** radicals attack the conjugated double bonds of pigment molecules. Pigmented carbon rings open into chains; double bonds become hydroxyl groups; large molecules become smaller, simpler, lighter ones that absorb less light (Gopikrishna, 2021; Setzer, 2021).
4. **End products:** further oxidation gives CO2 and water.
5. **Saturation point:** only hydrophilic, colourless structures remain; lightening plateaus. Continuing breaks down the enamel organic matrix — porosity, weakness, sensitivity — so stop (Gopikrishna, 2021; Luk and Anagnostaki, 2023).
6. **Rebound:** re-formation of double bonds and rehydration cause slight darkening (Setzer, 2021).

## 3. Carbamide peroxide breakdown

### Explanation

"a solution of 10% carbamide peroxide agent breaks down into 6.5% urea and 3.5% hydrogen peroxide" (Haywood, da Costa and Berry, 2013) [SRC]; Grossman's gives "approximately 3.4% hydrogen peroxide and 6.5% urea" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. "The carbamide peroxide dissociates into H2O2 and urea"; "The urea degrades into ammonia and carbon dioxide" (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]; Luk and Anagnostaki describe "two parts of ammonia and carbon dioxide and one part of hydrogen peroxide" (Luk and Anagnostaki, 2023) [SRC]. Urea raises pH: "Urea is responsible for maintaining an alkaline environment (pH 8) which potentiates the action of hydrogen peroxide" (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Summitt's: above 8 "for a number of hours" (Haywood, da Costa and Berry, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Carbamide peroxide:** CO(NH2)2·H2O2 → urea + H2O2 (about one-third). 10% carbamide peroxide gives about 3.5% H2O2 + 6.5% urea (Haywood, da Costa and Berry, 2013); Grossman's gives 3.4% H2O2. Urea → ammonia + CO2, raising pH to about 8, which potentiates H2O2 (Gopikrishna, 2021). H2O2 → water + oxygen radicals, which bleach as above.

**Limitation:** exact chemistry unknown; dehydration, surface cleansing and oxidation of the enamel matrix to a more opaque material also contribute (Rotstein, 2019; Luk and Anagnostaki, 2023).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Redox: oxidizer = bleach, reducer = stain; low molecular weight H2O2 diffuses in.
- Radicals: O•, OH•, HO2• (perhydroxyl, most reactive, alkaline pH).
- Rings → chains → hydroxyl groups → CO2 + H2O; less light absorbed.
- Saturation point: stop, or the enamel matrix breaks down.
- 10% carbamide peroxide ≈ 3.5% H2O2 + 6.5% urea.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Saying the stain is "dissolved" or "washed out" rather than oxidized.
- Omitting the saturation point.
- Writing that carbamide peroxide bleaches directly; it acts through the hydrogen peroxide it releases.
