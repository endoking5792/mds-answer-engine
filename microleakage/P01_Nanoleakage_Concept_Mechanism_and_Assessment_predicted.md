## STUDY MODE
### S1. Concept
Sano and co-workers found silver inside the hybrid layer with no gap between composite and hybrid layer, and called it nanoleakage "to distinguish it from microleakage through gaps" (Nakabayashi and Pashley, 1998). Sikri defines it as leakage around collagen fibrils in the hybrid layer that resin has not fully infiltrated, seen at the bottom of the hybrid layer and/or along its width depending on the bonding system (Sikri, 2020). Sturdevant places the porosities in the hybrid layer or at the transition to mineralised dentin (Ritter, 2019). Craig stresses that it can occur "even when the overall bond ... is intact" (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

Scale: Phillips gives the nanoleakage gap as less than 0.1 µm in most cases, against microleakage along gaps wider than 1 µm (Anusavice et al, n.d.). Sikri gives the pathways as porosities "less than 50 m"; the unit sign is lost in the extraction and is probably nm [Likely]. Nanoleakage does not occur at enamel, which has no collagen matrix (Anusavice et al, n.d.).

### S2. How it develops
- Incomplete infiltration: acid demineralises a zone that resin does not fully fill, leaving pores (Sikri, 2020). Silver travels along the bottom of the demineralised zone (Nakabayashi and Pashley, 1998).
- Too deep an etch: a long etch leaves an etched but unimpregnated zone that is mechanically weak and promotes nanoleakage (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Sikri says deeper-etching systems show more nanoleakage (Sikri, 2020).
- Hydrophilicity and residual water: two-step etch-and-rinse adhesives are more hydrophilic and permeable than three-step systems, so they take up water and show more nanoleakage; simplified systems (two-step etch-and-rinse, one-step self-etch) are worst (Hilton et al, 2013). Sturdevant describes a spotted pattern (incomplete infiltration) and a reticular pattern or "water trees" (water not removed from the adhesive) (Ritter, 2019).
- Consequence: water-filled voids accelerate bond degradation by slow hydrolysis, so the early seal is not preserved (Sikri, 2020). Craig suspects it contributes to hydrolytic degradation and later microleakage (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Exception: nanoleakage and water treeing are absent in the hybrid layer of transparent (sclerotic) dentin (Sikri, 2020).

### S3. How it is assessed
- Silver tracers: silver nitrate, silver methenamine, ammoniacal silver nitrate; 50% aqueous silver nitrate is commonly used; silver enters the hybrid layer even without a marginal gap (Sikri, 2020). Specimens are immersed in silver nitrate for 24 h, then sectioned (Nakabayashi and Pashley, 1998).
- Microscopy (Sikri, 2020): SEM at 200x or 1000x, with drying and embedding artefacts; TEM resolving below 1.0 nm; FESEM to 1.5 nm, 3 to 6 times better than SEM; CLSEM with rhodamine B, non-destructive and needing no drying; coherent diffraction imaging. Craig defines nanoleakage testing as silver nitrate immersion viewed by TEM (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Summitt shows correlative TEM and FE-SEM of silver grains (Hilton et al, 2013).

### S4. Prevention (brief)
Self-etch adhesives were developed partly to reduce the nanoleakage of etch-and-rinse systems (Sikri, 2020). Solvent-free hydrophobic layers reduce permeability, and 0.2% chlorhexidine for 30 s left only scattered silver after 2 years of storage (Hilton et al, 2013).

## EXAM MODE
**(362 words; about 16 min at 22 words/min)**

**Definition.** Nanoleakage is leakage of tracer or fluid around collagen fibrils within the hybrid layer that resin has not fully infiltrated (Sikri, 2020). Sano et al coined the term on finding silver in the hybrid layer with no gap, to distinguish it from microleakage through gaps (Nakabayashi and Pashley, 1998). It can occur with the overall bond intact (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

**Microleakage vs nanoleakage.**
| | Microleakage | Nanoleakage |
|---|---|---|
| Site | restoration margin | inside hybrid layer |
| Size | gap wider than 1 µm | under 0.1 µm |
| Bacteria | can pass | probably cannot |
| Enamel | occurs | does not occur |
(Anusavice et al, n.d.)

**How it develops.**
1. Incomplete resin infiltration of demineralised dentin leaves pores at the base or across the hybrid layer (Sikri, 2020).
2. Over-etching: a zone deeper than resin can reach stays weak (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
3. Hydrophilic simplified adhesives and residual water raise permeability (Hilton et al, 2013): spotted pattern in the hybrid layer, reticular "water trees" in the adhesive (Ritter, 2019).
4. Water in voids causes slow hydrolysis and bond degradation over time (Sikri, 2020); MMP-driven loss is reduced by chlorhexidine (Hilton et al, 2013).

**Assessment.**
1. Silver tracers: 50% aqueous silver nitrate, silver methenamine, ammoniacal silver nitrate (Sikri, 2020), usually 24 h immersion, then sectioning (Nakabayashi and Pashley, 1998).
2. Microscopy: SEM (drying artefacts), TEM (below 1 nm), FESEM (1.5 nm), CLSEM with rhodamine B (no drying) (Sikri, 2020). Craig uses the term for the silver nitrate/TEM method (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

**Where books differ.** Sikri (2020) says self-etch shows less nanoleakage; Summitt (Hilton et al, 2013) lists one-step self-etch among the worst. Sikri calls it a significant problem; Nakabayashi and Pashley (1998) call its significance unclear, and Craig (Sakaguchi et al, 2019) says it has never been shown clinically.

**Prevention.** Avoid over-etching (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); self-etch adhesives (Sikri, 2020); a hydrophobic coat and chlorhexidine (Hilton et al, 2013).

**Conclusion.** Nanoleakage is a hybrid-layer property, not a marginal gap. It matters for long-term bond durability, which laboratory tracer tests do not measure directly [Likely] (Sakaguchi et al, 2019; Ritter, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Term from Sano et al: silver in hybrid layer with no gap (Nakabayashi and Pashley, 1998).
- Under 0.1 µm vs microleakage over 1 µm; absent in enamel (Anusavice et al, n.d.).
- Causes: incomplete infiltration, over-etching, hydrophilic simplified adhesives, residual water.
- Patterns: spotted (hybrid layer) and reticular "water trees" (adhesive) (Ritter, 2019).
- Test: 50% silver nitrate, then TEM or SEM; CLSEM with rhodamine B needs no drying (Sikri, 2020).
- Effect: slow hydrolysis and loss of bond durability; clinical relevance debated (Sikri, 2020; Sakaguchi et al, 2019; Ritter, 2019).
- Absent in transparent dentin (Sikri, 2020).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Equating nanoleakage with a small marginal gap: it occurs with no gap and the bond intact.
2. Stating flatly that self-etch adhesives are free of, or always lower in, nanoleakage; books disagree.
3. Saying it occurs at enamel.
4. Listing methylene blue as the nanoleakage tracer; the books describe silver tracers.
5. Presenting it as a proven cause of clinical failure.
