## ANSWER

### Definition
Nanoleakage is leakage of tracer or fluid around collagen fibrils within the hybrid layer that resin has not fully infiltrated; it is seen at the bottom of the hybrid layer and/or along its width, depending on the bonding system (Sikri, 2020). Sano and co-workers found silver inside the hybrid layer with no gap between composite and hybrid layer, and called it nanoleakage "to distinguish it from microleakage through gaps" (Nakabayashi and Pashley, 1998). Sturdevant places the porosities in the hybrid layer or at the transition to mineralised dentin (Ritter, 2019). Craig stresses that it can occur "even when the overall bond ... is intact" (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### Scale: microleakage vs nanoleakage
Phillips gives the nanoleakage gap as less than 0.1 µm in most cases, against microleakage along gaps wider than 1 µm (Anusavice et al, n.d.). Sikri gives the pathways as porosities "less than 50 m"; the unit sign is lost in the extraction and is probably nm [Likely]. Nanoleakage does not occur at enamel, which has no collagen matrix (Anusavice et al, n.d.).

| | Microleakage | Nanoleakage |
|---|---|---|
| Site | restoration margin | inside hybrid layer |
| Size | gap wider than 1 µm | under 0.1 µm |
| Bacteria | can pass | probably cannot |
| Enamel | occurs | does not occur |
(Anusavice et al, n.d.)

### How it develops
1. **Incomplete resin infiltration.** Acid demineralises a zone that resin does not fully fill, leaving pores at the base of or across the hybrid layer (Sikri, 2020). Silver travels along the bottom of the demineralised zone (Nakabayashi and Pashley, 1998).
2. **Too deep an etch (over-etching).** A long etch leaves an etched but unimpregnated zone, deeper than resin can reach, that is mechanically weak and promotes nanoleakage (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Sikri says deeper-etching systems show more nanoleakage (Sikri, 2020).
3. **Hydrophilicity and residual water.** Two-step etch-and-rinse adhesives are more hydrophilic and permeable than three-step systems, so they take up water and show more nanoleakage; simplified systems (two-step etch-and-rinse, one-step self-etch) are worst (Hilton et al, 2013). Sturdevant describes two patterns: a spotted pattern in the hybrid layer (incomplete infiltration) and a reticular pattern or "water trees" in the adhesive (water not removed from the adhesive) (Ritter, 2019).
4. **Consequence: hydrolysis.** Water-filled voids accelerate bond degradation by slow hydrolysis over time, so the early seal is not preserved (Sikri, 2020). Craig suspects it contributes to hydrolytic degradation and later microleakage (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). MMP-driven loss is reduced by chlorhexidine (Hilton et al, 2013).
5. **Exception.** Nanoleakage and water treeing are absent in the hybrid layer of transparent (sclerotic) dentin (Sikri, 2020).

### How it is assessed
1. **Silver tracers.** Silver nitrate, silver methenamine and ammoniacal silver nitrate are used; 50% aqueous silver nitrate is commonly used; silver enters the hybrid layer even without a marginal gap (Sikri, 2020). Specimens are usually immersed in silver nitrate for 24 h, then sectioned (Nakabayashi and Pashley, 1998).
2. **Microscopy** (Sikri, 2020):
   - SEM at 200x or 1000x, with drying and embedding artefacts;
   - TEM, resolving details below 1.0 nm;
   - FESEM, resolving to 1.5 nm, 3 to 6 times better than SEM;
   - CLSEM with rhodamine B, non-destructive and needing no drying;
   - coherent diffraction imaging.
3. Craig defines nanoleakage testing as the method of silver nitrate immersion viewed by TEM (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Summitt shows correlative TEM and FE-SEM of silver grains (Hilton et al, 2013).

### Prevention
- Avoid over-etching (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Self-etch adhesives were developed partly to reduce the nanoleakage of etch-and-rinse systems (Sikri, 2020).
- Solvent-free hydrophobic layers reduce permeability, and 0.2% chlorhexidine for 30 s left only scattered silver after 2 years of storage (Hilton et al, 2013).

### Conclusion
Nanoleakage is a hybrid-layer property, not a marginal gap. It matters for long-term bond durability, which laboratory tracer tests do not measure directly [Likely] (Sakaguchi et al, 2019; Ritter, 2019).

## TEXTBOOK DISAGREEMENTS
- **Self-etch vs etch-and-rinse.** Sikri, Textbook of Conservative and Restorative Dentistry, First eBook Edition (2020): self-etch/self-priming systems show less leakage, and nanoleakage is the "main drawback" of etch-and-rinse. Hilton et al, Summitt's Fundamentals of Operative Dentistry, 4th ed (2013): one-step self-etch is among the worst, with two-step etch-and-rinse. Ritter, Sturdevant's Art and Science of Operative Dentistry, 2nd South Asia ed (2019): silver penetrates both types; universal adhesives in self-etch mode show less.
- **Clinical significance.** Sikri, First eBook Edition (2020): a "significant problem even with the newer adhesives". Nakabayashi and Pashley, Hybridization of Dental Hard Tissues, edition not stated (1998): "unclear", spaces too small for bacteria. Sakaguchi et al, Craig's Restorative Dental Materials, 14th ed (2019): never shown to be a clinical problem, yet "suspected" of driving hydrolysis. Ritter, Sturdevant 2nd South Asia ed (2019): relevance and methods debated. Anusavice et al, Phillips' Science of Dental Materials, 12th ed (year not stated, n.d.): reduces bond longevity.
- **Size.** Sikri, First eBook Edition (2020): porosities "less than 50 m" (unit lost). Anusavice et al, Phillips 12th ed (n.d.): less than 0.1 µm.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Term from Sano et al: silver in hybrid layer with no gap (Nakabayashi and Pashley, 1998).
- Under 0.1 µm vs microleakage over 1 µm; absent in enamel (Anusavice et al, n.d.).
- Causes: incomplete infiltration, over-etching, hydrophilic simplified adhesives, residual water.
- Patterns: spotted (hybrid layer) and reticular "water trees" (adhesive) (Ritter, 2019).
- Test: 50% silver nitrate, then TEM or SEM; CLSEM with rhodamine B needs no drying (Sikri, 2020).
- Effect: slow hydrolysis and loss of bond durability; clinical relevance debated (Sikri, 2020; Sakaguchi et al, 2019; Ritter, 2019).
- Absent in transparent dentin (Sikri, 2020).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Equating nanoleakage with a small marginal gap: it occurs with no gap and the bond intact.
2. Stating flatly that self-etch adhesives are free of, or always lower in, nanoleakage; books disagree.
3. Saying it occurs at enamel.
4. Listing methylene blue as the nanoleakage tracer; the books describe silver tracers.
5. Presenting it as a proven cause of clinical failure.
