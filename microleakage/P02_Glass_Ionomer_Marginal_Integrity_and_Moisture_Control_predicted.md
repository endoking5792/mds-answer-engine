## ANSWER

### Definition
Marginal integrity is the continuity of the seal between restoration and cavity wall. Glass-ionomer seals by chemical adhesion: carboxyl groups of the cement bond to calcium ions of the tooth (Sikri, 2020).

### Why glass-ionomer should seal
- **Ion-exchange adhesion.** The carboxyl to calcium bond is chemical (Sikri, 2020). Craig adds that adhesion enhances marginal sealing compared with non-adhesive cements, though it does little for retention (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Mount goes furthest: ion exchange is "the only material" that gives a union "proof against microleakage" (Mount et al, 2016), and the Atlas states there is "no microleakage at the margin" (Mount, 1994).
- **Bonds to a moist surface.** Being hydrophilic, it bonds to a slightly wet surface (Sikri, 2020).
- **Coefficient of thermal expansion close to tooth** (Sikri, 2020).
- **Fluoride release and renewal of broken ionic bonds** (Sikri, 2020).

### Why it can still leak: the moisture problem and other factors
1. **Early water contact.** Matrix-forming ions leach out during the initial set and may interfere at the tooth-restoration interface. Sikri asks for isolation from moisture for at least 30 minutes after placement; solubility in oral fluids remains a problem for 24 hours after insertion (Sikri, 2020). These are two different figures: 30 minutes is isolation, 24 hours is solubility. Sturdevant: GIC lutes must be protected immediately with petroleum jelly or varnish, because they are quite soluble in the first 24 h after mixing (Ritter, 2019).
2. **Desiccation.** Excessive drying crazes and cracks the surface; if the crazing reaches the margin, marginal leakage is considerable (Sikri, 2020). Phillips: the cement stays vulnerable to dehydration until it matures over a few weeks, so a type II restoration is varnished before dismissal (Anusavice et al, n.d.).
3. **Maturation time.** McCabe and Walls say maturation continues for at least an hour, up to 24 hours with some materials, and the surface must be protected for that time (McCabe and Walls, 2008). Mount advises maturation under a waterproof sealant for a minimum of 24 hours (Mount, 1994).
4. **Early hand finishing.** Sharp hand instruments before full set tear the margins, causing ditching (Sikri, 2020). Van Noort says only that hand carving damages marginal integrity, so trim from restoration towards tooth (van Noort and Barbour, 2013).
5. **Low powder:liquid ratio.** This raises solubility (Sikri, 2020).
6. **Long term.** Pickard lists marginal dissolution and shrinkage of GIC as a cause of loss of marginal integrity (Banerjee and Watson, 2011). Goldstein 3rd ed reports laser studies in which GIC and RMGI "failed to prevent microleakage" (Goldstein, 2018).

### The isolation dilemma
McCabe and Walls describe a "dichotomy": rubber dam gives the best moisture control but risks desiccating the cement; cotton rolls or dry guards are usually adequate. Saliva on the cavity leaves a pellicle that interferes with bonding; it is removed with poly(acrylic) acid for 15 seconds (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

### Resin-modified GIC
Rapid light-activated setting reduces the effect of moisture contamination, but RMGI has "occasionally" leaked more than chemically cured GIC. The reasons are resin polymerisation shrinkage, fast setting with no stress relief, and less water and carboxylic acid reducing wetting. Water uptake expansion partly compensates (Sikri, 2020). Phillips states the same mechanism without the "occasionally" qualifier (Anusavice et al, n.d.). McCabe and Walls: the resin protects RMGI from moisture, so varnish or resin coating is not advocated for most products, and RMGI can be finished immediately, whereas conventional GIC finishing is best delayed 24 hours (McCabe and Walls, 2008). Under composite, a light-cured GIC base performs better than a chemically cured one, because it adheres at once and gains early strength (Sikri, 2020). Seltzer and Bender 2nd ed, by contrast, reports a quantitative study ranking RMGI first for preventing bacterial leakage in nonexposed Class V restorations (100%, no leakage) (Hargreaves et al, 2012).

### Surface protection: which agent
Sikri: two coats of varnish or a layer of unfilled resin. Unfilled resin resists water better, yet Sikri still prefers varnish because it adheres closely to the surrounding enamel (Sikri, 2020). McCabe and Walls: unfilled methacrylate resins are "by far the most effective" (McCabe and Walls, 2008). The Atlas shows a varnished GIC dyed at 7 minutes with severe dye penetration, and a resin-sealed one with none (Mount, 1994). For lutes, Sturdevant advises petroleum jelly or varnish (Ritter, 2019).

### Measures to reduce microleakage
- Clean the cavity; condition with polyacrylic, tannic or citric acid (Sikri, 2020); polyacrylic acid for 15 s removes salivary pellicle (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Correct powder:liquid ratio and manipulation (Sikri, 2020).
- Isolation: rubber dam risks desiccation, so cotton rolls usually suffice (McCabe and Walls, 2008); isolate for at least 30 minutes (Sikri, 2020).
- Petroleum jelly during finishing; rotary, not hand, instruments (Sikri, 2020).
- Surface sealing: two coats of varnish or unfilled resin (Sikri, 2020). Unfilled resin is the most effective (McCabe and Walls, 2008); a varnished GIC showed severe dye penetration, a resin-sealed one none (Mount, 1994).
- Delay final finishing of conventional GIC by 24 hours (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Under composite, prefer a light-cured GIC base (Sikri, 2020).

### Conclusion
Glass-ionomer seals well when moisture balance is controlled and the surface is sealed; claims that it never leaks are Mount's position, and the dye images in his Atlas (courtesy of Smales) show leakage under varnish alone (Mount, 1994).

## TEXTBOOK DISAGREEMENTS
- **Protection period.** Sikri, Textbook of Conservative and Restorative Dentistry, first eBook ed (2020): isolate at least 30 min; solubility lasts 24 h. McCabe and Walls, Applied Dental Materials, 9th ed (2008): maturation at least 1 h, up to 24 h. Mount, An Atlas of Glass-Ionomer Cements, 2nd ed (1994): waterproof sealant for at least 24 h. Phillips' Science of Dental Materials, 12th ed (Anusavice et al, n.d.): vulnerable to dehydration for a few weeks. Sturdevant's Art and Science of Operative Dentistry, 2nd South Asia ed (Ritter, 2019): lutes soluble for the first 24 h.
- **Best surface protector.** Sikri (2020): varnish preferred because it adheres to enamel, though unfilled resin resists water better. McCabe and Walls 9th ed (2008): unfilled resin by far the most effective. Mount Atlas 2nd ed (1994): resin-sealed GIC showed no dye, varnished GIC severe dye. Sturdevant 2nd South Asia ed (Ritter, 2019): petroleum jelly or varnish.
- **Does GIC leak?** Mount, Preservation and Restoration of Tooth Structure, 3rd ed (Mount et al, 2016): "proof against microleakage"; Mount Atlas 2nd ed (1994): "no microleakage at the margin". Sikri (2020): minimises leakage, but leaks if crazed. McCabe and Walls 9th ed (2008): not stated as absolute. Pickard's Manual of Operative Dentistry, 9th ed (Banerjee and Watson, 2011): marginal dissolution and shrinkage cause loss of marginal integrity. Goldstein, Esthetics in Dentistry, 3rd ed (2018): GIC and RMGI "failed to prevent microleakage" in laser studies.
- **RMGI versus conventional GIC.** Sikri (2020): RMGI occasionally leaks more. Phillips 12th ed (Anusavice et al, n.d.): RMGI shows more microleakage, without that qualifier. McCabe and Walls 9th ed (2008): RMGI needs no varnish. Seltzer and Bender's Dental Pulp, 2nd ed (Hargreaves et al, 2012): RMGI ranked first, 100% with no bacterial leakage, in nonexposed Class V restorations. Mount (1994; 2016): not addressed in the passages used.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Chemical bond: carboxyl groups to tooth calcium (Sikri, 2020).
- Isolate at least 30 min; solubility for 24 h; two figures, not one (Sikri, 2020).
- Both water contact and desiccation damage the margin (Sikri, 2020; McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Rotary finishing with petroleum jelly; no hand carving (Sikri, 2020; van Noort and Barbour, 2013).
- Sealant: Sikri prefers varnish; McCabe and Walls and Mount favour unfilled resin.
- RMGI can leak more than conventional GIC (Sikri, 2020; Anusavice et al, n.d.) but needs no varnish (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- "Proof against microleakage" is Mount's view, not shared by Pickard or Goldstein.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Writing "keep GIC dry for 24 hours" as Sikri's figure; Sikri says isolate 30 minutes, solubility lasts 24 hours.
2. Treating desiccation as harmless; crazing at the margin causes leakage.
3. Recommending hand instruments for finishing.
4. Stating that GIC "never leaks" as settled fact.
5. Recommending varnish for RMGI routinely.
