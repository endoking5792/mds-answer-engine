## STUDY MODE

### S1. Definition
Sikri defines microleakage as the clinically undetectable passage of bacteria and their products, fluids, molecules or ions along the gaps of the cavity and restoration interface (Sikri, 2020, p.435). The definition is about restorations; it does not mention root fillings. Summitt adds that no material gives a hermetic seal, and that the effects range "from marginal stains to sensitivity and chronic pulpitis" (Hilton et al, 2013).

### S2. Restorative implications
1. **Postoperative sensitivity.** Leakage links oral fluid to the pulp through fluid movement in the tubules; worse in proximal and cervical cavities and with resin (Sikri, 2020, p.435). Ingle adds that leakage around provisional crowns is a common cause of postoperative sensitivity (Ingle's Endodontics, 7th ed, n.d.).
2. **Recurrent caries.** Two separate numbers: bacteria under 1.0 µm across pass easily, and a gap over 10 µm gives room for nutrients (Sikri, 2020, p.435).
3. **Pulpal pathology.** Bacterial products pass through the tubules to the pulp; bacterial leakage, not material toxicity, is the greater pulpal threat (Sikri, 2020, p.435). Mount et al (2016) call microleakage the principal reason for pulpal inflammation after restoration.
4. **Marginal discoloration** that is difficult to remove by conventional means, often needing replacement (Sikri, 2020, p.436).
5. **Breakdown of luting cements** (Sikri, 2020, p.436).

### S3. Endodontic (coronal) implications
Cohen states coronal leakage "has been proposed" to cause failure, on in vitro evidence, and that leakage can occur quickly through well-obturated canals (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021). Gutmann gives reinfection of the apical tissues in as little as 19 days, with relative certainty within 3 months (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011). Hence the coronal seal matters as much as the apical seal [Likely].

### S4. Differences between textbooks
| Point | Side A | Side B |
|---|---|---|
| Weight of coronal leakage | Gutmann and Lovdahl, 5th ed (2011): reinfection in 19 days, near certain in 3 months | Cohen 12th ed (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021): significance "challenged"; poor root filling and poor restoration have similar odds; Torabinejad et al, 6th ed (2021): "clinically less significant" than lab studies suggest |
| Main cause of post-treatment disease | Gutmann and Lovdahl (2011): coronal leakage reinfects | Siqueira et al, Treatment of Endodontic Infections 2nd ed (2022): persistent infection is the most common cause |
| Retreatment after exposure | Torabinejad et al (2021): one guideline 2 to 3 months, others 2 to 3 weeks | Cohen 12th ed (2021): no clear evidence to retreat solely because a temporary was in place over 3 months |

## EXAM MODE
**(177 words; about 8 min at 22 words/min)**

**Definition.** Microleakage is the clinically undetectable passage of bacteria, their products, fluids, molecules or ions along gaps at the cavity and restoration interface (Sikri, 2020).

**Restorative implications (Sikri, 2020)**
1. Postoperative sensitivity from fluid movement in dentinal tubules; worse in proximal and cervical cavities.
2. Recurrent caries: bacteria smaller than 1 µm enter, and gaps over 10 µm hold nutrients.
3. Pulpal inflammation: bacterial leakage, more than material toxicity, injures the pulp (also Mount et al, 2016).
4. Marginal discoloration that is difficult to remove by conventional means, often needing replacement.
5. Breakdown of luting cements.

**Endodontic (coronal) implications**
1. Coronal leakage can reinfect a root-filled canal in as little as 19 days (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).
2. Leakage occurs even through well-obturated canals, so the coronal seal is as important as the apical seal (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021).
3. The evidence is mainly in vitro. Cohen calls its clinical significance challenged (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021); Torabinejad et al (2021) call it less significant clinically.

**Prevention.** Prompt permanent restoration after root canal treatment (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Definition: clinically undetectable passage at the cavity and restoration interface (Sikri, 2020).
- Five restorative effects: sensitivity, recurrent caries, pulpal disease, discoloration, luting-cement breakdown (Sikri, 2020).
- Bacteria under 1 µm; gap over 10 µm houses nutrients: two figures, not one (Sikri, 2020).
- Coronal reinfection in as little as 19 days (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).
- Clinical weight of coronal leakage is disputed (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021; Torabinejad et al, 2021).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Merging Sikri's two numbers into "bacteria pass 10 µm gaps".
2. Extending Sikri's definition to root fillings; it is written for restorations.
3. Stating coronal leakage as a proven cause of endodontic failure; Cohen calls it proposed, on in vitro evidence.
4. Listing causes of leakage instead of its consequences.
