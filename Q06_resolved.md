## STUDY MODE

### S1. How to read "1971 to 2006" [orientation]
The window opens at the discovery and closes just before nano-ionomers (2007) and the modern bioactive era. A good answer tells the story as a chain: a problem, a fix, a new problem. Four eras fit: (1) origins and launch, (2) refinement of the chemistry, (3) modification with resin and metal, (4) high-viscosity and minimal-intervention use.

### S2. Origins [basic science]
Silicate cement was tooth-coloured and released fluoride, but dissolved quickly (Hilton et al, 2013). Zinc polycarboxylate (1960s) was the first cement for which an inherent adhesion to tooth could be shown, but its tensile strength was low (McCabe and Walls, 2008; Mount, 1994). Replacing zinc oxide with the ion-leachable glass of silicate cement gave GIC (McCabe and Walls, 2008). The invention by Wilson and Kent is dated 1972 by Mount et al (2016), Powers and Wataha (2013), van Noort and Barbour (2013), Banerjee and Watson (2015) and Sikri (2020) Ch.20, 1971 by Sikri (2020) Ch.1, and 1969 by Hill (2007); Craig's 14th edition says "the 1960s" in one place and "the 1970s" in another (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). The first commercial product, ASPA, followed in 1976 (van Noort and Barbour, 2013; Mount et al, 2016). Sturdevant 2nd South Asia ed says Wilson and Kent introduced glass ionomers "in the early 1970s" (Ritter et al, 2019), and Goldstein 3rd ed says these materials "first appeared in the early 1970s" (Goldstein et al, 2018). Sturdevant 2nd South Asia ed also calls GIC "hybrids of silicate and polycarboxylate cements" (Ritter et al, 2019); it does not use the name ASPA or the "more than 30 years" statement of the 5th ed, which are not covered in the 2nd South Asia edition.

### S3. 1972-1976: first products and clinical use
The cement was first reported by Wilson and Kent in 1972 and recommended for cervical abrasion lesions, though the early versions lacked aesthetics and translucency (Mount et al, 2016); its first commercial product, ASPA (aluminosilicate polyacrylate), was made available in 1976 (van Noort and Barbour, 2013); first clinical use was reported in 1975 (Bhat et al, 2019); original auto-cure materials were introduced in 1976 (Mount et al, 2016), and Marzouk describes ASPA as the newest tooth-coloured restoration, introduced in the mid-seventies (Marzouk et al, undated). Early restorations often failed because they were left unsealed, so they took up water or dried and cracked (Mount et al, 2016).

### S4. Late 1970s to 1980s: refining the chemistry
- Gelling of the 50% polyacrylic acid liquid was reduced or eliminated by using copolymers (maleic and acrylic acid in McCabe and Walls, 2008; itaconic and maleic acid in Mount, 1994).
- Tartaric acid sharpened the set and allowed lower-fluoride, more translucent glasses (Mount, 1994).
- Water-hardening cements, with freeze-dried polyacid in the powder, appeared in 1981 (van Noort and Barbour, 2013).
- Strength was pursued with metals: the amalgam-alloy admix ("miracle mix") is credited to Simmons in 1983 (Sikri, 2020), "early 1980s" in Sturdevant 5th ed (Roberson et al, 2006) and in Sturdevant 2nd South Asia ed (Ritter et al, 2019), and glass cermet to McLean and Gasser in 1985 (Sikri, 2020; Bhat et al, 2019) or 1987 (Garg and Garg, 2011, who also give sintering at 800 C). Garg and Garg (2011) describe miracle mix as silver alloy powder blended with glass ionomer powder 1:6 (Garg and Garg, 2011). Mount et al (2016) caution that alloy admixes improve abrasion resistance and radiopacity but not other physical properties much, and that the metal technologies are no longer as popular. Bonsor and Pearson (2013) class miracle mix as an admix (alloy added to the powder), distinct from a cermet (silver sintered onto the glass), whereas Sikri calls it a silver cermet.
- The Type I luting, II restorative and III lining and base classification used by Mount is adapted from Wilson and McLean (1988) (Mount, 1994).

### S5. Late 1980s to 1990s: resin modification and high viscosity [classification]
- **Resin-modified GIC** arose in the 1980s, adding water-soluble polymer or polymerisable resin (HEMA) so the cement set by light and resisted water early (Hill, 2007); the first resin-modified GIC, Vitrebond (3M), is dated 1989 in Sikri Ch.1 (Sikri, 2020); Craig dates the invention of RMGIC to the late 1980s (Sakaguchi et al, 2019) and Powers to the early 1990s (Powers and Wataha, 2013).
- **Tri-cure** added a chemical dark cure (Bhat et al, 2019).
- **High-viscosity GIC** (Fuji VIII and IX) arrived in the 1990s, developed mainly for ART (Bhat et al, 2019); Sikri Ch.1 dates it 1995 (Sikri, 2020). Sturdevant 5th ed dates the ART filling material from about 1990 (Roberson et al, 2006); Sturdevant 2nd South Asia ed also says "since 1990" but states the technique is not recommended in routine clinical use and is restricted to areas with limited access to dental treatment (Ritter et al, 2019). Van Noort reports promising results in primary teeth (van Noort and Barbour, 2013).
- **Compomer** (polyacid-modified composite) is resin-based, not a true GIC (Powers and Wataha, 2013; Mount et al, 2016). Its date varies: Dyract (Dentsply), the first compomer, 1993 (Sikri, 2020); introduced in 1995 (Powers and Wataha, 2013); "the late 1990s" (Hill, 2007).

### S6. 2000-2006: consolidation
Sikri's chronology gives the dated events (Sikri, 2020): a fast-setting GIC from 3M ESPE in 2000; giomers from Shofu in 2002; fibre-reinforced GIC (Lohbauer et al) and CPP-ACP-added GIC (Mazzaoui et al) in 2003; titanium tetrafluoride (Pamir et al) and iron oxide (Hurrell-Gillingham et al) additions in 2005; hydroxyapatite and hydroxyapatite/zirconia (Gu et al) and boric acid (Prentice et al) additions in 2006. These were laboratory modifications; Mount et al describe antibacterial and remineralising additive experiments, undated, with equivocal results (Mount et al, 2016). Pegoraro et al (2007), citing the proceedings of the 2006 European glass-ionomer conference, anticipate several further improvements. Nano-ionomers followed in 2007, just outside the window (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### S7. The lesson of the timeline
Each stage fixed one weakness but never all: handling and strength improved, yet brittleness and water sensitivity remained, which is why newer research tries to raise fracture resistance without losing ion exchange (Mount et al, 2016).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 34 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Glass ionomer cement (glass polyalkenoate cement) arose in the early 1970s as a hybrid of silicate and zinc polycarboxylate cements (McCabe and Walls, 2008) and has been refined by more than 30 years of research (Sturdevant 5th ed, Roberson et al, 2006; not covered in the 2nd South Asia ed). Its history is a series of fixes to handling, strength and aesthetics.

### 2. Background: before 1971
| Cement | Gift | Flaw |
|---|---|---|
| Silicate | fluoride release, tooth-coloured (Hilton et al, 2013) | high solubility, poor strength |
| Zinc polycarboxylate (1960s) | first inherent adhesion to tooth (McCabe and Walls, 2008; Mount, 1994) | low tensile strength, opaque (McCabe and Walls, 2008) |
| Glass ionomer | adhesion plus fluoride plus translucency (van Noort and Barbour, 2013) | brittle, water sensitive |

### 3. Discovery and launch (1969-1976)
- Wilson and Kent: 1972 (Mount et al, 2016; Sikri, 2020 Ch.20), early 1970s (Sturdevant 2nd South Asia ed, Ritter et al, 2019), 1971 (Sikri, 2020 Ch.1), 1969 (Hill, 2007); Craig says "the 1960s" in one place (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- First commercial product ASPA, 1976 (van Noort and Barbour, 2013; Mount et al, 2016); first clinical use reported 1975 (Bhat et al, 2019).
- First use: cervical abrasion lesions; poor aesthetics (Mount et al, 2016).
- Early failure: unsealed cement took up water or dried out and cracked (Mount et al, 2016).

### 3A. The first product and its names
- **First commercial material:** ASPA, aluminosilicate polyacrylic acid, a hybrid of silicate powder and polycarboxylate liquid; early materials were very technique sensitive (Sturdevant 5th ed, Roberson et al, 2006).
- **Fuji ASPA (as reported by Bhat):** polyacrylic acid liquid with a citric acid conditioner, powders in three shades and two colour pigments (Bhat et al, 2019).
- **Alternative names:** ASPA cement, glass polyalkenoate, glass ionomer cement, Ionofill, Chemfill (Bhat et al, 2019).
- **Why it spread:** it released fluoride like silicate cement, and adhesion to enamel and dentine made it replace silicates quickly after 1976 (Mount et al, 2016).

### 3B. The fluoride story behind the interest
In the 1950s silicate cement was noticed to release fluoride, and in 1957 fluoride from silicate cements was shown to reduce adjacent enamel solubility (Hilton et al, 2013; Mount et al, 2016). This earlier finding is why a fluoride-releasing, adhesive successor attracted so much interest.

### 4. Refinement of chemistry (late 1970s to 1980s)
- **Copolymers:** itaconic and maleic acid reduce gelling of the liquid (McCabe and Walls, 2008; Mount, 1994).
- **Tartaric acid:** sharper set, lower-fluoride, translucent glass (Mount, 1994).
- **Water-hardening cement:** 1981 (van Noort and Barbour, 2013).
- **Metal-reinforced:** miracle mix (Simmons, 1983) and glass cermet (McLean and Gasser, 1985, Sikri, 2020; 1987, Garg and Garg, 2011); the gain in physical properties was small (Mount et al, 2016).
- **Classification:** Wilson and McLean (1988), adapted by Mount (Mount, 1994).

### 5. Resin modification (late 1980s to 1990s)
- **RMGIC:** HEMA added to the liquid; light set first, early water resistance, longer working time with a quick true set (Hill, 2007; Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- **First product:** Vitrebond (3M), 1989 (Sikri, 2020).
- **Tri-cure:** adds redox dark cure for bulk placement (Bhat et al, 2019).
- **Date disagreement:** late 1980s (Craig) against early 1990s (Powers) (Sakaguchi et al, 2019; Powers and Wataha, 2013).

### 6. High-viscosity and minimal intervention (1990s)
- **High-viscosity GIC** (Fuji VIII, IX): packable, quicker to finish, better wear resistance; mainly for ART (Bhat et al, 2019) and primary teeth (van Noort and Barbour, 2013); 1995 in Sikri Ch.1 (Sikri, 2020).
- **Compomer:** resin-based, no ion-exchange bond (Powers and Wataha, 2013; Mount et al, 2016); Dyract 1993 (Sikri, 2020), 1995 (Powers and Wataha, 2013), late 1990s (Hill, 2007).
- **GIC liners and bases:** until the 1990s zinc oxide-eugenol, calcium hydroxide and zinc phosphate were the materials of choice; then polycarboxylate and glass-ionomer cements became more dominant (Roulet et al, 2001).

### 7. 2000-2006
- Dated events (Sikri, 2020): fast-setting GIC 2000; giomers 2002; fibre-reinforced and CPP-ACP-added GIC 2003; titanium tetrafluoride and iron oxide 2005; hydroxyapatite/zirconia and boric acid 2006.
- Antibacterial (chlorhexidine, chitosan) and remineralising (CPP-ACP) additives: undated experiments, equivocal results (Mount et al, 2016); releasing other ions is an ongoing aim (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Pegoraro et al (2007), citing the 2006 European conference, anticipate further improvements.
- Nano-ionomer followed in 2007 (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).

### 8. Uses that grew over the period
Restoration of cervical lesions, luting, lining and base, fissure sealant, ART and primary teeth, and later bone replacement in surgery (Mount et al, 2016; Mount, 1994).

### 9A. Summary table: what each stage gave and what it left unsolved
| Stage | Gain | Still unsolved |
|---|---|---|
| ASPA (1972-76) | adhesion plus fluoride, translucent (van Noort and Barbour, 2013) | unsealed water uptake and cracking (Mount et al, 2016) |
| Copolymers, tartaric acid (late 1970s) | stable liquid, sharper set, translucency (Mount, 1994) | brittleness |
| Water-hardening (1981) | polyacid kept dry in the powder, so no liquid gelling (McCabe and Walls, 2008) | moisture sensitivity (van Noort and Barbour, 2013) |
| Metal-reinforced (1983-85) | abrasion resistance, radiopacity (Mount et al, 2016) | colour, weak interface (Sikri, 2020); other properties little improved (Mount et al, 2016) |
| RMGIC (1989; late 1980s-1990s) | early water resistance, command set (Hill, 2007) | HEMA, shrinkage, water uptake (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013) |
| High-viscosity (1990s; 1995) | packable, ART, wear resistance (Bhat et al, 2019; Sikri, 2020) | strength little changed (van Noort and Barbour, 2013) |
| Additives (2000-06) | antibacterial, remineralising and other modifications (Sikri, 2020; Mount et al, 2016) | equivocal results (Mount et al, 2016) |

### 9. Conclusion
Over 1971-2006 GIC moved from a brittle, unsealed, opaque filling to a family of luting, restorative, resin-modified and high-viscosity materials. Brittleness and water sensitivity remained the unsolved problems (Mount et al, 2016).

### 10. Points an examiner expects in this answer
- State the parentage (silicate plus polycarboxylate) and the name ASPA before any dates (Sturdevant 5th ed, Roberson et al, 2006).
- Give each date with the development it belongs to, not as a bare list.
- Separate true glass ionomers (conventional, metal-reinforced, resin-modified, high-viscosity) from resin-based look-alikes such as compomer (Mount et al, 2016).
- Close by naming the unsolved problems: brittleness, water sensitivity and the debated fluoride benefit (Mount et al, 2016; Sikri, 2020).
