## STUDY MODE

### Principles
- All sealers are toxic when freshly mixed; toxicity is greatly reduced on setting (Berman et al, 2021; Gatewood, 2007; Gopikrishna, 2021: "all root canal cements are irritating in their freshly mixed state, but on setting, most become relatively inert").
- Cell-culture systems are one cell type, not homeostatic and lack lymphatics and defence cells; the human body has polymorphonuclear leukocytes, plasma cells and macrophages, so in vitro results overstate clinical harm; ZOE is the example (Berman et al, 2021).
- No sealer is universally nontoxic; investigators report contradictory findings; clinical and in vitro data often disagree (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- Sealers resorb, but not all extruded sealer is predictably removed; breakdown products may impair periradicular cell proliferation, specifically in the presence of an apical lesion; sealer should not be routinely placed in the periradicular tissue (Berman et al, 2021). Extruded material can reduce healing probability by as much as 25% (Gopikrishna, 2021).

### Evidence by class
**ZOE.** Free eugenol contributes most to cytotoxicity of the fresh material (Gatewood, 2007); ZOE shows mild cytotoxicity of little clinical significance (Sikri, 2019); irritating because of eugenol (Gopikrishna, 2021); resorbs if extruded (Berman et al, 2021). Shrinkage and solubility lead to leaching through the foramen (Azim and Aksel, 2022).
**Epoxy resin.** Significant toxicity unset; at 24 h among the lowest (Gatewood, 2007). AH-26 released formaldehyde; AH Plus does not (Berman et al, 2021). AH-26 more cytotoxic and genotoxic than Topseal and AH Plus in vitro (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). Epoxy sealers are more compatible than ZOE (Gopikrishna, 2021); overfilling with AH-26 may cause paresthesia (Sikri, 2019).
**Polyketone and polyvinyl (Diaket).** Elevated cellular toxicity (Gatewood, 2007); inflammatory reaction at the periapex (Sikri, 2019).
**Calcium hydroxide.** Least toxic eluates in one cell-culture study (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010); calcium-containing sealers less toxic than lead- or magnesium-containing ones (N2, RCB) (Sikri, 2019).
**Glass ionomer.** Good biocompatibility (Gatewood, 2007).
**Methacrylate resin.** Higher cytotoxicity and inflammatory response than AH Plus or calcium hydroxide sealers owing to resin monomers (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Elution compounds toxic in vitro; Epiphany more cytotoxic than Sealapex at 1 h but less at 24 h; primate studies: EndoREZ mean inflammation 1.5 at 26 days and 0.8 at 90 days; guinea-pig data severe for EndoREZ (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). Unreacted monomer and the oxygen-inhibited layer are toxic; conversion is at best under 70% (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). **TEGDMA:** present in EndoREZ (Berman et al, 2021; Jain, 2016); TEGDMA and HEMA cause gene mutations and DNA damage in vitro (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010), whose statement that neither EndoREZ nor Epiphany contains these components is contradicted by the textbook formulations.
**Silicone.** RoekoSeal less toxic than Epiphany; GuttaFlow less toxic than AH Plus, Epiphany and Resilon at 72 h (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010); biocompatible (Berman et al, 2021).
**Bioceramic.** Grossman's 14th ed.: most biocompatible (Gopikrishna, 2021); calcium silicate biocompatibility and mineralisation superior to epoxy (Azim and Aksel, 2022). **However** EndoSequence BC sealer was more cytotoxic than AH Plus, taking 5 weeks versus 3 weeks to become non-cytotoxic (Berman et al, 2021). MTA Fillapex reported cytotoxic and genotoxic across several cell-culture studies and severe subcutaneous inflammation at 90 days (Torabinejad, MTA, Ch.10); its resinous components, mainly salicylate resin and silica, decrease cell viability (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Systematic review: tricalcium silicate materials have similar biological properties to MTA, with low cytotoxicity (Berman et al, 2021). Even large extrusion into large lesions gave 96.8% healing in one series (calcium_silicate Q2).
**Medicated sealers.** Paraformaldehyde sealers (N2, Endomethasone, SPAD, Riebler's paste): severe, permanent periradicular toxicity; neurotoxic; osteomyelitis and paresthesia after overextension; N2 declared "unacceptable" by the ADA in 1974 (Berman et al, 2021; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Azim and Aksel, 2022). Iodoform-containing sealers evoke cytotoxic responses (Sikri, 2019).

### Testing and interpretation
Cell culture (osteoblasts, periodontal ligament cells), genotoxicity, implantation and animal root-filling histology. Periapical response may be inflammation, encapsulation, no reaction or resorption; healing within 30-60 days after a mild reaction counts as biocompatible (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). Sample preparation (oxygen-inhibited layer) confounds results (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim — "cytotoxicity" rankings are method-dependent and textbooks disagree on bioceramic sealers.** Grossman's and Azim and Aksel rank calcium silicate sealers as most biocompatible, while Cohen's cites EndoSequence BC as more cytotoxic than AH Plus, and MTA Fillapex is cytotoxic and genotoxic (Gopikrishna, 2021; Azim and Aksel, 2022; Berman et al, 2021; Torabinejad, MTA, Ch.10). INTERPRETATION: biological behaviour differs between products within a class; avoid extrusion whichever sealer is used. LIMITATION: few comparative clinical data; in vitro models lack host defences; the TEGDMA statement in one review is contradicted by textbook formulations. [Likely]

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 863; about 39 min at 22 words/min)**

### Introduction
Cytotoxicity matters because sealer contacts periradicular tissue through the foramen and accessory canals and is often extruded. All sealers are toxic when freshly mixed and much less when set, but tests and textbooks differ on the ranking of individual sealers.

### General principles
- Toxicity is greatest when the sealer is unset and diminishes on setting.
- Cell-culture systems use one cell type, lack lymphatics and defence cells and are not homeostatic; they overstate clinical harm (ZOE is the example).
- Investigators report contradictory results; clinical and in vitro data often disagree.
- Sealers resorb but extruded sealer is not predictably removed; breakdown products may impair proliferation of periradicular cells, so sealer should not be routinely placed beyond the apex.

### Evaluation methods
Cell culture (fibroblasts, osteoblasts, periodontal ligament cells), genotoxicity assays, implantation and animal root-filling histology. A periapical reaction may be inflammation, encapsulation, no reaction or resorption; healing within 30-60 days after mild irritation counts as biocompatible. Oxygen-inhibited layers and sample preparation confound results.

### Cytotoxicity by class
| Class | Findings |
|---|---|
| **ZOE** | Free eugenol drives the toxicity of the fresh material; marked in culture but little clinical significance; resorbs; Common Complications states eugenol's neurotoxic effect persists after setting |
| **Epoxy resin** | Toxic unset; among the lowest at 24 h; AH-26 released formaldehyde and was more cytotoxic and genotoxic than AH Plus; findings contradictory (some aged specimens promoted proliferation); overfill with AH-26 may cause paresthesia |
| **Polyvinyl (Diaket)** | Inflammatory reaction at the periapex |
| **Calcium hydroxide** | Least toxic eluates in one study; calcium-containing sealers less toxic than lead- or magnesium-containing ones |
| **Glass ionomer** | Good biocompatibility |
| **Methacrylate** | Monomer-related toxicity; Epiphany more cytotoxic than Sealapex at 1 h but less at 24 h; unreacted monomer and the oxygen-inhibited layer are toxic; newer resin sealers showed toxicity that did not fall with time; EndoREZ contains TEGDMA |
| **Silicone** | RoekoSeal and GuttaFlow less toxic than Epiphany and AH Plus |
| **Calcium silicate** | Grossman's: most biocompatible; Cohen's: EndoSequence BC took 5 weeks to become non-cytotoxic against 3 for AH Plus; MTA Fillapex cytotoxic and genotoxic; tricalcium silicate materials resemble MTA with low cytotoxicity |
| **Paraformaldehyde** | N2, Endomethasone: severe, permanent toxicity, neurotoxicity, osteomyelitis; N2 classed "unacceptable" by the ADA in 1974 |

### Selected experimental findings
- Primate studies of EndoREZ gave mean inflammation scores of 1.5 at 26 days and 0.8 at 90 days, whereas guinea-pig implantation showed a severe reaction [LIT: Pameijer and Zmener, 2010].
- Conversion of methacrylate resin sealers is at best below 70%, leaving unreacted monomer; the oxygen-inhibited layer at the surface is toxic and can be removed by a final EDTA rinse [LIT].
- TEGDMA and HEMA caused gene mutations and DNA damage in vitro [LIT]; EndoREZ contains triethyleneglycol dimethacrylate, so the review's statement that EndoREZ and Epiphany contain neither is contradicted by Cohen's and Jain.
- Calcium silicate sealers: biocompatibility and mineralisation were superior to epoxy in some studies; MTA Fillapex reduced cell viability through its resin and salicylate components and caused severe subcutaneous inflammation at 90 days.

### Extruded sealer: what the evidence says
Extruded sealer is common (about 50%) and, in the studies cited by Azim and Aksel, did not alter outcome whatever its type, though some remained at four years. Problem Solving in Endodontics warns that encapsulated sealer can prevent complete healing even when the tooth is symptom-free; Grossman's gives a possible 25% reduction in healing; Harty's calls the evidence equivocal. Lead and mercury in N2 spread to distant organs; paraformaldehyde causes irreversible dysesthesia when forced through the foramen.

### Factors modifying toxicity
Setting state and time; degree of conversion of resins; solubility and leaching; extrusion volume; presence of infection; host defences.

### Mechanisms of toxicity
Free **eugenol** and zinc from ZOE; **formaldehyde** liberated on setting by AH-26 and by paraformaldehyde sealers; **unreacted resin monomers** (UDMA, TEGDMA, HEMA) and the oxygen-inhibited layer in methacrylate sealers; **lead and mercury** and **paraformaldehyde** in N2; the **resin and salicylate components** of MTA Fillapex; and the **solubility and leaching** of sealers that shrink or dissolve. Radiopacifier particles are insoluble and may remain in tissue, engulfed by macrophages or enclosed by fibrous capsules.

### Why rankings differ
Reported rankings depend on the test system, the sealer's age, the cell type and the exposure time. Examples from the textbooks: epoxy resin is described as the least toxic of all sealers at 24 h (Applied Dental Materials) and as more compatible than ZOE; calcium silicate sealers are called the most biocompatible (Grossman's) yet EndoSequence BC took longer than AH Plus to become non-cytotoxic (Cohen's); methacrylate sealers are more cytotoxic than AH Plus (Azim and Aksel). Marked toxicity in culture does not predict clinical failure, as ZOE shows, and a favourable culture result does not guarantee periapical healing, which also depends on infection control.

### Clinical implications
Avoid extrusion; use thin films and a small volume; remove EDTA and use a final rinse compatible with resin sealers; never use paraformaldehyde sealers. The effect of extruded material on healing is disputed: as much as 25% reduction (Grossman's), equivocal (Harty's), about 50% extrusion with no outcome effect and 96% repair (Azim and Aksel).

### Conclusion
No sealer is entirely non-toxic. Toxicity falls with setting, in vitro results exaggerate clinical harm and rankings differ between books, so sealer choice should rest on clinical behaviour and extrusion control.

## SECOND AUDIT PASS (2026-10-16)
Additional textbooks read after the first pass; additions and conflicts only (nothing above was removed):
- **Harty's:** all ZOE cements are cytotoxic and the cellular response may last longer than with other materials, but this is usually not apparent clinically; most extruded sealer is absorbed with time; there is "equivocal evidence" that extrusion reduces success. **This conflicts in strength with Grossman's 14e "as much as 25%" reduction**; record both.
- **Problem Solving in Endodontics** (Ch.12): extruded sealer may remain encapsulated, and apparent radiographic healing may hide a persistent chronic inflammatory reaction around irritating sealer.
- **Common Complications:** ZOE eugenol toxicity "persistent even after setting"; epoxy findings are contradictory (toxic before setting and decreasing vs no cytotoxicity and proliferation on aged specimens); newer resin sealers showed toxic effects that did not decrease with time; silicone sealers slightly cytotoxic fresh and aged; calcium silicate sealers cytocompatible.
- **Endodontic Prognosis:** once the microbial aetiology is accepted and tissue reactions overcome initial toxicity, some irritation does not impair clinical performance; focus has shifted to leakage and adhesion.
- **Principles and Practice (2002):** all commonly used sealers show some toxicity, greatest unset, diminishing with setting and time.
- **Applied Dental Materials:** ZOE is cytotoxic and mutagenic in high doses without clinical significance; epoxy least toxic of all sealers at 24 h.
