## 2. Textbook contradictions kept side by side — DECIDED (Niranjan, 2026-10-16)
| Point | Source A | Source B | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| ZOE sealer formula | 42/27/15/15/1 (Cohen's, Grossman's, Gatewood) | 40/30/15/15/1 (Sikri) | **Prefer A** for the exam line — independently confirmed by three sources (Grossman's own book, Cohen's, Gatewood); Sikri's figure is a lone variant. Keep both printed, A as primary. |
| Methacrylate generations | Four (Cohen's, Jain) | Five (Sikri) | **Prefer A, with composition-level evidence, not just authority.** Cohen's 12e (2021) states explicitly "four generations... have been marketed," cited; Jain agrees. Sikri's "fifth" (ADSeal, Dia-ProSeal) is called "an improved version of the fourth generation technology" by Sikri itself — and checking Sikri's own composition table (verified 2026-10-16), Paste A of both ADSeal and Dia-ProSeal is listed as **epoxy resin**, not a methacrylate at all. Sikri appears to have misclassified two epoxy-resin products under its methacrylate section. Updated in Q04. |
| Bioceramic retreatability | "No worse than epoxy" (Cohen's) | 20% patency (Jain); "most difficult" (Sikri) | **Keep both** — genuine, live clinical controversy; no textbook basis to prefer one. Exam line: state retreatability of bioceramic sealers is debated, give both views. |
| Bioceramic biocompatibility | "Most biocompatible" (Grossman's) | BC sealer 5 vs 3 weeks to non-cytotoxic (Cohen's); MTA Fillapex cytotoxic, genotoxic | **Not a true conflict — clarify.** MTA Fillapex contains a salicylate resin component (it is not a pure calcium-silicate sealer), which is the likely reason for its poor biocompatibility; this is compatible with calcium-silicate sealers as a class being low-cytotoxicity. State all three findings together with this distinction rather than picking a side. |
| Bioceramic set time | 165 min initial / under 6 h final (Cohen's, verified in text); 3-4 h (Common Complications); 3.5 h CeraSeal | 12-24 h final set (Azim and Aksel, verified in text: "CSBSs... complete their final setting after 12 to 24 hours") | **Keep both — genuinely irreconcilable.** Both are specific, well-cited, current (2021/2022) textbook claims about the same material class's final set time that flatly disagree. No responsible way to pick one; flag as an open contradiction, possibly reflecting different sealer brands in each book's underlying studies. |
| Toxicity on setting | Falls on setting (most texts) | Eugenol effect persists; new resin sealers' toxicity does not fall (Common Complications) | **Not a true conflict.** General rule plus a stated exception, not disagreement: toxicity generally falls after setting, except eugenol-releasing (ZOE) and some resin sealers. Write as rule-plus-exception. |
| Extruded sealer and healing | Up to 25% lower healing probability (Grossman's) | "Equivocal evidence" (Harty's); Azim and Aksel: 96% repair, ~50% extrusion without effect | **Keep all three — this is what "equivocal" means.** Different studies, different findings; present as the genuine spread of evidence (already done reasonably in the Critical Evidence Synthesis sections of obturation Q15/Q17). |
| Epiphany self-cure | 15-30 min (Gatewood) | About 45 min (Jain) | **Keep both** — a journal review versus a textbook, no primary manufacturer spec sheet available to adjudicate; product self-cure time is also sensitive to temperature/humidity. |
| Resilon fracture resistance | Superior (Teixeira, in Ingle's; Azim and Aksel) | Stiffness too low to reinforce (Ingle's, same book) | **Keep both, lean toward B as the more current view.** Teixeira's reinforcement claim is the older, promotional-era finding; the modulus/stiffness argument is the more mechanistic and matches the post-2014 reassessment that followed Resilon's withdrawal (its monoblock/reinforcement claims were not borne out clinically). State both, flag B as better supported. |
| Resilon composition | Polycaprolactone (Grossman's own composition list; confirmed by Azim and Aksel) | "Polyurethane" (Grossman's own introductory line, same chapter) | **RESOLVED — verified directly in `mds-textbooks` (Grossman's 14e Ch.16, lines 285-290).** This is Grossman's own internal inconsistency, not a cross-textbook disagreement: the intro line says "a high-performance polyurethane," the composition section two lines later says "a polycaprolactone core material." Polycaprolactone is correct (matches Azim and Aksel and the literature); "polyurethane" is Grossman's own error. Q7 already uses polycaprolactone and flags the intro line as the error — confirmed correct. |
| Root reinforcement by sealer | BC sealer raised fracture force (Harty's) | No strengthening with methacrylate sealers (Azim and Aksel) | **Not a conflict — different sealer classes.** These describe bioceramic and methacrylate sealers respectively; state both as separate, compatible findings about different materials. |
| Primary monoblock example | MTA apexification; GP without sealer (Jain) | GP without sealer; Hydron alone (Sikri) | **Not a conflict — complementary examples.** Both list "GP without sealer" as a shared example; the second example differs because both are valid separate illustrations of the same concept (one homogeneous material, one interface). Keep both as additional examples, not competing claims. |
