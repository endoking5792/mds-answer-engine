## STUDY MODE

### Why resin sealers
Gutta-percha does not bond to dentin or to ZOE or epoxy sealers (AH-26, AH Plus); an ideal sealer should bond to dentin and to the core and so prevent microleakage (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). Hydrophilic methacrylate resins form a hybrid layer and resin tags, borrowing from adhesive restorative dentistry.

### Types
**Epoxy-resin sealers** (AH-26, AH Plus, Topseal, Diaket as polyketone class): good handling, adhesion to dentin, toxic when unset; do not bond to gutta-percha (Gatewood, 2007; Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). AH-26 released formaldehyde on setting and was replaced by AH Plus (Berman et al, 2021, via digest).
**Methacrylate-based resin sealers (MBRS):** four marketed generations; first (Hydron) obsolete (Berman et al, 2021, via digest). The two dominant systems (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010):
| | EndoREZ | RealSeal (Resilon/Epiphany) |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Hydrophilic, two-component, dual-cure, self-priming sealer | Dual-cure resin sealer with self-etch primer and Resilon core |
| Chemistry | Bismuth radiopacifier, diurethane dimethacrylate, triethylene glycol dimethacrylate, peroxide and photo-initiator | UDMA, PEGDMA, EBPADMA, BisGMA, barium borosilicate, calcium hydroxide; core polycaprolactone with bioactive glass |
| Core | Gutta-percha or resin-coated gutta-percha (RCGP) cones | Resilon cones or pellets |
| Weakness | Does not bond to plain gutta-percha | Core-sealer coupling questioned; roughness raises bond strength |

### Bonding conditions
Smear-layer removal with 17-19% EDTA or dilute citric acid (preserves collagen); NaOCl followed by 17% EDTA or 50% citric acid best for tag penetration; leave dentin slightly moist (at least 3 mm moisture on the last paper point; low vacuum 1-2 s); over-drying produces poor tags, excess water traps droplets; flush EDTA after NaOCl, because residual oxygen forms an oxygen-inhibited layer; push-out bond strength 1.33 MPa when NaOCl was the last rinse versus about 8 MPa after EDTA (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

### Advantages
Hybrid layer and tags (EndoREZ tags 400-1000 μm); low viscosity and good flow; automix delivery; monoblock potential; leakage lower in several studies; clinical success: EndoREZ 91.03% at 2 years and 86.3% cumulative at 5 years, Resilon-Epiphany 91.6% at 2 years and 93.1% at 4 years (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

### Limitations
Technique-sensitive moisture control; polymerisation shrinkage and incomplete conversion (below 70%); possible monomer toxicity (TEGDMA, HEMA); no proven root reinforcement (Grande et al); contradictory leakage data; hydrolysis concerns for Resilon polymer; long-term data limited (Gatewood, 2007; Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). HEMA extruded beyond the apex may sensitise (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

### Retreatment
Epiphany/Resilon was removed faster and with less residual material than gutta-percha/AH Plus with K3 files or ProFile with heat and chloroform in straight canals (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

### Comparison with traditional sealers (ZOE, calcium hydroxide, glass ionomer)
Traditional sealers seal by adaptation and rely on a solid core without bonding, show shrinkage and solubility (ZOE, calcium hydroxide), and are generally easier to remove; resin sealers bond to dentin, are less soluble, but are more technique- and moisture-sensitive (Gatewood, 2007; Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). No class has randomised-trial proof of superiority (Berman et al, 2021, via digest).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim — resin sealers deliver a better seal in principle and in many laboratory studies, but the clinical benefit over conventional sealers is not demonstrated.** Leakage results are contradictory and depend on whether canals were dried (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010); clinical success rates of 86-94% are similar to conventional sealers (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010); root reinforcement was not shown (Grande et al). INTERPRETATION: choose resin sealers when moisture can be managed and the benefit of bonding is wanted, not as a proven superior class. LIMITATION: the review is authored by the developers of the EndoREZ studies; independent long-term data are limited. [Likely]

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 760; about 35 min)**

### Introduction
Resin-based sealers were developed because gutta-percha does not bond to dentin or to conventional sealers and microleakage follows. They include epoxy resins and the newer methacrylate-based resin sealers.

### Classification
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Epoxy resin | AH-26, AH Plus, Topseal |
| Methacrylate-based (MBRS) | EndoREZ, RealSeal (Epiphany/Resilon), MetaSEAL (4-META) |

### Composition and mechanism
Dual-cure dimethacrylates (UDMA, BisGMA, TEGDMA, PEGDMA) with fillers (bismuth, barium); self-etch primer or self-priming; hydrophilic; bond by hybrid layer and resin tags in smear-layer-free, slightly moist dentin; with resin-coated cones or a Resilon core they aim at a monoblock.

### Technique
17% EDTA final rinse after NaOCl; remove excess with paper points leaving slightly moist dentin; apply primer (RealSeal) or sealer with auto-mix tip; place cones; cure coronal 2 mm (40 s) and allow self-cure.

### Advantages
Bonding and hybrid layer; low viscosity and good adaptation; lower leakage in several studies; success about 86-94% at 2-5 years; Resilon removable with chloroform.

### Limitations
Moisture sensitivity; polymerisation shrinkage and incomplete conversion; monomer toxicity; no root reinforcement; contradictory evidence; expensive.

### Comparison with traditional sealers
| Feature | Traditional (ZOE, Ca(OH)2, GIC) | Resin |
|---|---|---|
| Bond | Adaptation; GIC chemical | Hybrid layer; tags |
| Solubility/shrinkage | ZOE soluble, shrinks | Low solubility; polymerisation shrinkage |
| Moisture | Dry canal | Slightly moist |
| Technique | Simple | Sensitive |
| Biocompatibility | Long record | Early irritation, then healing |
| Retreatment | Solvents | Chloroform, heat; moderate |

### Conclusion
Resin sealers bring adhesion to obturation, but benefits depend on moisture control and are not proven clinically superior; they remain a choice, not a replacement.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Gutta-percha does not bond to AH-26, AH Plus, ZOE.
- EndoREZ bonds to dentin not to plain GP: use RCGP.
- 17% EDTA final rinse; slightly moist dentin; oxygen-inhibited layer.
- Success 91.03% (2 y), 86.3% (5 y) EndoREZ; 91.6% (2 y) Resilon.
- No root reinforcement (Grande).
