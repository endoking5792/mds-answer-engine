## STUDY MODE

### Why resin sealers
Gutta-percha does not bond to dentin, nor to ZOE or epoxy sealers (AH-26, AH Plus); an ideal sealer should bond to both dentin and core and so prevent microleakage (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). Hydrophilic methacrylate resins form a hybrid layer and tags, borrowing from adhesive restorative dentistry.

### Types
**Epoxy-resin sealers** (AH-26, AH Plus, Topseal): good handling, adhesion to the organic phase of dentin (probably collagen), dimensional stability, antibacterial properties; do not bond to gutta-percha; toxic when unset (Azim and Aksel, 2022; Gatewood, 2007). AH-26 released formaldehyde while setting and was reformulated as AH Plus (Weine; Berman et al, 2021). Epoxy sealers are recommended with warm vertical obturation to offset sealer shrinkage and minimise voids (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Diaket is polyvinyl resin/polyketone (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Methacrylate-based resin sealers (MBRS).** Generations: **four** in Cohen's and Jain (Hydron, obsolete; EndoREZ; Epiphany/Resilon with self-etch primer; MetaSEAL/RealSeal SE self-adhesive) (Berman et al, 2021; Jain, 2016), **five** in Sikri, who adds epoxy-based ADSeal and Dia-ProSeal as the fifth (Sikri, 2019) — record both. The two dominant systems (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010):
| | EndoREZ | RealSeal/Epiphany with Resilon |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Hydrophilic, two-component, dual-cure, self-priming | Dual-cure sealer with self-etch primer and Resilon core |
| Chemistry | Bismuth radiopacifier, UDMA, triethylene glycol dimethacrylate (TEGDMA), peroxide and photoinitiator; Cohen's and Jain agree | UDMA, PEGDMA, EBPADMA, BisGMA, barium borosilicate, calcium hydroxide; Resilon core polycaprolactone with bioactive glass, bismuth oxychloride, barium sulfate (Azim and Aksel, 2022) |
| Core | Gutta-percha or resin-coated gutta-percha (RCGP) | Resilon cones or pellets |
| Weakness | Does not bond to plain gutta-percha | Polycaprolactone degrades enzymatically, creating a sealer-core gap (Azim and Aksel, 2022); Resilon withdrawn about 2014 |
**MetaSEAL** (4-META, self-adhesive) is third-generation in Cohen's.

### Bonding conditions
Smear-layer removal with 17-19% EDTA or dilute citric acid; leave dentin slightly moist (low vacuum 5 s then paper point 1 s improved adhesion of AH Plus, Epiphany, iRoot SP and MTA Fillapex; flooding harmed it) (Berman et al, 2021); over-drying gives poor tags; flush EDTA after NaOCl, because residual oxygen leaves an oxygen-inhibited layer; push-out bond strength 1.33 MPa when NaOCl was the last rinse versus about 8 MPa after EDTA (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

### Advantages
Hybrid layer and tags (EndoREZ tags 400-1000 μm); low viscosity and good flow; automix delivery; monoblock potential; lower leakage in several studies (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). Clinical: EndoREZ 91.03% at 2 years and 86.3% cumulative at 5 years, Resilon-Epiphany 91.6% at 2 years and 93.1% at 4 years (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010); EndoREZ with gutta-percha 92.1% cumulative success at 10 years in a retrospective study without a control (Berman et al, 2021).

### Limitations
Technique-sensitive moisture control; polymerisation shrinkage: resin-based sealers shrink about 7.81% on setting, and the **C-factor** in a canal is about 1,000 versus 5 for a Class I cavity, so the bond cannot resist the shrinkage stress (Azim and Aksel, 2022); conversion below 70%; monomer toxicity; methacrylate sealers showed **no superior sealing or dentin strengthening** over conventional sealers and higher cytotoxicity and inflammation than AH Plus or a calcium hydroxide sealer (Azim and Aksel, 2022); contradictory leakage data; HEMA extruded beyond apex may sensitise (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

### Retreatment
Epiphany/Resilon was removed faster, with less residue, than gutta-percha/AH Plus in straight canals (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). Resin sealers' retreatability otherwise moderate; epoxy removable with solvent and heat but harder than ZOE.

### Comparison with traditional sealers (ZOE, calcium hydroxide, glass ionomer)
Traditional sealers seal by adaptation without bonding, with solubility and shrinkage (ZOE, calcium hydroxide), and are generally easier to remove; resin sealers bond to dentin, are less soluble, but are more technique- and moisture-sensitive. No class has randomised-trial proof of superiority (Berman et al, 2021).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim — resin sealers bond better in principle and in many laboratory studies, but clinical benefit over conventional sealers is not demonstrated.** Laboratory leakage data conflict and depend on canal moisture (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010); clinical success 86-94% is similar to conventional sealers; Azim and Aksel (2022) report no superior sealing or strengthening and the C-factor explains why bonded interfaces in canals fail. INTERPRETATION: choose resin sealers when moisture can be managed, not as a proven superior class. LIMITATION: the Pameijer and Zmener review is by the EndoREZ developers; the 10-year EndoREZ study had no control. [Likely]

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 669; about 30 min at 22 words/min)**

### Introduction
Gutta-percha does not bond to dentin, nor to zinc oxide-eugenol or epoxy sealers, so a bonded sealer was sought to prevent microleakage. Hydrophilic methacrylate sealers borrow from adhesive restorative dentistry to form a hybrid layer and resin tags. Epoxy resin sealers (AH Plus) are also resin-based but bond only to dentin.

### Types
**Epoxy resin sealers** (AH-26, AH Plus, Topseal): good handling, adhesion to dentin (probably its collagen), low solubility, dimensional stability and antibacterial properties. AH-26 released formaldehyde on setting and was reformulated as AH Plus. AH Plus is a two-paste epoxide-amine system with a working time of about 4 h. They do not bond to gutta-percha and are toxic when unset.

**Methacrylate resin sealers (MBRS):**
| Generation (Cohen's, four) | Example | Features |
|---|---|---|
| First | Hydron (poly-HEMA) | Mid-1970s, polymerised in situ without a core; obsolete in the 1980s |
| Second | EndoREZ | Non-etching, hydrophilic, dual-cure (diurethane dimethacrylate with triethyleneglycol dimethacrylate); resin tags; used with resin-coated gutta-percha |
| Third | Epiphany / RealSeal with Resilon | Self-etching primer plus dual-cure resin sealer; primer incorporates the smear layer |
| Fourth | MetaSEAL, RealSeal SE | Self-adhesive; no separate primer |
Sikri counts five generations by adding ADSeal and Dia-ProSeal (epoxy-based) as the fifth.

### Bonding conditions
Remove the smear layer with 17-19% EDTA or dilute citric acid. Leave the dentin slightly moist (low vacuum for about 5 s then a paper point for about 1 s); over-drying gives poor tags and flooding traps water. Flush EDTA after NaOCl because residual oxygen forms an oxygen-inhibited layer; push-out strength of EndoREZ was about 1.33 MPa after a final NaOCl rinse against about 8 MPa after EDTA [LIT: Pameijer and Zmener, 2010].

### Clinical technique
1. Final irrigation: NaOCl for disinfection, then 17% EDTA for one minute to remove the smear layer; flush so that no NaOCl or hydrogen peroxide remains.
2. Remove excess fluid with a low-vacuum tip and paper points, leaving the dentin slightly moist.
3. Apply the primer (Epiphany) or use a self-priming or self-adhesive sealer (EndoREZ, MetaSEAL), mixed through an auto-mix tip.
4. Seat the core (resin-coated gutta-percha or Resilon) to working length.
5. Light-cure the coronal 2 mm for about 40 s and allow the rest to self-cure; the dual-cure sealers set slowly in the canal.
6. Restore promptly.

### Advantages
Hybrid layer and resin tags (EndoREZ tags 400-1000 μm [LIT]); low viscosity and good flow; auto-mix delivery; monoblock potential; lower leakage in several laboratory studies. Clinical success: EndoREZ 91.03% at 2 years and 86.3% at 5 years, Resilon-Epiphany 91.6% at 2 and 93.1% at 4 years [LIT]; EndoREZ with gutta-percha 92.1% cumulative success at 10 years in an uncontrolled study (Cohen's).

### Limitations
- **Technique sensitivity:** moisture control and smear layer removal.
- **Polymerisation shrinkage:** resin-based sealers shrink about 7.81%, and the C-factor in a canal is about 1,000 against 5 in a Class I cavity, so the bond cannot resist shrinkage stress.
- **Incomplete conversion** below 70% and monomer release.
- **Cytotoxicity:** methacrylate sealers showed higher cytotoxicity and inflammatory response than AH Plus or a calcium hydroxide sealer; their toxicity did not decrease with time in several studies.
- **No proven superiority:** no better sealing or root strengthening than conventional sealers.
- **Resilon core:** degraded enzymatically, creating a core-sealer gap; withdrawn 2014.

### Comparison with traditional sealers
| Feature | Traditional (ZOE, Ca(OH)2, GIC) | Resin |
|---|---|---|
| Bond | Adaptation; GIC chemical | Hybrid layer and tags |
| Solubility and shrinkage | ZOE soluble and shrinks | Low solubility; polymerisation shrinkage |
| Moisture | Dry canal | Slightly moist |
| Technique | Simple | Sensitive |
| Biocompatibility | Long record | Higher cytotoxicity |
| Retreatment | Solvents | Moderate; Resilon removed with chloroform |

### Retreatment and evidence
Epiphany/Resilon was removed faster and with less residue than gutta-percha with AH Plus in straight canals in one study; epoxy sealers are removable with chloroform and heat, but less easily than zinc oxide-eugenol. Clinically, resin sealers perform no better than conventional sealers: older sealer classes give the same outcome, no randomised trial shows a class superior, and the Pameijer and Zmener review comes from the EndoREZ developers.

### Conclusion
Resin sealers add adhesion to obturation, but benefit depends on moisture control, shrinkage is a problem, and clinical superiority is unproven.

## SECOND AUDIT PASS (2026-10-16)
Additional textbooks read after the first pass; additions and conflicts only (nothing above was removed):
- **Common Complications:** for the newer resin sealers designed to bond dentin, several studies reported toxic effects that did not decrease with time; epoxy resin sealers showed no bioactive or osteogenic properties but higher dentin bond strength, low solubility and adequate dimensional stability.
- **Applied Dental Materials:** AH Plus sets by addition polymerisation (no residual monomer), working time about 4 h, soluble in chloroform; some patients are allergic to epoxy resin.
- **Endodontic Prognosis:** composite (resin) sealers show the same clinical outcome as ZOE and epoxy.
- **Harty's:** a separate adhesive layer may improve the bond strength of UDMA sealers; interfacial biofilm susceptibility of EndoREZ depends on the etching system; the final rinse can positively influence adhesion.
