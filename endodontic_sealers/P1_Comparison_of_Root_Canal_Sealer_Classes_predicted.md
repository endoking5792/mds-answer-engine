### Important caveat
No randomised-trial evidence shows one class superior (Berman et al, 2021, via digest). Leakage follows the sealer more than core or technique (Chugal and Lin, via digest). Greater radiopacity is not better sealing.

### Practical selection
Routine lateral compaction, retreatment likely: ZOE or epoxy. Hydraulic single cone, open apex or extrusion-prone: bioceramic. Bonded monoblock wanted and moisture controllable: resin. Glass ionomer where chemical bonding and fluoride release are wanted and retreatment is unlikely.

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 300)**

### Introduction
Six classes of sealers are in use; each trades sealing, biocompatibility and retreatability.

### Comparison
| Feature | ZOE | Ca(OH)2 | GIC | Epoxy | Resin | Bioceramic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bond | None | None | Chemical | Dentin only | Hybrid layer | Chemical |
| Solubility | High | High | Low | Low | Low | Low |
| Antibacterial | Yes | Limited | Limited | Little | Little | Alkaline |
| Toxicity | Fresh high | Low | Low | Fresh high | Mild | Low |
| Moisture | Dry | Dry | Condition | Dry | Slightly moist | Moist |
| Retreatment | Easy | Easy | Hard | Moderate | Moderate | Debated (hard) |

### Selection
ZOE/epoxy routine; bioceramic hydraulic and open apex; resin for bonded monoblock; GIC niche.

### Conclusion
No class is proven superior; choose by moisture control, extrusion risk and need for retreatment.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- No RCT superiority; sealer matters more than core.
- Retreatment hardest: GIC; bioceramic debated across textbooks.
- Moisture: resin slightly moist; ZOE dry; bioceramic uses it.

## SECOND AUDIT PASS (2026-10-16)
Additional textbooks read after the first pass; additions and conflicts only (nothing above was removed):
- **Endodontic Prognosis:** ZOE, epoxy, calcium hydroxide-salicylate, silicone and composite sealers show the same clinical outcome; bioceramic sealers lack sufficient clinical data. This should sit under the "Evidence" row of the matrix.
- **Harty's:** only limited research suggests selection influences outcome; sealer extrusion needs minimising; MTA-based sealers set slowly.
- **Applied Dental Materials:** no evidence calcium hydroxide sealers confer any advantage.
