## STUDY MODE

### Smear layer
A 1-2 μm layer of organic and inorganic debris and possibly bacteria formed by instrumentation; its removal may allow better sealer adaptation and adhesion (Gatewood, 2007). Resin sealers need it removed to form a hybrid layer and tags (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

### Irrigant sequence
- Final EDTA 17-19% or dilute citric acid preserves dentin collagen (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- 5% NaOCl followed by 17% EDTA or 50% citric acid is the most effective for resin-tag penetration (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- NaOCl and hydrogen peroxide leave oxygen that inhibits polymerisation and lower bond strength; chlorhexidine did not (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- Push-out bond strength of EndoREZ: 1.33 MPa when NaOCl was the last rinse (oxygen-inhibited layer present), about 8 MPa after NaOCl then EDTA (with or without a final distilled-water rinse); the oxygen-inhibited layer disappeared with EDTA as the last rinse (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- EDTAC (with cetrimide) improves dentin wettability and sealer adhesion when used as final irrigant (Guruswamy Pandian et al, 2026).

### Dentin moisture
- Hydrophilic resin sealers need moist dentin for tags and a hybrid layer; thorough drying creates a hydrophobic environment and poor penetration; too much water entraps droplets and disrupts bonding (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- Zmener's study: blot drying and low-vacuum 1-2 s drying left canals slightly moist and leaked least; ethanol drying and flooded canals leaked more; the last paper point should show at least 3 mm moisture (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- Calcium silicate sealers use residual tubular moisture to set (calcium_silicate Q2).
- Cohen's protocol: low vacuum 5 s then a paper point for 1 s left the canal slightly moist and gave the best adhesion of AH Plus, Epiphany, iRoot SP and MTA Fillapex, whereas flooded canals harmed adhesion (Berman et al, 2021). This differs from the 1-2 s low-vacuum timing in Pameijer and Zmener; both recorded.
- ZOE and epoxy sealers are traditionally used in dried canals [Likely].

### Clinical protocol (resin sealer)
NaOCl during shaping → 17% EDTA 1 min → flush with saline or 2% chlorhexidine → remove excess with a low-vacuum luer tip (1-2 s) and paper points, leaving dentin slightly moist → sealer.

### Why it matters
Explains contradictory leakage studies: many canals were dried thoroughly before testing hydrophilic sealers (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 280)**

### Smear layer
1-2 μm debris layer; removal permits sealer penetration and adhesion.

### Irrigation
| Step | Purpose |
|---|---|
| NaOCl | Disinfection, tissue dissolution |
| 17% EDTA 1 min | Remove smear layer, preserve collagen |
| Saline or 2% chlorhexidine | Flush; avoid residual oxygen |
| Not NaOCl or H2O2 as last rinse | Oxygen inhibits polymerisation |

### Moisture
Resin sealers: slightly moist dentin (low-vacuum 1-2 s; paper point with 3 mm moisture); too dry: no tags; too wet: water droplets. Bioceramic uses tubular moisture.

### Bond strength
EndoREZ push-out 1.33 MPa (NaOCl last) versus about 8 MPa (EDTA last).

### Conclusion
Sealer performance depends on the irrigation sequence and dentin moisture as much as on the product.

Additional textbooks read after the first pass; additions and conflicts only (nothing above was removed):
- **Harty's:** smear-layer removal is desirable for adaptation and to remove bacteria, but it is questioned because open tubules may allow diffusion of irritant filling materials and escape or proliferation of bacteria; penetration of sealer into tubules shows a lack of correlation with seal in non-bonded fillings. - **Harty's:** the final rinse can have a positive influence on adhesion of resin sealers; GuttaFlow requires all irrigant traces removed with water or isopropyl alcohol before use.
