## STUDY MODE

### Why sealers matter
Gutta-percha has no adhesive qualities and cannot seal the canal alone; a sealer fills the space between canal wall and core, fills voids, lateral and accessory canals and the interstices between cones, and lubricates cone placement (Berman et al, 2021, via digest; Gatewood, 2007). The sealer has "as much or more importance than the core material" for clinical outcome (Gatewood, 2007), and leakage and bacterial penetration along a root filling appear to depend mainly on the sealer rather than the core or placement method (Chugal and Lin, via digest).

### Ideal requirements (Grossman)
Tacky, adheres to canal wall and core; hermetic seal; radiopaque; no setting shrinkage; no tooth staining; bacteriostatic (or at least not bacteria-promoting); slow set; insoluble in tissue fluids; tissue tolerant; soluble in a common solvent for retreatment (Gatewood, 2007, Table 1; Berman et al, 2021, via digest). No sealer meets all of them. Greater radiopacity is not better sealing: very radiopaque sealers can mask voids (Berman et al, 2021, via digest).

### Classification (Gatewood, 2007; Berman et al, 2021, via digest)
Zinc oxide-eugenol (ZOE); polymers (epoxy resin, polyketone); calcium hydroxide-based; glass ionomer; methacrylate-resin based; and the newer calcium silicate (bioceramic) sealers. Berman et al count five major classes in current use and state that no randomised-trial evidence shows the superiority of one over another (via digest).

### Composition, properties and biocompatibility by class
| Class | Composition and setting | Properties | Biocompatibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ZOE** (Pulp Canal Sealer, Tubli-Seal; Grossman's formula 1958) | Powder: zinc oxide 42%, Staybelite resin 27%, bismuth subcarbonate 15%, barium sulfate 15%, sodium borate 1%, mixed with eugenol. Sets by zinc eugenolate crystals embedded with zinc oxide (Gatewood, 2007) | Longest clinical track record; ample working time, sets faster at body temperature and humidity; shrinks, soluble over time, may stain; resorbs if extruded (Gatewood, 2007; Berman et al, 2021, via digest) | Free eugenol drives cytotoxicity of the unset material; marked in cell culture but clinically safe record (cell cultures lack defence mechanisms) (Gatewood, 2007; Berman et al, 2021, via digest) |
| **Calcium hydroxide** (Sealapex, Apexit) | Ca(OH)2 in a salicylate/ZOE or epoxy base | Needs to dissolve to release ions, which conflicts with long-term insolubility; short-term seal adequate, long-term stability questioned (Gatewood, 2007) | Intended osteogenic and cementogenic effect; no objective data for the biological effect of paste (Gatewood, 2007); promotes osteogenesis and cementogenesis at the apical barrier but limited antibacterial action against *E. faecalis* (Guruswamy Pandian et al, 2026) |
| **Polymer: epoxy resin** (AH-26, AH Plus) | Epoxy-amine resin | Good handling and adhesion to dentin (Gatewood, 2007); AH-26 replaced by AH Plus after formaldehyde release on setting was identified (Berman et al, 2021, via digest) | Significant toxicity when unset, among the lowest of sealers at 24 h (Gatewood, 2007) |
| **Polymer: polyketone** (Diaket) | Resin-reinforced zinc oxide-diketone chelate | Tacky, good dentin adhesion, difficult handling, low solubility (Gatewood, 2007) | Elevated cellular toxicity (Gatewood, 2007) |
| **Glass ionomer** (Ketac-Endo) | Glass-ionomer cement | Chemical bond to dentin; viscous, short working time; hard and insoluble in GP solvents so difficult retreatment (Gatewood, 2007); hard to condition apically and in the middle third (Berman et al, 2021, via digest) | Good biocompatibility (Gatewood, 2007) |
| **Methacrylate resin** (EndoREZ, RealSeal/Epiphany) | Dual-cure dimethacrylates with fillers; primer; resin-coated cones | Hybrid layer and tags; hydrophilic; needs moist dentin (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010) | Mildly irritating early then healing; conflicting in vitro results (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010) |
| **Calcium silicate / bioceramic** (EndoSequence BC, iRoot SP, BioRoot RCS) | Tricalcium and dicalcium silicate, zirconia or tantalum oxide radiopacifier; sets hydraulically using tubular moisture; pH about 12 (calcium_silicate Q2) | Under 0.1% expansion; long set (about 165 min initial); chemical bond; retreatment difficult (patency regained in 20% in one study) (calcium_silicate Q2) | Alkalinity bactericidal; osteoconductive; little inflammation if extruded; MTA Fillapex differs and showed genotoxicity (calcium_silicate Q2) |

### Fresh comparison of the classes (what the examiner wants in one view)
- **Seal:** methacrylate resin and bioceramic bond to dentin; ZOE and epoxy seal by adaptation; glass ionomer bonds chemically.
- **Solubility and shrinkage:** worst for ZOE and calcium hydroxide sealers; set bioceramic and epoxy sealers are best (bioceramics expand slightly).
- **Antimicrobial effect:** ZOE (eugenol, zinc), bioceramic (alkalinity) and calcium hydroxide (limited); resin sealers little.
- **Retreatment:** ZOE and epoxy removable with solvents or heat; glass ionomer and bioceramic hardest; Resilon system removed faster than GP/AH Plus in straight canals (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- **Moisture:** resin sealers need slightly moist dentin; bioceramic sealers use tubular moisture; dry canal harms both (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010; calcium_silicate Q2).

### Biocompatibility principles
All sealers are cytotoxic when freshly mixed and much less once set, so extrusion should be avoided whichever is used (Berman et al, 2021, via digest). In vitro and in vivo results often disagree and none of the sealers is totally non-irritating, yet all are used with clinical success; biocompatibility is judged by whether healing follows an initial mild reaction within 30-60 days (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim — sealer chemistry matters more than core material or technique, but no class is proven clinically superior.** Leakage appears determined mainly by the sealer (Chugal and Lin, via digest), yet no randomised trial shows superiority of one class (Berman et al, 2021, via digest); in vitro cytotoxicity and leakage rankings contradict each other and correlate poorly with clinical outcome (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). INTERPRETATION: choose by case (retreatment likelihood, moisture control, extrusion risk) rather than by laboratory ranking. LIMITATION: laboratory leakage and cytotoxicity data predominate; long-term clinical data exist mainly for ZOE, epoxy and the older resin systems. [Likely]

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 930; about 40 min)**

### Introduction
A root canal sealer fills the space between the dentinal wall and the core material, fills lateral canals and voids, and acts as a lubricant for cone placement. Gutta-percha cannot seal alone; the sealer's quality influences the outcome as much as the core.

### Ideal properties (Grossman)
Tacky and adhesive; hermetic seal; radiopaque; no shrinkage; no tooth staining; bacteriostatic; slow set; insoluble in tissue fluids; tissue tolerant; soluble in a common solvent. No sealer meets all.

### Classification
ZOE-based; calcium hydroxide-based; polymers (epoxy, polyketone); glass ionomer; methacrylate resin; calcium silicate (bioceramic).

### Composition, properties, biocompatibility
| Class | Composition | Key properties | Biocompatibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| ZOE (Pulp Canal Sealer, Tubli-Seal) | ZnO 42%, Staybelite 27%, bismuth subcarbonate 15%, BaSO4 15%, borate 1% + eugenol; zinc eugenolate | Long record, antimicrobial; shrinks, soluble, stains | Free eugenol cytotoxic when unset; resorbs if extruded |
| Calcium hydroxide (Sealapex) | Ca(OH)2 in resin base | Needs solubility for action; long-term stability doubtful | Biological effect not proven |
| Epoxy resin (AH-26, AH Plus) | Epoxy-amine | Good adhesion and handling | Toxic when fresh, low at 24 h |
| Polyketone (Diaket) | Zinc oxide-diketone chelate | Tacky, low solubility | Higher cytotoxicity |
| Glass ionomer (Ketac-Endo) | GIC | Bonds to dentin; hard to remove | Good |
| Methacrylate resin (EndoREZ, RealSeal) | Dimethacrylates, fillers, primer | Hybrid layer, moist dentin | Early mild irritation, then healing |
| Bioceramic (BC Sealer, iRoot SP) | Calcium silicates, ZrO2, pH about 12 | Hydraulic set, slight expansion, bonds | Bioactive; low inflammation on extrusion |

### Comparative points
Bonding: resin, bioceramic and glass ionomer. Antimicrobial: ZOE, bioceramic. Retreatment: easiest ZOE and epoxy; hardest bioceramic and glass ionomer. Moisture: resin needs slightly moist canal; bioceramic uses tubular water.

### Biocompatibility
Fresh sealers toxic, set sealers little; extrusion to be avoided; healing within 30-60 days after mild reaction is acceptable; in vitro tests do not predict clinical result.

### Selection
ZOE or epoxy for routine lateral compaction; bioceramic for hydraulic single-cone and open apex or perforation-prone cases; resin when monoblock is intended and the canal can be kept slightly moist.

### Conclusion
Sealer choice rests on seal, solubility, biocompatibility and retreatability; no class is clinically proven superior, so technique and extrusion control matter.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Listing brands without composition or biocompatibility.
2. Claiming a class is clinically superior (no randomised evidence).
3. Forgetting that toxicity falls once set.
4. Calling a more radiopaque fill a better fill.
