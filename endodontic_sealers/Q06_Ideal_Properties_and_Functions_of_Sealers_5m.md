## STUDY MODE

### Functions
- Fill the space between canal wall and core (Berman et al, 2021).
- Fill voids, irregularities, lateral and accessory canals.
- Fill interstices between cones in lateral compaction.
- Lubricate cone placement.
- Provide the seal, since gutta-percha does not adhere (Gatewood, 2007).
- Antimicrobial action; binding agent; radiopacity to show canal anatomy (Sikri, 2019, six functions: antimicrobial, binding, filler, lubricant, radiopacity, obturating material).

### Ideal properties
**Grossman's criteria, eleven (Cohen's Box 9.1; Berman et al, 2021):**
1. Tacky when mixed, for good adhesion to canal wall when set.
2. Hermetic seal.
3. Radiopaque, visible on radiograph.
4. Very fine powder, mixing easily with liquid.
5. No shrinkage on setting.
6. No staining of tooth structure.
7. Bacteriostatic, or at least not encouraging bacterial growth.
8. Slow setting.
9. Insoluble in tissue fluids.
10. Tissue tolerant (nonirritating to periradicular tissue).
11. Soluble in a common solvent if retreatment is needed.

Grossman's 14th ed. Box 16.8 lists its own eleven: excellent seal; adhesion between itself, walls and filling; radiopaque; non-staining; dimensionally stable; easily mixed; easily removed; insoluble; bactericidal or discouraging bacterial growth; non-irritating; slow setting (Gopikrishna, 2021) — the two lists differ in wording and order. Sikri gives fifteen requirements (Sikri, 2019). Ingle's gives its own list [read in chapter; wording to confirm].
**ISO 6876:** flow over 20 mm; film thickness under 50 μm; solubility under 3%; dimensional change -1% to +0.1%; radiopacity over 3 mm Al (Azim and Aksel, 2022).

No sealer has all of them. Radiopacity is not sealing; very radiopaque sealers can mask voids (Berman et al, 2021).

## EXAM MODE

### Functions
Fills space between canal wall and core; fills voids and lateral canals; fills gaps between cones; lubricant for cone seating; produces the seal; antimicrobial action; radiopacity.

### Ideal properties (Grossman; eleven)
| Property | Reason |
|---|---|
| Tacky | Adhesion to wall and core |
| Hermetic seal | Prevents leakage |
| Radiopaque | Visible on radiograph |
| Very fine powder | Mixes easily |
| No shrinkage | No gaps |
| No tooth staining | Esthetics |
| Bacteriostatic | Does not support bacteria |
| Slow set | Working time |
| Insoluble | Stability |
| Tissue tolerant | Periapical healing |
| Soluble in a solvent | Retreatment |

### ISO 6876
Flow over 20 mm; film under 50 μm; solubility under 3%; radiopacity over 3 mm Al.

### Remark
No sealer meets all properties; radiopacity does not equal seal.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Listing properties without functions.
2. Forgetting solubility in solvent for retreatment.
3. Stopping at ten properties (very fine powder is the one often missed).

## SECOND AUDIT PASS (2026-10-16)
Additional textbooks read after the first pass; additions and conflicts only (nothing above was removed):
- **Harty's** gives twelve requirements: nonirritating, insoluble, dimensionally stable, hermetic seal, radiopaque, bacteriostatic, sticky with good adhesion once set, good penetration into dentinal tubules, easily mixed, nonstaining, good working time, easily removable; and purposes: luting the core, filling discrepancies, lubricant, antimicrobial agent, and marker for accessory canals, resorptive defects and root fractures. It states no current material satisfies all.
- **Principles and Practice (2002)** adds that sealer should be dimensionally stable "or even expand slightly", have adequate working time then set reasonably soon, and be soluble in a solvent.
- **Applied Dental Materials:** minimal film thickness is desirable because sealer is the material most likely to degrade; extended-working-time products exist for multi-canal cases.
