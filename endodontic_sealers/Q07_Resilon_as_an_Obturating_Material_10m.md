## STUDY MODE

### Definition
Resilon is a thermoplastic synthetic polymer root-filling material based on polyester (polycaprolactone) containing bioactive glass and radiopaque fillers, about 65% by weight filler, introduced to bring dentin-bonding technology to obturation (Gatewood, 2007; Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). Later marketed as RealSeal (SimpliFill, InnoEndo and Resinate were folded into the RealSeal name) (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

### System
- **Core:** Resilon cones (master and accessory) and pellets for backfill; used in the same techniques as gutta-percha (Gatewood, 2007).
- **Sealer:** Epiphany, a dual-curable dentin-resin composite sealer; matrix of ethoxylated bisphenol A dimethacrylate, Bis-GMA, UDMA and hydrophilic difunctional methacrylates; about 70% filler (calcium hydroxide, barium sulfate, barium glass, silica) (Gatewood, 2007).
- **Primer:** self-etch primer with sulfonic acid-terminated functional monomer, HEMA, water and initiator (Gatewood, 2007).

### Principle: the monoblock
Primer forms a hybrid layer with dentin, which bonds to sealer, which bonds to the Resilon core; the aim is a mono-block (Gatewood, 2007; Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

### Technique (Gatewood, 2007)
17% EDTA to remove smear layer → Epiphany primer → Epiphany sealer → Resilon cone → obturate; light-cure the coronal 2 mm for 40 s; self-cure of the remainder in 15-30 min.

### Evidence
- Leakage: some in vitro tests superior to gutta-percha/sealer, others no difference (Gatewood, 2007; Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- Dog study: less apical periodontitis with Resilon than with gutta-percha/sealer (Gatewood, 2007).
- Increased fracture resistance in vitro (Gatewood, 2007), but later well-standardised testing found Resilon and EndoREZ combinations unable to reinforce roots (Grande et al, in Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- Clinical: 91.6% success at 24 months and 93.1% at 4 years (Debelian; Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- Retreatment: can be softened and dissolved by chloroform; removed faster and with less residue than gutta-percha/AH Plus with K3 or ProFile, in straight canals (Gatewood, 2007; Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- Concerns: alkaline and enzymatic hydrolysis of the polymer; success of dentin bonding in the canal; sealer-core coupling questioned, roughening the core raises bond strength (Gatewood, 2007; Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 330; about 15 min)**

### Definition
Resilon is a thermoplastic polycaprolactone-based resin core material with bioactive glass and radiopaque fillers (about 65% filler), used with Epiphany resin sealer to form a "monoblock".

### Components
| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| Core | Resilon cones and pellets; handles like gutta-percha |
| Primer | Self-etch (sulfonic acid monomer, HEMA) |
| Sealer | Epiphany: dual-cure Bis-GMA/UDMA, about 70% filler incl. calcium hydroxide |

### Technique
EDTA 17% → primer → sealer → Resilon point → cure coronal 2 mm (40 s), self-cure rest.

### Advantages
Bonding system (monoblock); reduced leakage in some studies; thermoplastic (usable with warm techniques); retreatment with chloroform; success about 91-94%.

### Disadvantages
Hydrolysis concern; bonding uncertainties in canal; technique sensitive; no root reinforcement; contradictory leakage studies; cost.

### Conclusion
Resilon is a bonded obturation system with promising results, but benefits over gutta-percha are not firmly proven.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Polycaprolactone core, 65% filler.
- Epiphany: dual-cure, 70% filler, calcium hydroxide.
- Cure 40 s coronal; self-cure 15-30 min.
- Chloroform removable.
