## STUDY MODE

### Status first
**Resilon was withdrawn from the market in 2014** owing to failure of long-term stability and success (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Older exam answers describe it as current; write it as a historically important system and state the withdrawal.

### Definition
Resilon is a synthetic, resin-based polycaprolactone polymer containing bioactive glass, bismuth oxychloride and barium sulfate, developed to adhere to root canal dentin (Azim and Aksel, 2022). It is a thermoplastic polyester with difunctional methacrylate resin (Gopikrishna, 2021) and about 65% by weight filler (Jain, 2016). Marketed later as RealSeal (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010). Note Grossman's 14th ed. introduces the group as "a high-performance polyurethane" in the introductory line, but its own composition list says polycaprolactone — the composition list is the one to use (Gopikrishna, 2021).

### System
- **Core:** cones (ISO sized) and pellets; usable with lateral compaction, warm vertical compaction and thermoplastic injection (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Sealer:** Epiphany, dual-cure resin; EBPADMA, Bis-GMA, UDMA, hydrophilic difunctional methacrylates, about 70% filler (calcium hydroxide, barium sulfate, barium glass, silica) (Gatewood, 2007; Jain, 2016).
- **Primer:** self-etch; sulfonic acid-terminated functional monomer, HEMA, water, initiator (Jain, 2016).

### Principle: the monoblock
Primer forms a hybrid layer with dentin, which bonds to sealer, which bonds to the Resilon core (see Q8).

### Technique
17% EDTA → Epiphany primer → Epiphany sealer → Resilon cone or pellet → obturate; light-cure coronal 2 mm for 40 s for an immediate coronal seal. **Self-cure time conflicts:** 15-30 min (Gatewood, 2007) versus about 45 min (Jain, 2016) — both recorded.
**Temperature:** Resilon needs about 150 °C for thermoplastic techniques versus about 200 °C for gutta-percha (Gopikrishna, 2021); softens at a lower temperature (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).

### Evidence
- Leakage: significantly less leakage than gutta-percha/AH Plus in some studies (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019), no difference in others (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- Fracture resistance: superior to gutta-percha/AH-26 in one study (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Azim and Aksel, 2022 "higher fracture resistance"), but a cohesive-strength and stiffness study found both Resilon and gutta-percha too low to reinforce the root (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019) — **textbooks conflict, record both**.
- Biodegradation: polycaprolactone is degraded enzymatically, creating a gap between sealer and core; the high C-factor in a canal (about 1,000 versus 5 in Class I) means the bond fails under shrinkage stress (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Tay et al: biodegradation by bacterial and salivary enzymes needed further study (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Clinical: a 100-tooth retrospective study found no healing difference at 2-25 months (Cotton et al, in Azim and Aksel, 2022); **Barborka et al: 56% success at 6 years versus 88% for gutta-percha; and a later study found 5.3 times more periapical lesions with Resilon** (Azim and Aksel, 2022). Earlier favourable figures: 91.6% at 24 months and 93.1% at 4 years (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).
- Retreatment: softened by chloroform; removed faster than gutta-percha/AH Plus in straight canals (Pameijer and Zmener, 2010).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count 328; about 15 min at 22 words/min)**

### Introduction
Resilon is a synthetic, resin-based thermoplastic root-filling material introduced to bring dentin-bonding technology to obturation. It was withdrawn from the market in 2014 because of poor long-term stability and success, so it is now of historical and exam importance.

### Composition
A polycaprolactone-based polymer with difunctional methacrylate resin, bioactive glass, bismuth oxychloride and barium sulfate, with about 65% filler by weight. It was supplied as ISO-sized cones and pellets and later marketed as RealSeal.

### The system
| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Resilon cones (master and accessory) and pellets; usable with lateral compaction, warm vertical compaction and thermoplastic injection; softens at about 150 °C against about 200 °C for gutta-percha |
| **Primer** | Self-etch: sulfonic acid-terminated functional monomer, HEMA, water, initiator |
| **Sealer** | Epiphany: dual-cure resin (EBPADMA, Bis-GMA, UDMA, hydrophilic dimethacrylates), about 70% filler including calcium hydroxide |

### Principle
The primer forms a hybrid layer with dentin, the sealer bonds to the primed dentin and to Resilon, giving a "monoblock".

### Technique
17% EDTA to remove the smear layer, primer, sealer, Resilon cone or pellet, then light-cure the coronal 2 mm for 40 s for an immediate seal; the rest self-cures (15-30 min in Gatewood, about 45 min in Jain).

### Advantages
Bonding system; less leakage than gutta-percha with AH Plus in some studies; thermoplastic; removable with chloroform; early clinical success 91.6% at 24 months and 93.1% at 4 years.

### Disadvantages and why it failed
- Polycaprolactone is degraded enzymatically, creating a gap between sealer and core.
- The C-factor in a canal is about 1,000 against 5 in a Class I cavity, so the bond cannot resist shrinkage.
- No root reinforcement; one study found higher fracture resistance, another found the stiffness too low.
- Barborka et al: 56% success at 6 years against 88% for gutta-percha; a later study found 5.3 times more periapical lesions.
- Technique sensitivity and cost.

### Conclusion
Resilon was a bonded obturation system whose early promise was not borne out; it was withdrawn in 2014 and gutta-percha with sealer remains standard.

## SECOND AUDIT PASS (2026-10-16)
Additional textbooks read after the first pass; additions and conflicts only (nothing above was removed):
- **Common Complications** describes Resilon/Epiphany as an attempt to form a monoblock bond between wall, core and sealer, and notes the standard ISO sizes and shapes; it also describes resin-coated gutta-percha with EndoREZ as a parallel attempt.
- **Endodontic Prognosis** cites the Cotton et al (2008) retrospective study (no outcome difference at 2-25 months), matching Azim and Aksel.
- I found no statement in the second-pass chapters that contradicts the 2014 withdrawal; older textbooks simply predate it.
