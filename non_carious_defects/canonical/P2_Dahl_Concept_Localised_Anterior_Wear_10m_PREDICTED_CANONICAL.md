## 1. The problem and the definition

### Explanation

In localised anterior wear the worn teeth usually remain in contact because of dento-alveolar compensation (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; OVD and freeway space are unaltered as the alveolus remodels (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC]. There is no room for restorative material, and reducing worn teeth further for crowns is inappropriate (Ibbetson, 2001) [SRC].

Alani and Patel define the Dahl concept as "a method of purposefully inducing physiological tooth movement" to create restorative space in worn dentitions where dentoalveolar compensation has maintained contact (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC]. Bartlett and Ricketts describe it as using intrusive forces and the eruptive potential of teeth to create differential movement between groups of teeth (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]. The OVD is raised only by restoring the anterior teeth (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC]. Dahl and colleagues first investigated it in 1975 (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC]; Ibbetson describes Dahl's original removable cobalt-chromium anterior bite planes worn for more than 3 months (Ibbetson, 2001) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Problem.** Slowly worn anterior teeth stay in contact by dentoalveolar compensation, so there is no space to restore them without further reduction (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011).

**Dahl concept** (first investigated by Dahl and colleagues in 1975; Alani and Patel, 2025): deliberate relative axial tooth movement. An appliance or restoration on the worn anterior teeth raises the OVD locally and props the posterior teeth apart; anterior teeth intrude and posterior teeth erupt until posterior contacts return, leaving space anteriorly (Alani and Patel, 2025).

## 2. Mechanism: how the space is made

### Explanation

The books agree that both intrusion and eruption occur, but they weight them differently:
- Bartlett and Ricketts: initial movement is mainly intrusion of the teeth opposing the appliance, followed by overeruption of the separated teeth (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; overeruption usually ends up exceeding intrusion, giving a small OVD increase (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC].
- Alani and Patel: the split is 60:40 eruption to intrusion (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC], so a 2 mm increase involves 1.2 mm over-eruption and 0.8 mm intrusion (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC], with the caveat that 2 mm anteriorly equals about a third of that posteriorly (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC].
- Ibbetson, reporting Dahl: in older patients the space came primarily from intrusion (Ibbetson, 2001) [SRC].

The gingiva moves with the teeth, reducing the need for crown lengthening (Ibbetson, 2001) [SRC], and tooth tissue and pulpal health are preserved (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]. Patients with worn teeth have a proven dento-alveolar adaptive capacity, which is the probable reason it works (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Mechanism**
- Intrusion of loaded anterior teeth plus eruption of unloaded posterior teeth.
- Ratio: about 60 eruption : 40 intrusion (Alani and Patel, 2025); Bartlett and Ricketts: intrusion first, eruption later dominates; Dahl: mainly intrusion in older patients (Ibbetson, 2001).
- Gingiva follows the teeth; no tooth reduction, pulp preserved.
- Works because worn dentitions have proven dentoalveolar adaptive capacity (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011).

## 3. Indications and contraindications

### Explanation

Dahl movement applies only when wear is localised to a few teeth (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; in generalised wear it is ineffective and full-mouth rehabilitation is indicated (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]. Pre-existing mandibular dysfunction symptoms normally contraindicate it (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC], as do poor hygiene or probing depths over 3 mm (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]. It is less successful in Class III and Class II division 1 incisor relationships and slower in Class II division 2 (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; if too few teeth meet in the intercuspal position a Dahl movement is unlikely to succeed (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]. Kaidonis et al note that Class II division 2 deep bites typically show severely worn anteriors with pristine posteriors (Kaidonis et al, 2016) [SRC]. Implants or ankylosed teeth cannot move and are a contraindication [RECALL].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Indications:** localised anterior wear (erosion or attrition) with no interocclusal space; healthy periodontium; stable posterior occlusion; enough teeth meeting in intercuspal position (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011).

**Contraindications:** generalised wear; TMD symptoms; periodontitis (pockets over 3 mm); implants or ankylosed teeth in the area; Class III or Class II division 1 (less successful) (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011). Class II division 2 deep bites respond more slowly.

## 4. Appliances and restorations

### Explanation

Early Dahl appliances were partial dentures or metal occlusal splints (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; fixed cast nickel-chromium appliances cemented with glass ionomer improved compliance (Ibbetson, 2001) [SRC]. Today most clinicians use composite build-ups shaped to ideal contours (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC], which act as both appliance and provisional restoration.

**Design.** Alani and Patel build an accentuated flat inclined cingulum platform (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC] so that lower incisors load the upper teeth along their long axes, with 2+ mm of material (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC] and 0.5 mm gingival clearance (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC]. Where attrition continues they prefer harder indirect surfaces (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC]: indirect composite, resin-nano ceramic or metal palatal veneers, gold being kinder to opposing teeth (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC].

**Steps.** Casts articulated in retruded contact position (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; wax-up as the blueprint (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC]; composite placed and adjusted so that all Dahl teeth have even contacts (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Appliances:** removable cobalt-chromium bite plane (original); fixed cast metal palatal appliance cemented with GIC; now direct composite build-ups; palatal veneers (indirect composite, ceramic, gold).

**Technique** (Alani and Patel, 2025; Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011)
1. Prevention; articulated casts in RCP; diagnostic wax-up and mock-up.
2. Bond composite (no preparation) to wax-up shape: flat cingulum platform, at least 2 mm thick.
3. Even contacts on all restored teeth; shallow guidance.
4. Review until posterior contacts return.

## 5. Outcomes and evidence

### Explanation

Sufficient space usually appears in 3-4 months (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; Gough and Setchell found 3-9 months (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; Alani and Patel show a case with contacts re-establishing over 8 months (Alani and Patel, 2025) [SRC]. Bartlett and Ricketts call the approach reliable and effective (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; composites last 3-5 years (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC], and residual composite is not reliable retention for later crowns (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]. A ten-year prospective study of 283 composite Dahl restorations in 26 patients reported median survival of 5.8 years, failure mainly by wear, fracture and marginal discolouration, rare biological complications and high satisfaction (Gulamali et al, 2011) [LIT; abstract only]. The technique is not universally accepted, with scepticism particularly in North America (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Outcome:** posterior contacts return in about 3-9 months (Bartlett and Ricketts, 2011). Composite Dahl restorations: median survival 5.8 years, failures mainly wear, fracture, discolouration; biological complications rare (Gulamali et al, 2011). Composites are maintained by repair, later replaced by crowns or indirect palatal veneers. Advantages: additive, reversible, no crown lengthening. Disadvantages: transient bite discomfort, speech change, maintenance. Patient consent must cover the temporary posterior open bite and the need for long-term review.

## High-yield points
- Dahl concept = relative axial tooth movement to create space for localised anterior wear.
- Dahl, Krogstad and Karlsen, 1975; original appliance a removable cobalt-chromium bite plane.
- Space from intrusion plus eruption; 60:40 eruption:intrusion (Alani and Patel).
- Space in about 3-9 months.
- Only for localised wear; contraindicated with TMD or active periodontitis.
- Composite "Dahl" restorations median survival 5.8 years in one ten-year study.

## Common exam mistakes
- Using the Dahl concept for generalised wear.
- Writing that only eruption (or only intrusion) creates the space.
- Leaving only a few restored teeth in contact, which overloads and fractures them.
- Forgetting prevention of the cause before building up teeth.
- Treating residual composite as crown retention.
