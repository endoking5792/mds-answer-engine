## Explanation

The useful insight is that the probe, more than the radiograph, separates the two. Periodontal bone loss always starts at the crest, so a periodontal lesion probes as a cone and an intact sulcus rules periodontal disease out (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]. A sinus tract from the pulp probes as a narrow break of about 1 mm (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]. Gulabivala and Ng warn that a lateral endodontic abscess can produce a wide "blow-out" defect that still heals with endodontics alone (Gulabivala, Darbar and Ng, 2014) [SRC], so width is suggestive, not decisive. The final test is the response to root canal treatment (Rotstein, 2017) [SRC].

**Reading the probe.** Walton and Torabinejad's chapter is the most practical source. Periodontal disease starts at the crest, so a periodontal lesion probes as a cone; a sinus tract probes as a narrow (about 1 mm) defect with normal depths 1 mm either side; a "blow-out" lesion drops precipitously over the width of a swelling and reattaches within a week after endodontic treatment; a true combined lesion probes as a cone with a sinus-type drop at its base (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]. Probing must be distinguished from sounding, which goes through the attachment to bone (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]. Gulabivala and Ng warn that a lateral endodontic abscess can produce a wide and deep "blow-out" profile yet heal with endodontics alone (Gulabivala, Darbar and Ng, 2014) [SRC], so width alone does not prove periodontal origin.

## ✎ Exam core

**A. Pathways of communication**
1. **Apical foramen**: the main route (Rotstein, Zabalegui and Al-Hezaimi, 2019).
2. **Lateral and accessory canals**: 30 to 40% of teeth, mostly apical; furcation canals in about 28% of molars (Gutmann); only 2% associated with pockets (Rotstein, Zabalegui and Al-Hezaimi, 2019; Glickman and Iacono, 2021; Rotstein, 2017). Clues: a "notch" on the root, sealer extrusion; two-view radiographs show about 30% of them (Rotstein, Zabalegui and Al-Hezaimi, 2019).
3. **Dentinal tubules**: communicate only when cementum is lost; dentine exposed at the CEJ in about 18% of teeth (Gulabivala, Darbar and Ng, 2014; Rotstein, Zabalegui and Al-Hezaimi, 2019).
4. **Developmental**: palatogingival groove (lateral incisors 4.4%) (Glickman and Iacono, 2021).
5. **Pathological**: perforation, vertical root fracture ("halo"), resorption (Glickman and Iacono, 2021).

**B. Differentiating endodontic from periodontal origin**
| Feature | Endodontic | Periodontal |
|---|---|---|
| Pulp vitality | Non-vital (test each root) | Vital |
| Pain | Acute, spontaneous | Dull, chronic |
| Percussion | Vertical | Lateral, mild |
| Probing | Sulcus normal except a narrow (about 1 mm) defect | Pockets over 3 mm, conical, often generalised |
| Gutta-percha tracing | To the apex | To the pocket |
| Mobility | Rare, localised | Common, generalised |
| Junctional epithelium, gingiva | Normal | Apical migration, inflammation, recession |
| Plaque and calculus | Not causative | Present |
| Response to RCT | Heals; sinus closes in days | Does not resolve |
*(Gopikrishna, 2021; Walton and Torabinejad, 2002; Gulabivala, Darbar and Ng, 2014)*

**Additional points**
- A deep solitary narrow pocket in a mouth without periodontal disease suggests endodontic origin (Rotstein, Zabalegui and Al-Hezaimi, 2019).
- A sinus admits only a gutta-percha cone; an infrabony pocket admits wider instruments (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- A periodontal lesion will not resolve with root canal treatment even if the tooth is pulpless (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).
- Narrow defects on both buccal and lingual suggest a vertical root fracture (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).
- Radiographs from more than one angle (Glickman and Iacono, 2021).
- Healing after root canal treatment gives a retrospective diagnosis (Rotstein, 2017).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Foramen, lateral canals, tubules; only 2% of lateral canals relate to pockets.
- Narrow probing is endodontic; conical is periodontal.
- Intact sulcus excludes periodontal disease.

- Testing only one cusp of a multirooted tooth.
