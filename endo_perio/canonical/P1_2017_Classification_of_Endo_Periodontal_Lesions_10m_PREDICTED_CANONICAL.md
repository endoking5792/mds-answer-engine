## Explanation

**Why a new classification was needed.** Herrera et al reviewed the literature for the 2017 Workshop and identified two problems with Simon's 1972 scheme: its categories depend on the primary source of infection, and the history of the disease is usually unavailable to the clinician; moreover, knowing the source does not change treatment, because both the canal and the periodontium need treating (Herrera et al, 2018) [LIT]. The 1999 AAP classification had included these lesions as a single category, "combined periodontal-endodontic lesions", under "periodontitis associated with endodontic lesions"; it reflected the present condition, but it implied that all such lesions occur in periodontitis patients and was too generic to guide treatment (Herrera et al, 2018) [LIT]. Cohen's adds that endo-periodontal lesions occur in teeth with or without root damage and in patients with or without periodontitis, and that there is no evidence of a unique pathophysiology (Glickman and Iacono, 2021) [SRC].

**The organising idea.** The new scheme asks the questions that decide prognosis and treatment: is the root damaged; does the patient have periodontitis; how extensive is the periodontal destruction around this tooth (Herrera et al, 2018) [LIT]. Its prognostic groups are hopeless (usually root damage), poor and favourable (Herrera et al, 2018) [LIT].

**Its own limits.** The authors state that the evidence on these lesions is limited, mostly case reports (Herrera et al, 2018) [LIT], and they list as unresolved whether lesions with root damage belong in this category at all. I found no study testing the reliability of the grades [Likely].

## ✎ Exam core

**Definition (2017).** An endo-periodontal lesion is a pathological communication between the pulpal and periodontal tissues at a given tooth, occurring in acute or chronic form (Papapanou et al, 2018; Herrera et al, 2018). Key signs: a deep pocket reaching or near the apex, and a negative or altered response to pulp tests (Herrera et al, 2018).

**2017 classification (Herrera et al, 2018)**
| Endo-periodontal lesion | Sub-group | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| With root damage | Root fracture or cracking | |
| | Root canal or pulp chamber perforation | |
| | External root resorption | |
| Without root damage | In periodontitis patients | 1: narrow deep pocket, one surface; 2: wide deep pocket, one surface; 3: deep pockets, more than one surface |
| | In non-periodontitis patients | Grades 1 to 3 as above |

**Prognostic groups**: hopeless (usually root damage), poor, favourable (Herrera et al, 2018).

**Diagnosis in two steps** (Herrera et al, 2018): first look for root damage (history of trauma, instrumentation, post preparation; examine for fracture, perforation, resorption); then periodontal charting with vitality and percussion tests.

**Comparison with earlier classifications**
| Feature | Simon et al 1972 | Oliet and Pollock | Weine | AAP 1999 | 2017 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basis | Origin and progression | Treatment needed | Clinical presentation and therapy | Present condition | Root damage, periodontitis, extent |
| Categories | 5 (+ concomitant, Belk and Gutmann) | 3 groups | Classes I to IV | 1 ("combined") | 2 groups, 3 grades |
| Needs disease history | Yes | No | Partly | No | No |
| Links to prognosis | Indirect | Indirect | Yes | No | Direct |
*(Gopikrishna, 2021; Weine, 2004; Herrera et al, 2018)*

**Merits of the 2017 scheme**: uses findings available at the visit; separates root damage, which carries the worst prognosis; recognises lesions in patients without periodontitis; links directly to the keep-or-extract decision (Herrera et al, 2018).

**Limitations**: evidence base limited to case reports and small studies; grades not validated in the sources reviewed; does not explain pathogenesis, for which Simon's scheme is still taught (Herrera et al, 2018; Gopikrishna, 2021). Gulabivala and Ng had already noted that no classification especially helps diagnosis or treatment (Gulabivala, Darbar and Ng, 2014).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Two groups: with and without root damage.
- Root damage: fracture or crack, perforation, external resorption.
- Grades 1 to 3 by pocket width and number of surfaces, in periodontitis and non-periodontitis patients.
- Replaces the single 1999 "combined periodontal-endodontic lesion".

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Confusing the EPL grades with periodontitis stages and grades.
- Saying the 2017 classification is based on origin; it is based on present findings.
- Leaving out root damage.
