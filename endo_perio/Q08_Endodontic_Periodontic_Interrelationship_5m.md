## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: about 8 minutes)*

The pulp and periodontium are one biological unit, connected embryologically, anatomically and through the vascular system; disease of one can affect the other (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Pathways** (Rotstein, Zabalegui and Al-Hezaimi, 2019):
1. Apical foramen: the main route.
2. Lateral and accessory canals: 30 to 40% of teeth, mostly apical; only 2% associated with pockets in 1000 periodontally diseased teeth (Rotstein, 2017).
3. Dentinal tubules: exposed at the CEJ in about 18% of teeth.
4. Others: palatogingival groove, perforation, root fracture.

**Pulp to periodontium**: necrotic pulp products cause apical, lateral or furcal bone loss; endodontic infection is a local modifying risk factor for periodontitis if untreated (Glickman and Iacono, 2021).

**Periodontium to pulp**: controversial; the pulp survives while the main apical vessels are intact; periodontal instruments can sever lateral-canal vessels (Glickman and Iacono, 2021; Rotstein, Zabalegui and Al-Hezaimi, 2019).

**Clinical result**: endo-perio lesions, classified by Simon et al (1972) (Gopikrishna, 2021); endodontic treatment precedes periodontal therapy regardless of cause (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Three main pathways: foramen, lateral canals, tubules.
- Pulp survives periodontitis while apical vessels are intact.
