# Dye detection of caries.

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Definition and rationale → classification → dentine dyes and products → technique → mode of action → evidence: books differ → limitations → conclusion.

## Definition and rationale

**Caries detection (disclosing) dyes** were developed to guide removal of **infected dentine** (superficial, necrotic, bacterially invaded) while preserving **affected dentine** (inner, partly demineralised, minimally infected), avoiding over-reduction (Maltz et al., 2019). Staining carious dentine was proposed by **Fusayama** (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019). An ideal dye is specific, staining only its intended tissue (Sikri, 2020).

## Classification (Sikri, 2020)

1. **Enamel caries:** Procion (stains irreversibly), calcein (binds calcium), fluorescent Zyglo ZL-22 (in vitro only), brilliant blue (enhances transillumination), iodide penetration. None is yet clinical.
2. **Dentine caries:** acid red preferred, as fuchsin and methylene blue are toxic.

## Dentine dyes and products

- **0.5% basic fuchsin** in propylene glycol, the first dye, was abandoned over carcinogenicity for **1% acid red** (Maltz et al., 2019).
- **Caries Detector** (Kuraray): 1% acid red 52 in propylene glycol; **Caries Finder Green:** 1% FD&C green; **Snoop:** dark blue (Maltz et al., 2019).

## Technique (Maltz et al., 2019)

1. Clean and air-dry the cavity.
2. Apply one drop for 5 s (Caries Finder) or 10 s (Caries Detector); rinse.
3. Remove stained dentine with an excavator or slow round steel bur without irrigation.
4. Reapply until the floor no longer stains.

## Mode of action

Propylene glycol penetrates only **loosened collagen with broken cross-links**; the dye stains **organic matrix, not bacteria**, so normal circumpulpal dentine and the DEJ also stain (Maltz et al., 2019).

## Evidence: books differ

| Source | View |
|---|---|
| Singh (2020) | reliable: acid red stains denatured collagen "of the infected dentin only" |
| Hilton and Summitt (2013) | aids removal of demineralised dentine; bonding no concern |
| Maltz et al. (2019) | poor indicator: sensitivity 65.1%, specificity 37.6% (70% expected); bonding effect unclear |
| Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter (2019); Banerjee and Watson (2015) | stains deeper collagen, not bacteria; over-preparation; should be avoided |

Most books, and the mode of action, contradict Singh.

## Limitations

Sound deep dentine stains, risking **pulp exposure** (Hilton and Summitt, 2013; Maltz et al., 2019); over-excavation with burs; stain persisting under aesthetic restorations. Sealed carious dentine harbours less infection than complete removal, so stain-free excavation is no longer the aim (Maltz et al., 2019).

## Conclusion

Dyes stain demineralised matrix, not bacteria: at most an adjunct to minimally invasive excavation.
