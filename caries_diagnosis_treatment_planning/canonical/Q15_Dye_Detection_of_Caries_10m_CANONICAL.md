# Dye detection of caries.

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Definition and rationale → classification → dentine dyes and products → technique → mode of action → evidence → limitations → conclusion.

## Definition and rationale

**Caries detection (disclosing) dyes** were developed to guide removal of **infected dentine** (superficial, necrotic, bacterially invaded) while preserving **affected dentine** (inner, partly demineralised, minimally infected, remineralisable), and so to avoid over-reduction of dentine (Maltz et al., 2019). Dyes work because caries creates microvoids and porosity; an ideal dye is safe, specific and removable (Sikri, 2020).

## Classification (Sikri, 2020)

1. **Enamel caries:** Procion (stains irreversibly), calcein (binds calcium), fluorescent Zyglo ZL-22 (in vitro only, needs UV), brilliant blue (enhances fibre-optic transillumination), iodide penetration (enamel porosity). None is yet clinical.
2. **Dentine caries:** basic fuchsin, then acid red; methylene blue is slightly toxic, so acid red is preferred.

## Dentine dyes and products

- **0.5% basic fuchsin** in propylene glycol, the first dye (1970s), was abandoned over carcinogenicity for **1% acid red** (a food dye) (Maltz et al., 2019).
- **Caries Detector** (Kuraray): 1% acid red 52 in propylene glycol; **Caries Finder Green:** 1% FD&C green; **Snoop:** dark blue; **Caries Check:** acid red or brilliant blue in polypropylene glycol. Green and blue dyes avoid the red background at the infected-affected interface (Maltz et al., 2019).

## Technique (Maltz et al., 2019)

1. Clean and air-dry the cavity.
2. Apply one drop for 5 s (Caries Finder) or 10 s (Caries Detector, Seek, Snoop).
3. Rinse with water.
4. Remove stained dentine with an excavator or slow round steel bur without irrigation.
5. Reapply until the floor no longer stains.

## Mode of action

Propylene glycol penetrates only **loosened collagen with broken cross-links**; the dye stains **organic matrix of less mineralised dentine, not bacteria**, so normal circumpulpal dentine and the DEJ also stain (Maltz et al., 2019).

## Evidence: books differ

| Point | Hilton and Summitt (2013) | Maltz et al. (2019) |
|---|---|---|
| Value | aid removal of demineralised dentine | poor indicator of carious dentine; sensitivity 65.1%, specificity 37.6% (below the 70% expected) |
| Effect on bonding | no concern on balance of evidence | unclear |

Both warn that porous **sound deep dentine stains**, risking over-excavation and **pulp exposure** (Hilton and Summitt, 2013; Maltz et al., 2019).

## Limitations

False staining of sound dentine, debris and the DEJ; over-excavation with burs; stain persisting under aesthetic restorations. Sealed carious dentine harbours less infection than complete removal, so stain-free excavation is no longer the aim (Maltz et al., 2019).

## Conclusion

Dyes stain demineralised matrix rather than bacteria; good-quality evidence for their use is lacking, so they are at most an adjunct to hardness-based, minimally invasive excavation.
