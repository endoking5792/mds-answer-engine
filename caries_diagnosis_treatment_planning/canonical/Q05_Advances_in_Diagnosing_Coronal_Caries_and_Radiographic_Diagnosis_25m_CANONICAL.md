# Enumerate the advances in the methods for diagnosing coronal caries and add a note on developments in Radiographic diagnosis of Dental caries.

**Marks:** 25 · Essay

## 1. Introduction

**Caries diagnosis** includes both **detection** of a lesion and assessment of its **activity** (Amaechi et al., 2013). Conventional visual, tactile and radiographic methods detect only 20-30% of minute early changes (Kim, 2019), and a lesion must lose about 30% of its mineral before it shows on a radiograph (Muruppel and Fried, 2023). Because non-cavitated lesions can be arrested without drilling, newer methods aim to **detect early, quantify, monitor and avoid ionising radiation**. Most work on four physical principles: **light scattering, mineral density, fluorescence and tissue porosity** (Banerjee and Watson, 2011).

## 2. Enumeration of advances in coronal caries diagnosis

| Group | Method | Principle and key point |
|---|---|---|
| A. Visual criteria | **ICDAS II / ICCMS**, Nyvad criteria, ADA CCS, CAST | Two-digit ICDAS code (restoration status, caries severity) on clean, dry teeth; pooled sensitivity and specificity 0.7 (Ekstrand and Braga, 2019) |
| B. Transillumination | **FOTI** | Light through the tooth; porous lesion appears dark on a light background; sensitivity 21-96%, specificity 74-88% (Hogan, Pretty and Ellwood, 2019) |
| | **DIFOTI** | Digital camera with FOTI; better than film and digital radiography for early approximal enamel lesions in vitro (Caneppele et al., 2020) |
| | **NILT (DIAGNOcam)** | ~780 nm near-infrared, X-ray-free imaging (Kühnisch, 2019); better than digital radiography for initial proximal lesions (Caneppele et al., 2020) |
| | **NIR imaging** | Enamel most transparent near 1300 nm (Fried, 2019a) |
| C. Fluorescence | **QLF** | Blue-light autofluorescence loss quantifies early lesions; red fluorescence shows bacteria (Kim, 2019) |
| | **Laser fluorescence (DIAGNOdent, pen)** | 655 nm laser excites bacterial porphyrins; reading 0-99; a second-opinion device (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) |
| | **Fluorescence cameras** (SoproLife, VistaProof, Spectra) | Blue or violet LEDs; caries shown red (Caneppele et al., 2020) |
| | **FACE** | 405 nm violet light during excavation: infected dentine red, sound green (Lennon and Buchalla, 2019) |
| D. Electrical | **ECM**, **ACIST (CarieScan)** | Sound enamel insulates; conductance rises with demineralisation; ACIST scores 0-100 (Muruppel and Fried, 2023; Caneppele et al., 2020) |
| E. Photothermal | **Canary System** | Photothermal radiometry and luminescence (PTR-LUM) read crystal structure (Amaechi, 2019) |
| F. Other optical | **OCT** | Cross-sectional reflectivity images (Fried, 2019b) |
| | **Midwest Caries I.D.** | LED reflection; 92% of occlusal and 80% of proximal lesions (Caneppele et al., 2020) |
| G. Acoustic | **Ultrasound** | 18 MHz pulse-echo separates sound from demineralised enamel (Yanıkoğlu and Tağtekin, 2019) |
| H. Biochemical | **Bioluminescence (CALCIVIS)** | Calcium-sensitive photoprotein shows active demineralisation (Longbottom and Vernon, 2019) |
| I. Dyes | **Caries detector dyes** | Stain demineralised dentine matrix; a poor indicator of carious dentine (Maltz et al., 2019) |
| J. Imaging advances | Digital, subtraction and computer-aided radiography; CBCT | See section 3 |

Of these, laser fluorescence, light-induced fluorescence and ACIST are FDA approved (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).

## 3. Note on developments in radiographic diagnosis

### 3.1 Conventional bitewing: improved use

- **Accuracy (books differ):**

| Source | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|
| Schwendicke and Göstemeyer (2019) | 0.2-0.3 early proximal; 0.4-0.6 dentine | 0.9 or above |
| Caneppele et al. (2020) | 59% proximal | 95% |
| Kim (2019) | at most 50% proximal, 40% early proximal | not stated |

- Radiographs **underestimate lesion depth** (Amaechi et al., 2013) and seldom show cavitation; about half of lesions in outer dentine are cavitated (Schwendicke and Göstemeyer, 2019).
- **Depth scoring:** E1, E2 (enamel halves), D1, D2, D3 (dentine thirds); E1-D1 mainly non-restorative (Schwendicke and Göstemeyer, 2019); ICCMS scores RA1-RA3, RB4, RC5-RC6 (Zeller, Young and Novy, 2019).
- **Individualised film holders** give identical projections for monitoring; bitewings are prescribed by **caries risk**, not at fixed intervals (Schwendicke and Göstemeyer, 2019).

**Diagram:** proximal surface of a molar showing E1, E2, D1, D2, D3 zones.

### 3.2 Xeroradiography

Selenium-plate xerographic imaging with edge enhancement; twice as sensitive as D-speed film with one-third of the radiation (Sikri, 2020).

### 3.3 Digital radiography

- **Sensors:** CCD or CMOS solid-state sensors (direct, immediate image) and photostimulable phosphor plates (Göstemeyer and Schwendicke, 2019); direct digital radiography is also called radiovisiography (RVG) (Sikri, 2020).
- **Dose** per image falls by 30-80%, though more images are often taken (Göstemeyer and Schwendicke, 2019).
- **Accuracy** equals film (Göstemeyer and Schwendicke, 2019; Amaechi et al., 2013).
- **Image processing:** zoom, contrast, grey-scale inversion, edge enhancement (Göstemeyer and Schwendicke, 2019).

### 3.4 Digital subtraction radiography

Two standardised images are subtracted so that unchanged anatomy disappears and progression or arrest stands out (Göstemeyer and Schwendicke, 2019); superior to film for proximal and recurrent caries (Sikri, 2020).

### 3.5 Computer-aided detection: books differ

Logicon Caries Detector reached sensitivity 90.5% and specificity 88.3% (Caneppele et al., 2020), whereas commercial programmes are at best as accurate as human observers (Göstemeyer and Schwendicke, 2019).

### 3.6 CT, CBCT and MRI

- **CT** is limited by cost and radiation (Sikri, 2020).
- **CBCT**, first described in 1998, matches bitewings for unrestored proximal caries but suffers scatter and beam-hardening artefacts near restorations (Price, 2019).
- **Books differ:** Sikri (2020) describes CBCT as widely used for caries diagnosis with the least radiation hazard; Price (2019) states caries is not a primary indication for CBCT, and Göstemeyer and Schwendicke (2019) put its dose 10-100 times that of bitewings. The safer reading is to assess caries on CBCT taken for other reasons only.
- **MRI** is not suited for caries detection (Sikri, 2020).

## 4. Clinical significance

- New devices are **adjuncts**: laser fluorescence is an adjunct, not a primary tool (Muruppel and Fried, 2023); fluorescent tools still have only research interest (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).
- Every added test raises the chance of a **false-positive** diagnosis (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).
- The standard of care remains **visual examination of clean, dry teeth with radiographs as indicated** (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).

## 5. Conclusion

Advances in coronal caries diagnosis move from subjective, tissue-destructive probing towards quantitative, radiation-free optical, electrical and biochemical methods, while radiography has moved to digital, subtraction and three-dimensional imaging. Each aids monitoring of early lesions, but none replaces careful visual examination with risk-based bitewings.
