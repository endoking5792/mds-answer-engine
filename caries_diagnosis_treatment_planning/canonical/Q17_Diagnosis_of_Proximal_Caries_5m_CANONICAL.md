# Diagnosis of Proximal Caries.

**Marks:** 5 · Short note

The proximal lesion forms **just cervical to the contact area**, hidden by the adjacent tooth (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).

1. **Visual-tactile:** isolated, clean, dry teeth; a late lesion shows a **pinkish-grey shadow through the marginal ridge**; a curved probe is used only gently (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). A **hard brown spot** below the contact is inactive: no restoration (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
2. **Bitewing radiograph:** the best aid (Singh, 2020). Sensitivity is 0.2-0.3 for early lesions, specificity about 0.9; depth scored E1, E2, D1-D3 (Schwendicke and Göstemeyer, 2019). It cannot show cavitation or activity (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).
3. **Tooth separation:** an elastomeric separator for 2-7 days (Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona, 2019) opens about 0.7 mm for inspection (Cortés, Martignon and Douglas, 2019).
4. **FOTI:** especially convenient for anterior teeth (Amaechi et al., 2013).
5. **Newer aids:** near-infrared transillumination at ~780 nm (Kühnisch, 2019); DIAGNOdent pen with an approximal tip (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019).

**Cavitation risk on bitewings:** Pitts and Rimmer found none in outer enamel, 40.9% in outer and 100% in inner dentine halves (Zeller, Young and Novy, 2019); Singh (2020) gives only 20-40% at D2.
