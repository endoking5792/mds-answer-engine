# Dental caries - etiological factors and methods of diagnosis

**Marks:** 20 · Long essay

**Answer blueprint:** Definition → Theories of etiology → Etiological factors (biofilm, tooth, saliva, diet, time, modifying factors) → Methods of diagnosis: principles → visual-tactile examination → radiographs → conventional adjuncts → newer methods → caries activity tests → Conclusion.

## Definition

**Dental caries** is a localised chemical dissolution of the tooth surface caused by metabolic activity in the dental biofilm (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). It is multifactorial, caused mainly by an imbalance of the oral flora in the presence of fermentable carbohydrate (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019); no single theory explains it (Sikri, 2020).

## Theories of etiology

| Theory | Proposed by | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| **Acidogenic (chemicoparasitic)** | Miller in 1889 | Oral bacteria ferment carbohydrate to acid, which dissolves the inorganic part; proteolysis then destroys the organic part |
| **Proteolytic** | Gottlieb in 1944 | Proteolytic enzymes attack the organic pathways (lamellae, rod sheaths) first |
| **Proteolysis-chelation** | Schatz and co-workers in 1955 | Breakdown products of organic matrix chelate calcium, even at neutral pH |
| **Ionic see-saw** | Levine in 1977 | Continuous de- and remineralisation; net ion loss starts caries |
| **Caries balance** | Featherstone in 1999 | Outcome of pathological versus protective factors |

(Sikri, 2020).

## Etiological factors

Keyes in 1960 described a triad of host, microflora and substrate; Newbrun in 1982 added **time** (Singh, 2020). Caries thus needs **host (tooth, saliva), microflora, diet and time** interacting (Sikri, 2020; Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019).

**Diagram:** Four overlapping circles (tooth, biofilm, diet, time), caries at the centre, modifiers around.

### 1. Biofilm (microflora)

- The acquired pellicle forms within 2 hours and is colonised by streptococci; frequent sugar causes an **ecological shift** to acidogenic, aciduric mutans streptococci and lactobacilli; communities, not one pathogen, are responsible (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019).
- **Site flora:** mutans streptococci in pits and smooth surfaces, lactobacilli in deep dentine, Actinomyces on roots (Sikri, 2020).
- **Books differ:** Sikri (2020) calls caries a bacterial infection; Kidd and Fejerskov (2016) call it a biofilm-induced disease of resident flora, mutans being present even in caries-free mouths.

### 2. Host: tooth

- **Pits and fissures** are most susceptible, then **proximal enamel** below the contact, then exposed roots (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019); mandibular first molars are most susceptible (Sikri, 2020).
- Critical pH is **5.5 for enamel, 6.2 for dentin** (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019); cementum dissolves from pH 6.7 (Sikri, 2020), so roots demineralise more readily.

### 3. Host: saliva

Adults secrete 1-1.5 L daily, providing clearance, **bicarbonate buffering**, antibacterial proteins and calcium-phosphate supersaturation for remineralisation; after radiotherapy, hyposalivation can destroy teeth within months (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019).

### 4. Diet (substrate)

- **Frequency** of sucrose matters more than quantity (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019).
- **Vipeholm study** (436 adults): risk greatest when sugar is eaten between meals and in retentive form (Sikri, 2020).

### 5. Time

pH takes about two hours to recover after sugar (Sikri, 2020).

### 6. Modifying factors

Low fluoride exposure, xerostomic medication, age, and lower income and education raise risk (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019). Sikri (2020) describes a genetic theory; Kidd and Fejerskov (2016) find no evidence that inheritance explains group differences.

## Methods of diagnosis

### Principles

**Detection** asks whether a lesion is present; **diagnosis** adds whether it is active or arrested and whether cavitated (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016; Amaechi et al, 2013). A **CPITN ball-ended probe (0.5 mm)**, not a sharp explorer, assesses roughness (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).

### 1. Visual-tactile examination

- **ICDAS:** codes 0 (sound after 5 s drying) to 6 (extensive cavity with visible dentine), staging depth (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).
- **Nyvad criteria:** active lesions plaque-covered, matt and rough; inactive lesions shiny and smooth (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).
- Visual inspection of clean, dry, well-lit teeth with radiographs as indicated remains the **standard of care** (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).

### 2. Radiographs

**Bitewings** detect approximal and occlusal dentine lesions; sensitivity only 0.2-0.3 for early lesions, specificity about 0.9 (Schwendicke and Göstemeyer, 2019). About 25% mineral loss is needed before radiolucency (Boushell and Shugars, 2019); radiographs cannot show activity (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).

### 3. Conventional adjuncts

**Tooth separation** with elastics allows direct inspection after a few days; in **fibre-optic transillumination** (0.5 mm beam) lesions shadow dark (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).

### 4. Newer methods

| Method | Principle |
|---|---|
| **QLF** | 405 nm light; fluorescence loss measures mineral loss (Kim, 2019) |
| **DIAGNOdent** | 655 nm laser; bacterial porphyrin fluorescence, reading 0-99 (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) |
| **ACIST (CarieScan)** | Alternating-current impedance of demineralised tissue (Boushell and Shugars, 2019) |

Boushell and Shugars (2019) state their accuracy is not established; Kidd and Fejerskov (2016) regard fluorescence devices as research tools.

### 5. Caries activity tests

Lactobacillus count, Snyder, Alban and salivary reductase tests estimate susceptibility (Sikri, 2020), but bacterial tests add little to risk prediction (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019).

## Conclusion

Caries is a biofilm-sugar-host imbalance over time; diagnosis records activity and cavitation, visually, with bitewings.
