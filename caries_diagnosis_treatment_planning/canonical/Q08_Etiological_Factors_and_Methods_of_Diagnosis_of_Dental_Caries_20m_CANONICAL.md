# Dental caries - etiological factors and methods of diagnosis

**Marks:** 20 · Long essay

**Answer blueprint:** Definition → Theories of etiology → Etiological factors (biofilm, host: tooth and saliva, diet, time, modifying factors) → Detection and diagnosis: principles → Clinical visual-tactile methods → Radiographs → Adjunct and newer methods → Caries activity tests → Conclusion.

## Definition

**Dental caries** is a localised chemical dissolution of the tooth surface caused by metabolic activity in the dental biofilm (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). It is multifactorial, caused mainly by an imbalance of the oral flora in the presence of fermentable carbohydrate (Ritter et al, 2019); no single theory explains it (Sikri, 2020).

## Theories of etiology

| Theory | Proposed by | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| **Acidogenic (chemicoparasitic)** | Miller in 1889 | Oral bacteria ferment carbohydrate to acid, which dissolves the inorganic part; proteolysis then destroys the organic part |
| **Proteolytic** | Gottlieb in 1944 | Proteolytic enzymes attack the organic pathways (lamellae, rod sheaths) first |
| **Proteolysis-chelation** | Schatz and co-workers in 1955 | Breakdown products of organic matrix chelate calcium, even at neutral pH |
| **Ionic see-saw** | Levine in 1977 | Continuous de- and remineralisation; net ion loss starts caries |
| **Caries balance** | Featherstone in 1999 | Outcome of pathological versus protective factors |

(Sikri, 2020).

## Etiological factors

Caries needs four interacting factors: **host (tooth, saliva), microflora, diet and time** (Sikri, 2020; Ritter et al, 2019).

**Diagram:** Four overlapping circles (tooth, biofilm, diet, time), caries in the centre; outer ring of modifiers (fluoride, saliva, behaviour, income, education).

### 1. Biofilm (microflora)

- The acquired pellicle forms within 2 hours and is colonised by streptococci (Ritter et al, 2019).
- Frequent sugar causes an **ecological shift** to acidogenic, aciduric mutans streptococci and lactobacilli; communities, not one pathogen, are responsible (Ritter et al, 2019).
- **Site flora:** mutans streptococci in pits and smooth surfaces, lactobacilli in deep dentine, Actinomyces on roots (Sikri, 2020).
- **Books differ:** Sikri (2020) calls caries a bacterial infection; Kidd and Fejerskov (2016) call it a biofilm-induced disease of resident flora, mutans being present even in caries-free mouths.

### 2. Host: tooth

- **Pits and fissures** are most susceptible, then **proximal enamel** just gingival to the contact, then exposed roots (Ritter et al, 2019); mandibular first molars are the most susceptible teeth (Sikri, 2020).
- Critical pH is **5.5 for enamel, 6.2 for dentin** (Ritter et al, 2019); cementum dissolves from pH 6.7 (Sikri, 2020), so root caries progresses faster.

### 3. Host: saliva

Adults secrete 1-1.5 L a day, providing clearance, **bicarbonate buffering**, antibacterial proteins and calcium-phosphate supersaturation for remineralisation (Ritter et al, 2019). Hyposalivation (drugs, radiotherapy) can destroy teeth within months.

### 4. Diet (substrate)

- **Frequency** of sucrose matters more than quantity (Ritter et al, 2019).
- **Vipeholm study** (436 adults): risk greatest when sugar is eaten between meals and in retentive form (Sikri, 2020).
- Stickiness, slow clearance and bedtime intake raise cariogenicity.

### 5. Time

pH takes about two hours to recover after sugar (Sikri, 2020);

### 6. Modifying factors

Fluoride, oral hygiene, socio-economic status, medication and age. Sikri (2020) describes a genetic theory; Kidd and Fejerskov (2016) find no evidence that inheritance explains group differences.

## Methods of diagnosis

### Principles

**Detection** asks whether a lesion is present; **diagnosis** adds whether it is active or arrested and whether cavitated (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016; Amaechi et al, 2013). Teeth must be clean, dry and well lit. A **CPITN ball-ended probe (0.5 mm)**, not a sharp explorer, assesses roughness (Ritter et al, 2019).

### 1. Visual-tactile examination

- **ICDAS:** codes 0 (sound after 5 s drying) to 6 (extensive cavity with visible dentine), staging depth.
- **Nyvad criteria:** active lesions plaque-covered, matt and rough; inactive lesions shiny and smooth (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).
- Visual inspection of clean, dry teeth with radiographs as indicated remains the **standard of care** (Ritter et al, 2019).

### 2. Radiographs

**Bitewings** detect approximal and occlusal dentine lesions; sensitivity only 0.2-0.3 for enamel lesions, specificity about 0.9 (Schwendicke and Göstemeyer, 2019). About 25% mineral loss is needed before radiolucency (Ritter et al, 2019); radiographs cannot show activity (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).

### 3. Conventional adjuncts

- **Tooth separation** with elastics for direct inspection after a few days.
- **Fibre-optic transillumination:** lesions shadow dark in a 0.5 mm beam (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).

### 4. Newer methods

| Method | Principle |
|---|---|
| **QLF** | 405 nm light; fluorescence loss measures mineral loss (Kim, 2019) |
| **DIAGNOdent** | 655 nm laser; bacterial porphyrin fluorescence, reading 0-99 (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) |
| **ACIST (CarieScan)** | Alternating-current impedance of demineralised tissue (Ritter et al, 2019) |

Ritter et al (2019) state their accuracy is not established; Kidd and Fejerskov (2016) regard fluorescence devices as research tools.

### 5. Caries activity tests

Lactobacillus count, Snyder, Alban and reductase tests estimate susceptibility (Sikri, 2020), but bacterial tests add little to individual risk prediction (Ritter et al, 2019).

## Conclusion

Caries is a biofilm-sugar-host imbalance over time; diagnosis records activity and cavitation, mainly visually, supported by bitewings.
