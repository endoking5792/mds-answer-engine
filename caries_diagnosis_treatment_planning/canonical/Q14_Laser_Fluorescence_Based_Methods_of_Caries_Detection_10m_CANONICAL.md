# Laser fluorescence-based methods of caries detection

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Introduction → principle → DIAGNOdent devices → technique → readings and cut-offs → accuracy → limitations → related fluorescence methods → conclusion.

## Introduction

Caries emits fluorescence when excited by red light of about 650 nm. **DIAGNOdent** (KaVo), developed in the late 1990s for occlusal sites, was followed in the early 2000s by the **DIAGNOdent pen** for approximal sites as well (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019). It derives from the work of Hibst and Gall (Muruppel and Fried, 2023).

## Principle

A **655 nm diode laser** excites **bacterial porphyrins** (protoporphyrin IX, coproporphyrin); the emitted fluorescence passes a 680 nm filter and its intensity correlates with bacterial contamination, and so with lesion depth (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019; Muruppel and Fried, 2023).

## Technique

1. Clean the teeth; rinse off prophylaxis paste, since paste and calculus give false-positive signals.
2. Calibrate each tip on the ceramic standard, then on a sound aspect of the tooth (buccal occlusal third).
3. Occlusal: touch the sapphire tip to the fissure without pressure and tilt it in a circle.
4. Approximal: insert the wedge tip below the contact from buccal and from lingual; record the higher peak.
5. Note the peak reading (0-99) (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019).

## Accuracy

Sensitivity 0.68-0.96 (enamel) and 0.67-0.81 (dentine); specificity 0.6-0.85 and 0.79-0.87 (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019). Occlusal enamel: 79% and 74% (Caneppele et al., 2020). It is more sensitive than traditional methods, but **false positives** limit it as a principal method (Ritter, Boushell and Walter, 2019); hence an **adjunct, second-opinion device** (Muruppel and Fried, 2023; Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019).

## Advantages and limitations

- **Advantages:** no ionising radiation; lesions can be monitored (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019).
- **Limitations:** single-point reading without image; does not detect cavitation; approximal tip too thick for tight contacts; unhelpful for secondary caries (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019); cannot separate active from inactive lesions (Caneppele et al., 2020) or superficial from deep dentine caries (Muruppel and Fried, 2023).
- **Root caries, books differ:** evidence discouraging (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) versus reliable with high sensitivity in a 2021 clinical study (Muruppel and Fried, 2023).

## Related fluorescence methods

- **QLF:** 488 nm laser autofluorescence of enamel; unlike DIAGNOdent, does not correlate with dentine decay (Muruppel and Fried, 2023).
- **Dye-enhanced laser fluorescence:** demineralised tissue absorbs fluorescent dye and fluoresces strongly (Sikri, 2020).
- **LED devices:** Midwest Caries I.D. replaced the laser with a red LED (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019); VistaProof (405 nm) shows caries red (Caneppele et al., 2020); FACE shows infected dentine red during excavation (Lennon and Buchalla, 2019).

## Conclusion

Laser fluorescence gives a quantitative, radiation-free second opinion for occlusal and approximal caries, but needs clean teeth, calibration and clinical confirmation.
