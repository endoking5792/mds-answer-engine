# Laser fluorescence-based methods of caries detection

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Introduction → principle → technique → readings → accuracy → advantages and limitations → related methods → conclusion.

## Introduction

Caries fluoresces under red light of about 650 nm. **DIAGNOdent** (KaVo) appeared in the late 1990s for occlusal sites, and the **DIAGNOdent pen** in the early 2000s for approximal sites as well (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019), from the work of Hibst and Gall (Muruppel and Fried, 2023).

## Principle

A **655 nm diode laser** excites **bacterial porphyrins**; fluorescence is read through a 680 nm filter, and its intensity correlates with bacterial contamination and so with lesion depth (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019; Muruppel and Fried, 2023).

## Technique (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019)

1. Clean teeth (paste and calculus give false positives).
2. Calibrate each tip on the ceramic standard, then on a sound tooth surface.
3. Occlusal: place the tip without pressure and tilt it in a circle.
4. Approximal: wedge tip below the contact from buccal and lingual; take the higher value.
5. Record the peak reading (0-99).

## Readings: books differ

| Source | Sound | Enamel | Dentine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neuhaus and Lussi (2019), occlusal in vivo | 0-13 | 14-20 | >20 |
| Same, pen approximal | 0-7 | 8-16 | >16 |
| Muruppel and Fried (2023) | not stated | 5-25 | >35 |
| Sikri (2020) | -9 to 0 | 10-17 | above 17 |

## Accuracy

Sensitivity 0.68-0.96 (enamel) and 0.67-0.81 (dentine), specificity 0.6-0.85 and 0.79-0.87 (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019); occlusal enamel 79% and 74% (Caneppele et al., 2020). **False positives** limit it as a principal method (Boushell and Shugars, 2019): it is an **adjunct, second-opinion device** (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019; Muruppel and Fried, 2023).

## Advantages and limitations

- **Advantages:** no ionising radiation; monitoring possible (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019).
- **Limitations:** point reading, no image; no detection of cavitation; approximal tip too thick for tight contacts; unhelpful for secondary caries (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019); cannot separate active from inactive lesions (Caneppele et al., 2020).
- **Root caries, books differ:** evidence discouraging (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) versus reliable with high sensitivity in a recent clinical study (Muruppel and Fried, 2023).

## Related methods

- **QLF** uses 488 nm light and, unlike DIAGNOdent, does not correlate with dentine decay (Muruppel and Fried, 2023).
- **Dye-enhanced laser fluorescence:** demineralised tissue absorbs dye and fluoresces strongly (Sikri, 2020).

## Conclusion

Laser fluorescence is a quantitative, radiation-free second opinion that needs clean teeth, calibration and clinical confirmation.
