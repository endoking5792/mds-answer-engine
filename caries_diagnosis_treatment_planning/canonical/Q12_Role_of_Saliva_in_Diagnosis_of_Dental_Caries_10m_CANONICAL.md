# Role of saliva in diagnosis of dental caries

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Introduction → clinical signs of hyposalivation → flow rate → buffer capacity and pH → microbial counts → biomarkers → diagnostic value → conclusion.

## Introduction

Saliva is **nature's anticaries agent**; susceptibility depends more on its **quantity** than composition (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019). Salivary analysis therefore assesses risk and explains activity rather than detecting lesions; it is indicated with hyposalivation (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019) or unexplained high caries (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).

## Clinical signs of hyposalivation

Mirror sticking, frothy saliva, no floor-of-mouth pooling (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016); droplets from lower-lip minor glands taking over 60 seconds; ropey saliva (Ngo, Wolff and Hume, 2016).

## Salivary flow rate

After 90 minutes without food or drink, resting saliva is collected for 10 minutes, then paraffin-stimulated for 5 minutes (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). **Stimulated flow is the factor most strongly linked with caries** (Ngo, Wolff and Hume, 2016).

| Source | Normal stimulated | Low |
|---|---|---|
| Kidd and Fejerskov (2016) | 1–2 ml/min (resting 0.3–0.5) | — |
| Ngo, Wolff and Hume (2016) | — | < 0.7 ml/min |
| Amaechi et al (2013) | — | < 0.7 stimulated, < 0.2 resting |
| Torres et al (2020) | 1.5–3 ml/min | 0.05–0.5 severe |

## Buffer capacity and pH

Bicarbonate, the main buffer (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019), rises with stimulation (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). Buffer capacity (lactic acid needed to drop pH from 7.0 to 6.0) is **inversely related to caries** (Sikri, 2020). Healthy pH is 6.0–7.5 (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016); resting pH below 5.8 scores red, above 6.8 green (Ngo, Wolff and Hume, 2016).

## Salivary microbial counts

*S. mutans* and lactobacilli above 10⁵ CFU mean high risk; high lactobacilli suggest a sugary diet (Ngo, Wolff and Hume, 2016) and progression of initial lesions (Caneppele et al, 2020).

## Biomarkers

Antibacterial proteins (lysozyme, lactoperoxidase, lactoferrin) are similar in caries-active and caries-free people (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019). Kidd and Fejerskov (2016) call the biomarker search nonsense; Ngo, Wolff and Hume (2016) expect antibody- or enzyme-based chairside tests.

## Diagnostic value: books differ

- Low counts predict low risk, high counts not high risk (Amaechi et al, 2013; Ngo, Wolff and Hume, 2016).
- Predictive value is unproven (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019) or poor (Ricketts, Chadwick and Hall, 2011); *S. mutans* is not universally reliable (Love and Tanner, 2017).

## Conclusion

Flow rate is most useful; buffer, pH and counts support risk profiling and motivation.
