# Caries Risk Assessment.

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Definition → risk factors, indicators and protective factors → components of assessment → tools → risk categories → risk-based management → conclusion.

## Definition

**Caries risk** is the probability of an individual developing a certain number of lesions reaching a given stage during a specified period (Amaechi et al, 2013); its assessment predicts caries before the disease appears (Sikri, 2020).

## Risk factors, indicators and protective factors

- **Risk factor:** directly increases the probability of disease, e.g. sucrose frequency, low fluoride, hyposalivation (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019).
- **Risk indicator:** a disease sign: white or brown spots, cavities, restorations in the past 3 years. **Past caries experience is the best predictor** (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019).
- **Protective factors** (fluoride, saliva) are weighed against pathologic factors in the **caries balance** (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019).
- High-risk thresholds: resting flow < 0.2 and stimulated < 0.7 mL/min; carbohydrate intake > 7 times per day (Amaechi et al, 2013).

## Components of assessment

Subjective (history), objective (examination, saliva) and synthesis (overall risk) sections (Amaechi et al, 2013). Salivary and bacterial tests have unproven predictive value (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019).

## Tools

| Tool | Features |
|---|---|
| Cariogram | Nine weighted factors; "chance of avoiding caries"; no social factors (Ngo, Wolff and Hume, 2016) |
| CAMBRA | Any new lesion in 3 years = high; plus hyposalivation = extreme (Ngo, Wolff and Hume, 2016) |
| ADA form | Lesions in last 3 years: one or two moderate, three or more high (age 6+) (Amaechi et al, 2013) |
| Traffic Light-Matrix | Red/yellow/green per factor: 15 factors (Ngo, Wolff and Hume, 2016) or nineteen (Sikri, 2020) |
| ICCMS | Links detection and assessment to individual management (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019) |

## Risk categories: books differ

CAMBRA uses four levels, ADA three; Banerjee and Watson (2015) find low/high sufficient (high: two or more new or restored lesions in 2 years).

## Risk-based management

| Risk | Recall | Bitewings |
|---|---|---|
| High | 3-month; fluoride varnish; 5000 ppm paste | 6–12 months (Ferreira Zandoná and Ritter, 2019); 3 to 6 months (Amaechi et al, 2013) |
| Moderate | 4–6-month | 12 to 18 months (Amaechi et al, 2013) |
| Low | 9–12-month | 24 months (Amaechi et al, 2013) |

Recall should not be fixed at 6 months; risk is reassessed as it changes (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

## Conclusion

No single factor predicts caries well (Ngo, Wolff and Hume, 2016); assessment individualises prevention and recall, while controlling present lesions remains the priority (Amaechi et al, 2013).
