# Discuss latest developments in diagnostic aids in conservative dentistry.

**Marks:** 25 · Long essay

## Introduction

**Diagnostic aids** supplement the history, visual-tactile examination and conventional radiographs. New aids arose because conventional methods miss early disease: a radiolucency appears only after about **25% mineral loss** (Boushell and Shugars, 2019), conventional methods work only once at least one-third of the enamel thickness is involved (Sikri, 2020), and the sharp explorer is now **contraindicated** for pit-and-fissure caries detection (Boushell and Shugars, 2019). An ideal aid is accurate, sensitive, specific and reproducible, carries no micro-organisms between sites, and is manageable in time and cost (Sikri, 2020).

## Classification of recent diagnostic aids

| Purpose | Developments |
|---|---|
| Caries: X-ray based | Digital radiography (CCD/CMOS sensors, phosphor plates), computer-aided analysis, subtraction radiography, CBCT |
| Caries: light based | FOTI/DIFOTI, near-infrared transillumination (NILT), QLF, DIAGNOdent, LED fluorescence camera, NIR reflectance, OCT |
| Caries: electrical | Electronic caries monitor (ECM), impedance spectroscopy (CarieScan) |
| Caries: other | Photothermal radiometry (Canary), ultrasound, bioluminescence (CALCIVIS) |
| Magnification and records | Loupes, operating microscope, intraoral camera, digital photography, digital impressions |
| Cracks | Transillumination, NILT, dyes, cusp-loading devices |
| Shade | Spectrophotometer, colorimeter, digital camera |

## Digital radiography and three-dimensional imaging

- **Receptors:** CCD or CMOS sensors give an instant image; phosphor plates are film-like and laser-scanned (Göstemeyer and Schwendicke, 2019; Sikri, 2020).
- **Gains:** dose per image falls by **30-80%**, no processing, easy enhancement and storage, accuracy at least equal to film; but dentists take more digital radiographs, so the real dose saving is smaller (Göstemeyer and Schwendicke, 2019).
- **Computer-aided detection** reduces observer subjectivity but is not yet more accurate than a human observer (Göstemeyer and Schwendicke, 2019). **Subtraction radiography** is superior to film for proximal and recurrent caries (Sikri, 2020).
- **CBCT:** dose 10-100 times that of bitewings (Göstemeyer and Schwendicke, 2019); sensitivity 0.44-0.61 against 0.31-0.33 for 2D images, but unreliable for recurrent caries, so caries is read only as an incidental finding on scans taken for other reasons (Price, 2019).

## Optical caries detection

Demineralised enamel scatters more light and fluoresces less than sound enamel.

| Aid | Principle | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FOTI / DIFOTI** | White light through the tooth; porous lesion appears dark on a light background | Adjunct to visual examination; DIFOTI stores images for monitoring (Hogan, Pretty and Ellwood, 2019) | No lesion depth (Sikri, 2020) |
| **NILT** (DIAGNOcam, 2012) | ~780 nm light passed through bone and tooth, imaged occlusally | X-ray free, unlimited repeatability, detects cracks (Kühnisch, 2019) | Cannot show lesion relation to pulp (Kühnisch, 2019) |
| **QLF** | Blue light; fluorescence loss ΔF gives depth (r = 0.88 with microradiography); red fluorescence ΔR gives bacterial activity | Measures mineral and bacteria together, quantitatively (Kim, 2019) | Sensitive to ambient light (Kim, 2019) |
| **DIAGNOdent** | 655 nm laser excites bacterial porphyrins; score 0-99 | Second-opinion and monitoring device (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) | Spot reading, no image, no cavitation (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) |
| **LED fluorescence camera** (Spectra) | Violet-blue light: porphyrins red, sound enamel green; scale 0-5 | Image-based (Boushell and Shugars, 2019) | Unrestored pits and fissures only |
| **NIR reflectance, OCT** | Enamel most transparent near 1300 nm; OCT gives cross-sections 3-5 mm into enamel | No stain interference; OCT images caries around restorations (Fried, 2019a; Fried, 2019b) | Not yet commercially available |

**Diagram:** tooth with three light paths: transmitted (lesion dark), reflected (lesion white), fluorescence (lesion less green, more red).

## Electrical and other physical methods

- **ECM:** conductance rises in proportion to demineralisation (Sikri, 2020).
- **CarieScan (ACIST):** colour and numerical scale with a management recommendation (Boushell and Shugars, 2019); not for secondary or root caries or restoration integrity (Sikri, 2020).
- **Canary system:** pulsed laser converted to heat and luminescence (PTR-LUM), reading up to 5 mm deep; detects secondary caries at intact restoration margins and beneath sealants (Amaechi, 2019).
- **Ultrasound:** distinguishes intact from demineralised enamel; needs glycerin coupling; cannot show activity (Yanıkoğlu and Tağtekin, 2019).
- **Bioluminescence (CALCIVIS):** a calcium-sensitive photoprotein glows over actively demineralising enamel, mapping **activity** rather than presence (Longbottom and Vernon, 2019).

## The books differ

| Point | One view | Other view |
|---|---|---|
| DIAGNOdent | Sufficient clinical validation (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) | Systematic review: false positives limit it as a principal method (Boushell and Shugars, 2019) |
| CarieScan | Sensitivity and specificity both 92.5% (Sikri, 2020) | Accuracy of FDA-approved devices not firmly established (Boushell and Shugars, 2019) |
| CBCT for caries | Widely used, particularly in caries diagnosis, though the same page says its role is not established (Sikri, 2020) | Not recommended as a primary caries technique (Price, 2019) |

## Magnification and digital documentation

Loupes (2-4×) and microscopes (2.5-20×) reveal minute lesions and spare sound tooth; intraoral and SLR cameras record surfaces for later comparison; **digital impressions** give virtual study models (Boushell and Shugars, 2019). Goldstein and Salama (2018) call the microscope and intraoral camera essential.

## Crack detection

Dyes or transillumination show the line; **cusp-loading devices** identify propagating cracks (Boushell and Shugars, 2019; Torres et al, 2020). NILT, FOTI and the intraoral camera also reveal cracks (Kühnisch, 2019; Hogan, Pretty and Ellwood, 2019; Goldstein and Salama, 2018).

## Shade-matching devices

Computer-assisted shade analysis is more accurate and consistent than the eye; 80% of patients notice a shade difference in their restorations. **Spectrophotometers** read reflectance about every 10 nm; **colorimeters** filter three or four bands; a digital camera alone is not effective. Spot devices show trends; complete-tooth devices map colour but suit only anterior teeth; translucency mapping remains inadequate (Chu et al, 2010).

## Clinical significance

- **Adjuncts only:** the standard of care remains visual inspection of clean, dry, well-lit teeth with radiographs as indicated (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
- Operative treatment is irreversible, so a **highly specific** aid (few false positives) is preferred (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
- **Pulp status** before restoration: large restorations need pulpal and periapical evaluation (Boushell and Shugars, 2019); test teeth considered for restoration, not every tooth (Torres et al, 2020).

## Conclusion

Diagnosis has moved from probe and film to non-ionising, quantitative, image-based aids that allow lesions to be monitored. None is accurate alone; findings are combined with visual examination and risk assessment.
