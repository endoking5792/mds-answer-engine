## Introduction

**Caries diagnosis** is the professional "summation of all relevant information regarding history, signs and symptoms, test results, and probable causes" to identify past or present disease (Huysmans, 2019).

## Classification of methods

Garg and Garg (2011) group them as **visual-tactile** (conventional; advances such as illumination, FOTI, DIFOTI, dyes), **radiographic** (conventional; digital, subtraction, tuned aperture CT, magnetic resonance microimaging), **electrical conductance** and **laser-based** methods.

## Detection, assessment and diagnosis

Three questions per surface (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016):

1. **Detection:** is a lesion present?
2. **Diagnosis:** is it active or arrested?
3. **Cavitation:** is the surface intact, and can the patient clean it?

**Diagram:** clean, dry surface → detection → assessment → diagnosis → no treatment / non-restorative / restore.

## Prerequisites for examination

Teeth must be **clean, dry and well illuminated** (Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona, 2019). Drying replaces pore water (refractive index 1.3) with air (1.0), increasing contrast with enamel (1.6). A white spot visible on the **wet** tooth is over halfway through enamel; one seen **only after drying** is less than halfway (Amaechi et al., 2013).

## Conventional methods

1. **History and risk factors:** age, fluoride exposure, diet, smoking, socio-economic status and general health (Singh, 2020).
2. **Visual examination:** cavitation, roughness, opacity and discoloration on clean, dried teeth (Garg and Garg, 2011).
3. **Tactile examination:** a sharp explorer "catch" is contraindicated because it can cavitate a remineralisable lesion; a **CPITN probe with a 0.5 mm ball** assesses roughness (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
4. **Bitewing radiography:** for proximal lesions, sensitivity is only **0.2-0.3** for early (enamel) lesions and **0.4-0.6** for dentine lesions, with specificity **about 0.9**; depth is scored E1, E2, D1-D3 (Schwendicke and Göstemeyer, 2019). Radiographs cannot judge activity or cavitation (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016) and underestimate lesion depth (Amaechi et al., 2013).
5. **Fibre-optic transillumination (FOTI):** the lesion appears dark on a light background; dentine lesions show an orange-brown or bluish shadow. It is non-irradiating and repeatable (Hogan, Pretty and Ellwood, 2019).
6. **Temporary tooth separation:** an orthodontic elastomeric separator left for **2-7 days** allows direct inspection of proximal surfaces (Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona, 2019).

## Visual scoring systems

- **ICDAS:** seven codes (0-6), developed from the Ekstrand-Kidd-Ricketts system and grouped clinically as initial (1-2), moderate (3-4) and extensive (5-6); pooled kappa **0.67 or above** (Ekstrand and Braga, 2019).
- **Nyvad criteria:** based on surface features (texture, lustre, location) rather than depth, so they record **activity** (Machiulskiene, 2019).
- **ADA CCS:** sound, initial, moderate and advanced, combining clinical and radiographic findings (Zeller, Young and Novy, 2019).

## Recent advances

| Method | Principle | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| DIFOTI | CCD camera replaces the eye | Images stored for monitoring (Hogan, Pretty and Ellwood, 2019) |
| Near-infrared transillumination (DIAGNOcam) | ~780 nm light through the tooth | Sensitivity 99% for proximal dentine caries (Kühnisch, 2019) |
| QLF | 405 nm blue light; fluorescence loss (ΔF) and red bacterial fluorescence (ΔR) | ΔF correlates with lesion depth, r = 0.88 (Kim, 2019) |
| Laser fluorescence (DIAGNOdent) | 655 nm laser excites bacterial porphyrins | Reading 0-99 (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) |
| Electrical conductance (ECM) | Conductance rises with porosity | Probe on the tooth surface (Mount et al., 2016) |
| Canary System | Photothermal radiometry and luminescence | Examines up to 5 mm deep (Amaechi, 2019) |
| Optical coherence tomography | Cross-sectional images of internal structure | Axial resolution better than 10 μm (Fried, 2019) |

## Where the books differ

| Issue | View 1 | View 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp explorer | Contraindicated (Boushell and Shugars, 2019); probing adds no diagnostic value (Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona, 2019) | Useful if drawn gently across the lesion (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016; Machiulskiene, 2019) |
| Magnification | Improves accuracy by 50% (Amaechi et al., 2013) | Evidence limited and mixed (Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona, 2019) |
| Drying time | 5 s per surface (Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona, 2019) | 10-15 s for Nyvad scoring (Machiulskiene, 2019) |
| Fluorescence devices | Only of research interest (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016); false positives limit DIAGNOdent as a principal method (Boushell and Shugars, 2019) | Clinical evidence supports use in primary care (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) |

Both sides agree that a probe must never be forced to "catch" a fissure.

## Clinical significance

Devices **support, not replace**, visual examination; greater sensitivity is no licence for more surgery (Ferreira Zandona and Longbottom, 2019). Diagnosis drives treatment: arrested lesions need none, active non-cavitated lesions non-restorative care, and most cavitated lesions, being non-cleansable, restoration (Fontana and Huysmans, 2019).

## Conclusion

No single method suffices: a clean, dry visual-tactile examination with a scoring system, bitewings and selected adjuncts, repeated over time, is most reliable.
