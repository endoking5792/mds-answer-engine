## Introduction

**Caries diagnosis** is the professional "summation of all relevant information regarding history, signs and symptoms, test results, and probable causes" to identify past or present disease (Huysmans, 2019). Its aim is to find lesions early enough for non-operative care.

## Detection, assessment and diagnosis

Three questions are answered for every surface (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016):

1. **Detection:** is a lesion present?
2. **Diagnosis:** is it active or arrested?
3. **Cavitation:** is the surface intact or cavitated, and can the patient clean it?

**Diagram:** flowchart. Clean, dry surface → detection → assessment (severity, activity, cavitation) → diagnosis → arrested: no treatment / active non-cavitated: non-restorative / cavitated, non-cleansable: restore.

## Prerequisites for examination

Teeth must be **clean, dry and well illuminated** (Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona, 2019). Drying replaces pore water (refractive index 1.3) with air (1.0), increasing contrast with enamel (1.6); **5 s** per surface suffices. A white spot visible on the **wet** tooth is over halfway through enamel; one seen **only after drying** is less than halfway (Amaechi et al., 2013).

## Conventional methods

1. **History and risk factors:** age, fluoride exposure, diet, smoking, socio-economic status and general health (Singh, 2020).
2. **Visual examination:** opacity, loss of lustre, discoloration, grey shadow under a marginal ridge, cavitation.
3. **Tactile examination:** a sharp explorer "catch" is contraindicated because it can cavitate a remineralisable lesion; a **CPITN probe with a 0.5 mm ball** assesses roughness (Ritter et al., 2019).
4. **Bitewing radiography:** for proximal lesions, sensitivity is only **0.2-0.3** for early (enamel) lesions and **0.4-0.6** for dentine lesions, with specificity **about 0.9**; depth is scored E1, E2, D1-D3 (Schwendicke and Göstemeyer, 2019). Radiographs cannot judge activity or cavitation (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016) and underestimate lesion depth (Amaechi et al., 2013).
5. **Fibre-optic transillumination (FOTI):** the lesion appears dark on a light background; dentine lesions show an orange-brown or bluish shadow. It is non-irradiating and repeatable (Hogan, Pretty and Ellwood, 2019).
6. **Temporary tooth separation:** an orthodontic elastomeric separator left for **2-7 days** allows direct inspection of proximal surfaces (Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona, 2019).

## Visual scoring systems

- **ICDAS:** seven codes (0-6), developed from the Ekstrand-Kidd-Ricketts system and grouped clinically as initial (1-2), moderate (3-4) and extensive (5-6); pooled kappa **0.67 or above** (Ekstrand and Braga, 2019).
- **Nyvad criteria:** based on surface features (texture, lustre, location) rather than depth, so they record **activity** (Machiulskiene, 2019).
- **ADA CCS:** sound, initial, moderate and advanced, combining clinical and radiographic findings (Zeller, Young and Novy, 2019).

## Recent advances

| Method | Principle | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| DIFOTI | CCD camera replaces the eye | Images stored for monitoring (Hogan, Pretty and Ellwood, 2019) |
| Near-infrared transillumination (DIAGNOcam) | ~780 nm light through the tooth | Sensitivity 99% for proximal dentine caries (Kühnisch, 2019) |
| QLF | 405 nm blue light; fluorescence loss (ΔF) and red bacterial fluorescence (ΔR) | ΔF correlates with lesion depth, r = 0.88 (Kim, 2019) |
| Laser fluorescence (DIAGNOdent) | 655 nm laser excites bacterial porphyrins | Reading 0-99 (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) |
| Electrical conductance (ECM) | Conductance rises with porosity | Probe on the tooth surface (Mount et al., 2016) |
| Canary System | Photothermal radiometry and luminescence | Examines up to 5 mm deep (Amaechi, 2019) |
| Optical coherence tomography | Cross-sectional images from back-scattered light | Axial resolution better than 10 μm (Fried, 2019) |
| Digital radiography | Electronic sensor, image processing | As accurate as film (Amaechi et al., 2013) |

## Where the books differ

| Issue | View 1 | View 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp explorer | Contraindicated (Ritter et al., 2019); probing adds no diagnostic value (Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona, 2019) | Useful if drawn gently across the lesion (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016; Machiulskiene, 2019) |
| Magnification | Improves accuracy by 50% (Amaechi et al., 2013) | Evidence limited and mixed (Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona, 2019) |
| Fluorescence devices | Only of research interest (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016); false positives limit DIAGNOdent as a principal method (Ritter et al., 2019) | Clinical evidence supports use in primary care (Neuhaus and Lussi, 2019) |

Both sides agree that a probe must never be forced to "catch" a fissure.

## Clinical significance

Devices **support, not replace**, visual examination; greater sensitivity is no licence for more surgery (Ferreira Zandona and Longbottom, 2019). The diagnosis drives treatment: arrested lesions need no intervention, active non-cavitated lesions need non-restorative care, and most cavitated lesions are non-cleansable and need restoration (Fontana and Huysmans, 2019).

## Conclusion

No single method suffices. A clean, dry visual-tactile examination with a scoring system, bitewings for proximal surfaces and selected adjuncts, repeated over time, gives the most reliable diagnosis.
