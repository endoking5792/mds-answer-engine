# Discuss importance of diagnosis and treatment planning in operative dentistry

**Marks:** 20 · Essay

**Answer blueprint:** Definitions → importance → steps from assessment to plan → factors and approach in planning → phases of the treatment plan → interdisciplinary sequencing → informed consent → conclusion.

## Definitions

- **Diagnosis:** the art or act of inferring the nature or cause of a condition from its signs and symptoms; it precedes treatment in all cases (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- **Prognosis:** the forecast of the course of disease or of the response to treatment (Banerjee and Watson, 2015), graded excellent, good, fair, poor or hopeless (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
- **Treatment plan:** a carefully sequenced series of services that eliminates or controls aetiological factors, repairs damage and creates a functional, maintainable environment (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).

## Importance

1. **Success:** operative success depends heavily on the plan (Boushell and Shugars, 2019); poorly sequenced treatment fails even when well executed (Rose, Haveman and Davis, 2013).
2. **Do no harm** (*primum non nocere*): a false-positive diagnosis means drilling a sound tooth (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
3. **Treats the cause:** lesions are the result of the disease, not its cause (Boushell and Shugars, 2019), so restoring without risk control invites recurrence.
4. **Tooth preservation:** restorations last 5 to more than 15 years and each replacement enlarges the cavity (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
5. **Prognosis before treatment:** the operator must reach a proper diagnosis and prognosis before any plan (Sikri, 2020).
6. **Efficiency and protection:** planning brings efficiency, satisfaction and lower legal risk (Sikri, 2020); the record is the basis in legal disputes (Torres et al, 2020).

## Steps from assessment to plan

Reasons for seeking care → medical and dental history → clinical examination → diagnosis → risk assessment → prognosis → treatment plan (Boushell and Shugars, 2019). The chief complaint is recorded verbatim.

**Diagram:** these seven steps as a vertical flowchart with a recall arrow back to examination.

## Planning: approach and influencing factors

- **Problem-oriented planning:** list problems, then weigh options for each; this prevents tunnel vision (Rose, Haveman and Davis, 2013).
- Intervention is recommended only when a tooth is diseased, a restoration is defective, or deterioration is likely. **No intervention** must always be one option. The plan includes strategies to reduce future risk (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
- **Influencing factors:** the patient's functional, esthetic and technical demands; the dentist's knowledge and training; laboratory support; dentist-patient compatibility; availability of specialists (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
- The plan is **dynamic**, not a static list (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).

## Phases of the treatment plan

| Phase | Aim and content (Boushell and Shugars, 2019) |
|---|---|
| Urgent | Manage pain, swelling, bleeding and infection first, after medical history review |
| Control | Remove aetiological factors and stabilise: extractions, endodontics, scaling, caries removal or arrest, replacement of defective restorations, caries-control measures |
| Re-evaluation | Time for inflammation to resolve; review pulpal response, home care and motivation |
| Definitive | Endodontic, periodontal, orthodontic, surgical and prosthodontic care, only once disease is controlled |
| Maintenance | Risk-based recall: 3-4 months if high risk, 9-12 months if low risk |

The first three phases often run together, and the principle of **greatest need** decides the order: pain first, elective esthetics last (Boushell and Shugars, 2019). Within disease control, severity, esthetics and use of time set the order, working by quadrant (Rose, Haveman and Davis, 2013).

**The books differ:**

| Point | Views |
|---|---|
| Number of phases | Five (Boushell and Shugars, 2019); six, with a separate **systemic phase** using the ASA classification (Torres et al, 2020); three (Banerjee and Watson, 2015) |
| Esthetics | Last (Boushell and Shugars, 2019); may be urgent for anterior teeth (Torres et al, 2020) |
| Low-risk recall | 9-12 months (Boushell and Shugars, 2019); about 1 year (Torres et al, 2020) |

Patients in pain cannot decide well, so urgent care precedes the definitive plan (Torres et al, 2020).

## Interdisciplinary sequencing

- **Endodontics** is completed before the restoration (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
- **Periodontics** generally precedes operative care. A tooth with a questionable periodontal prognosis should not receive an extensive restoration. After periodontal surgery, wait at least 6 weeks according to Boushell and Shugars (2019), but 3-6 months according to Torres et al (2020).
- **Orthodontics:** restore carious lesions with amalgam or composite before orthodontic treatment (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
- **Oral surgery:** remove impacted or hopeless teeth before operative treatment (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).
- **Direct before indirect** restorations where possible (Boushell and Shugars, 2019), although Torres et al (2020) accept the reverse order.

## Informed consent

The patient is told the findings, alternatives with risks and costs, and the result of no treatment; monitoring is often a reasonable alternative (Boushell and Shugars, 2019).

## Conclusion

Diagnosis decides whether to intervene, and the treatment plan decides how and in what order. Together they prevent harm, control disease and preserve tooth structure.
