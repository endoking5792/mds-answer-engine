## Evidence and uncertainty notes

- **[RECALL] / unverified:** none.
- **Interpretation / synthesis:** Flossing before examination is from Longbottom and Ferreira Zandona (Ch.2, essential before approximal examination), condensed into item 1 without a separate citation.
- **Conflicting sources:** cavitation probability by radiographic depth: Zeller et al. report Pitts and Rimmer's halves-based data (outer enamel 0%, inner enamel 10.8%, outer half dentine 40.9%, inner half dentine 100% in permanent teeth) whereas Singh gives 60-80% of outer-third-dentine (D1) lesions non-cavitated and about 20-40% cavitation at D2. Different depth scales (halves vs thirds) partly explain the gap; Schwendicke and Göstemeyer add that cavitation rates vary with caries risk (a third in low-risk to near 100% in high-risk) and are probably lower today. Shown in one line as the two figures; explanation kept here for length.
- **Numerical claims:** 0.2-0.3 and 0.9 (q17_d11_sens, q17_d11_spec); 2-7 days (q17_d2_sep); 0.7 mm (q17_d3_07); ~780 nm (q17_d15_780); 40.9% and 100% (q17_d7_409, q17_d7_100); 20-40% (q17_sgh_2040); E1-E2, D1-D3 (q17_d11_ed). Pitts and Rimmer's study year (1992) is not given in the answer.
