## STUDY MODE

### S1. Why GIC suits children [treatment relevance]
GIC bonds without much preparation, releases fluoride, tolerates moisture better than composite, and is easy to place in uncooperative children [SRC:A332]. Mount says many deciduous teeth can be restored safely with glass ionomer alone [SRC:A340]. Sikri lists Class I to VI cavities, rampant caries and nursing-bottle caries as uses in deciduous teeth [SRC:G11]. Summitt cautions that even in primary teeth fluoride-releasing materials should be used selectively, weighing the tooth's expected life against strength and wear [SRC:C161].

### S2. Sealants [clinical decision-making]
GIC sealants bond chemically to enamel and dentine, release fluoride for a long time, are more technique-tolerant, and are the material of choice for partially erupted teeth under an operculum [SRC:A329]. Recommendations include caries-active children, difficult moisture control, uncooperative patients and no light-curing equipment [SRC:A332]. The protocol is pumice cleaning, 10-20% polyacrylic acid for 10 seconds, wash, light drying [SRC:A330]. A systematic review of mostly laboratory studies found GI and resin-modified GI sealants consistently showed higher fluoride release and recharge than resin sealants [LIT:Dobrzynski2025]. Retention was poorer in earlier studies, related to first-generation GIC sealants [SRC:A331]. GIC sealants are more soluble than resin sealants but leach fluoride that may be cariostatic [SRC:C280].

### S3. Cermets [classification]
Cermet (ceramic-metal) powder is made by sintering silver or gold onto glass particles, and up to 5% titanium dioxide may be added to restore colour [SRC:A35] [SRC:A36]. Silver cermet has about 40% by weight micro-fine silver [SRC:A52]. McCabe says cermets are grey, unsuitable for aesthetic restorations, and have greater compressive strength and fatigue limit, but flexural strength and abrasion remain limited [SRC:B37] [SRC:B38]. Sikri says glass cermet is preferred for small Class I cavities in deciduous teeth and for core preparation [SRC:G30]. Fluoride release of cermet is said to be similar to other GICs by Mount [SRC:A136], but McCabe and Bonsor say silver cermets release much less [SRC:B38] [SRC:B348].

### S4. Evidence in children [prognosis]
ART with high-viscosity GIC performed no worse than conventional restorations in primary molars (single and multi-surface) in trials up to 5 years, though only 2 of 14 trials had low risk of bias [LIT:Frencken2021]. An umbrella review found GIC a good choice in both dentitions with more evidence in the primary dentition, especially ART [LIT:Panetta2024]. In molar-incisor hypomineralisation molars, 5-year survival was lower for glass hybrid (59.2%) than for fibre-reinforced composite (75.5%) [LIT:Dokumacigil2026].

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Glass ionomer cement is widely used in children because it bonds, releases fluoride, tolerates moisture and needs little cooperation [SRC:A332].

### 2. Restorations in primary teeth
- Class I to VI cavities, rampant and nursing-bottle caries [SRC:G11].
- Many deciduous teeth can be restored with GIC alone; GIC is often enough in anterior teeth [SRC:A340].
- High-viscosity GIC for posterior deciduous teeth; strength is little different from conventional GIC, but wear resistance is better [SRC:B262].
- Glass cermet for small Class I cavities in deciduous teeth [SRC:G30].
- Resin-modified GIC or composite for aesthetic anterior lesions [SRC:A334].

### 3. Stabilisation and transitional use
Rampant caries: transitional restorations of GIC or RMGIC [SRC:C2123]; hypoplastic first permanent molars temporarily restored with GIC then stainless steel crowns [SRC:A334].

### 4. Hall technique and crown cementation
Preformed metal crowns cemented with GIC over carious primary molars without excavation [SRC:G62]; GIC is the preferred cement for stainless steel crowns after pulp therapy in primary molars [SRC:A334].

### 5. Fissure sealants
- Chemical bond to enamel and dentine; prolonged fluoride release; technique tolerant [SRC:A329].
- Material of choice for partially erupted teeth under an operculum [SRC:A329].
- Protocol: pumice, 10-20% polyacrylic acid 10 seconds, wash, light dry, place [SRC:A330].
- Recommended in caries-active children and where moisture control is poor [SRC:A332].
- More soluble than resin but leaches fluoride [SRC:C280]; higher fluoride release and recharge than resin sealants in laboratory studies [LIT:Dobrzynski2025].

### 6. Cermets
| Feature | Silver cermet and glass cermet |
|---|---|
| Composition | glass sintered with silver or gold; 5% titanium dioxide whitener [SRC:A35] [SRC:A36] |
| Properties | greater compressive strength and fatigue limit; limited flexural strength [SRC:B38] |
| Disadvantage | grey colour, metal-carboxylate interface failure [SRC:G31] |
| Uses | small Class I in primary teeth, cores, root surfaces, tunnels [SRC:G30] [SRC:A136] |

### 7. Evidence
ART with high-viscosity GIC in primary molars: no significant difference from conventional restorations to 5 years [LIT:Frencken2021]; evidence best for the primary dentition [LIT:Panetta2024]; in MIH molars glass hybrid survival 59.2% against 75.5% for fibre-reinforced composite at 5 years [LIT:Dokumacigil2026].

### 8. Limitations
Wear and fracture in multi-surface or load-bearing cavities, opacity, early moisture sensitivity, need for varnish or resin coat [SRC:C161] [SRC:G14].

### 9. Conclusion
GIC is a first-line material in paediatric restorations, sealing and stabilisation; cermet is a niche variant; the evidence is best for single-surface cavities and primary teeth.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Uses: Class I-VI in primary teeth, rampant caries, transitional, Hall technique cement, sealant [SRC:G11] [SRC:G62].
- GIC sealant: material of choice for partially erupted teeth [SRC:A329].
- Cermet: glass plus sintered silver; grey; small Class I in deciduous teeth [SRC:A35] [SRC:G30].
- ART in primary molars: no significant difference from conventional to 5 years [LIT:Frencken2021].

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Using a low-powder, weak GIC as a sealant.
2. Calling cermet aesthetic.
3. Treating the evidence from primary teeth as proof for permanent molars.
