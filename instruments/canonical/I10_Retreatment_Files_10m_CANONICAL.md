## 1. Purpose and instruments

### Explanation

Cohen lists the mechanical systems available for gutta-percha removal: ordinary rotary files such as ProFile, a push-pull quarter-turn system (Canal Finder), and dedicated gutta-percha removal instruments, namely the GPX, the ProTaper Universal retreatment files and the Mtwo R (Roda et al, 2021) [SRC]. Retreatment rotaries differ from ordinary ones in having a cutting tip, which helps penetration of the root filling and is considered sufficiently safe in this use (Peters et al, 2021; Roda et al, 2021) [SRC]. Baumann and Beer describe specially shaped NiTi files for reinstrumentation (ProTaper Universal D1–D3) and the R-Endo system, whose handpiece gear is integrated with the engaging head of the file (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Retreatment files are NiTi rotary files with an active (cutting) tip designed to penetrate and remove gutta-percha and sealer from a previously filled canal (Roda et al, 2021; Peters et al, 2021).

**Systems** (Roda et al, 2021; Baumann and Beer, 2010; Sikri, 2019)
- ProTaper Universal Retreatment (D1, D2, D3).
- Mtwo R (15/0.05 and 25/0.05, active tip).
- R-Endo (integrated-gear handpiece).
- GPX (a dedicated gutta-percha removal instrument).

**Notes.** Mtwo R has an active tip that penetrates better in obturating material (Sikri, 2019). In R-Endo the handpiece gear is integrated with the file's engaging head (Baumann and Beer, 2010). GuttaCore can be removed with hand, rotary and retreatment instruments (Roda et al, 2021).

## 2. ProTaper retreatment files and technique

### Explanation

Ingle describes the ProTaper Retreatment Kit as three NiTi instruments of different length, diameter and taper: D1 16 mm, 30/09; D2 18 mm, 25/08; D3 22 mm, 20/07 (Ingle, year not printed) [SRC]. A pilot hole is made in the coronal canal with a heat carrier or the D1 instrument (Ingle, year not printed) [SRC]. Ingle advises dividing the root mentally into thirds and choosing instruments that fit passively in each, and says rotary instruments must turn at 800 to 1200 rpm to soften and engage the gutta-percha, with caution in underprepared canals (Ingle, year not printed) [SRC]. Sikri notes that ProTaper finishing files help complete removal of gutta-percha (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

**Technique**
1. Assess the level of the previous filling radiographically (Ingle, year not printed).
2. Ingle's file-and-chemical technique makes a pilot hole in the coronal canal with a heat carrier or the D1 file; for the rotary technique he advises choosing instruments that fit passively in each third of the root (Ingle, year not printed).
3. Work in thirds, with D1, then D2, then D3 [RECALL for this sequence], choosing instruments that fit passively; rotary speed of 800 to 1200 rpm softens and engages the gutta-percha (Ingle, year not printed).
4. Finish with ProTaper finishing files (Sikri, 2019) and remove residues with other means (below).

## 3. Merits and demerits

### Explanation

Ingle calls rotary instrumentation an efficient way to remove gutta-percha, but cautions in underprepared canals (Ingle, year not printed) [SRC]. Cohen reports that fracture of instruments or of the root occurred in several mechanical removal studies, and one study found no separation or canal defects when dedicated retreatment files were used (Roda et al, 2021) [SRC]. The books are not in direct contradiction; the dedicated files looked safer in one study but fracture remains a recognised risk. Residue remains: when Self-Adjusting Files followed ProTaper Universal retreatment files, canal residues fell by 66%, 68% and 81% in the coronal, apical and middle thirds compared with the retreatment files alone (Roda et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Merits**
- Active tip penetrates the filling (Roda et al, 2021); rotary instrumentation is efficient for removing gutta-percha (Ingle, year not printed).
- Dedicated files showed no separation in one study (Roda et al, 2021).

**Demerits**
- Fracture of instruments or root is reported with mechanical removal (Roda et al, 2021).
- Rotary instruments should be used with caution in underprepared canals and are generally not chosen when the canal does not accept them passively (Ingle, year not printed).
- Leaves residue; SAF used after ProTaper Universal retreatment files reduced canal residue by 66%, 68% and 81% in the coronal, apical and middle thirds compared with the retreatment files alone (Roda et al, 2021).
- Cutting tip can gouge a curved canal [RECALL].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- ProTaper Retreatment: D1 16 mm 30/09, D2 18 mm 25/08, D3 22 mm 20/07.
- Cutting (active) tip is the key difference from ordinary NiTi files.
- Speed 800–1200 rpm; passive use; work in thirds.
- Supplement with SAF or other files for residue.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
- A canal in thirds with D1, D2 and D3 tips marked at their working levels. Labels: D1, D2, D3, active tip, gutta-percha.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Giving the dimensions in the wrong order (D1 is the shortest and widest).
- Saying retreatment files have a safe, non-cutting tip.
- Claiming complete removal of gutta-percha.
