## 1. The concept: conforming shapes with a limited flute diameter

### Explanation

Peters et al describe two recent design strategies, "conforming" shaping and a limited maximum flute diameter, which provide an adaptable, larger envelope of motion while limiting dentin removal (TRUShape and XP-Shaper) (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. Most current systems have files with tapers greater than the .02 stipulated by the ISO norm, while some have variable tapers and others canal-conforming shapes with limited maximum flute diameters (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. The aim is to instrument more canal surface and to conserve dentin, but meaningful differences in clinical outcomes among design variations have yet to be ascertained (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Concept.** Conforming shaping with a limited maximum flute diameter gives an adaptable, larger envelope of motion while limiting coronal dentin removal (Peters et al, 2021). Most systems exceed the ISO .02 taper; some have variable taper or conforming shapes (Peters et al, 2021).

**Aims.** More canal surface instrumented, so that biofilm becomes susceptible to disinfection; more dentin preserved (Peters et al, 2021).

## 2. TRUShape and TruNatomy

### Explanation

Peters et al state that the strong S-shaped longitudinal design allows TRUShape to expand and contract during rotation, as described for the SAF, in an attempt to increase wall contact. It is marketed in sizes 20, 25, 30 and 40, all with a maximum fluted diameter of 0.8 mm, which limits coronal dentin removal, and used at 300 rpm it created adequate shapes in vitro (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. Its ability to create more canal wall contact has attracted attention in retreatment (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. TruNatomy includes a dedicated orifice modifier, a glide-path file, three shaping instruments, a special irrigation needle and conforming gutta-percha, and is operated at 500 rpm and 1.5 Ncm with gentle stroking (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Jain's account of TRUShape** (Jain, 2016): an S-shaped design conforming to canal areas larger than the nominal file size; claims of 36% less dentin removed, up to 32% less apical transportation and contact with up to 75% of canal walls (claims reported in the chapter, not independent data); .06 taper; orifice modifier with a 0.75 mm maximum fluted diameter.

(Peters et al, 2021.) TRUShape's limited flute diameter restricts coronal dentin removal (Peters et al, 2021).

## 3. XP-endo Shaper and Finisher (temperature-dependent alloy)

### Explanation

Peters et al describe the XP-Shaper and XP-Finisher as having strong curves in the longitudinal aspect and a small taper, so that the maximum fluted diameter is limited. The files are made of a NiTi alloy that changes conformation in the transition from room to body temperature, which leads to some stiffening and specific preparation outcomes (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. The finisher is thought of as an irrigation enhancement device and is run at about 800 rpm. Initial reports suggest that the XP-Shaper gave acceptable preparation and that the finisher had débridement potential similar to or better than other shaping and cleaning strategies (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**XP-Shaper and XP-Finisher** (Peters et al, 2021)
- Strong longitudinal curves and a small taper limit the maximum fluted diameter.
- The alloy changes conformation between room and body temperature, giving some stiffening (Peters et al, 2021); "thermally activated" is the usual trade description [RECALL].
- The finisher is considered an irrigation enhancement device, run at about 800 rpm.
- Early reports: acceptable preparation (shaper); débridement similar or better than other strategies (finisher).

## 4. Other heat-treated alloys

### Explanation

Peters et al describe Edge files, made primarily from heat-treated NiTi, with extended fatigue life reported at room temperature but limited research (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]; Vortex Blue showed greater resistance to cyclic fatigue and torque (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. Jain describes controlled-memory wire, introduced in 2010, that makes files extremely flexible without shape memory, used in HyFlex and Typhoon (Jain, 2016) [SRC]. The metallurgy is explained in I4.

#### ✎ Exam core

- *CM wire* (2010): extremely flexible, no shape memory; HyFlex, Typhoon (Jain, 2016).
- *Vortex Blue*: blue-wire version with greater cyclic fatigue and torque resistance (Peters et al, 2021).
- *Edge files*: heat-treated NiTi; extended fatigue life at room temperature; research limited (Peters et al, 2021).

## 5. Evidence and cautions

### Explanation

For the SAF, Peters et al report more wall contact than rotary files in vitro and shaping quality on a par with rotary instruments; for TRUShape they report attention to its wall contact in retreatment (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. No clinical outcome differences are shown (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Cautions**
- Data are largely in vitro or initial reports (Peters et al, 2021).
- More wall contact is a laboratory surrogate, not a proven clinical benefit [Likely] (Peters et al, 2021 on clinical outcomes).
- Thermally activated files behave differently at room and body temperature; follow the manufacturer's protocol [RECALL].
- Tango-Endo and other newer files are not described in the books used.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Conforming design + limited maximum flute diameter = more wall contact, less dentin removed.
- TRUShape: S-shaped, 0.8 mm maximum diameter, 300 rpm. TruNatomy: system, 500 rpm, 1.5 Ncm.
- XP-endo: thermally activated alloy; finisher about 800 rpm as an irrigation enhancement device.
- Outcome benefit not yet shown.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Presenting these files as proven to give better outcomes.
- Confusing XP-Shaper (shaping) and XP-Finisher (irrigation enhancement).
- Forgetting the dentin-conservation rationale.
