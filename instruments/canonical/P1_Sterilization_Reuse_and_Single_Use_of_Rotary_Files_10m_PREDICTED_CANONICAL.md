## 1. Cleaning before sterilization

### Explanation

Sikri states that in endodontics all debris must be removed from instruments before sterilization, and that the complex design of files makes debris collect along the flutes. Studies have shown that it is difficult to remove organic debris from endodontic files even with automated cleaning devices (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Ultrasonic cleaners remove biological matter with vibratory energy (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Enzyme cleaners may cause corrosion, rust, discoloration and loss of elasticity if used improperly (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Sequence** (Sikri, 2019): pre-soaking and cleaning, rinsing, drying, packaging in functional sets, then sterilization.

**Cleaning first** (Sikri, 2019)
- All debris must be removed before sterilization, because it can interfere with the process.
- Files hold debris along the flutes; it is difficult to remove completely, even with automated cleaners.
- Ultrasonic cleaning with a lid, basket and timer helps.
- Enzyme cleaners can cause corrosion, rust, discoloration and loss of elasticity if misused.

## 2. Methods and their effect on files

### Explanation

Sikri describes the autoclave as one of the most rapid and effective methods; dry heat kills bacteria without changing the sharpness of instruments; the glass bead sterilizer is considered effective only as an auxiliary method and beads under 1.0 mm may stick to instruments; and a hot salt sterilizer treats files and reamers in 5 seconds (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Peters et al report that in most studies sterilization procedures did not negatively affect torsional strength or fatigue resistance of most NiTi instruments, that austenitic and martensitic alloys behaved broadly alike, but that prolonged immersion in disinfectants, for example overnight, caused corrosion and lower torsional resistance, and that even two hours' immersion damaged the alloy of ProTaper, RaCe and ProFile (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. Chloride corrosion may lead to micropitting (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

| Method | Points (Sikri, 2019) |
|---|---|
| Autoclave | rapid and effective; steam under pressure |
| Dry heat | kills bacteria without blunting edges; no corrosion |
| Glass bead | auxiliary method only; beads may stick to instruments |
| Hot salt | files and reamers in 5 seconds; considered superior to molten-metal and glass-bead units, whose metal or beads can cling to wet instruments and clog canals |

**Effect on NiTi** (Peters et al, 2021): most studies found no negative effect of sterilization on torsional strength or fatigue resistance; chloride corrosion can cause micropitting; prolonged soaking in disinfectants, even 2 hours for some files, damages the alloy.

## 3. Reuse and single use

### Explanation

Sikri reports that because files are hard to clean, their cutting surfaces deteriorate during cleaning and sterilization and they wear in use, the Centre for Disease Control and Prevention suggests considering endodontic files as single-use devices (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Baumann and Beer add that fatigue in NiTi gives no warning, so rotary files should be inspected visually before reuse after sterilization (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Terauchi and Renton recommend discarding files used in severely curved canals (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]. Some systems are designed as single use: the SAF, and WaveOne, where a single-use file reduces instrument fatigue (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Clinical use also changes the alloy through work hardening (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Case for single use**
- Difficult to clean; cutting surface deteriorates with cleaning, sterilization and wear; CDC suggests considering files as single-use (Sikri, 2019).
- Rotary NiTi can break without warning from cyclic fatigue, whereas torsional loading may leave visible deformation, so every rotary file is inspected before reuse after sterilization (Baumann and Beer, 2010); clinical use most likely changes the alloy, potentially through work hardening (Peters et al, 2021).

**If reused**
- Inspect every file before use (Baumann and Beer, 2010); discard any deformed file [Likely].
- Terauchi and Renton advise discarding after use the reciprocating instruments used in severely curved canals (Terauchi and Renton, 2021).
- Avoid prolonged soaking in disinfectants (Peters et al, 2021). Jain notes that the fatigue and number of uses of each RaCe instrument can be controlled with the Safety MemoDisc (Jain, 2016); a fixed permitted number of uses is not stated in the books and is [RECALL].
- Keep sterilized files in their sealed sterile pack until the moment of use, and record how often each file has been used [RECALL].

**Designed single use.** SAF (Sikri, 2019); WaveOne, a single-use file that reduces fatigue (Sikri, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Clean debris first; flutes trap it.
- CDC: consider files as single use.
- Sterilization did not generally weaken NiTi in most studies; prolonged soaking in disinfectants does.
- Inspect before reuse; discard after use in severe curves.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
- Flowchart: use, clean, inspect, sterilize, decide on reuse or discard.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Omitting the cleaning step.
- Soaking NiTi files overnight in disinfectant.
- Assuming a file that looks intact is not fatigued.
