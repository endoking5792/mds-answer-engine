## 1. The two modes

### Explanation

Jain gives two distinct fracture mechanisms, torsional load and flexural cyclic fatigue; torsional load is transferred through friction against the canal wall, and flexural fatigue occurs with rotation in curved canals (Jain, 2016) [SRC]. Cyclic fatigue also occurs axially, when an instrument is bound and released by canal irregularities (Jain, 2016) [SRC]. Baumann and Beer describe the same two mechanisms and add that fatigue gives no warning, that torsional loading may leave visible deformation, and that the two interact (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Terauchi and Renton define cyclic fatigue as repetitive compressive and tensile stresses on the outer portion of a file rotating in a curved canal, and torsional failure as binding of the tip while the shank continues to rotate (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

| | Cyclic fatigue | Torsional failure |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | repeated compression and tension of a file rotating in a curve; also axial, when the file binds and releases | tip binds while the shank turns; shear when the strength is exceeded |
| Canal | curved | even straight |
| Warning | none | deformation may be visible |
| Source | Baumann and Beer, 2010; Jain, 2016; Terauchi and Renton, 2021 | same |

**Interaction.** Fatigue reduces torsional resistance and the reverse (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Terauchi and Renton, 2021). Relaxation: when the tip locks, the coronal end can often turn about two more times with reduced torque, and then torsional overload follows (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

## 2. Factors that raise or lower each mode

### Explanation

The books disagree on size and taper. Jain states that, as a general rule, flexible instruments are not very susceptible to torsional load but resist cyclic fatigue, so a file with greater taper and larger diameter is more susceptible to fatigue failure (Jain, 2016) [SRC]. Terauchi and Renton state that smaller files with smaller tapers resist cyclic fatigue better but are more susceptible to torsional fatigue, and that larger, more tapered files show the opposite (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]. The two statements agree on cyclic fatigue and size, and differ on the torsional behaviour of small files; the source texts do not reconcile them. Cohen adds that acute apical curves limit the use of higher-taper instruments because of cyclic fatigue risk (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. Fatigue is also reported to rise with torque (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Greater taper and diameter | more cyclic fatigue failure (Jain, 2016; Terauchi and Renton, 2021) |
| Smaller size and taper | more resistant to cyclic fatigue, but more susceptible to torsion (Terauchi and Renton, 2021); Jain says flexible files are little susceptible to torsion (Jain, 2016) |
| Acute apical curve | limits higher tapers (Peters et al, 2021) |
| Time in a curve | life is limited; minimise the time in the canal (Baumann and Beer, 2010) |
| Reversible deformation | about 7% before irreversible damage (Baumann and Beer, 2010) |

## 3. How resistance is tested

### Explanation

Baumann and Beer describe fatigue experiments in which the instrument life was measured on a steel model with a defined radius of curvature, with graphs of life against amplitude of oscillating movement, and separate high-cycle (more than 10,000 cycles to fatigue) and low-cycle (fewer than 10,000) fatigue (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. They state that the breakage torque of the instrument tip is measured according to ISO norms 3 mm from the tip and can be related to the working torque of each instrument (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Cohen's figures show a torque-to-failure test of a ProFile and a torque-testing platform recording torque, axial force and insertion depth (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC], and flexibility was measured by bending tests according to ISO 3630-1 (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. Cohen also reports that for hand K-type files the failure force is the same in both directions but failure occurs at half the number of rotations in the counterclockwise direction (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Tests described in the books**
- *Cyclic fatigue*: rotate the file in a steel model with a fixed radius of curvature and count rotations or time to fracture; results are expressed against curvature and amplitude; high-cycle against low-cycle fatigue (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- *Torsional strength*: torque to failure measured 3 mm from the tip (D3) under the ISO norm; compare with the working torque (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Peters et al, 2021).
- *Bending*: flexibility tested to ISO 3630-1 (Peters et al, 2021).
- *Platform tests*: torque, axial force and insertion depth recorded during instrumentation (Peters et al, 2021).
- *Direction*: hand K-files fail in the counterclockwise direction at half the rotations needed clockwise (Peters et al, 2021).

**Limit of such tests.** They are laboratory models; the books present no clinical test of fatigue [Likely].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Two modes: cyclic fatigue (curves, no warning) and torsional overload (tip locks).
- They interact: each lowers resistance to the other.
- Taper and diameter increase cyclic fatigue failure; books differ on torsion in small files.
- Breakage torque is measured at D3 per ISO; working torque often exceeds it.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
- A file bent in a curve with tension on the outer side and compression on the inner side. Labels: tension, compression, locked tip, torque.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Calling NiTi fracture always visible.
- Treating the modes as independent.
- Quoting a fixed number of rotations to failure, which depends on the model.
