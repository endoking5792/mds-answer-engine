## 1. Design and mode of action

### Explanation

Sikri describes the Self-Adjusting File (ReDent-Nova) as a hollow, compressible, thin-walled pointed cylinder whose internal part is a NiTi lattice, with an asymmetrically positioned tip and a continuous flow of irrigant through the hollow tube (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Cohen's chapter calls it a completely different approach in both file design and mode of operation (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC]. Ingle dates its introduction to 2010 (Ingle, year not printed) [SRC].

The file is compressed to enter a canal first prepared with a #20 K-file, then attempts to regain its original dimensions and so presses on the canal walls with a constant delicate force; in a round canal it takes a round cross-section and in an oval canal an oval one (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. The surface is lightly abrasive, so it removes dentin by a back-and-forth grinding motion, driven by an in-and-out vibrating handpiece at 3,000 to 5,000 vibrations a minute with 0.4 mm amplitude (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Cohen gives about 4,000 movements a minute at 0.4 mm and the same #20 glide path (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Design** (Sikri, 2019): a hollow, compressible, thin-walled NiTi lattice cylinder, 1.5 or 2.0 mm in diameter, with a lightly abrasive surface and an asymmetrically positioned tip. Irrigant flows continuously through the tube.

**Technique** (Sikri, 2019; Peters et al, 2021):
1. Glide path to a #20 K-file (1.5 mm file).
2. Insert the compressed file with vibration (3,000–5,000 per minute, 0.4 mm amplitude) to working length.
3. Continuous irrigation, 1–10 ml a minute.
4. Two cycles of 2 minutes (4 minutes per canal); single use.

## 2. Merits

### Explanation

Sikri lists these advantages. The file adapts to the three-dimensional canal and removes a uniform layer of dentin, giving a more uniform remaining dentin thickness, whereas rotary files follow the widest part of the canal and may leave thin mesial and distal walls (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. It keeps the apical part of curved canals closer to the original shape, which limits transportation (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Fatigue tests reported rotation for more than 150 hours at 900 rpm with a 5.0 mm deflection with no mechanical failure, and a damaged file can be retrieved easily (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Continuous irrigation with 3.0% sodium hypochlorite and 17% EDTA left the canal, including the apical third, free of debris and smear layer in the study Sikri quotes (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Cohen reports from in vitro data more wall contact than with rotary files, better débridement, superior antimicrobial efficacy and shaping on a par with rotary instruments (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Merits**
- Adapts to round, oval and flat canals; uniform dentin removal and uniform remaining wall thickness (Sikri, 2019).
- Less transportation in curved canals (Sikri, 2019).
- High fatigue durability; failure is by detachment of a lattice arch, and the file is easily retrieved (Sikri, 2019).
- Continuous irrigation with any irrigant; clean canals with smear layer removed (Sikri, 2019).
- More wall contact, better débridement and antimicrobial effect in vitro; shaping equal to rotary (Peters et al, 2021).

| Feature | Rotary NiTi file | Self-adjusting file |
|---|---|---|
| Core | Solid metal core | No solid metal shaft; hollow lattice (Sikri, 2019) |
| Motion | Continuous or reciprocating rotation | In-and-out vibration (Sikri, 2019) |
| Canal shape | Follows the widest part of the canal and machines it (Sikri, 2019) | Adapts to the cross-section (Sikri, 2019) |
| Irrigation | Between instrument passes [RECALL] | Continuous through the tube (Sikri, 2019) |

## 3. Demerits

### Explanation

Sikri does not list limitations. Ingle states that no NiTi system, SAF among them, was able to clean and prepare all the isthmi (Ingle, year not printed) [SRC]. Cohen's data are in vitro (Peters et al, 2021) [SRC], and Sikri's clinical support is described as initial studies only (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. The other limitations below are [RECALL].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Demerits**
- Evidence is largely in vitro; clinical studies are initial (Sikri, 2019; Peters et al, 2021).
- Needs a glide path to #20 before use (Peters et al, 2021), so it cannot be used in tight canals without prior enlargement.
- Needs a special handpiece and an irrigation pump (Sikri, 2019).
- Does not clean all isthmi (Ingle, year not printed).
- Single use only (Sikri, 2019): cost [RECALL].
- Prepared shape follows the canal and gives little apical taper for obturation; a learning curve for the irrigation set-up [RECALL].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Hollow NiTi lattice, 1.5 or 2.0 mm, vibrating 3,000–5,000 per minute, 0.4 mm amplitude.
- Glide path #20 first; 4 minutes per canal; single use.
- Merit: uniform dentin removal, adapts to oval canals, continuous irrigation.
- Demerit: in vitro evidence, special equipment, isthmi not cleaned.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
- A hollow lattice tube in a flat (oval) canal, compressed, with irrigant entering the lumen. Labels: lattice, lumen, irrigant, canal wall.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Calling it a rotary file: it vibrates in and out and does not rotate in the canal.
- Listing only merits.
- Forgetting the #20 glide path requirement.
