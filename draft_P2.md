## STUDY MODE

### S1. The one-line difference [basic science]
Resin-modified GIC is conventional GIC with 15-25% resin added, so it sets by acid-base reaction plus light-activated polymerisation, rather than by acid-base reaction alone [SRC:A201] [SRC:A84]. The aim was a longer working time with a quick true set and immediate finishing [SRC:E201].

### S2. Numbers from one manufacturer pair
Van Noort compares one maker's conventional and resin-modified products: working time 2 minutes against 3 minutes 45 seconds; setting time 4 minutes against 20 seconds; compressive strength 202 against 242 MPa; diametral tensile strength 16 against 37 MPa; shear bond to bovine enamel 4.6 against 11.3 MPa and to bovine dentine 4.3 against 8.2 MPa [SRC:B269].

### S3. What stays the same and what is lost
Both bond to tooth by ion exchange; the resin-modified type bonds slightly differently, with fewer free carboxyl groups and polymerisation shrinkage [SRC:B364]. Resin-modified GIC may be etched without harm and bonds to composite [SRC:B412]. The cost is HEMA: water uptake with swelling, possible cytotoxicity and less ion exchange as resin content rises [SRC:A92] [SRC:A66] [SRC:C188].

### 1. Introduction
Conventional GIC sets by acid-base reaction alone; resin-modified GIC adds a water-soluble resin (HEMA) so it also sets by light, giving a command set and early water resistance [SRC:A201] [SRC:G32].

### 3. Advantages of resin-modified over conventional
Command set, early water resistance, better aesthetics, higher strength and bond, immediate finishing, bond to composite as a base, repairability [SRC:G33] [SRC:B412].

### 4. Disadvantages of resin-modified
HEMA-related water uptake and swelling, discolouration after years, polymerisation shrinkage, possible cytotoxicity near the pulp, less ion exchange with more resin [SRC:A92] [SRC:C2120] [SRC:B364] [SRC:A66].

### 5. Choice
Conventional GIC where ion exchange, fluoride and simplicity matter (luting, transitional, ART); resin-modified GIC where aesthetics, early water resistance and finishing matter (Class III and V, sandwich base) [SRC:G51] [SRC:G54]. Mount notes that resin can overwhelm ion exchange, so resin-free auto-cure materials remain preferred where the biological aim is dominant [SRC:A197].

### 6. Conclusion
Resin modification trades some ion-exchange purity for handling, aesthetics and early strength; neither type replaces the other.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

- Cost: HEMA water uptake, cytotoxicity, less ion exchange [SRC:A92] [SRC:C188] [SRC:A66].
