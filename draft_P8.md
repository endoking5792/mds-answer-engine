## STUDY MODE

### S1. Compomer [basic science]
A compomer is a resin-matrix composite with ion-leachable aluminosilicate glass and acid-modified methacrylate monomers. It sets by free-radical polymerisation, not by acid-base reaction, because there is no water in the pack [SRC:B54]. Bhat gives the composition as dimethacrylate monomers with two carboxylic groups plus partly silanised glass filler, no water, and says it cannot bond to hard tooth tissue and releases much less fluoride than conventional GIC [SRC:B194]. Sikri says its properties are inferior to hybrid composites, though bond strengths are comparable to GIC [SRC:G34].

### S2. Giomer
A giomer is a one-paste, light-cured resin material with pre-reacted glass-ionomer (PRG) filler: the glass is reacted with polyacid before it is blended with the resin [SRC:B59] [SRC:G35]. There are surface-reacted (S-PRG) and fully reacted (F-PRG) fillers [SRC:B195]. It needs a bonding agent [SRC:G35]. Giomers do contain glass-ionomer components but cannot be called compomers because the acid-base reaction has already occurred [SRC:G35].

### S3. How they differ from true GIC [classification]
Mount says that the critical difference is that glass ionomers and resin-modified GIC set in the dark by acid-base reaction while compomers do not [SRC:A210]. A compomer cannot bond by ion exchange and gives no remineralising ion exchange [SRC:A209]. Compared with GIC, compomers have better aesthetics and mechanical properties, stain more, need water uptake for fluoride release and are usually used with etching [SRC:B411].

### S4. Clinical performance [prognosis]
Summitt reports poor Class II marginal adaptation of compomers at 6 months and 16% failure at 4 years in posterior teeth, so they should not be used in load-bearing Class I or II areas of permanent teeth, though they can be used in Class V and open-sandwich Class II [SRC:C162]. A meta-analysis of RCTs found no benefit of giomers on secondary caries (RR 1.09) while GICs did (RR 0.36) [LIT:Zailai2026].

### 1. Introduction
Compomers (polyacid-modified composite resins) and giomers are resin-based fluoride-releasing restoratives that borrow glass-ionomer components; neither is a true glass ionomer [SRC:A208] [SRC:G34].

### 2. Compomer
- **Composition:** dimethacrylate monomers with carboxylic groups, ion-leachable glass (partly silanated), initiators; no water [SRC:B194] [SRC:G34].
- **Setting:** free-radical polymerisation (light or chemical); acid-base reaction minimal and delayed until water uptake [SRC:B54] [SRC:A209].
- **Adhesion:** none intrinsic; needs a dentine bonding agent; usually etched [SRC:B388] [SRC:B294].
- **Fluoride:** no initial burst, low sustained release, much less than GIC [SRC:B76] [SRC:B194].
- **Shrinkage and water:** about 2-2.5% shrinkage; water sorption compensates in days [SRC:B294].
- **Uses:** low-stress Class III and V, primary teeth, Class II open sandwich; not Class I or II in load-bearing permanent teeth [SRC:C162] [SRC:B531].
- **Luting:** cast alloy crowns, PFM, gold inlays; all-ceramic generally contraindicated [SRC:B122].

### 3. Giomer
- **Composition:** surface- or fully pre-reacted glass-ionomer filler in a dimethacrylate resin matrix; one paste; Beautifil, Reactmer [SRC:B59] [SRC:G35].
- **Setting:** light polymerisation; acid-base reaction completed before blending [SRC:B59].
- **Adhesion:** needs bonding agent; single-step bonding agents give bond comparable to compomer [SRC:G35].
- **Properties:** smooth finish, good aesthetics, fluoride release and recharge claimed [SRC:G35].
- **Disadvantages:** little benefit for recurrent caries risk; expansion, so avoid under thin unsupported enamel [SRC:G35].

### 4. Comparison with glass ionomer
| Feature | Glass ionomer | Compomer | Giomer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Matrix | water-based polysalt [SRC:A78] | resin [SRC:B54] | resin [SRC:B59] |
| Setting | acid-base, dark set [SRC:A210] | polymerisation [SRC:B54] | polymerisation [SRC:B59] |
| Bond to tooth | chemical [SRC:C1122] | needs adhesive [SRC:B388] | needs adhesive [SRC:G35] |
| Fluoride | high, recharges [SRC:G23] | low, slow [SRC:B76] | comparable to compomer [SRC:B77] |
| Aesthetics | opaque [SRC:G29] | better [SRC:B411] | good [SRC:G35] |

### 5. Evidence
Compomers: poor Class II marginal adaptation at 6 months and 16% failure at 4 years in posterior teeth [SRC:C162]. Giomers: no secondary caries benefit in a meta-analysis (RR 1.09) [LIT:Zailai2026]. Mount & Hume note compomers are largely no longer available [SRC:A23].

### 6. Conclusion
Compomers and giomers are composite-like materials that release some fluoride; they lack the ion-exchange bond and the dark set of true glass ionomers, and are suited to low-stress sites.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Compomer = composite plus acid monomers plus glass; no water; sets by polymerisation [SRC:B54].
- Giomer = pre-reacted glass-ionomer filler in resin; needs bonding agent [SRC:G35].
- Neither bonds by ion exchange; neither sets in the dark like GIC [SRC:A209] [SRC:A210].
- Compomer: not for load-bearing Class I or II [SRC:C162].
