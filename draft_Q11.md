## STUDY MODE

### S1. What it is and why it was made [basic science]
Conventional GIC sets slowly and is water sensitive early, so manufacturers added a small amount of resin to give a "command set" with light [SRC:B189] [SRC:E201]. Sikri describes HEMA grafted onto the polyacrylic acid; light polymerises the methacrylate groups, then the acid-base reaction continues [SRC:G32].

### S2. Composition and setting
The liquid holds 15-25% resin (HEMA and other methacrylates) as well as polyacid, tartaric acid and water; the powder is a glass with light initiators [SRC:A201] [SRC:G16]. Setting has two reactions: acid-base and free-radical polymerisation. Any resin not set by light auto-cures by redox, so the material sets in the dark too [SRC:A203]. More resin means less acid-base reaction, until the glass-ionomer benefits are lost and the material is only a light-cured cement [SRC:A66].

### S3. Properties [treatment relevance]
Advantages: stronger and nearly insoluble, early water resistance, better aesthetics, long working time with snap set, immediate finishing, repairable, chemical and micromechanical bonding, less post-operative sensitivity [SRC:G33]. Fluoride release is similar to conventional GIC, slightly higher in the first weeks [SRC:B369]. Disadvantages: HEMA is hydrophilic, so it takes up water over 5-7 days with swelling and stain uptake [SRC:A92]; residual HEMA is 4.5-15% and can release into dentine [SRC:A202]; its colour can change after years [SRC:C276].

### S4. Uses and cautions
Used for Class III and V, liners, bases and bonding composite in sandwich restorations [SRC:G33] [SRC:G51]. Light only cures to the depth of penetration, so build up in increments [SRC:A88]. Do not use a dentine bonding agent before hybrid ionomer, since it reduces fluoride release [SRC:B140]. Avoid under all-ceramic restorations because of hygroscopic expansion [SRC:C2158].

### S5. Biocompatibility
HEMA release is linked to cytotoxicity in laboratory tests [LIT:Agha2021]; experimental RMGICs with less HEMA gave far higher cell viability than a commercial RMGIC liner in cell culture [LIT:Potiprapanpong2023].

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Resin-modified (light-cure or hybrid) glass ionomer cement is conventional GIC with a small amount of water-soluble resin, usually HEMA, added so that it sets by light as well as by acid-base reaction [SRC:G32] [SRC:E197]. It was introduced in the late 1980s or early 1990s [SRC:E201] [SRC:B428].

### 2. Composition
- **Powder:** ion-leachable fluoroaluminosilicate glass plus light-curing initiators [SRC:G16].
- **Liquid:** polyacrylic acid (some with methacrylate side groups), HEMA and other methacrylates, 15-25% resin, tartaric acid, water [SRC:A201].
- **Alternative names:** hybrid ionomer, resin ionomer, dual-cure and tri-cure GIC [SRC:B189].

### 3. Setting reaction
1. Light initiates polymerisation of the methacrylate groups, giving a quick set to the depth the light reaches.
2. Acid-base reaction continues in the dark between glass and polyacid.
3. Unreacted resin auto-cures by redox [SRC:A203] [SRC:G16].
4. Matures over days and weeks [SRC:A87].

### 3A. Variants
- **Resin-modified auto-cure:** very fine powder with a catalyst so that it sets hard without light; useful in minimal cavities where light does not penetrate [SRC:A205].
- **Light-sensitive powders:** some contain a light activator, so dispense just before mixing; capsules avoid the problem [SRC:A204].
- **Dual cure restoratives** show only very small water uptake (volume increase about 3%, of no clinical importance), unlike early dual-cure linings [SRC:A63].
- **Shrinkage:** light activation gives up to 1% shrinkage of the resin part, with the acid-base reaction continuing after [SRC:A62].

### 4. Properties
| Property | RMGIC |
|---|---|
| Strength | stronger than conventional; tensile 2,000-3,000 psi against 900-960 [SRC:G20] |
| Solubility | 0.08% against 0.3-0.5% [SRC:G20] |
| Working and set | long working time, snap set with light [SRC:G33] |
| Adhesion | chemical plus micromechanical; excellent bond to composite as a base [SRC:G33] |
| Fluoride | similar to conventional, slightly higher early [SRC:B369] |
| Water | rapid uptake over 5-7 days, swelling [SRC:A92] |

### 5. Advantages
Better aesthetics, early water resistance, immediate finishing, repairable, minimal post-operative sensitivity, condensable [SRC:G33].

### 6. Disadvantages
HEMA water uptake, swelling, stain, long-term colour change; polymerisation shrinkage; residual HEMA and possible cytotoxicity; less ion exchange as resin increases [SRC:A92] [SRC:A202] [SRC:C276] [SRC:A66].

### 7. Clinical uses
Class III and V restorations, liner and base under composite, sandwich technique, orthodontic bands, fissure sealant, primary teeth [SRC:G33] [SRC:G51].

### 8. Cautions
Use increments; do not place a bonding agent first; avoid under all-ceramic restorations; protect from dehydration [SRC:A88] [SRC:B140] [SRC:C2158] [SRC:G14].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Conventional GIC plus 15-25% HEMA-based resin; acid-base plus light, redox backup [SRC:A201] [SRC:A203].
- Advantages: early water resistance, command set, immediate finishing, better aesthetics [SRC:G33].
- Disadvantages: HEMA water uptake, swelling, colour change [SRC:A92].
- Example: Fuji II LC [SRC:G32].
