# Apexification.

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Definition → indications → materials → technique → mechanism and barrier types → time → success and drawbacks → conclusion.

## Definition

**Apexification** is "a method to induce a calcified barrier in a root with an open apex" in teeth with necrotic pulps (American Association of Endodontists, 2012), now also called **root-end closure** (Pitt Ford and Shabahang, 2002). The root does **not lengthen or thicken** (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021). Aim: a barrier against which filling is condensed without extrusion (Sikri, 2019).

## Indications and contraindications

- **Indications:** necrotic immature tooth, thin walls, no apical stop (Kahler, 2021).
- **Contraindications:** vertical root fracture, replacement resorption, very short roots, marginal periodontal breakdown, vital pulp (Pitt Ford and Shabahang, 2002).

## Materials

**Calcium hydroxide**, introduced by Kaiser in 1964 and popularised by Frank in 1966; **MTA** for one visit since Witherspoon and Ham in 2001 (Paranjpe and Noblett, 2021).

## Technique (calcium hydroxide)

1. Working length **2 mm short** of the radiographic apex (Gopikrishna, 2021).
2. Copious **0.5% NaOCl**.
3. Paste packed against apical tissue; seal.
4. Review; refill on washout.
5. Barrier seen radiographically and probed: gentle thermoplasticised obturation (Kahler, 2021; Cvek, 2007).

## Mechanism and types of barrier

High pH (12.5) kills bacteria; apical tissue forms **cementum-like hard tissue** (Cvek, 2007). Debridement and coronal seal matter most (Pitt Ford and Shabahang, 2002). The barrier is porous, **"Swiss cheese"** (Kahler, 2021).

**Frank's types** (Shabahang, 2019): (1) apex closes, canal recedes; (2) apex obliterated, canal unchanged; (3) no radiographic change, instrument meets a stop; (4) calcific bridge coronal to the apex.

## Time to barrier: books differ

| Source | Time |
|---|---|
| Gopikrishna (2021) | 6 months-4 years |
| Shabahang (2019) | 3 months-2 years |
| Diogenes, Botero and Kang (2021) | 3-9 months |
| Sikri (2019) | 9-18 months |
| Cvek (2007) | 6-18 months |

Dressing change: 3-monthly (Gopikrishna, 2021), 6-8 weekly (Sikri, 2019) or only on washout (Kahler, 2021); changing may even be counterproductive (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).

## Success and drawbacks

**Success:** no signs or symptoms, barrier present (Pitt Ford and Shabahang, 2002); no resorption or fracture (Sikri, 2019); **79-96%** (Cvek, 2007). **Drawbacks:** many visits, reinfection (Shabahang, 2019); cervical fracture **77%** in the least developed roots (Cvek, 2007).

## Conclusion

Calcium hydroxide apexification works but is slow and leaves a thin, fracture-prone root; the MTA plug is the one-visit alternative.
