## Explanation

**Definition: procedure or process?** The AAE glossary calls apexogenesis "A vital pulp therapy procedure performed to encourage continued physiological development and formation of the root end" (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC]. Grossman defines it as the treatment of a vital pulp by capping or pulpotomy to permit continued root growth and closure of the apex (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], while the same table calls it a physiological process (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Sikri calls it a histological term for continued physiologic development of the apex (2019) [SRC] and adds that it can be regarded as a very deep pulpotomy (2019) [SRC]. The books use the word both for the treatment and for the root growth that follows; this is terminology, not a factual disagreement [Likely]. The tools are the familiar vital pulp therapies: pulp capping, sealing the pulpal wound with calcium hydroxide or MTA (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC], and pulpotomy, removing the coronal pulp to keep the radicular pulp vital (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC].

**Why the immature vital tooth is different.** Root development continues (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC] for 2 to 3 years after eruption (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC], and losing the radicular odontoblasts arrests it (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC]. Only odontoblasts can make dentine (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC], and removing the pulp in a child deprives the tooth of physiologic dentine apposition (Cvek, 2007) [SRC], leaving thin cervical walls. Sikri's goals follow from this: keep Hertwig's sheath viable for root length (2019) [SRC]; let odontoblasts thicken the root and reduce fracture (2019) [SRC]; close the root end with a natural constriction (2019) [SRC]; and form a dentine bridge at the pulpotomy site (2019) [SRC]. Even 3-4 mm of vital apical pulp can complete the apex (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; when the apical pulp survives, Weine finds the root end and canal assume a near-normal size and shape (2004) [SRC].

**Indications.** An immature tooth whose remaining pulp is vital and uninflamed, as in carious exposures (Sikri, 2019) [SRC], or a trauma case with pulp exposure (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]; Grossman lists mechanical or carious exposure of permanent teeth with incomplete roots (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Luxated immature teeth still have a good chance of pulp survival (Cvek, 2007) [SRC]. Weine would attempt pulpotomy if there is any chance at all that the apical pulp is vital (2004) [SRC].

**Contraindications.** Irreversible pulpitis (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; periradicular radiographic changes from pulpal disease (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; marked calcification of chamber or canals (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; and any tooth where degenerative or inflammatory changes are expected (Cvek, 2007) [SRC]. Bogen and Chandler go further and still aim for root-end closure in teeth with irreversible pulpitis and open apices, using MTA with pulpectomy or regenerative procedures (2019) [SRC]; that is an extension of the term beyond its glossary sense [Likely].

**Case selection.** Cold testing may be the most reliable test (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; electric testing is less accurate in immature teeth (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. After a crown fracture the pulp stays vital for up to seven days (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC], with inflammation limited to the surface 2 mm (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; Gulabivala and Ng give the same 2 mm for a proliferating exposure (2014) [SRC]. Bleeding is the chairside guide: if it cannot be stopped after 5 to 10 minutes of sodium hypochlorite, remove the coronal pulp to the floor (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021) [SRC]; Bogen and Chandler allow 10-15 minutes before changing the diagnosis to irreversible pulpitis (2019) [SRC].

**Time since exposure: the books differ.** Grossman lists treatment within 48 hours as favourable (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC] and recommends partial pulpotomy for pinpoint exposures treated within 15-18 hours (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Cvek finds neither exposure size nor time interval critical when only the superficial pulp is removed (2007) [SRC]. Kahler moves to full pulpotomy after more than 72 hours (2021) [SRC]. Cvek's statement comes from his own partial pulpotomy series, Grossman's from general capping criteria, so they measure different things [Likely].

**Techniques.**
- *Indirect pulp capping*: dentine at least 0.5 mm thick over the pulp can be capped with a bioactive material (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC].
- *Direct pulp capping*: excellent prognosis in incompletely formed teeth (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC], but after trauma the success rate (80%) is below that of partial pulpotomy (95%) (Kahler, 2021) [SRC]; Grossman gives the same pair for young permanent teeth (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].
- *Partial (Cvek) pulpotomy*: removal of tissue until normal, uninflamed pulp is reached (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], with a diamond bur at high speed (Cvek, 2007) [SRC]. Its advantage is undisturbed dentine apposition in the cervical area (Cvek, 2007) [SRC].
- *Cervical (complete) pulpotomy*: removal of the whole coronal pulp (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], in anterior teeth up to but not beyond the cervical third of the canal (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Cvek amputates to fresh bleeding tissue when the exposure is necrotic or poorly vascularised (2007) [SRC]. Diogenes, Botero and Kang cover the stumps with more than 4 mm of a tricalcium silicate (2021) [SRC].

**Depth of partial pulpotomy: the books differ slightly.** About 2 mm below the exposure (Cvek, 2007) [SRC], or 1.5-2.0 mm in his step-by-step figure (Cvek, 2007) [SRC]; about 2 mm (Bakland, 2007) [SRC]; 2 to 3 mm (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021) [SRC]; 1 to 2 mm (Kahler, 2021) [SRC]. These are overlapping ranges round the 2 mm that matches Cvek's depth of inflammation; Bogen and colleagues go deeper because inflammation can progress several millimetres into the coronal pulp (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021) [SRC], which fits carious rather than fresh traumatic exposures [Likely].

**Materials.** Calcium hydroxide: Grossman fills the chamber at least 1-2 mm deep (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], and Sikri calls it the most predictable for long-term success (2019) [SRC]; against this, its bridges have tunnel defects (Bogen and Chandler, 2019) [SRC] and Bakland advises re-entering after bridge formation to clear the necrotic layer (2007) [SRC]. MTA: mixed 3:1 with distilled water (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], at least 2 mm thick (Gopikrishna, 2021; Bakland, 2007) [SRC], and better than calcium hydroxide in Grossman's view (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Biodentine: a 3-mm layer over a partial pulpotomy in Torabinejad's case (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC]. Bismuth-containing cements carry a higher staining risk in pulpotomy (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021) [SRC], and MTA discolouration matters in the aesthetic zone (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC]. Calcium silicate pulpotomies avoid the internal resorption seen with calcium hydroxide (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021) [SRC] and succeed in 83% to 100% (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021) [SRC]. Sikri's preference for calcium hydroxide and the Cohen authors' preference for silicate cements reflect the older and newer evidence bases [Likely]. The seal: an RMGIC or compomer base (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC] and a definitive restoration at the same visit (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021) [SRC].

**Follow-up and success.** Radiographs and pulp tests every 3 months (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Sikri also recalls 3-monthly (2019) [SRC] and expects root completion in one to two years (2019) [SRC]. Root growth should keep pace with the contralateral tooth (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021) [SRC]; root-end closure is the most reliable sign of success (Bogen and Chandler, 2019) [SRC]. Cvek's criteria include continued root development (2007) [SRC] and follow-up for at least 3 years (2007) [SRC], because all failures in his series appeared within 26 months (2007) [SRC]. His partial pulpotomy series healed in 96% (2007) [SRC] and 95% (2007) [SRC]. After full pulpotomy sensibility testing is impossible (Kahler, 2021) [SRC] and the prognosis is about 75% (Kahler, 2021) [SRC]. Diogenes, Botero and Kang review at 6 and 12 months, then yearly for 4 years (2021) [SRC]. If the pulp dies, the canal is cleaned (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC] and apexification begun if the apex is still immature (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

**Root canal treatment after apexogenesis: the books disagree.** For routine pulpectomy: Cvek treats cervical pulpotomy as temporary, to be followed by pulpectomy once the root is complete (2007) [SRC], because canal obliteration and necrosis are frequent late complications (2007) [SRC]; Weine undertakes routine treatment once the apex closes (2004) [SRC], after a formocresol pulpotomy (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Undecided: Kahler reports that some authors recommend it (2021) [SRC], reasoning that pulpectomy then succeeds in about 95% (2021) [SRC]; Sikri says controversy exists (2019) [SRC] and gives the case for re-entry (2019) [SRC]. Against: Diogenes, Botero and Kang say a calcium silicate full pulpotomy may be definitive (2021) [SRC], Bogen and colleagues advise avoiding pulpectomy in vital immature teeth (2021) [SRC], and Grossman's follow-up treats the canal only on pain or pulp death (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], which implies no routine pulpectomy [Likely]. The split follows the material and depth: the routine-pulpectomy advice comes from the calcium hydroxide (Cvek) and formocresol (Weine) era and from cervical pulpotomy; the newer silicate-cement chapters do not recommend it [Likely]. This is a change in practice over time, not one book being wrong.

## ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** "A vital pulp therapy procedure performed to encourage continued physiological development and formation of the root end" (American Association of Endodontists, 2012), by capping or pulpotomy (Gopikrishna, 2021). Contrast: apexification closes the apex of a non-vital tooth.

**Rationale.** Roots develop for 2 to 3 years after eruption (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021); only odontoblasts form dentine (Shabahang, 2019); pulp removal leaves thin cervical walls (Cvek, 2007).

**Goals** (Sikri, 2019): viable Hertwig's sheath and root length; thicker dentine walls; natural apical constriction; dentine bridge.

**Indications.** Immature tooth, vital uninflamed radicular pulp, carious or traumatic exposure (Sikri, 2019); 3-4 mm of vital apical pulp suffices (Gopikrishna, 2021).
**Contraindications.** Irreversible pulpitis, periradicular change, calcified canals (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Case selection.** Cold test most reliable, electric test unreliable (Shabahang, 2019). After trauma inflammation stays within 2 mm for days (Shabahang, 2019). Haemostasis with NaOCl within 5-10 minutes, otherwise go deeper (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021).

**Techniques**
| Procedure | Depth | Success |
|---|---|---|
| Indirect pulp cap | dentine ≥0.5 mm left | — |
| Direct pulp cap | at exposure | 80% |
| Partial (Cvek) pulpotomy | 2 mm (Cvek); 2-3 mm (Bogen) | 95% |
| Cervical pulpotomy | coronal pulp to orifices | about 75% |
(Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021; Kahler, 2021; Cvek, 2007)

**Materials.** Calcium hydroxide (bridge has tunnel defects) (Bogen and Chandler, 2019); MTA at least 2 mm (Gopikrishna, 2021), may stain; Biodentine. Silicate pulpotomies succeed in 83-100% without internal resorption (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021).

**Steps (partial pulpotomy).** Anaesthesia, rubber dam; high-speed diamond with water spray to about 2 mm; haemostasis; dressing; RMGIC base and bonded restoration the same visit.

**Follow-up.** Radiograph and pulp test every 3 months (Gopikrishna, 2021); root completes in 1-2 years (Sikri, 2019) and should match the contralateral tooth. Success: no symptoms, no radiolucency, continued root growth, continuous bridge, positive sensibility, at least 3 years' review (Cvek, 2007). Necrosis: apexification.

**Root canal treatment afterwards: books differ**
- Yes: Cvek (cervical pulpotomy temporary; canal obliteration); Weine.
- Debated: Sikri; Kahler ("some authors").
- No: full silicate pulpotomy may be definitive (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021); treat only on symptoms (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Apexogenesis = vital pulp; apexification = non-vital pulp.
- Roots develop for 2-3 years after eruption; only odontoblasts thicken the walls.
- Cvek: inflammation within 2 mm, pulp vital up to 7 days; amputate about 2 mm.
- Partial pulpotomy 95% against direct capping 80% after trauma; full pulpotomy about 75%.
- MTA at least 2 mm; final restoration at the same visit.
- Review 3-monthly; follow at least 3 years; compare with the contralateral root.
- Routine pulpectomy afterwards: yes in Cvek and Weine, optional in Sikri and Kahler, no in Torabinejad and Grossman.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Using "apexogenesis" for a non-vital tooth; that is apexification or regeneration.
- Removing the whole radicular pulp in a vital immature tooth; it stops root growth.
- Taking a 2 mm partial pulpotomy deeper without checking bleeding; the books tie depth to haemostasis.
- Stating that root canal treatment must always follow; the books disagree and the newer ones do not require it.
- Leaving a temporary restoration over the dressing; the final seal belongs at the same visit.
