# Apexogenesis

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Definition → rationale → indications → techniques → materials → follow-up → later root canal treatment → conclusion.

## Definition

**Apexogenesis** is vital pulp therapy "to encourage continued physiological development and formation of the root end" (American Association of Endodontists, 2012), by **capping or pulpotomy** (Gopikrishna, 2021). Unlike apexification, the pulp is vital and the root keeps growing.

## Rationale and goals

Roots develop for **2 to 3 years after eruption** (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021); only odontoblasts form dentine (Shabahang, 2019), so pulp removal leaves thin cervical walls (Cvek, 2007). Goals: viable Hertwig's sheath, thicker walls, natural apical closure, dentine bridge (Sikri, 2019).

## Indications and contraindications

- **Indications:** immature tooth, vital uninflamed radicular pulp, carious or traumatic exposure (Sikri, 2019).
- **Contraindications:** irreversible pulpitis, periradicular changes, constricted canals (Gopikrishna, 2021); avulsion or severe luxation, crown-root fracture needing a post, unrestorable tooth (Pitt Ford and Shabahang, 2002).

Cold testing is most reliable; after trauma inflammation is limited to the surface **2 mm** (Shabahang, 2019).

## Techniques

| Procedure | Level | Success |
|---|---|---|
| Direct pulp capping | At exposure | 80% |
| Partial (Cvek) pulpotomy | About 2 mm | 95% |
| Cervical pulpotomy | Coronal pulp | About 75% |

(Kahler, 2021; Gopikrishna, 2021)

**Partial pulpotomy depth: books differ** — 1.5-2.0 mm (Cvek, 2007), 2-3 mm (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021), 3-4 mm (Kang and Bogen, 2017). If bleeding persists after 5 to 10 minutes of NaOCl, go deeper; place a definitive restoration at the same visit (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021).

**Diagram:** immature incisor marking capping and pulpotomy levels.

## Materials

Calcium hydroxide bridges show tunnel defects (Bogen and Chandler, 2019). **MTA** at least 2 mm (Gopikrishna, 2021) may stain anterior teeth (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, 2017). Calcium silicate pulpotomies succeed in **83% to 100%** (Bogen, Dammaschke and Chandler, 2021).

## Follow-up and success

Radiographs and vitality tests every 3 months (Gopikrishna, 2021). **Success:** vital pulp, no signs or symptoms, continued root growth with canal narrowing, calcific bridge (Pitt Ford and Shabahang, 2002). If the pulp dies, start apexification (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## Root canal treatment afterwards: books differ

Cvek (2007) regards cervical pulpotomy as temporary, followed by pulpectomy; Sikri (2019) notes controversy; Pitt Ford and Shabahang (2002) find no histological support for routine extirpation, and Sigurdsson and Bourguignon (2017) treat only if necrosis develops.

## Conclusion

Apexogenesis keeps the pulp vital so the root lengthens and thickens; partial pulpotomy is most successful.
