# Calcium hydroxide apexification versus the MTA apical barrier (one-visit apexification): technique, success and drawbacks

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay · Predicted question

**Answer blueprint:** Principle → comparison table → technique → books differ → evidence → conclusion.

## Principle

**Calcium hydroxide apexification** (Kaiser in 1964, Frank in 1966) is **indirect**: tissue forms the barrier over months (Paranjpe and Noblett, 2021). The **MTA apical plug** ("one-step apexification") is **direct**: an artificial barrier placed at once (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021).

## Comparison

| Feature | Calcium hydroxide | MTA apical plug |
|---|---|---|
| Visits | Many; 3-monthly reviews | One or two |
| Time to obturation | 7 ± 2.5 months; books 3 months-4 years | 0.75 ± 0.5 months |
| Barrier | Induced, porous ("Swiss cheese"), may be incomplete | Cement plug; cementum forms over it |
| Dentine | Beyond one month may weaken dentine | No detrimental effect |
| Compliance | Attrition, loss to follow-up | Little dependence |
| Cost | Low, but many visits | High |
| Discolouration | Not listed | 22.7% in one series |
| Success | 79-96% | 81-100% |
| Root growth | None | None |

(Shabahang, 2019; Cvek, 2007; Bakland, 2007; Kahler, 2021; Tonini and Garo, 2024; Buerkle and Meissner, 2010)

## Technique

**Calcium hydroxide:** length 2 mm short; disinfect; pack paste against apical tissue; seal; refill on washout; obturate when the barrier is seen and probed (Gopikrishna, 2021; Kahler, 2021).

**MTA:** disinfect; calcium hydroxide 1 week; mix **3:1**; condense with pre-fitted pluggers; confirm radiographically; check set at **48 hours**; backfill gutta-percha; composite cervically (Gopikrishna, 2021; Kahler, 2021).

**Diagram:** two incisors: induced barrier versus MTA plug with backfill.

## Books differ

- **Plug thickness:** 3-4 mm (Gopikrishna, 2021), 4 mm (Bakland, 2007), 3-5 mm (Shabahang, 2019), at least 5 mm (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021).
- **Extrusion:** beyond 2 mm may retard healing (Shabahang, 2019) or overfill does not compromise outcome (Kang and Bogen, 2017).
- **Fracture:** risk clearly diminished (Buerkle and Meissner, 2010) versus root remains thin (Kim, 2016).

## Evidence

Meta-analysis: similar success; the difference is time (Tonini and Garo, 2024). MTA succeeded in 100% against 92% at final review (Shabahang, 2019). Calcium hydroxide is no longer preferred; a calcium silicate plug is now recommended (Widbiller and Galler, 2024).

## Conclusion

MTA is faster and as successful but costs more and may stain; neither thickens the root, so cervical fracture remains the main risk.
