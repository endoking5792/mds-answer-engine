## Explanation

**Two principles.** Calcium hydroxide apexification, introduced by Kaiser in 1964 and popularised by Frank in 1966 (Paranjpe and Noblett, 2021) [SRC], is indirect: it relies on the tissues forming a hard-tissue barrier (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC]. The MTA plug is direct and needs no prior induction of hard tissue (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC]; hence the name one-step apexification (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021; Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC]. Witherspoon and Ham reported single-visit MTA apexification in 2001 (Paranjpe and Noblett, 2021) [SRC]. Bakland cites Tittle and colleagues: no induced barrier is needed because the MTA plug itself is the apical stop (2007) [SRC].

**Calcium hydroxide technique (Q03 gives it in full).** Length at least 2 mm short of the radiographic apex (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; paste packed against the apical soft tissue to start hard-tissue formation (Kahler, 2021) [SRC]; Sigurdsson and Bourguignon first disinfect with a thin mix, then pack a thicker, almost dry mix (2017) [SRC]. Grossman changes the dressing at each 3-month recall (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Kahler leaves it another 3 months if there is no washout (2021) [SRC]. The canal is filled when the barrier shows on the radiograph and can be probed (Kahler, 2021) [SRC].

**MTA technique.** Calcium hydroxide is usually placed first: Grossman advises a week of it to raise the acidic pH of inflamed tissue before MTA (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], as do Tonini and Garo (2024) [SRC]; Sigurdsson and Bourguignon medicate 7 to 14 days (2017) [SRC] because MTA has minimal bactericidal effect (2017) [SRC]. **Whether that first dressing is needed: the evidence differs.** Shabahang reports a dog study in which it was not needed for root formation (2019) [SRC] and increased extrusion of MTA beyond the walls (2019) [SRC], and another study in which a week of calcium hydroxide improved MTA adaptation (2019) [SRC]; Tonini and Garo note that some clinicians prefer a single session (2024) [SRC]. A matrix is optional: calcium sulphate sponges (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, 2017) [SRC] or a collagen membrane (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC], whereas Shabahang finds one generally unnecessary (2019) [SRC]. MTA is mixed 3:1 with sterile distilled water to wet sand (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC] and condensed with pluggers pre-fitted to about 0.5 mm from working length (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC], with ultrasonic activation if needed (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. If the plug is poor it is rinsed out and redone (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. The set cement is checked at 48 hours (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; a long canal is backfilled with gutta-percha (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC], and Kahler bonds composite resin into the cervical canal below the marginal bone (2021) [SRC].

**Plug thickness: the books differ.** 3-4 mm (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; 3-5 mm (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; 4 mm (Bakland, 2007) [SRC], placed 1 mm from the radiographic apex (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC]; 4-5 mm (Kahler, 2021) [SRC]; at least 5 mm (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC]. The ranges overlap at 4 mm; only Diogenes, Botero and Kang set a floor above it [Likely].

**Time and visits.** Calcium hydroxide takes 3-9 months (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC], 6 months to 4 years (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], 3-18 months (Kahler, 2021) [SRC] or 6-24 months (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, 2017) [SRC] (Q03 sets out all the ranges). In Pradhan's comparison, treatment took 0.75 ± 0.5 months with MTA against 7 ± 2.5 months with calcium hydroxide (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; in Damle's, the MTA barrier formed in a mean (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC] of 4.5 ± 1.6 months (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC] against 7.9 ± 2.5 for calcium hydroxide (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC]. Silicate cements cut treatment to one or two visits and lessen the risks of poor compliance and fracture (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC].

**The barrier.** The calcium hydroxide barrier has a "Swiss cheese" consistency (Kahler, 2021) [SRC], is porous and may be permeable (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC], and may be an incomplete bridge (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. Over MTA a continuous band of cementum formed in Shabahang's dog study (2019) [SRC]; the plug is itself a matrix for immediate obturation (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC] and a scaffold for a hard-tissue barrier with a better seal (Paranjpe and Noblett, 2021) [SRC].

**Dentine and fracture.** Calcium hydroxide for more than one month may change dentine structure (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; cervical fractures fell from 77% in the least developed roots to 2% in mature teeth (Cvek, 2007) [SRC]. Kahler cites a study placing the cause in the stage of root development rather than the calcium hydroxide (2021) [SRC], a disagreement between studies that the books leave open [Likely]. MTA does not appear to harm dentine (Bakland, 2007) [SRC]; with a plug the canal meets calcium hydroxide for usually less than one month (Bakland, 2007) [SRC]; and the MTA plug has been shown to increase fracture resistance (Paranjpe and Noblett, 2021) [SRC]. Neither method grows root: failures are cervical fractures rather than apical (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC], root development ceases once the pulp is lost (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC], and arrested development is a main limitation of apexification (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021) [SRC].

**Compliance and cost.** Long calcium hydroxide therapy risks patient attrition (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC] and loss to follow-up when families move (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; in one series four calcium hydroxide patients returned late with teeth no longer restorable (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. MTA's own shortcomings are high cost, difficult handling and discolouration (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC], with a long setting time (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC]; Grossman's material table also lists it as relatively expensive (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Biodentine can be used in one visit without discolouration (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC].

**Success and the evidence.** Calcium hydroxide succeeds in 79-96% (Cvek, 2007) [SRC]; MTA healing rates are 81-100% (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC]. The meta-analysis found similar success (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]: no difference in clinical or radiographic success or barrier formation (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC], the real difference being time (Tonini and Garo, 2024) [SRC]; at final follow-up MTA reached 100% against 92% (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. El-Meligy and Avery saw two calcium hydroxide teeth reinfected and none with MTA (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. A single-appointment MTA series healed 81% (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; in a retrospective study of immature teeth, 96.5% of one-visit and 89% of two-visit cases healed (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; Tonini and Garo identify it as Witherspoon and colleagues' subgroup of 119 immature teeth (2024) [SRC]. Diogenes, Botero and Kang expect the same success rate either way (2021) [SRC]. So the books agree that MTA is faster and at least as successful; they do not show it is more successful [Certain].

**MTA-specific drawbacks.** Discolouration in 22.7% in Moore's series (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC], reduced by keeping the plug to the apical 4-5 mm (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; extrusion beyond 2 mm may retard healing (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]; placement needs an operating microscope (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, 2017) [SRC], and the technique of placement matters more to leakage than the material (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC]. **Calcium hydroxide drawbacks** are variable time (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC], coronal leakage and reinfection (Shabahang, 2019) [SRC], and dissolution by exudate (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**Principle.** Calcium hydroxide (Kaiser 1964, Frank 1966) is indirect: tissue forms the barrier over months (Paranjpe and Noblett, 2021). The MTA plug ("one-step apexification", Witherspoon and Ham 2001) is direct: an artificial barrier placed at once (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021).

**Comparison**
| Feature | Calcium hydroxide | MTA apical plug |
|---|---|---|
| Visits | Many; 3-monthly reviews | One or two |
| Time to obturation | 7 ± 2.5 months (Pradhan); books 3 months-4 years | 0.75 ± 0.5 months (Pradhan) |
| Barrier | Induced, porous "Swiss cheese", may be incomplete | 3-5 mm cement; cementum forms over it |
| Dentine and fracture | Beyond one month may weaken dentine; cervical fracture 77% to 2% by root stage | No harm to dentine; may raise fracture resistance |
| Compliance | Attrition, loss to follow-up | Little dependence |
| Cost | Inexpensive [RECALL] | High |
| Discolouration | No | 22.7% in one series |
| Success | 79-96% | 81-100% |
| Root growth | None | None |
(Shabahang, 2019; Cvek, 2007; Bakland, 2007; Kahler, 2021; Tonini and Garo, 2024)

**Technique: calcium hydroxide.** Length 2 mm short; disinfect; pack paste against apical tissue; seal; review 3-monthly, refill on washout; obturate when the barrier is seen and probed (Gopikrishna, 2021; Kahler, 2021).

**Technique: MTA.** Disinfect; calcium hydroxide 1 week (Gopikrishna, 2021) or none; optional collagen or calcium sulphate matrix; mix 3:1 to wet sand; condense with pre-fitted pluggers or ultrasonics; confirm the plug radiographically; check set at 48 hours; backfill gutta-percha; bonded resin cervically (Shabahang, 2019; Kahler, 2021).

**Books differ.** Plug thickness 3-4 mm (Gopikrishna, 2021), 4 mm (Bakland, 2007), 3-5 mm (Shabahang, 2019), at least 5 mm (Diogenes, Botero and Kang, 2021). Calcium hydroxide first: needed for infected canals (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, 2017) or unnecessary (Shabahang, 2019).

**Evidence.** Meta-analysis: similar success; the difference is time (Tonini and Garo, 2024). MTA 100% against 92% at final review (Shabahang, 2019). One-visit 96.5% against two-visit 89% (Shabahang, 2019).

**Conclusion.** MTA is faster and as successful, but costs more and may stain; neither thickens the root, so cervical fracture remains the main long-term risk.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Calcium hydroxide = indirect, induced barrier; MTA = direct, artificial barrier.
- Pradhan: 0.75 months (MTA) against 7 months (calcium hydroxide).
- Meta-analysis: same success; MTA wins on time and visits.
- Plug 3-5 mm, 4 mm most often; extrusion beyond 2 mm retards healing.
- Calcium hydroxide over one month weakens dentine; fracture 77% in the least developed roots.
- MTA: cost, handling, discolouration (22.7%); Biodentine avoids staining.
- Neither method lengthens or thickens the root.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Claiming MTA is more successful; the books say equally successful but faster.
- Saying the MTA plug makes the root grow; neither method does.
- Giving one plug thickness as fixed; the books range from 3 to at least 5 mm.
- Omitting disinfection before MTA; MTA itself has little antibacterial effect.
- Forgetting cost and discolouration when listing MTA drawbacks.
