## 3. Conflicts (shown side by side in the answers)

1. **Barium sulphate in the calcium hydroxide paste** (Q03): added 9:1 for radiopacity (Pitt Ford and Shabahang, 2002) vs avoid, it hides washout (Kahler, 2021). Kahler's reason is specific to judging the dressing at recall; plain paste is the safer choice [Likely].
2. **Changing the dressing** (Q03): changes <5 months apart give faster barriers (Cvek, 2007) vs changing "counterproductive to the formation of hard tissue" (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011), with Grossman 3 months, Sikri 6-8 weeks, Buerkle and Meissner 3-6 months, Pitt Ford and Shabahang only if density falls, Kahler/Harty's only on washout. Different studies; Gutmann's supporting reference is printed as an MTA-renewal study (slip 4).
3. **Does calcium hydroxide stimulate the barrier?** (Q03): "stimulating effect" with PDL proliferation (Buerkle and Meissner, 2010) vs "mistaken belief that it stimulates hard tissue formation" (Pitt Ford and Shabahang, 2002); Cvek's antibacterial account between them.
4. **Time to barrier** (Q03): now also 13-67 weeks, mean 34.2, incisors (Gutmann and Lovdahl) and ~1 year (Buerkle and Meissner), beside the eight drafted ranges.
5. **Calcium hydroxide success rate** (Q03): 79-96% (Cvek's own chapter) vs 74-96% citing Cvek 1993 and Andreasen 2000 (Buerkle and Meissner). Not resolvable without the papers [Likely slip or different series].
6. **Root development time after eruption** (Q04): 2-3 years (Diogenes et al), ~3 years (Pitt Ford and Shabahang), 1-5 years (Kang and Bogen; all teeth incl. molars).
7. **Partial pulpotomy depth** (Q04): 1-2 mm (Kahler), 1.5-2 / ~2 mm (Cvek), 2 mm (Bakland; Sigurdsson and Bourguignon; Buerkle and Meissner), 2-3 mm (Bogen et al; Careddu et al), 3-4 mm called the "Cvek technique" (Kang and Bogen).
8. **Routine root canal treatment after apexogenesis** (Q04): yes — Cvek (cervical pulpotomy), Weine (formocresol); debated — Sikri, Kahler; no — Pitt Ford and Shabahang (histology does not support it; calcific metamorphosis is not pathosis), Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, Buerkle and Meissner, ESE, Diogenes et al, Bogen et al. The drafter's "change over time" reading is corrected: the split follows depth/material (cervical and formocresol pulpotomy vs partial pulpotomy), since a 2002 calcium hydroxide-era chapter already argued against routine extirpation.
9. **MTA compaction** (P1): ultrasonic activation if needed (Shabahang, 2019) vs manual compaction preferable, ultrasonics risk extrusion (Kang and Bogen, 2017).
10. **MTA extrusion** (P1): >2 mm may retard healing (Shabahang) vs overfill does not compromise outcome (Kang and Bogen). Neither gives numbers there; avoid extrusion [Likely].
11. **Does the MTA plug reduce fracture?** (P1): fracture danger "clearly diminished" (Buerkle and Meissner; Paranjpe and Noblett, increased resistance) vs root remains thin, neither strengthens (Kim, 2016) and thin walls still mean increased risk (Widbiller and Galler, 2024). Reconciled: removes the calcium hydroxide and open-period effect, not the thin root [Likely].
12. **MTA success** (P1): "(Still) questionable, seems to be good" (Buerkle and Meissner, 2010) vs equal success in the meta-analysis (Shabahang; Tonini and Garo) and no difference in healing/survival (Widbiller and Galler, 2024). Older vs newer evidence.
