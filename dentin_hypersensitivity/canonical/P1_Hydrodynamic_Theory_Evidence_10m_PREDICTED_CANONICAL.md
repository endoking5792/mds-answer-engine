## 1. The theory and the evidence for it

### Explanation

Gysi (1900) first speculated that sensitivity was due to stimulus-evoked fluid shifts (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], and Brännström's experiments turned the hypothesis into a theory (Addy and Gillam, 2024; Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Later animal experiments directly linked transdentinal fluid movement with excitation of the intradental nerves (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. All stimuli except heat cause outward flow (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; nerves are more sensitive to outward than inward flow (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], with a threshold near 0.75 nL/s/mm² (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. A probe pushes fluid inward (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC] and its removal causes outward flow by elastic recoil (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]; air blasting is a very effective provocation (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC].

Clinical correlations: sensitive dentine has exposed, patent tubules (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], symptom severity correlates with the number of open tubules (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], sensitive cervical tubules are about twice as wide (Sikri, 2020) [SRC], and a minimum diameter of 0.83 µm has been reported as required for DH (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC]. Flow follows the fourth power of radius (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], so halving the radius cuts flow to one-sixteenth (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC] and tubules need not be sealed completely (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Smear layers reduce hydraulic conductance (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC] 10-30 times (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. The theory explains lesion localisation and initiation (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC] and why exposed but sclerosed dentine may be painless (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Theory (Gysi 1900; Brännström 1963).** Stimuli applied to exposed dentin move the fluid in open tubules; the rapid pressure change deforms mechanoreceptor A-delta and A-beta fibres at the pulp-dentin border and causes sharp pain (Addy and Gillam, 2024).

**Evidence for**
1. **Fluid movement with every stimulus:** cold, air, probing and osmotic solutions cause outward flow; heat causes inward flow and is less painful (Addy and Gillam, 2024; Sikri, 2020).
2. **Nerve recordings:** in animals, outward flow above about 0.75 nL/s/mm² fired intradental nerves in proportion to flow; inward flow did little (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024).
3. **Structure of sensitive dentin:** more and wider (about 2×) open tubules than non-sensitive dentin; pain severity correlates with the number of open tubules (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024; Sikri, 2020).
4. **Poiseuille physics:** flow ∝ r⁴; partial occlusion is enough to stop pain.
5. **Therapeutic proof:** smear layers, oxalates and resins that cut hydraulic conductance relieve DH; exposed sclerosed dentin is painless (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024; Olley and Bartlett, 2024).

## 2. Against the alternatives, and the limits

### Explanation

Trauma that destroys the odontoblast layer does not abolish DH (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], and dentine over a disrupted odontoblast layer may be sensitive (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]; odontoblasts were considered non-excitable (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Gysi showed that cocaine applied to dentine failed to anaesthetise it (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], which argues against direct nerve endings near the surface. But Markowitz and Pashley now regard the three-theory picture as obsolete, since all three act together (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]: odontoblasts are mechanosensitive (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], release ATP (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC] through pannexin-1 onto P2X3 receptors (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], and their deletion abolished cold pain in rats (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Cold and hyperosmotic stimuli also act directly on nerve endings (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Air-blast flow is only modestly reduced by a smear layer (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], and fluid flow may also create a streaming potential (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. The role of central sensitisation is unclear (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Against the alternatives**
- Direct neural: no nerves in the outer dentin; topical anaesthetic (cocaine, Gysi) on dentin fails without pressure (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024).
- Odontoblast transducer: process reaches only one-third of dentin; destroying odontoblasts did not abolish DH in older studies (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024).

**Limits of the hydrodynamic theory (refinements)**
- Odontoblasts are mechanosensitive (piezo channels) and release ATP onto P2X3 receptors on nerves; deleting odontoblasts abolished cold-evoked pain in rats (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024).
- Cold and hyperosmotic stimuli also act directly on nerve-ending receptors.
- Air-blast evaporation still moves fluid through partly occluded tubules.
- Fluid flow generates streaming potentials, so stimulation may also be electrical (Addy and Gillam, 2024).

**Clinical implications.** Because flow ∝ r⁴, partial occlusion (smear layer, oxalate, bioactive glass) relieves pain; because air blasts move fluid even through partly occluded tubules, the air-blast test is the most demanding test of a desensitiser (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024).

**Conclusion.** The hydrodynamic mechanism is the best-supported explanation and the basis of all treatment, but it is now seen as the trigger acting through both odontoblasts and nerves, not as an alternative to them. [Likely]

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Gysi 1900 proposed it; Brännström proved it.
2. Outward flow above about 0.75 nL/s/mm² fires A-delta fibres; inward flow does little.
3. Sensitive dentin: more and wider open tubules; flow ∝ r⁴.
4. Odontoblast ATP signalling and direct nerve activation refine, not replace, the theory.
