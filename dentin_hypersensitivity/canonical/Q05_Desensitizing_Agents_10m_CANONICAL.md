## 1. Definition and ideal requirements

### Explanation

Desensitising agents are products that relieve DH by reducing dentine permeability or by acting on the intradental nerves (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. Sikri's requisites include immediate and lasting relief (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]; Mosquim lists fast action, sustained efficacy, easy application and safety to the pulp (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. No ideal product exists (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition:** agents that relieve dentin hypersensitivity by blocking fluid movement in the dentinal tubules or by reducing the excitability of the intradental nerves (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024).

**Ideal requirements (Sikri, 2020; Mosquim et al, 2024)**
- Immediate and long-lasting relief.
- Easy to apply; well tolerated; painless.
- Non-irritant to pulp and gingiva.
- No staining; inexpensive.
- Resistant to acid and toothbrushing.
No current agent meets all of these (Hill and Gillam, 2024).

**Forms available:** dentifrices and mouthrinses (low concentration, home use, slow); gels and pastes, varnishes, solutions, primers and adhesives, cements and restorative materials (high concentration, professional use, rapid) (Mosquim et al, 2024).

## 2. Classification and mechanisms

### Explanation

The most popular classification is by mechanism of action (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]; another divides agents by delivery, in-office or home use (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. Some agents work both ways (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Potassium induces depolarisation of the nerve cells (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]; potassium ions have a desensitising action in animal studies (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. Strontium most likely occludes tubules with fine particles (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. NaF precipitates CaF₂ globules (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], but the crystals are very small (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Oxalates form calcium oxalate (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC] but are acid labile (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Bioactive glass contains calcium sodium phosphosilicate (Sikri, 2020) [SRC], with silica acting as a nucleation site for calcium phosphate (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Arginine is a positively charged amino acid in saliva (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Gluma is 5% glutaraldehyde with 35% HEMA (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC]; glutaraldehyde reacts with serum albumin (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC] and plugs tubules to about 200 µm (reported as "200 mm" in the extracted text) (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC]. Silver nitrate precipitates odontoblast process proteins (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. 10-MDP adhesives bond chemically to calcium (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Potassium nitrate is the one agent in Sikri's table acting on nerves (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Mineral occlusion is not permanent (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], whereas polymeric occlusion is more acid resistant (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Classification by mechanism (Mosquim et al, 2024; Markowitz and Gillam, 2024)**

| Group | Agents | How they act |
|---|---|---|
| I. Nerve desensitisation | Potassium nitrate, chloride, citrate, oxalate; low-power lasers | K⁺ depolarises and inactivates intradental nerves |
| II. Tubule occlusion by protein precipitation | Glutaraldehyde + HEMA (Gluma), silver nitrate, zinc chloride-potassium ferrocyanide, strontium chloride | Coagulate proteins in tubular fluid; HEMA polymerises; plugs to about 200 µm |
| III. Tubule occlusion by mineral deposition | NaF, SnF₂, amine fluoride, sodium silicofluoride; oxalates; calcium hydroxide, calcium phosphates, CPP-ACP; arginine-calcium carbonate; calcium sodium phosphosilicate (NovaMin), BioMin F; nano-hydroxyapatite; high-power lasers | Precipitate CaF₂, calcium oxalate, apatite or tin compounds in tubules |
| IV. Sealing or covering dentin | Varnishes, resin sealants, dentin bonding agents, GIC, RMGIC, composite; root coverage grafts | Physical barrier or hybrid layer |

**Classification by delivery:** in-office (high concentration, immediate effect) and at-home (dentifrices, mouthrinses, gels; slower) (Addy and Gillam, 2024).

**Points for the examiner**
- Some agents act both ways: potassium oxalate (crystals + K⁺), strontium (occlusion + nerve).
- Calcium fluoride crystals are small and quickly lost; oxalate crystals are acid labile; stannous and bioactive-glass deposits resist acid better.
- Polymeric sealants resist acid but wear and may stain (Mosquim et al, 2024).
- Examples: Sensodyne (KNO₃, SnF₂, NovaMin), Colgate Sensitive Pro-Relief (arginine), Duraphat (5% NaF varnish), Gluma Desensitizer, BisBlock (oxalate), Vanish XT (RMGIC), Teethmate (calcium phosphate).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Two mechanisms: nerve desensitisation (potassium) and tubule occlusion (everything else).
2. Occlusion by protein precipitation (Gluma, silver nitrate) or mineral deposition (fluoride, oxalate, bioactive glass, arginine).
3. Ideal agent: fast, lasting, non-irritant, non-staining, cheap, acid resistant; none exists.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Putting potassium nitrate under tubule occlusion.
2. Omitting the ideal requirements.
3. Not mentioning that occlusion by mineral deposits is lost under acid challenge.
