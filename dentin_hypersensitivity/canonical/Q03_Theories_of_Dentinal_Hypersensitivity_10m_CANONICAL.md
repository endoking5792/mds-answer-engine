## 1. Background

### Explanation

Enamel is not sensitive to drilling, but dentine is (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], yet nerves in dentine are restricted to its inner half (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Three hypotheses have been proposed to explain the sensitivity of dentine (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Dentin hypersensitivity is short, sharp pain arising from exposed dentin in response to thermal, evaporative, tactile, osmotic or chemical stimuli that cannot be ascribed to any other dental defect or disease (Addy and Gillam, 2024).

**The problem the theories explain:** enamel is insensitive, dentin is very sensitive, but nerves reach only the inner half of dentin (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024).

## 2. The three theories

### Explanation

**Direct neural stimulation.** Nerves would extend to the outer dentine; histology has not shown them there, and Sikri says the theory is not accepted (Sikri, 2020) [SRC].

**Odontoblast transducer.** Odontoblasts would act as receptors linked to nerves. Odontoblasts are not considered excitable and no synapse with nerves has been shown (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]; the process extends only about one-third of the way to the DEJ (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]; trauma that destroys the odontoblast layer does not abolish DH (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Trushkowsky concludes odontoblasts cannot contribute directly to transmission (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC].

**Hydrodynamic.** Gysi proposed it in 1900 (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], speculating that sensitivity was due to stimulus-evoked fluid shifts (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]; Brännström proved it (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. All stimuli cause outward flow except heat (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], which is why cold hurts more than heat (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]; nerves respond more to outward flow (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Flow varies with the fourth power of radius (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], so doubling the diameter raises flow 16-fold (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]; sensitive teeth have tubules twice as wide (Sikri, 2020) [SRC], and dentine is sensitive only if tubules are patent to the pulp (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. The resulting pain is A-fibre pain (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]; C fibres do not respond to these innocuous stimuli (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Fluid flow also produces a streaming potential, so nerve stimulation may also be electrical (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**1. Direct neural (nerve) theory**
- Nerve endings reach the DEJ and are stimulated directly.
- Against: no nerves in outer dentin; local anaesthetics on dentin do not abolish pain. Not accepted (Sikri, 2020).

**2. Odontoblast transducer (receptor) theory**
- The odontoblast process acts as a receptor and passes the stimulus to nerves at the pulp-dentin border.
- Against: odontoblasts were considered non-excitable with no synapse; the process extends only one-third of the way; DH persists after the odontoblast layer is destroyed (Sikri, 2020; Markowitz and Pashley, 2024).

**3. Hydrodynamic theory (Gysi 1900; Brännström 1960s)**
- Stimuli displace fluid in open tubules; the pressure change deforms mechanoreceptor A-delta (and A-beta) fibres at the pulp-dentin border, giving sharp pain.
- Cold, air, probing and osmotic stimuli cause outward flow; heat causes slow inward flow, so cold hurts more (Addy and Gillam, 2024; Sikri, 2020).
- Poiseuille: flow ∝ r⁴; doubling tubule diameter gives a 16-fold increase; sensitive dentin has more and twice-as-wide open tubules (Sikri, 2020).
- Explains why tubule-occluding agents work. Widely accepted.
- Supporting evidence: animal recordings show nerve firing in proportion to outward flow; sensitive cervical dentin has many more open tubules on SEM than non-sensitive dentin (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024).

## 3. Current concept

### Explanation

Markowitz and Pashley consider the three competing theories obsolete because all three act together (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Mechanically stimulated odontoblasts release ATP (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]; with odontoblasts deleted, cold no longer evoked pain in rats (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]; nerves respond directly to some stimuli (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Pulpal inflammation increases sensitivity (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Modern view (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024):** the three mechanisms work together rather than compete.
- Odontoblasts are mechanosensitive (piezo channels) and excite nerves by releasing ATP (paracrine signalling, not a synapse); deleting odontoblasts abolished cold pain in rats.
- Cold and hyperosmotic stimuli also activate receptors on nerve endings directly.
- Fluid flow through open tubules remains the trigger for both; inflammation lowers the threshold.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Three theories: direct neural, odontoblast transducer, hydrodynamic.
2. Hydrodynamic (Gysi 1900, Brännström): fluid flow activates A-delta fibres; flow ∝ r⁴.
3. Cold, air and probe cause outward flow; heat inward, so cold hurts more.
4. Current view: odontoblasts (ATP, piezo) and direct nerve activation act with the hydrodynamic mechanism.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Writing that heat causes outward flow.
2. Dismissing the odontoblast entirely without mentioning the newer ATP-signalling evidence.
3. Forgetting the r⁴ relationship, which explains why partial occlusion works.
