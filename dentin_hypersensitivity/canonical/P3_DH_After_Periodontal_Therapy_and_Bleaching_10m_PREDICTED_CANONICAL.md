## 1. After periodontal therapy (root sensitivity)

### Explanation

The European Federation of Periodontology recommended "root sensitivity" for this condition (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; Sikri uses the same term (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Deep bacterial invasion of tubules has been reported in periodontally involved teeth (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; Sikri notes micro-organisms invading the root tubules (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Prevalence after periodontal treatment is 60-90% (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]: up to 57% after scaling in one study (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC] and 85-95% in periodontal patients in another (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC]. After non-surgical therapy it is 62.5-90% at 1 day (Taha and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; after open-flap debridement it rose from 30% to 79% at 1 week (Taha and Gillam, 2024) [SRC] and after surgery fell to 21.7% at 8 weeks (Taha and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], so reassurance and monitoring should usually be sufficient (Taha and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

Scaling and root planing can remove 20-50 µm of cementum (printed as "20 to 50 mm" in the extracted article) (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC]. Von Troil found DH in 50% of patients after scaling (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC], while Wallace and Bissada attributed it mainly to surgery that exposes large root areas (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC]. The timing, with a peak at 1-3 weeks (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC], decline within 6 weeks (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC] and onset 7-14 days after scaling (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], fits plaque acids dissolving the instrumentation smear layer, which in health prevents 86% of fluid movement (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC]. Nerve sprouting peaks at 1 week (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], matching postoperative symptoms (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Plaque control helps prevent it (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], as plaque-covered dentine has wider tubules (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Smokers report more pain (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC].

Management follows the UK Forum's periodontal patient group (Gillam and Ramseier, 2024) [SRC], with short-term 0.2% chlorhexidine (Gillam and Ramseier, 2024) [SRC], CSPS prophylaxis paste (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], avoidance of over-instrumentation (Sikri, 2020) [SRC], and root coverage where suitable (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Root sensitivity (European Federation of Periodontology):** short, sharp pain from root dentin exposed by periodontal disease or its treatment (Addy and Gillam, 2024; Sikri, 2020).

**How it differs from classical DH:** occurs in periodontally involved teeth, often generalised; much higher prevalence; tubules may contain bacteria; follows a predictable time course after treatment (Addy and Gillam, 2024).

**Incidence:** 60-90% after periodontal therapy; 62.5-90% one day after non-surgical treatment; 30% before and 79% one week after flap surgery, falling to about 22% at 8 weeks (Taha and Gillam, 2024; Sikri, 2020).

**Mechanism**
- Scaling removes 20-50 µm of thin cervical cementum and exposes tubules (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011).
- The instrumentation smear layer first protects; plaque acids dissolve it in 7-14 days, so DH peaks at 1-3 weeks (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009).
- Nerve sprouting and pulpal inflammation peak at 1 week; bacteria may invade root tubules.
- Natural occlusion reduces DH within about 6-8 weeks.

**Management**
- Warn the patient beforehand; reassure and monitor (Taha and Gillam, 2024).
- Gentle instrumentation; avoid over-planing (Sikri, 2020).
- Plaque control; 0.2% chlorhexidine short-term (Gillam and Ramseier, 2024).
- Desensitising paste or fluoride varnish after each phase; arginine or CSPS prophylaxis paste; desensitising dentifrice at home (Mosquim et al, 2024).
- Root coverage surgery in the corrective phase where indicated.

## 2. After tooth bleaching

### Explanation

Bleaching may involve a different mechanism from DH (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC], and Sikri lists vital bleaching in the differential diagnosis (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Trushkowsky describes it as a reversible pulpitis (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC]; peroxide and urea reach the pulp in 5-10 minutes (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC]. About 60% of patients are affected (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC], more with higher peroxide concentration (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC]. The TRPA1 receptor, activated by oxidising compounds, has been proposed as the cause (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], with direct activation of oxidation receptors on odontoblasts and nerves (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Potassium nitrate in bleaching trays reduces but does not abolish sensitivity (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC]; 5% KNO₃ can be placed in the tray (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC] for 10-30 minutes (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC]. In-office bleaching must not burn the gingiva (Sikri, 2020) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Incidence:** about 60% of patients; more with higher peroxide concentration (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011).

**Mechanism (different from classical DH)**
- Hydrogen peroxide and urea penetrate intact enamel and dentin and reach the pulp in 5-10 minutes: a reversible pulpitis (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011).
- Oxidising agents directly activate TRPA1 and oxidation receptors on odontoblasts and nerves (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024).
- Dehydration and ill-fitting trays add to it.
- Usually generalised; stops when bleaching ends.

**Management**
- Lower peroxide concentration; shorter or less frequent applications; well-fitting trays.
- 5% potassium nitrate (with fluoride) in the tray for 10-30 minutes before or after bleaching, or desensitising dentifrice for 2 weeks beforehand (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011).
- Fluoride or CPP-ACP between sessions; analgesics if needed.
- Protect the gingiva in in-office bleaching (Sikri, 2020).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Root sensitivity: 60-90% after periodontal therapy; peaks at 1-3 weeks; settles in 6-8 weeks.
2. Delay explained by plaque acids dissolving the smear layer.
3. Bleaching sensitivity: about 60%; peroxide reaches the pulp in 5-10 minutes; a reversible pulpitis; potassium nitrate in the tray helps.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW

1. Time course of sensitivity after periodontal therapy (rise to 1-3 weeks, fall by 8 weeks).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Treating post-scaling sensitivity aggressively instead of reassuring and monitoring.
2. Calling bleaching sensitivity classical DH; the mechanism is different.
