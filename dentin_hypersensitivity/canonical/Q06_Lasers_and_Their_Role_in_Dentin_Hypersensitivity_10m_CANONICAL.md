## 1. Lasers used and mechanisms

### Explanation

Lasers treat DH by desensitising nerve cells, sealing dentine and anti-inflammatory effects (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Sikri explains the nerve effect as increased endorphins with blocked C-fibre depolarisation (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Low-power lasers may increase odontoblastic activity (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], and photobiomodulation can promote tertiary dentine that obliterates the tubules from the inside (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023) [SRC], acting mainly on pain control (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023) [SRC]. Erbium and CO₂ wavelengths are well absorbed by water and hydroxyapatite (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Nd:YAG is used at 0.3-2 W (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC] and Er,Cr:YSGG at 0.25-0.5 W (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]; the GaAlAs diode ranges from 780 nm (low power) to 980 nm (high power) (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], with low-power use giving more promising outcomes (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Arapostathis calls Nd:YAG the laser of choice for DH (Arapostathis, Velonis and Chala, 2023) [SRC], acting by sealing tubules only to a small extent (5-25%) (Arapostathis, Velonis and Chala, 2023) [SRC] and by blocking Aβ and C fibres (Arapostathis, Velonis and Chala, 2023) [SRC]. Trushkowsky argues that true sealing needs melting and resolidification of the surface (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC], which does not occur, and that relief comes from protein coagulation (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC]. Sikri lists the CO₂ (10600 nm) (Sikri, 2020) [SRC] and He:Ne (632.5 nm) lasers among others (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Pulp temperature rise is generally below 1.5°C (Sikri, 2020) [SRC], and a rise above 5.0°C affects the pulp (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Sikri also lists Nd:YAG at 1064 nm, Er:YAG at 2940 nm and GaAlAs at 810 nm; the 2780 nm wavelength of Er,Cr:YSGG in the exam table is not stated in these chapters [RECALL].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Classification of lasers used for DH (Mosquim et al, 2024; Sikri, 2020)**

| Type | Lasers (wavelength) | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| Low-level (photobiomodulation) | He:Ne (632.5 nm), GaAlAs diode (780-810 nm) | Analgesia: alters nerve membrane, blocks C-fibre depolarisation, raises endorphins; stimulates odontoblasts and tertiary dentin; anti-inflammatory |
| High-power | Nd:YAG (1064 nm), diode 980 nm, Er:YAG (2940 nm), Er,Cr:YSGG (2780 nm), CO₂ (10600 nm) | Melting, coagulation of tubular proteins and partial sealing of tubules; Nd:YAG also blocks nerve conduction |

**Mechanisms**
1. Nerve: interferes with depolarisation of A-delta and C fibres (immediate analgesia).
2. Tubule occlusion: coagulation and precipitation of proteins; surface melting and recrystallisation (only 5-25% of tubules sealed by Nd:YAG) (Arapostathis, Velonis and Chala, 2023).
3. Biological: stimulated odontoblasts lay down tertiary dentin that blocks tubules from inside (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023).

## 2. Technique, safety and evidence

### Explanation

A systematic review included 14 studies at 780-2780 nm (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023) [SRC]. Photobiomodulation is applied at mesial, distal and central points, repeated every 48-72 hours until symptoms disappear (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023) [SRC], with any surface desensitiser applied after the laser (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023) [SRC]; potassium nitrate gel plus 808 nm at 35 J/cm² for three sessions reduced sensitivity in an RCT (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023) [SRC]. Reported effectiveness ranged from 5% to 100% (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC], with less effect in severe DH (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC]. CO₂ with fluoride gave complete relief in 97% in one study (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC]; Nd:YAG reduced DH to air by 65% and to touch by 77% (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC]; but a split-mouth controlled trial found no difference from placebo at 16 weeks (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC]. Fluoride varnish followed by Nd:YAG occluded 90% of tubules in vitro (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC]. High-power and low-power lasers did not differ significantly at 3 months (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Combining lasers with topical agents may be promising (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], as occluding agents with low-power lasers seal tubules and reduce nerve stimulation together (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. The effect may be transient (Trushkowsky and Oquendo, 2011) [SRC]; drawbacks are high cost, complexity and diminishing effect (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]; it is not a clinically recommended protocol (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], and placebo must be considered (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Sikri also notes cost and limited availability (Sikri, 2020) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Technique (photobiomodulation; Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023)**
- Clean and dry the area; irradiate perpendicular to the sensitive dentin at mesial, distal and central points.
- Repeat every 48-72 hours until symptoms resolve (for example, 808 nm, 35 J/cm², three sessions).
- Apply a desensitiser (fluoride varnish, potassium nitrate gel) after the laser.

**Safety:** pulp temperature rise generally below 1.5°C; above 5°C damages the pulp (Sikri, 2020). Eye protection for all.

**Evidence (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009; Mosquim et al, 2024)**
- Effectiveness reported from 5% to 100%; less effective in severe DH.
- Nd:YAG reduced air-blast DH by 65% and tactile by 77%; CO₂ with fluoride gave 97% complete relief.
- But a controlled split-mouth trial of Nd:YAG showed no difference from placebo at 16 weeks.
- Low-power and high-power lasers give similar results; combination with topical agents appears better than either alone.

**Advantages:** immediate relief; localised; no pulp damage at correct settings.
**Limitations:** cost, equipment, training, variable parameters, transient effect, large placebo component; not yet a clinically recommended stand-alone protocol (Mosquim et al, 2024).

**Role:** an adjunct for localised DH that has failed simpler measures, preferably combined with an occluding agent and with control of the cause.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Low-level lasers act on nerves and stimulate tertiary dentin; high-power lasers coagulate and partly seal tubules.
2. Nd:YAG is the most studied; GaAlAs diode is cheap and compact.
3. Pulp temperature rise must stay below 5°C (usually below 1.5°C).
4. Results vary from 5% to 100% relief; placebo is large; combine with a topical agent.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Claiming lasers melt and seal all tubules; Nd:YAG seals only 5-25%.
2. Omitting pulp-safety limits.
3. Presenting lasers as first-line treatment.
