## 1. Principles and diagnosis

### Explanation

DH is short sharp pain from exposed dentine that cannot be ascribed to other causes (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], so the diagnosis is reached by exclusion (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]; without a definite diagnosis treatment fails (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Purely symptomatic treatment is logical but not biological (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; controlling the causes is the first step (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. In-office treatment suits localised severe DH (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], OTC products generalised mild DH (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], conservative before invasive (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], and reversible before irreversible (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC]. The Canadian Advisory Board algorithm (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC] ends by reviewing the diagnosis if pain persists (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Short, sharp pain from exposed dentin to thermal, evaporative, tactile, osmotic or chemical stimuli, not ascribable to any other dental defect or disease (Addy and Gillam, 2024).

**Principles (Canadian Advisory Board, in Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009; Mosquim et al, 2024)**
1. Diagnose by exclusion (caries, cracked tooth, pulpitis, leaking restorations, postoperative sensitivity); record pain with air blast, probe and VAS.
2. Identify and remove causes before treating.
3. Generalised mild DH: home-use products. Localised severe DH: in-office agents.
4. Reversible and non-invasive before invasive; review and re-diagnose if pain persists.

**Record at the first visit:** teeth and sites affected; triggering stimuli; air-blast and probe scores; diet diary (acid frequency and timing); brushing method, force and brush type; reflux or vomiting; bruxism; recent scaling or bleaching; tooth wear (BEWE) and recession depth.

## 2. Treatment

### Explanation

Agents reduce dentine permeability or target the nerves (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. Prevention: no brushing for at least half an hour after acid (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Home: potassium nitrate, introduced empirically (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], depolarises nerves (Sikri, 2020) [SRC] over about 2 weeks (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; OTC products take 2-4 weeks (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]; stannous fluoride (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], arginine (Sikri, 2020) [SRC] and calcium sodium phosphosilicate (Sikri, 2020) [SRC] occlude tubules. In-office: burnishing (Sikri, 2020) [SRC], fluoride varnish for 3-6 months (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], oxalates (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], Gluma (Al-Sabbagh, Brown and Thomas, 2009) [SRC], adhesives after a 5-second etch (Sikri, 2020) [SRC], iontophoresis (Sikri, 2020) [SRC], lasers (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], RMGIC restorations (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC] and root coverage (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Pulpectomy is not a valid routine option (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**A. Remove causes (prevention)**
- Diet: reduce frequency of acidic food and drink; do not brush for at least 30 minutes after acid (Sikri, 2020).
- Brushing: soft brush, light force, low-abrasive paste.
- Treat reflux and vomiting; plaque control; avoid over-instrumentation in scaling.

**B. Home-use (OTC) treatment: 2-4 weeks for relief**
- Nerve desensitisation: 5% potassium nitrate (or chloride, citrate) dentifrice.
- Tubule occlusion: stannous fluoride, strontium acetate, 8% arginine-calcium carbonate, calcium sodium phosphosilicate (NovaMin), BioMin F, nano-hydroxyapatite dentifrices; fluoride or oxalate mouthrinses.
- Brush twice daily, little water, apply a smear of paste to sensitive areas; do not rinse after brushing; continue long-term because the effect is lost when use stops.

**C. In-office treatment**
- Burnishing (50-80% fall in fluid flow).
- 5% NaF varnish (relief 3-6 months), 2% NaF, SnF₂; iontophoresis with fluoride.
- Oxalates (potassium, ferric): calcium oxalate crystals in tubules.
- Glutaraldehyde 5% + HEMA 35% (Gluma): coagulates tubular proteins.
- Dentin bonding agents and resin sealants; GIC or RMGIC; composite for NCCLs.
- Lasers: Nd:YAG, Er:YAG, CO₂, GaAlAs diode.

**D. Surgical:** root coverage for recession.

**E. Last resort:** pulpectomy for intractable pain only.

**F. Review** at 2-4 weeks; reinforce prevention; repeat or escalate.

(Sikri, 2020; Mosquim et al, 2024; Markowitz and Gillam, 2024)

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Diagnose by exclusion; remove the cause before treating.
2. Generalised mild DH: potassium or occluding dentifrice for 2-4 weeks.
3. Localised severe DH: varnish, oxalate, Gluma, bonding agent, laser.
4. Restoration or root coverage for tissue loss; pulpectomy only as last resort.
