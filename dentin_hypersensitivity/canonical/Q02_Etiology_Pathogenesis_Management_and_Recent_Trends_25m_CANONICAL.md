## 1. Definition

### Explanation

DH is short sharp pain arising from exposed dentine in response to thermal, evaporative, tactile, osmotic or chemical stimuli that cannot be ascribed to any other dental defect or pathology (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; Sikri calls it an exaggerated response of exposed dentin to external stimuli (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. The definition makes it a diagnosis of exclusion (Q1, section 1). The European Federation of Periodontology recommended the term root sensitivity for the same pain in periodontally involved teeth (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition (Holland et al, 1997).** Short, sharp pain arising from exposed dentin in response to stimuli, typically thermal, evaporative, tactile, osmotic or chemical, that cannot be ascribed to any other form of dental defect or disease (Addy and Gillam, 2024). It is a diagnosis of exclusion. Root sensitivity (European Federation of Periodontology) is the same pain from roots exposed by periodontal disease or its treatment.

## 2. Etiology and pathogenesis

### Explanation

**Etiology.** For DH to occur, dentine has to be exposed (lesion localisation) and the tubules opened (lesion initiation) (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. Localisation follows enamel loss or gingival recession (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC]; initiation usually follows loss of the smear layer (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC]. Erosion is the most important factor in both steps (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC], acting additively or synergistically with toothbrush and toothpaste abrasion (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

**Pathogenesis.** In the hydrodynamic mechanism, flow varies with the fourth power of the tubule radius (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], so doubling the diameter gives a 16-fold rise (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. The current concept is that nerve, odontoblast and hydrodynamic mechanisms act together (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]: the stimulated odontoblast releases ATP onto nerves (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], deleting odontoblasts abolished cold-evoked pain in rats (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC], and cold acts directly on the nerve endings (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC]. Pulpal inflammation makes exposed dentine hypersensitive (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Etiology: two steps (Addy and Gillam, 2024; Olley and Bartlett, 2024)**
1. **Lesion localisation:** dentin exposed by enamel loss (erosion, abrasion, attrition, so-called abfraction) or by gingival recession (brushing trauma, periodontal disease, scaling and periodontal surgery, thin biotype, orthodontics, crown margins).
2. **Lesion initiation:** smear layer removed and tubules opened by dietary acids (high titratable acidity: fruit juices, wine, carbonated and sports drinks), gastric acid, heavy brushing, or plaque acids after scaling.
Erosion is the most important factor; erosion and abrasion act synergistically.

**Pathogenesis**
- Hydrodynamic theory (Gysi, Brännström): stimuli move tubular fluid, mostly outward; the pressure change activates A-delta fibres at the pulp-dentin border. Flow ∝ r⁴, so wide open tubules are hypersensitive (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024; Sikri, 2020).
- Recent concept: odontoblasts are mechanosensitive (piezo channels) and excite nerves by releasing ATP; cold also stimulates nerve endings directly; the three classical theories act together (Markowitz and Pashley, 2024).
- Pulpal inflammation and nerve sprouting after injury lower the pain threshold.
- Natural defences (smear layer, salivary minerals, plasma proteins, sclerotic and tertiary dentin) narrow the tubules; acid and brushing remove them, so DH is cyclical and marks active tooth wear (Olley and Bartlett, 2024).

## 3. Management (conventional)

### Explanation

A management approach that treats only symptoms is logical but not biological (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. Desensitisers either reduce dentine permeability or target intradental nerves (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], and the most popular classification is by mechanism (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. No product is ideal (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. OTC products take 2-4 weeks (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC] and suit mild to moderate or generalised DH (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]; in-office agents suit localised severe DH (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

1. **Diagnosis by exclusion**; record pain with air blast, tactile probe and VAS.
2. **Remove causes:** dietary counselling, atraumatic brushing, no brushing straight after acid, treat reflux, plaque control.
3. **Home care (generalised, mild-moderate):** dentifrices with 5% potassium nitrate (nerve), strontium, stannous fluoride, arginine-calcium carbonate, calcium sodium phosphosilicate (tubule occlusion); results in 2-4 weeks (Mosquim et al, 2024).
4. **In-office (localised, severe):**
   - Occlusion by precipitation: 5% NaF varnish (CaF₂), oxalates (calcium oxalate crystals), calcium phosphates.
   - Protein precipitation: glutaraldehyde 5% + HEMA 35% (Gluma), silver nitrate.
   - Sealing: dentin bonding agents, resin sealants, GIC or RMGIC, composite restoration of NCCLs.
   - Iontophoresis with fluoride; lasers.
5. **Surgical:** root coverage for recession.
6. **Pulpectomy:** last resort.

No current agent is ideal (immediate and lasting relief, non-irritant, acid resistant, cheap), which drives the search for newer agents (Hill and Gillam, 2024).

## 4. Recent trends

### Explanation

**Bioactive glasses.** Bioglass 45S5 was developed by Larry Hench in the late 1960s (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC] as a bone substitute, never designed as a toothpaste additive (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. It dissolves faster at acidic pH, so bioactive glasses behave as smart materials (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. The hydroxycarbonate apatite it forms is more soluble than hydroxyapatite (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. BioMin F contains fluorine in a sodium calcium fluoro phosphosilicate glass (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC] that forms fluorapatite, more acid resistant than hydroxyapatite (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], and dissolves at pH 7 over 10-12 hours (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. A 5% BioMin F toothpaste reduced DH more than an 8% arginine toothpaste in one trial (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Replacing Na₂O with K₂O gives a glass that also releases potassium (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. A network meta-analysis found CSPS most beneficial for all three stimuli (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

**Nano-hydroxyapatite.** Particles below 5 µm are needed to enter the tubules (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; most hydroxyapatite toothpastes contain no fluoride (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]. Sikri lists carbonated nano-hydroxyapatite toothpaste as a successful desensitiser (Sikri, 2020) [SRC].

**Arginine.** Commercial toothpastes contain 8% arginine (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; the chemistry of the arginine-calcium complex is poorly understood (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], and the deposit may not resist acid challenge (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]. Sikri describes the 8% arginine, calcium carbonate and 1450 ppm fluoride dentifrice (Sikri, 2020) [SRC].

**Stannous fluoride.** 0.454% SnF₂ toothpaste has been reported more effective than MFP (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], with a relatively acid-resistant stannous deposit (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

**Oxalate mouthrinse.** A 1.4% potassium oxalate mouthwash reduced sensitivity, particularly after 4 weeks (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

**Self-assembling peptide.** P11-4 forms a scaffold that nucleates apatite (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; the first self-assembled peptide product for DH is Curodont DeSenz (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], which gave a 64% reduction in hydraulic conductance in vitro (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

**Calcium phosphate cement.** Teethmate Desensitizer is a mixture of tetracalcium phosphate and dicalcium phosphate anhydrous (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; the phase formed first is octacalcium phosphate (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], whose acid durability is untested.

**Flake glass varnish.** Flake glasses are thin plates of glass (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC] that align in a resin varnish, resist wear and reduce the oxygen-inhibited layer.

**Pellicle-forming polymers.** Polygamma glutamic acid, the basis of fermented soybeans (natto) (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], chelates enamel calcium and may protect against erosion and DH.

**Stimulating tertiary dentine.** Treatments that cause tertiary dentine formation should last longer than surface occlusion (Hill and Gillam, 2024) [SRC]; low-power lasers are proposed to increase odontoblastic activity (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], and photobiomodulation can promote tertiary dentine that obliterates tubules from inside (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023) [SRC].

**Lasers and combinations.** A systematic review included 14 laser studies at 780-2780 nm (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023) [SRC]; an RCT combined potassium nitrate gel with 808 nm photobiomodulation at 35 J/cm² for three sessions (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023) [SRC]. Combining lasers with topical desensitisers may be promising (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], but lasers are not yet a clinically recommended protocol (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC].

**Protease inhibitors.** Inhibitors of MMPs and cathepsins (chlorhexidine, EGCG, proanthocyanidin, TiF₄) are being evaluated against erosive wear and DH (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC].

**Pharmacological targets.** Piezo antagonists such as gadolinium (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC] and ATP-channel blockers are research targets; the TRPV1 route is unsuitable because DH neurons lack TRPV1 (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

**Behaviour change.** Motivational interviewing works for many health behaviours (Gillam and Ramseier, 2024) [SRC] using the RULE principles (Gillam and Ramseier, 2024) [SRC], but no published study has tested it in DH (Gillam and Ramseier, 2024) [SRC].

**Diagnosis and monitoring.** The CHI score measures DH severity (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC] and the BEWE screens tooth wear (Olley and Bartlett, 2024) [SRC]. Addy and Gillam suggest that change in fluid flow could be assessed by dentine capacitance or impedance (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC], and pain measured centrally with an electroencephalogram (Addy and Gillam, 2024) [SRC].

**Guidelines and surgery.** The Indian Society of Periodontology has published a diagnostic guide and flowchart (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC]; the 2018 classification of gingival recession defects and phenotype guides root coverage (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC], which reduces DH (Mosquim et al, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**A. Newer tubule-occluding (biomimetic) agents**
- **Calcium sodium phosphosilicate (NovaMin, 45S5 bioactive glass):** releases Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻, raises pH, forms hydroxycarbonate apatite in tubules; "smart" (dissolves faster in acid). Best ranked in network meta-analysis for all three stimuli (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024; Hill and Gillam, 2024).
- **Fluoro calcium phosphosilicate (BioMin F):** forms acid-resistant fluorapatite; slow fluoride release over 10-12 h; smaller particles enter tubules; outperformed arginine paste in one trial (Mosquim et al, 2024; Hill and Gillam, 2024). **Potassium-releasing glasses** combine occlusion with nerve action.
- **Nano-hydroxyapatite** (and zinc- or strontium-substituted HA): biomimetic coating; particles <5 µm enter tubules (Hill and Gillam, 2024).
- **8% arginine + calcium carbonate (Pro-Argin):** instant relief, prophylaxis paste before and after scaling; now combined with sodium trimetaphosphate (Mosquim et al, 2024).
- **Stabilised stannous fluoride (0.454%):** acid-resistant tin deposit; also anti-erosive (Addy and Gillam, 2024).
- **1.4% potassium oxalate mouthrinse and strips** (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024).
- **Self-assembling peptide P11-4 (Curodont DeSenz):** forms a protein scaffold that nucleates apatite; 64% fall in dentin permeability in vitro (Hill and Gillam, 2024).
- **Calcium phosphate cement (Teethmate Desensitizer):** tetracalcium + dicalcium phosphate set as octacalcium phosphate, then apatite, inside tubules (Hill and Gillam, 2024).
- **Flake glass varnish:** aligned glass flakes in resin; durable, wear-resistant in-office barrier (Hill and Gillam, 2024).
- **Pellicle-forming polymers:** polygamma glutamic acid protects against erosion (Hill and Gillam, 2024).
- **Protease (MMP, cathepsin) inhibitors** to slow erosive wear of dentin (Mosquim et al, 2024).

**B. Biological approaches**
- Stimulating **tertiary dentin** (bioactive ions, low-power lasers) to seal tubules from within; expected to last longer than surface occlusion (Hill and Gillam, 2024; Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023).
- **Pharmacological targets** from the odontoblast discoveries: piezo antagonists, blockers of ATP release (pannexin-1) and P2X3 receptors (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024).

**C. Lasers**
- Photobiomodulation (diode 780-980 nm) for analgesia and tertiary dentin; high-power Nd:YAG, Er:YAG, Er,Cr:YSGG and CO₂ for tubule sealing.
- Combination of laser with fluoride varnish or potassium nitrate gel (for example 808 nm, 35 J/cm², three sessions 72 h apart) (Cronshaw and Mylona, 2023; Mosquim et al, 2024).

**D. Management concepts**
- **Biological rather than symptomatic** management: diagnosis by exclusion, control of erosion and abrasion first.
- **Patient-group guidelines** (UK Expert Forum; Indian Society of Periodontology flowchart, 2022) (Mosquim et al, 2024).
- **Motivational interviewing** (RULE: resist, understand, listen, empower) to change diet and brushing (Gillam and Ramseier, 2024).
- **Root coverage** planned with the 2018 recession classification (Mosquim et al, 2024).
- Standardised clinical trial methods, because placebo accounts for 20-60% of relief (Mosquim et al, 2024).

**E. Diagnosis and monitoring**
- Indices: CHI score for DH severity and BEWE for erosive wear, with photographs and study casts to track progression (Olley and Bartlett, 2024).
- Proposed objective measures: dentin capacitance or impedance as a surrogate for fluid flow, and central (EEG) recording of pain, in place of subjective scores (Addy and Gillam, 2024).
- Recognising DH as a clinical sign of an active tooth wear process, which shifts the focus to prevention (Olley and Bartlett, 2024).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS (compressed)

The textbooks present the newer agents as advances, but the evidence differs by agent. Bioactive glass (CSPS) has the strongest clinical support, ranked best for all stimuli with high to moderate certainty in a network meta-analysis (Markowitz and Gillam, 2024). Self-assembling peptides, Teethmate, flake glass varnish, polygamma glutamic acid and protease inhibitors rest mainly on in-vitro permeability or SEM data, which cannot stand alone as proof of clinical benefit (Hill and Gillam, 2024; Mosquim et al, 2024). Mineral deposits from most occluding agents are not permanent and can be removed by acid (Mosquim et al, 2024), so durability, not initial occlusion, is the real test. Write these agents as "promising, evidence mainly laboratory" unless a clinical trial is named. [Likely]

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Etiology: lesion localisation then lesion initiation; erosion is the key factor.
2. Pathogenesis: hydrodynamic, with odontoblast ATP signalling and direct nerve activation acting together.
3. Bioactive glass: NovaMin forms hydroxycarbonate apatite; BioMin F forms fluorapatite (more acid resistant).
4. Self-assembling peptide P11-4 (Curodont DeSenz) and calcium phosphate cement (Teethmate) are new in-office occluders.
5. Low-power lasers act on nerves and stimulate tertiary dentin; high-power lasers seal tubules.
6. Recent concept: prevention-based biological management, patient-group guidelines and motivational interviewing.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW

1. Bioactive glass reaction: glass particle in tubule, ion release, apatite layer forming on the tubule wall (section 4).
2. Lesion localisation and initiation at the CEJ (section 2).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Listing only potassium nitrate and fluoride as "recent"; these are decades old.
2. Presenting in-vitro occlusion as proof of clinical efficacy.
3. Omitting the change from symptomatic to biological, prevention-led management.
4. Calling NovaMin a fluoride product; BioMin F is the fluoride-containing glass.
