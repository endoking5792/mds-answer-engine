T2|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "The glass ionomer cement is composed of a powder and a"] GIC consists of a powder and a liquid which, when mixed, begin a setting reaction that forms a solid material; the glass in the powder is described as a fluoride-containing calcium aluminosilicate.
T3|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "acid attacks the surface of the glass particles"] Conventional GIC sets by an acid-base reaction: polyacrylic acid attacks the glass surface and calcium, aluminum, sodium and fluoride ions are released into the aqueous medium; polyacrylic chains cross-link with calcium ions to form a solid mass.
T3|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "the calcium is replaced by the aluminum ions in"] In the next phase calcium is replaced by aluminum ions within 24 h; sodium and fluoride do not take part in the cross-links but combine and are released as sodium fluoride.
T3|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "adding strength to the cement (maturation process)"] The cross-linked phase becomes hydrated over time using the same mixing water, adding strength (maturation); unreacted glass is covered by a silica gel sheath formed by leaching of cations; the process may last up to 7 days; pH is acidic right after mixing but tends to neutralize within 24 h.
T3|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "to the material, so that some of the disadvantages of chemical"] RMGIC has, in addition to the acid-base reaction, a chemical and/or light-activated polymerization mechanism, using hydrophilic monomers and methacrylate-modified polyacrylic acid, giving fast curing when light-activated; some systems also have an activator/initiator system that completes cure of the resin component without light.
T8|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "so that some of the disadvantages of chemical"] The resin-modified (RMGIC) design is presented as minimizing some disadvantages of purely chemical-curing materials.
T4|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "chemical bonding to the tooth"] Positive properties of GICs as a protective material: chemical bonding to tooth structure, fluoride release and antimicrobial effect.
T6|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "coefficient of linear thermal expansion similar to the tooth structure"] Conventional GIC has a coefficient of linear thermal expansion similar to tooth structure; in RMGIC the coefficient varies with the percentage of resin component incorporated.
T16|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "the conventional one has been proven to be less toxic"] Conventional GIC is reported less cytotoxic than the resin-modified type; HEMA in the composition increases cytotoxicity, probably through residual uncured monomer diffusing through dentinal tubules because of its low molecular weight.

T16|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "the conventional GIC should not be used as a material for"] Even conventional GIC should not be used for direct pulp protection because it shows some toxicity in direct contact with pulp, though it is very biocompatible for indirect protection even in deep preparations.

T9|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "the restorative material is applied over, pro-"] When conventional GIC is a liner/base the overlying restorative material protects it from syneresis and water absorption; if it completely fills the preparation and contacts saliva, its surface needs an impermeable coating (cavity varnish, clear nail varnish or dental adhesive).
T8|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.320 | ANCHOR: "the resinous matrix reduces drastically the syneresis"] With RMGIC no surface protection is needed because the resin matrix drastically reduces syneresis and water absorption ("umbrella effect").

T10|[BOOK: Modern Op Dent | FILE: 14_9_Protection_of_the_Dentin_Pulp_Complex.md | PDF p.331 | ANCHOR: "a layer of GIC is applied over the whole internal wall"] Direct pulp capping: calcium hydroxide cement over the exposure, then GIC over the whole internal wall if the final restoration is immediate; alternatively fill the whole cavity with GIC and wait 45-60 days, test cold sensitivity and radiograph, then remove the superficial GIC and place the final restoration.

T2|[BOOK: Pickard Guide MI | FILE: 10_7_Restorative_materials_and_their_relationship_to_tooth_stru.md | PDF p.129 | ANCHOR: "Glass ionomer cement (GIC) is a water-based, plastic direct dental"] GIC is defined as a water-based plastic direct restorative cement formed by an acid-base reaction between a polyalkenoic acid and ion-leachable fluoro-calcium (strontium) aluminosilicate glass particles.
T2|[BOOK: Pickard Guide MI | FILE: 10_7_Restorative_materials_and_their_relationship_to_tooth_stru.md | PDF p.129 | ANCHOR: "Powder: calcium fluoro-aluminosilicate glass particles with strontium"] Powder is calcium fluoro-aluminosilicate glass with strontium (for radio-opacity); silica affects transparency, alumina affects opacity and setting time and can raise compressive strength, calcium fluoride lowers fusion temperature, raises strength, enhances translucency and has therapeutic effect; structure is a tetrahedral complex around a central aluminium ion with alkaline-earth cations (sodium, potassium, calcium, strontium).
T2|[BOOK: Pickard Guide MI | FILE: 10_7_Restorative_materials_and_their_relationship_to_tooth_stru.md | PDF p.129 | ANCHOR: "itaconic acid copolymer solution in water plus"] Liquid is an itaconic acid copolymer solution in water plus tartaric acid (5-15%, maintains working time and assists setting); anhydrous forms have vacuum-dried polyacrylic acid in the powder and are mixed with water with or without dilute tartaric acid.
T3|[BOOK: Pickard Guide MI | FILE: 10_7_Restorative_materials_and_their_relationship_to_tooth_stru.md | PDF p.129 | ANCHOR: "Setting reaction: acid–base reaction with three stages"] Three stages: (1) dissolution, with sol formation from glass outer layers as polyacid releases calcium, strontium, aluminium and fluoride ions; (2) gelation and hardening, calcium binding carboxylate groups over 4 to 5 minutes giving a clinically hard surface (initial set), silaceous hydrogel maturing over 24 hours with aluminium cross-linking up to 7 days, causing volumetric shrinkage of up to 3%; (3) hydration, continuing over 4 to 6 months, improving properties, water uptake causing expansion that offsets the initial shrinkage.
T9|[BOOK: Pickard Guide MI | FILE: 10_7_Restorative_materials_and_their_relationship_to_tooth_stru.md | PDF p.129 | ANCHOR: "Surface protection at this stage is advisable to ensure regulation"] During stage 2 surface protection is advisable to regulate water movement in and out of the hydrogel so maturation can occur.
T4|[BOOK: Pickard Guide MI | FILE: 10_7_Restorative_materials_and_their_relationship_to_tooth_stru.md | PDF p.129 | ANCHOR: "GICs have the ability to adhere chemically to mineralized dental tissues"] GICs adhere chemically to mineralized tissues by diffusion and adsorption; to enamel, the polyacid displaces phosphate and Ca2+ from hydroxyapatite into the GIC matrix, then pH rises and minerals re-precipitate forming an ion-enriched layer at the interface.
