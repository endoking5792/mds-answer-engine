# F. Live-literature evidence for the GIC dossier

Provenance note: every entry below was read in this session as a PubMed record (title + full abstract, retrieved through the PubMed MCP get_article_metadata tool). No full text was opened, so "OPENED: abstract" applies throughout. Numbers are copied from the abstracts. "Not stated in abstract" means the abstract does not give that item; it does not mean the paper lacks it. URLs are PubMed IDs or DOIs exactly as returned by the tool (PMID-based: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/<PMID>/ ; DOI-based: https://doi.org/<DOI>).

### Claim 1: ART/HVGIC survival in permanent and primary teeth vs amalgam/composite
- SOURCE: Frencken et al., 2021, Br Dent J | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1038/s41415-021-2701-0 (PMID 33883705) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review with meta-analyses of RCTs
- RESULT: 14 eligible randomised trials; low risk of bias for two reports. No statistically significant difference in weighted mean survival percentages of ART/HVGIC vs traditional (amalgam, composite) restorations for single- and multiple-surface restorations in primary molars and single-surface restorations in posterior permanent teeth at years 1, 2, 3 and 5. At years 4.3 and 6.3 the difference was statistically significant, favouring ART/HVGIC. No significant difference for multiple-surface restorations in posterior permanent teeth. Authors conclude ART with HVGIC can replace traditional restorations in single- and multiple-surface cavities in primary molars and single-surface cavities in posterior permanent teeth, "particularly for amalgam".
- LIMITATIONS: Only 2 of 14 reports were low risk of bias; homogeneity achieved only for single-surface ART restorations at 1 and 2 years in primary dentition (as stated in abstract). Other limitations not stated in abstract.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Can conclude ART/HVGIC performs no worse than amalgam/composite for single-surface (and primary multi-surface) cavities in trials up to 5 years. Cannot conclude equivalence for multi-surface permanent-tooth restorations (no significant difference found, but the abstract does not support equivalence).

- SOURCE: Ruengrungsom et al., 2018, J Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1016/j.jdent.2018.07.008 (PMID 30017937) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review (PubMed 1983-March 2018; 67 articles included; ROBINS-I; annual failure rates, AFRs)
- RESULT: For single-surface occlusal or multi-surface GIC restorations the conventional technique showed better survival than the ART technique regardless of dentition. AFRs of approximal/multi-surface GIC restorations were greater than single-surface (occlusal) ones, in both dentitions. RMGIC with the conventional technique "seems to be promising" for approximal cavities in primary teeth. GIC-ART is an alternative for single-surface occlusal restorations; for load-bearing approximal restorations (especially primary teeth) it "should be carefully considered".
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract (single database, PubMed, plus hand search; included non-randomised designs, ROBINS-I used).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Cavity extent (single vs multi-surface) is a key determinant of GIC survival; ART is best supported for single-surface occlusal cavities. Cannot conclude from this abstract that ART equals conventional technique in approximal load-bearing cavities. Note this partly conflicts in emphasis with Frencken 2021 (different inclusion rules and metric).

- SOURCE: Panetta et al., 2024, J Funct Biomater | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.3390/jfb15020048 (PMID 38391901) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: umbrella review of systematic reviews (13 included)
- RESULT: Follow-up 6 months to 6 years. Per AMSTAR-2 none of the included reviews met all criteria and none had an a priori design; ROBIS: 7 low risk, 3 low except one unclear, 2 high. GRADE: all classed "Class II" (conflicting evidence); level of evidence "Level B". Authors: GICs a good choice in both dentitions, primary dentition has more evidence (especially ART); "still questionable" whether GIC is good in the medium/long term for permanent and primary teeth.
- LIMITATIONS: "Many of the included studies presented a high risk of bias and low quality"; technique, GIC type, cavity type and operator experience strongly influence performance.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Higher-order evidence on GIC longevity is conflicting/moderate-low quality. Cannot make strong claims of long-term superiority/equivalence.

### Claim 2: RMGIC vs composite vs conventional GIC in NCCL / Class V (retention)
(Targeted search for a Cochrane NCCL review [Veitz-Keenan] returned no record in PubMed; not cited. See UNRESOLVED.)
- SOURCE: Arbildo-Vega et al., 2025, J Clin Exp Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.4317/jced.62997 (PMID 40950519; PMC12424605) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs (GIC vs composite resin in NCCL)
- RESULT: 296 records, 18 articles in quantitative synthesis. "No statistically significant differences" between CR and GIC in restoration of NCCLs; authors conclude no differences in clinical performance over time. (Abstract does not give pooled effect sizes; it does not separate conventional GIC from RMGIC.)
- LIMITATIONS: RoB 2.0 and GRADEpro were used but the abstract gives no bias/certainty ratings. Search to October 2023.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Pooled RCT evidence shows no detected difference between GIC and composite in NCCLs. Cannot say how RMGIC vs conventional GIC compare, or give numeric retention rates, from this abstract.

- SOURCE: Chaple Gil et al., 2026, J Funct Biomater | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.3390/jfb17040160 (PMID 42042267) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review + frequentist network meta-analysis of RCTs (74 trials; PROSPERO-registered; RoB 2; CINeMA)
- RESULT: Marginal-adaptation network: 57 studies, 28 interventions, 6798 patients, 1772 events. Retention-loss network: 61 studies, 33 interventions, 7338 patients, 584 events. Selective-etch with universal adhesive + nanocomposite reduced marginal adaptation failure compared with RMGI; certain self-etch and non-universal combinations increased risk. For retention loss, selective-etch and etch-and-rinse with universal adhesives + nanocomposite had lower failure rates; some self-etch/non-universal strategies performed less favourably. "Overall confidence was predominantly high", downgraded mainly for imprecision and heterogeneity.
- LIMITATIONS: Downgrading for imprecision and heterogeneity (stated). Search date 25 January 2026.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Best-performing NCCL strategies in the network were adhesive + nanocomposite protocols; RMGI was not superior on marginal adaptation. Cannot extract an RMGI retention percentage from the abstract.

- SOURCE: Fernández et al., 2025, J Esthet Restor Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1111/jerd.70052 (PMID 41159592) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: umbrella review (16 systematic reviews 2012-2025, >15,000 restorations, 6-120 months; AMSTAR 2 and GRADE)
- RESULT: Conventional composites >90% 5-year survival. Bulk-fill failure rates <7% short-medium term. Ormocer-based composites failure rate 12.1%. "GICs exhibited lower survival but remain useful in high caries risk or moisture challenged conditions." "The sandwich technique with RMGIC bases improved retention in NCCLs."
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract beyond long-term bulk-fill data being limited.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Umbrella-level evidence ranks composites as most reliable, GIC lower survival overall, RMGIC/sandwich useful in specific indications. Cannot conclude from the abstract exactly which NCCL retention figures apply to RMGIC alone. Note: the two reviews above (no difference GIC vs CR in NCCL; GIC lower survival overall) differ in scope; do not merge them.

### Claim 3: Fluoride release/recharge of GIC vs RMGIC vs composite etc.; does it reduce secondary caries?
- SOURCE: Justen et al., 2024, Dent Mater | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1016/j.dental.2024.06.014 (PMID 38880724) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review with network meta-analysis of in-vitro studies (85 studies, 79 meta-analysed; RoBDEMAT)
- RESULT: "Despite the Alkasite nature of Cention-N, GICs may still demonstrate the greatest fluoride releasing ability from all direct restorative materials." Cention-N similar physico-mechanical properties to RBCs, stronger than GICs.
- LIMITATIONS: In-vitro only (stated design); other limitations not stated in abstract.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Laboratory fluoride release is highest for GICs among direct materials. Cannot translate in-vitro release into clinical caries prevention.

- SOURCE: Dobrzynski et al., 2025, Materials (Basel) | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.3390/ma18235350 (PMID 41374193; PMC12693251) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review of (mostly) laboratory studies, 16 studies on sealants
- RESULT: GI and RMGI sealants (Fuji VII, Fuji IX, Fuji II LC, Ketac Molar, Vitremer) "consistently demonstrated higher fluoride release and superior recharge capacity" than resin-based sealants (Clinpro, Helioseal F, BeautiSealant). Most studies: biphasic release, initial burst in first 24-48 h then slower phase lasting several weeks. Storage medium, pH, temperature significantly affected release.
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Supports the burst-then-sustained release pattern and recharge advantage of GI-based materials over resin-based sealants in vitro. This is sealant data; cannot be extended to restorations without caution.

- SOURCE: Ge et al., 2023, Dent Mater | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1016/j.dental.2023.10.008 (PMID 37838608) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs (PROSPERO CRD42022380959)
- RESULT: 64 studies; 8310 GIC restorations vs 5857 amalgam/composite restorations; follow-up 1-10 years. Secondary caries, GIC vs amalgam: primary teeth RR 0.55 (95% CI 0.41-0.72); permanent teeth RR 0.20 (0.11-0.38). GIC vs composite: primary RR 0.92 (0.77-1.10); permanent RR 0.77 (0.39-1.51). Conventional GIC vs RMGIC: primary RR 1.12 (0.67-1.87); permanent RR 1.63 (0.34-7.84).
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract (English-language publications only; certainty rating not given).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Clinically, GIC has fewer secondary caries than amalgam but NOT fewer than composite. So high fluoride release has not been shown to translate into an advantage over composite. Cannot attribute the amalgam difference specifically to fluoride (the abstract does not test mechanism).

- SOURCE: Zailai et al., 2026, Cureus | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.7759/cureus.102221 (PMID 41585617) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs (40 RCTs, 5,506 restorations, permanent teeth, follow-up >=12 months; RoB 2; TSA)
- RESULT: Bioactive materials (GIC, RMGIC, giomers, bioactive resins, alkasites) reduced secondary caries risk by 45% vs conventional (composite, amalgam): RR 0.55 (95% CI 0.46-0.65; p<0.001; I2 = 0%). Subgroup: GICs RR 0.36 (p=0.002); giomers RR 1.09 (p=0.87), no benefit. Certainty "moderate due to risk of bias in a subset of studies".
- LIMITATIONS: Moderate certainty because of risk of bias in some studies (stated). Published in a lower-tier journal (Cureus); the pooled comparator mixes amalgam and composite, which may drive the effect (reviewer's note, not from the abstract).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Pooled RCT data support a lower secondary-caries incidence with GIC-type materials. Because Ge 2023 found no difference vs composite, treat the "bioactive superior" conclusion as dependent on comparator (amalgam). Giomers showed no benefit.

- SOURCE: Klimas et al., 2025, Materials (Basel) | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.3390/ma18133187 (PMID 40649675) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review of in-vitro studies (23 included)
- RESULT: Of 12 studies on silver-modified GICs, 6 reported increased fluoride release; 7 on zinc-modified, 4 on strontium. Addition of strontium, titanium oxide, silver nanoparticles or zirconium oxide increases fluoride release; sintered silver reduces it; zinc oxide results discrepant.
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract (in-vitro; heterogeneous).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Filler/metal modifications change fluoride release in vitro, in both directions. No clinical inference possible.

### Claim 4: Alkasite (Cention N)
- SOURCE: Justen et al., 2024, Dent Mater | DOI 10.1016/j.dental.2024.06.014 (PMID 38880724) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: SR + NMA of in-vitro studies (85 studies)
- RESULT: As above. Properties analysed included color change, degree of conversion, hardness, microleakage, roughness, bond strength to dentin, compressive/diametral tensile/flexural strength, wear, alkalinizing effect, antibacterial activity, calcium and fluoride release. Cention-N similar to RBCs and stronger than GICs; GICs greatest fluoride release.
- LIMITATIONS: In-vitro; not stated further in abstract.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Laboratory properties are composite-like; this is in-vitro evidence only.

- SOURCE: Laporte et al., 2026, J Funct Biomater | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.3390/jfb17020093 (PMID 41745556) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review and meta-analysis of clinical studies (14 qualitative, 10 quantitative; searched to 31 Oct 2024)
- RESULT: "Comparable clinical performance" of alkasite vs resin composites for retention and secondary caries incidence; "superior outcomes" vs glass ionomer cements. (Abstract gives no pooled numbers.) Described as releasing fluoride, calcium and hydroxyl ions.
- LIMITATIONS: Clinical heterogeneity and, in several studies, moderate risk of bias (stated).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Short-term clinical evidence indicates alkasite is a clinically acceptable alternative. Follow-up lengths and numbers are not in the abstract, so long-term durability cannot be concluded.

- SOURCE: Bonchev et al., 2025, J Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1016/j.jdent.2025.105916 (PMID 40541917) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: narrative review of in-vitro and in-vivo studies (literature to March 2025)
- RESULT: Alkasites show comparable or superior physical/mechanical properties to commonly used restoratives; bulk-fill application; suited to Class I, II, V cavities in primary and permanent teeth; release hydroxide, calcium and fluoride ions that "may contribute" to remineralisation; hydroxyl release may raise pH and neutralise acid.
- LIMITATIONS: Narrative design; not stated otherwise in abstract.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Mechanism (pH-responsive ion release) is described, but the abstract states "may contribute" -- not demonstrated clinically.

- SOURCE: Padilla-Ocampo et al., 2025, Cureus | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.7759/cureus.83784 (PMID 40491641) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: quasi-experimental pilot, 16 children, 36 restorations (alkasite n=28, Fuji IX n=8), 6 months
- RESULT: Dislodgement alkasite 3.6% (1/28) vs GIC 75% (6/8), p=0.000 (Fisher); fracture 0/28 vs 3/8 (37.5%), p=0.008.
- LIMITATIONS: Materials assigned "according to clinical criteria" (not randomised), unequal groups (28 vs 8), 6-month follow-up (stated by authors: longer studies needed).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Hypothesis-generating only. Cannot cite as evidence of superiority over GIC.

### Claim 5: Bioactive / ion-releasing restoratives -- clinical vs in vitro
- SOURCE: Popa et al., 2026, J Clin Med | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.3390/jcm15010373 (PMID 41517620; PMC12786899) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review (3 RCTs, 86 children, 305 restorations; 1 in-vitro study) of ACTIVA BioACTIVE in primary teeth
- RESULT: 12-month clinical success with ACTIVA 97.5-97.8% vs 95.0-97.8% for bulk-fill and conventional composites. 24 months (split-mouth): clinical success 93.0% (ACTIVA) vs 95.3% (compomer); radiographic 86.0% vs 88.3%, within predefined non-inferiority margin. ACTIVA placement 2.4 +/- 0.6 min shorter than compomer. In vitro SBS to primary dentin 4.29 +/- 0.65 vs 2.47 +/- 0.32 MPa (RMGIC); calcium release at 21 days 0.77 +/- 0.13 vs 0.53 +/- 0.12 ppm.
- LIMITATIONS: Only 3 RCTs and 1 in-vitro study; follow-up 1-2 years (stated/obvious from abstract).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: ACTIVA performs comparably to compomer/composite in primary molars for up to 2 years. Cannot conclude bioactivity gives a clinical (caries-preventive) advantage.

- SOURCE: Raghip et al., 2025, J Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1016/j.jdent.2025.105912 (PMID 40518039) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: double-blind split-mouth RCT, 22 patients, 44 Class I restorations, 24 months
- RESULT: No failures or secondary caries in either group over 24 months; significant differences at 24 months in surface luster, occlusal wear, anatomical form, and material fracture (p<0.05) favouring ACTIVA.
- LIMITATIONS: Small sample (22 patients); other limitations not stated in abstract.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: ACTIVA is similar to bulk-fill composite at 2 years in small RCT; secondary-caries outcome is uninformative (zero events).

- SOURCE: Bhavana et al., 2024, Int J Clin Pediatr Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.5005/jp-journals-10005-2963 (PMID 39650299) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: split-mouth RCT, 31 children, 62 primary molars, 12 months
- RESULT: Surface texture significantly better with ACTIVA than RMGIC at 6 months (p=0.002) and 12 months (p=0.001); marginal discoloration (p=0.267 at 6 mo, 0.339 at 12 mo) and retention (p=0.161, 0.064) not significantly different.
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: ACTIVA retention is similar to RMGIC at 12 months; no evidence of clinical superiority.

- SOURCE: Al-Hasan et al., 2022, Oper Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.2341/21-048-L (PMID 35917246) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: in-vitro (64 extracted molars, SBS, SEM)
- RESULT: ACTIVA had reduced SBS to sound and caries-affected dentin compared with Fuji II LC after 24 h and two months (p=0.01); SBS of ACTIVA stable on ageing; mineral-like deposits seen on aged debonded dentin surfaces by SEM "might support" tissue repair potential. Authors suggest composite coverage in stress-bearing areas.
- LIMITATIONS: In vitro; not stated otherwise.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: In-vitro bond strength of ACTIVA is lower than a conventional RMGIC; bioactivity (deposits) is only suggestive.

- SOURCE: Danelon et al., 2025, J Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1016/j.jdent.2025.105892 (PMID 40490048) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review and meta-analysis (32 studies; clinical trials, in situ, in vitro) -- metallic agents in GIC/RMGIC
- RESULT: Silver, ZnO, TiO2, copper enhanced antimicrobial properties; only two studies neutral. Meta-analysis: GIC with 5% TiO2 or 2% ZnO nanoparticles significantly reduced S. mutans viability; silver zeolite 1% and 5% strong effects; silver or ZnO in RMGIC improved activity against S. mutans and L. acidophilus. Risk of bias low.
- LIMITATIONS: Authors say further in vivo research needed, and effect on mechanical, esthetic and cytotoxic properties must be assessed.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Antibacterial effect shown in vitro/in situ. Cannot conclude reduced secondary caries or longer longevity (the abstract says it "may").

- SOURCE: Guo et al., 2024, J Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1016/j.jdent.2024.105288 (PMID 39103074) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: literature review (21 studies) of nanosilver-modified GIC
- RESULT: 13 studies on antimicrobial properties, all supported enhanced antimicrobial effect; 19 reported mechanical properties but "inconclusive"; 4 found nanosilver did not influence bond to dentine; fluoride release, colour stability and cytotoxicity results "all inconclusive".
- LIMITATIONS: Inconclusive mechanical/cytotoxicity data (stated).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Antimicrobial gain is consistent in vitro; safety and mechanical trade-offs unresolved.

- SOURCE: Zhang et al., 2025, J Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1016/j.jdent.2025.106149 (PMID 41052633) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: scoping review, 48 studies, hydroxyapatite-modified GIC
- RESULT: HA enhanced mechanical, chemical, biological properties, particularly flexural strength and fluoride release; compressive and diametral tensile strength variable.
- LIMITATIONS: Heterogeneity; authors call for standardised methods and clinical trials.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Laboratory promise only; no clinical validation.

- SOURCE: Dobrzynski et al., 2026, Materials | DOI 10.3390/ma19163393 (PMID 42654547) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review of in-vitro studies (23 studies; QUIN tool: 20 medium, 3 low risk) -- orthodontic GIC additives
- RESULT: Fluoride release increased with nCaF, TMPnano, nanofluorapatite, hydroxyapatite, nanochitosan, SNP/ZnONP, ZnSO; antibacterial activity most evident for CHX, DMAHDM, MDPB, propolis, silibinin, nanosilver, zinc systems. Meta-analysis not done because of heterogeneity.
- LIMITATIONS: Substantial methodological heterogeneity; further standardised long-term and clinical studies required.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Chlorhexidine-type additives are antibacterial in vitro (orthodontic cements); no clinical recommendation possible. No clinical evidence was found in this session for zirconia-reinforced GIC, glass carbomer, or chlorhexidine-GIC in restorations (see UNRESOLVED).

### Claim 6: Sandwich / bi-layered restorations
- SOURCE: de Paula et al., 2019, J Adhes Dent | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.3290/j.jad.a43696 (PMID 31802065) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs (NCCL, sandwich technique vs composite only; GRADE)
- RESULT: 3645 articles initially; 6 retrieved, three being follow-ups of the same RCT; 4 studies analysed; all at unclear risk of bias. Only loss of retention differed: lower with sandwich at 3 years, RR 7.5 (95% CI 2.1-27.2; p=0.002). Secondary outcomes not influenced. Retention evidence of moderate quality; other outcomes low.
- LIMITATIONS: "Limited number of available studies" (stated); all unclear risk of bias.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Higher retention for the sandwich technique in NCCLs at 3 years, based on very few trials. Cannot conclude for Class I/II restorations or compare closed vs open sandwich (not addressed in abstract). Do not quote the RR as a precise effect size given the wide CI.

- SOURCE: Fernandez et al., 2025, J Esthet Restor Dent | DOI 10.1111/jerd.70052 (PMID 41159592) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: umbrella review
- RESULT: "The sandwich technique with RMGIC bases improved retention in NCCLs." (see Claim 2)
- LIMITATIONS: see Claim 2.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Corroborates de Paula for NCCL.

- SOURCE: Ismail et al., 2025, Restor Dent Endod | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.5395/rde.2025.50.e13 (PMID 40234007; PMC12151766) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review and meta-analysis of laboratory studies (29 studies; 3 in meta-analysis)
- RESULT: 24 studies on new GIs and 5 on GI repair. Meta-analysis of three studies: no significant difference in mean MPa for RMGI treated with phosphoric acid or Er,Cr:YSGG laser before an etch-and-rinse adhesive. Surface treatment is "crucial" for bonding regardless of GI condition; for GI repair both mechanical and chemical treatment recommended.
- LIMITATIONS: Meta-analysis based on only 3 studies; bias assessment details not given in abstract.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Bond of composite to GI/RMGI depends on surface treatment (lab). Cannot conclude clinical longevity.

- SOURCE: Manihani et al., 2021, Dent Res J (Isfahan) | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.4103/1735-3327.326645 (PMID 34760063; PMC8543098) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review of in-vitro studies on extracted human teeth (10 articles, 2000-Sept 2018)
- RESULT: Self-etch adhesives gave greater composite-to-GIC/RMGIC bond strength than total-etch adhesives; primer on unset GIC gave higher bond strength than on set GIC; self-etch over uncured RMGIC (co-curing) better than over cured RMGIC.
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract (10 laboratory studies only).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Supports a lab-based bonding protocol rationale. No evidence on open vs closed sandwich was opened (see UNRESOLVED).

### Claim 7: Biocompatibility / pulp response; HEMA cytotoxicity of RMGIC
- SOURCE: Agha et al., 2021, Dent Mater | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1016/j.dental.2021.08.003 (PMID 34462139) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: in-vitro (HPLC monomer release; MTT assay on normal human oral fibroblasts NHOF-1)
- RESULT: Abstract states RMGIC cytotoxicity "has been associated with" HEMA release. Three experimental THFM-containing materials released less or similar monomer vs commercial products; two (F3, F4) had effects similar to negative control medium on NHOF-1 cells.
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract (cell-line test of extracts).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Supports HEMA release as a mechanistic cause of in-vitro RMGIC cytotoxicity and a route to reduce it. Cannot conclude anything on in-vivo pulp response.

- SOURCE: Potiprapanpong et al., 2023, Int J Mol Sci | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.3390/ijms241210231 (PMID 37373383; PMC10299549) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: in-vitro
- RESULT: Cell viability with extracts from experimental RMGICs 89-98% and TheraCal LC 93% vs 4% for Vitrebond (commercial RMGIC). Monomer conversion Vitrebond 96% vs 21-51% experimental. Fluoride release 137 ppm (5 wt% HEMA experimental) vs 88 ppm (Vitrebond), p<0.01. Authors state adding HEMA did not significantly change cytotoxicity within the experimental series.
- LIMITATIONS: Experimental materials lower in flexural strength (31 MPa vs 46 MPa for Vitrebond, p<0.01); in-vitro only.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Vitrebond (a lining RMGIC) extract was strongly cytotoxic in vitro (4% viability). Cannot generalise to restorative RMGICs or in-vivo pulp response. Do not attribute cytotoxicity solely to HEMA from this abstract (other components differ).

- SOURCE: Potiprapanpong et al., 2021, Polymers (Basel) | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.3390/polym13162742 (PMID 34451281; PMC8399068) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: in-vitro
- RESULT: Experimental RMGICs with methacrylate-functionalised polyacids (0 or 5 wt% HEMA) showed higher relative cell viability than Vitrebond; "toxic" HEMA described by authors as the motivation. (Abstract gives no viability percentages.)
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Corroborates in-vitro concern about HEMA-containing lining RMGIC; no clinical inference.

- SOURCE: Roussou et al., 2021, Molecules | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.3390/molecules26164912 (PMID 34443499; PMC8399195) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: in-vitro (GC/MS, ICP-OES eluates; sulforhodamine B viability on human gingival fibroblasts; Activa, Fuji II LC, Fuji IX etc.)
- RESULT: HEMA among organic eluates detected; "Activa Bioactive Restorative was found to be biocompatible"; elution depended on composition, solvent, storage time.
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract (the abstract compares a conventional and a bioactive resin for cell survival, not GICs).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Confirms HEMA elution from RMGIC in vitro. Cannot rank Fuji II LC cytotoxicity vs others from the abstract.

- SOURCE: Malacarne et al., 2022, Crit Rev Toxicol | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1080/10408444.2022.2101914 (PMID 36102112) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: systematic review (13 manuscripts; EPHPP: 8 strong/moderate, 5 weak)
- RESULT: Six studies reported genotoxicity of the (modified) GICs tested; two concluded genotoxicity was time-dependent.
- LIMITATIONS: 5 of 13 studies weak quality (stated).
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: Some genotoxic signal exists in lab studies of modified GICs; clinical relevance unknown. Use cautiously.

### Claim 8: Glass hybrid (EQUIA Forte) posterior restorations -- clinical survival
- SOURCE: Miletic et al., 2024, Clin Oral Investig | URL or DOI exactly as opened: DOI 10.1007/s00784-024-05491-y (PMID 38243032) | OPENED: abstract | STUDY TYPE: randomised multicentre split-mouth trial, 180 patients, two-surface Class II molar restorations, 5 years, FDI-2 criteria
- RESULT: No significant difference in overall survival/success (p>0.05). Success (FDI-2 1-3): EQUIA Forte 81.9% (average AFR 3.9%) vs Tetric EvoCeram 90.7% (AFR 1.9%). Survival (FDI-2 1-4): 94.5% vs 94.4%, AFR 1.1% both.
- LIMITATIONS: Not stated in abstract.
- CAN CONCLUDE / CANNOT CONCLUDE: In moderate-large two-surface Class II cavities glass hybrid survived as well as nano-hybrid composite over 5 years. Success rate was numerically lower (81.9% vs 90.7%); the abstract says no significant difference overall.
