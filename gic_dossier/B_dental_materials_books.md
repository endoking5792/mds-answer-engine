T10|[BOOK: Phillips12 | FILE: Chapter_14.md | PDF p.342 | ANCHOR: "Clinical applications of hybrid ionomers include liners, fissure"] Hybrid ionomer applications: liners, fissure sealants, bases, core buildups, restoratives, adhesives for orthodontic brackets, repair of damaged amalgam cores/cusps, retrograde root filling materials; surface conditioning with mild acid essential; hybrid containing nonreactive filler needs a dentin bonding system; HEMA can cause pulpal inflammation and allergic contact dermatitis - less biocompatible than conventional GIC; exotherm drawback. Fissure sealants: low-viscosity hybrid or conventional GIC; one study retention poor after 1 year but patches retained in fissures with no caries (p.343).
T12|[BOOK: Phillips12 | FILE: Chapter_14.md | PDF p.343 | ANCHOR: "As a liner or base material, hybrid ionomer serves as an inter"] Hybrid ionomer liner/base: intermediate bonding material under composite; not indicated for direct pulp capping (use calcium hydroxide first); dentin adhesion reduces gap at gingival margins compensating for composite shrinkage; reduces technique sensitivity of resin bonding agents; anticariogenic; sandwich technique recommended for Class II and V composites in moderate to high caries risk patients.
T8|[BOOK: Phillips12 | FILE: Chapter_14.md | PDF p.343 | ANCHOR: "The monomers in hybrid GICs make the cements more trans"] Hybrid GIC restorations: more translucent; fluoride release unchanged from conventional GIC; diametral tensile strength higher (not compressive) due to lower modulus and more plastic deformation; HEMA water absorption causes expansion up to about 8% by volume (linked to all-ceramic crown fracture as core or luting cement, but in vitro all-ceramic crowns cemented with hybrid ionomer did not crack after 12 months in water).
T9|[BOOK: Phillips12 | FILE: Chapter_14.md | PDF p.343 | ANCHOR: "Porosity in cement is the result of air incorporation during"] Porosity: from air in mixing; one study: mixing method no influence on small pores but hand mixing more large pores than triturator; later study: hand mixing higher compressive strength for luting cement, capsule mixing stronger restorative cement (small difference); porosity ranking hand-mixed: polycarboxylate highest, resin cement least, zinc phosphate and GIC intermediate; static mixing (dual cartridges) takes less time, no air mixed in, low porosity expected (little data).
T8|[BOOK: Phillips12 | FILE: Chapter_14.md | PDF p.343 | ANCHOR: "A hybrid product with a composition between that of calcium"] Calcium aluminate GIC (luting fixed prostheses): monocalcium aluminate from sintering Al2O3 and CaO about 1:1 molar; water dissolves Ca2+, Al(OH)4-, OH-, weak acid-base; hydraulic cement; powder: calcium aluminate, polyacrylic acid, tartaric acid, strontium-fluoro-alumino-glass, strontium fluoride; liquid 99.6% water + 0.4% additives; GIC part gives early properties, calcium aluminate gives basic pH during cure, less microleakage, excellent biocompatibility, long-term strength; polyacrylic acid cross-linked by Ca2+ and disperses calcium aluminate; capsules (Fig 14-21); working time 2 min, setting 5 min, film thickness 15 +/- 4 um, setting expansion 0.4% in vitro, hydroxyapatite formation in phosphate buffer, favourable 2-year clinical study (p.343-344).
T8|[BOOK: Phillips12 | FILE: Chapter_14.md | PDF p.344 | ANCHOR: "Compomer is a polyacid-modified composite made by incor"] Compomer: glass particles of GIC in water-free polyacid liquid monomer with initiator; integrates fluoride release of GIC with durability of composites; usually one-paste light-cure restoratives, powder-liquid for luting; water-free with nonreactive filler, reactive silicate glass, sodium fluoride, polyacid-modified monomers (diester of HEMA with butane carboxylic acid), photoactivators; set by photopolymerization; absorbs saliva water starting slow acid-base reaction -> fluoride release; luting powder strontia-alumina-fluorosilicate glass, metal oxides, initiators; liquid methacrylate/carboxylic monomers, multifunctional acrylates, water.
T4|[BOOK: Phillips12 | FILE: Chapter_14.md | PDF p.344 | ANCHOR: "Compomer restorative materials require a dentin-bonding"] Compomer restoratives need dentin-bonding agent (no water so not self-adhesive); bond of one-paste compomers with DBA similar or higher than hybrid ionomers; powder-liquid compomer luting cements self-adhesive (water makes mixture acidic); mainly low-stress Class III and V; tooth etched before DBA; finished like composites; luting: cement on prosthesis only, light-cure margin; chemical-cure compomers set in about 3 minutes in mouth (10+ on bench), gel at 90 s after mixing end.
T6|[BOOK: Phillips12 | FILE: Chapter_14.md | PDF p.345 | ANCHOR: "Water absorption by restorative compomers, which is as high"] Compomer water absorption up to 3.5% by weight (needed to complete acid-base and fluoride release); less fluoride than GIC/hybrid; compressive and flexural strength decrease on storage in saline (not in composites); silane-treated glass reduces uptake; surface hardness and microtensile strength unchanged. Clinical: not all compomers acceptable in Class V; color stability problem (refractive index change, staining); 24-month study: as good as resin sealants except marginal integrity; as good as composites for Class I/II; acceptable as luting for orthodontic bands.
