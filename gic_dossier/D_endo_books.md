## T3 Setting reaction

- [BOOK: Cohen | FILE: 11_11_Periradicular_surgery.md | PDF p.1467 | ANCHOR: "consists of aqueous polymeric acids, such as polyacrylic acid"] GIC consists of aqueous polymeric acids such as polyacrylic acid plus basic glass powders such as calcium aluminosilicate.
- [BOOK: Cohen | FILE: 11_11_Periradicular_surgery.md | PDF p.1468 | ANCHOR: "GIC sets by a neutralization reaction of aluminosilicate"] GIC sets by a neutralization reaction in which the aluminosilicate is chelated with carboxylate groups to cross-link the polyacids; a substantial amount of glass remains unreacted and acts as reinforcing filler. GICs can be light- or chemically cured.
- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.608 | ANCHOR: "Glass ionomers consist primarily of alumina, silica, and polyalkenoic acid"] Conventional glass ionomers consist primarily of alumina, silica and polyalkenoic acid and are self-curing materials.
- [BOOK: DentineHypersensitivity | FILE: 09_9_Future_Strategies_for_the_Development_of_Desensitizing_Pro.md | PDF p.185 | ANCHOR: "Al-O-Si bonds in glass (ionomer) polyalkenoate cements"] Al-O-Si bonds in glass-ionomer (polyalkenoate) cements are acid-hydrolysed; Al2O3 acts as an intermediate oxide in the glass (analogy to Zn-O-Si bonds in zinc-containing bioactive glass).

## T4 Adhesion/bonding

- [BOOK: Cohen | FILE: 09_9_Obturation_of_the_cleaned_and_shaped_root_canal_system.md | PDF p.1096 | ANCHOR: "it is difficult to properly treat the dentinal walls in the"] Difficult to condition apical and middle third dentinal walls to receive GI sealer. Disadvantages: harder to remove in retreatment; minimal antimicrobial activity.
- [BOOK: Cohen | FILE: 26_26_Bleaching_procedures.md | PDF p.2998 | ANCHOR: "adhesive bond strength between glass-ionomer cements and composite resins to dentin"] Consensus: adhesive bond strength of glass-ionomer cements and composite resins to dentin and enamel is temporarily compromised after bleaching due to peroxide remnants inhibiting resin polymerization; optimal bonding re-established after 3 weeks; at least 1 week recommended.
- [BOOK: Ingles | FILE: 05_Chapter_5_Pulpal_Pathosis.md | PDF p.134 | ANCHOR: "creates a good seal to dentin and materials such as glass"] After setting, MTA creates a good seal to dentin and to materials such as glass ionomer cements, copolymers and composites.
- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.608 | ANCHOR: "They derive mechanical retention from microporosities created on the"] Derive mechanical retention from microporosities created on dentin by etching with mild acids, creating a hybrid layer-like interface (Fig 14-16).
- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.609 | ANCHOR: "glass-ionomer materials form a “dynamic” bond"] Unlike resins, GI forms a "dynamic" bond: as the interface is stressed bonds break and new bonds form; this, plus low polymerization shrinkage and coefficient of thermal expansion similar to tooth structure, helps clinical success.
- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.609 | ANCHOR: "Treatment of the dentin surface with a polyacrylic acid conditioner removes"] Fig 14-16: polyacrylic acid conditioner removes smear layer and partially demineralizes dentin; chemical interaction forms an interaction layer between glass ionomer and dentin; hydrogel layer seen around fluoride-releasing leachable glass fillers.
- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.610 | ANCHOR: "do not require a dentin bonding agent"] True RMGIs use same bonding procedures as traditional GIs and need no dentin bonding agent because they contain hydrophilic monomers such as 2-hydroxyethyl methacrylate (HEMA); Fig 14-17 shows interaction layer partially resin-infiltrated and a filler-free resinous layer above dentin.
- [BOOK: DentineHypersensitivity | FILE: 07_7_Treatment_Modalities.md | PDF p.136 | ANCHOR: "carboxylic groups in the material replace phosphate"] Chemical bond is ionic: carboxylic groups in the material replace phosphate ions and form ionic bonds with calcium ions of hydroxyapatite; GI-based materials long the main option because of good bonding to hypermineralised tissue and ideal elastic modulus.
- [BOOK: DentineHypersensitivity | FILE: 09_9_Future_Strategies_for_the_Development_of_Desensitizing_Pro.md | PDF p.177 | ANCHOR: "the same polymer used in glass ionomer cements and which enables"] Poly(acrylic acid) is the polymer used in glass ionomer cements and enables bonding to the apatite phase of enamel and dentine; used in NovaMin pastes to chelate calcium and adhere glass particles to the tooth.
- [BOOK: Trauma Atlas | FILE: 25_25_Restoration_of_Traumatized_Teeth_with_Resin_Composites.md | PDF p.737 | ANCHOR: "Glass ionomer-based materials have a chemical bond to tooth tissues"] GI materials chemically bond to tooth tissue; RMGIC introduced 1988 with light-curing resin giving higher resistance to early moisture contact/desiccation and higher mechanical characteristics; first used as bases/restoratives; in 1995 a modern RMGIC dentin-enamel adhesive developed.
- [BOOK: Trauma Atlas | FILE: 25_25_Restoration_of_Traumatized_Teeth_with_Resin_Composites.md | PDF p.737 | ANCHOR: "After pretreatment of the cavity with a weak polyalkenoic acid"] After weak polyalkenoic acid pretreatment, adhesion by micromechanical interlocking via submicron hybrid layer (0.5-1 um) plus ionic bonds between carboxyl groups of the GI and calcium of residual hydroxyapatite.
- [BOOK: Gulabivala | FILE: 14_14_The_restorative_endo_interface.md | PDF p.364 | ANCHOR: "moisture in the core may then affect the physical properties of"] Moisture absorbed in composite cores may affect physical properties of acid-based luting cements such as zinc phosphate, glass ionomer or polycarboxylate; eugenol temporary cements soften cores.
- [BOOK: CurrentTherapy | FILE: 05_6_pdf_p_137_166.md | PDF p.143 | ANCHOR: "they do not bond to gutta-percha"] GICs and RMGICs bond to root dentin and have been marketed as sealers but do not bond to gutta-percha, so a true monoblock is not formed.
- [BOOK: Prognosis | FILE: 10_10_Restoration_of_Endodontically_Treated_Teeth.md | PDF p.174 | ANCHOR: "prevents adhesion of glass ionomer to dentin"] Zinc oxide-based sealers inhibit polymerization of dentin adhesives/composite and prevent adhesion of glass ionomer to dentin; excess sealer must be removed from pulpal floor and walls.

## T5 Fluoride

- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.608 | ANCHOR: "They release fluoride for a period of time after initial"] Release fluoride for a period after placement; described as the only restorative materials that form a chemical bond to tooth structure.
- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.609 | ANCHOR: "potential for ion migration, both inward to and outward from the"] GIs are water-based so ion migration inward and outward gives advantages such as fluoride release and recharging; contrasted with hydrophobic composites that barely support ion migration; however GIs lack physical properties for load-bearing areas.

## T6 Properties

- [BOOK: Cohen | FILE: 23_23_Restoration_of_the_endodontically_treated_tooth.md | PDF p.2761 | ANCHOR: "Conventional glass ionomer cements have compressive strengths ranging between 100 and"] Conventional GIC: compressive strength 100-200 MPa; Young modulus about 5 GPa; more mechanically resistant than zinc phosphate; bonds to dentin 3-5 MPa; advantages ease of manipulation, chemical setting, bonding to tooth and post.
- [BOOK: Prognosis | FILE: 10_10_Restoration_of_Endodontically_Treated_Teeth.md | PDF p.178 | ANCHOR: "Glass ionomer cements have good physical properties, seal against dentin, and"] Post cementation: GIC has good physical properties, seals against dentin, releases fluoride; needs moisture control and protection during initial set; polycarboxylate poor and to be avoided; zinc phosphate brittleness allows post removal at retreatment.

## T8 Modified/newer materials (RMGIC, cermet, bioactive etc.)

- [BOOK: Cohen | FILE: 11_11_Periradicular_surgery.md | PDF p.1468 | ANCHOR: "Silver has been incorporated into GIC to improve the physical properties"] Silver incorporated into GIC to improve compressive and tensile strength and creep resistance; long-term surface changes can occur in silver-GIC that may affect its stability in periradicular tissues. Both forms of GIC suggested as alternative root-end filling material.
- [BOOK: Cohen | FILE: 11_11_Periradicular_surgery.md | PDF p.1471 | ANCHOR: "attempts to combine the various properties of composite resins and glass"] Resin-ionomer suspension (Geristore) and compomer (Dyract) combine properties of composites and glass ionomers; published literature as root-end fillings limited; both require light activation and resin-dentin bonding agents to attach to the tooth.
- [BOOK: Cohen | FILE: 09_9_Obturation_of_the_cleaned_and_shaped_root_canal_system.md | PDF p.1096 | ANCHOR: "consists of a glass ionomer–impregnated GP cone with a glass ionomer"] Activ GP (Brasseler USA): GP cone impregnated with glass ionomer with external GI coating plus GI sealer; 0.04 and 0.06 taper, single-cone technique designed to bond canal wall to master cone, alleged monoblock.
- [BOOK: Cohen | FILE: 23_23_Restoration_of_the_endodontically_treated_tooth.md | PDF p.2759 | ANCHOR: "Resin-modified glass ionomers have moderate strength, greater than glass ionomers"] RMGI: moderate strength (greater than GI, less than composite); adequate for moderate buildups but hygroscopic expansion can fracture ceramic crowns and fragilized roots; dentin bond close to bonded composite and significantly higher than traditional GI; today composites have replaced GI materials for cores.
- [BOOK: Ingles | FILE: 38_Chapter_38_Endodontic_Therapy_in_the_Pediatric_Patient.md | PDF p.1005 | ANCHOR: "RMGICs are more cytotoxic to the pulp cells than conventional GICs"] RMGIC (Vitremer, Fuji II LC) = GI powder plus light-cure resin, improved physical properties, substantially reduced initial hardening time; more cytotoxic to pulp cells than conventional GIC due to unpolymerized monomers, not for direct pulp contact; no reported clinical evidence of reparative dentin; Farooq et al. reported 93% success of IPT over average four years with RMGI liner/base without Ca(OH)2.
- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.610 | ANCHOR: "were developed to overcome some of the undesirable properties of the"] RMGIs developed to overcome undesirable properties of traditional GIs; e.g., Vitremer (3M ESPE), Fuji II LC (GC): glass-ionomer powder plus light-cure resin allowing immediate light polymerization; resin protects from dehydration and improves physical/mechanical properties and esthetics.
- [BOOK: DentineHypersensitivity | FILE: 07_7_Treatment_Modalities.md | PDF p.131 | ANCHOR: "which is a light-cured resin-modified glass ionomer cement (RMGIC) based on"] Vanish XT Extended Contact Varnish (3M ESPE) is a light-cured RMGIC based on methacrylate-modified polyalkenoic acid; indications are remineralizing and tubule occlusion; only one clinical trial showed benefit of RMGIC Clinpro XT for dentin hypersensitivity at 6 months (Madruga et al. 2017).
- [BOOK: DentineHypersensitivity | FILE: 10_10_Remineralization_of_the_Early_Carious_Lesion_with_Emphasi.md | PDF p.204 | ANCHOR: "RMGICs show a similar ability to conventional GICs to remineralize affected"] Systematic review: RMGICs show similar ability to conventional GICs to remineralize affected dentin; additives such as bioactive glass (BAG) or CCP-ACP improve remineralizing potential; supports RMGIC as restorative after selective caries removal.
- [BOOK: DentineHypersensitivity | FILE: 10_10_Remineralization_of_the_Early_Carious_Lesion_with_Emphasi.md | PDF p.204 | ANCHOR: "synthesis of Glass ionomer cements (GIC) modified with calcium phosphate nanoparticles"] In vitro study: GIC modified with calcium phosphate nanoparticles (nCaP) showed higher compression and diametral tensile resistance, non-cytotoxic behaviour and adequate cell viability on human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells.
- [BOOK: Sikri | FILE: 20_20_Obturation_of_Root_Canal_Spaces.md | PDF p.379 | ANCHOR: "The working time of the glass ionomer sealer is improved by"] ActiV GP: conventional GP cones coated with GI sealer; working time of GI sealer improved by reducing particle size to "nanoparticle level (2.0 um)" as printed; glass-ionomer coating facilitates dentin adhesiveness.
- [BOOK: Sikri | FILE: 22_22_Postendodontic_Restorations.md | PDF p.424 | ANCHOR: "A combination of glass-ionomer cement and silver alloy in the ratio"] Glass-cermet: glass-ionomer plus silver alloy in 7:1 ratio used as core; has mechanical properties needed for core and reduces caries by releasing fluoride; black colour limits use to under metal or metal-ceramic crowns.
- [BOOK: Sikri | FILE: 22_22_Postendodontic_Restorations.md | PDF p.424 | ANCHOR: "Composite resin in combination of glass-ionomer cement has also been used"] Resin modified glass-ionomer (composite combined with GIC) also used as core material.

- [BOOK: Harty | FILE: 05_5_Maintaining_Dental_Pulp_Vitality.md | PDF p.84 | ANCHOR: "Cements based on calcium hydroxide, glass ionomer or calcium silicate all"] Under amalgam, calcium hydroxide, glass ionomer or calcium silicate cements all appear satisfactory and are widely used; choice in deep cavities without exposure is largely operator preference.

## T14 Advantages/disadvantages/performance

- [BOOK: Cohen | FILE: 11_11_Periradicular_surgery.md | PDF p.1468 | ANCHOR: "GICs are susceptible to attack by moisture during the initial setting"] GICs are susceptible to moisture during initial setting, giving increased solubility and decreased bond strength; moisture/blood contamination adversely affected outcome of GIC root-end fillings, significantly more often in failed cases.
- [BOOK: Ingles | FILE: 27_Chapter_27_Vital_Pulp_Therapy.md | PDF p.803 | ANCHOR: "may exhibit less microleakage if placed in conjunction with a bonding"] Amalgam restorations may show less microleakage when placed with bonding resin or resin-modified glass-ionomer liner. Amalgam modulus 28-60 GPa vs dentin 12-18 GPa.
- [BOOK: Grossman | FILE: 16_Chapter_16_Obturation_of_the_Radicular_Space.md | PDF p.364 | ANCHOR: "Glass ionomer sealers are not popular because of the difficulty in"] GI sealers not popular because difficult to remove from canal walls in retreatment; solvents are ineffective against them.
- [BOOK: Grossman | FILE: 18_Chapter_18_Prosthodontic_Considerations_in_Endodontically_Tr.md | PDF p.387 | ANCHOR: "has been shown to be weak in tensile and compressive strengths"] Glass ionomer cement (Type IX) as core: weak in tensile and compressive strength, low fracture resistance, low modulus of elasticity, poor condensability, high solubility; use as core material should be avoided ("should not be used as a core buildup material").
- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.609 | ANCHOR: "lack the physical properties required for use in load-bearing areas"] GIs lack the physical properties required for load-bearing areas.
- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.610 | ANCHOR: "with a reported annual failure rate of less than 3% over"] RMGI clinical review: retention generally good, annual failure rate under 3% over 13 years; margins likely to deteriorate, some wear and loss of anatomical form mid to long term; fewer problems than large Class II composites with secondary caries and postoperative sensitivity.
- [BOOK: Seltzer | FILE: Chapter_14_Caries_Restorative_Dentistry_and_the_Pulp.md | PDF p.622 | ANCHOR: "limited repair and recharging potential that is present in conventional glass"] Contemporary restorative materials lack autonomic healing mechanisms except for the limited repair and recharging potential of conventional glass ionomers.
- [BOOK: DentineHypersensitivity | FILE: 07_7_Treatment_Modalities.md | PDF p.136 | ANCHOR: "monomeric materials offer more resistance compared to glass ionomers upon toothbrushing"] Resin materials resist toothbrushing abrasion and acidic erosion better than glass ionomers (Peumans et al. 2020); GI drawbacks mentioned include high surface roughness and colour alteration over time.
- [BOOK: Sikri | FILE: 22_22_Postendodontic_Restorations.md | PDF p.428 | ANCHOR: "comparatively easier than glass-ionomers, composites and dual-cure cements"] Excess set zinc phosphate and polycarboxylate cement is easier to clean than glass-ionomer, composite and dual-cure cements; clean excess before complete set.
- [BOOK: Sikri | FILE: 24_24_Bleaching_of_Discolored_Teeth.md | PDF p.461 | ANCHOR: "Bleaching may increase the solubility of glass-ionomer and other cements"] Bleaching may increase solubility of glass-ionomer and other cements and reduce enamel-resin bond strength, especially in first 24 hours; avoid bleaching agents at least 24 hours before resin-based treatment.
- [BOOK: Gulabivala | FILE: 02_2_Biological_and_clinical_rationale_for_vital_pulp_therapy.md | PDF p.50 | ANCHOR: "the pooled survival rates of single-surface ART restorations using high-viscosity glass"] Meta-analysis (de Amorim 2011): pooled survival of single-surface ART restorations with high-viscosity GI in permanent teeth 85% (95% CI 77%, 91%) at 3 years and 80% (95% CI 76%, 83%) at 5 years; pooled 1-year survival for multiple-surface restorations 80%.
- [BOOK: Gulabivala | FILE: 08_8_Non_surgical_root_canal_treatment.md | PDF p.231 | ANCHOR: "Glass ionomer cements have been recommended because of their adhesive properties"] GICs recommended for temporary seal because of adhesion, but bulk use gives significant setting contraction that may compromise part of the margin; resin-reinforced GI appears to have good antibacterial properties.
- [BOOK: Gulabivala | FILE: 12_12_The_perio_endo_interface.md | PDF p.335 | ANCHOR: "Some operators prefer glass ionomer cement but this can disintegrate in"] Root amputation: root canal orifice dressed and sealed with amalgam; some prefer GIC but it can disintegrate in the mouth and is not preferred.
- [BOOK: Gulabivala | FILE: 14_14_The_restorative_endo_interface.md | PDF p.365 | ANCHOR: "Cermets or metal reinforced glass ionomers have also been recommended as"] Cermets/metal-reinforced GIs recommended as cores, but strength does not compare with amalgam or composite; only suitable as a space-filler, not as structural core giving principal retention and resistance form.
- [BOOK: CurrentTherapy | FILE: 05_6_pdf_p_137_166.md | PDF p.163 | ANCHOR: "coronal leakage of the Activ GP system was probably due to"] Study: coronal leakage with Activ GP probably due to volume increase of GIC sealer; unlikely to improve fracture resistance of endodontically treated teeth; uses single-cone or passive accessory cone (harpooning) technique to protect coating.
- [BOOK: CurrentTherapy | FILE: 06_7_pdf_p_167_178.md | PDF p.175 | ANCHOR: "it is a slow-setting material that requires many hours to achieve"] Post cements: glass ionomer has adequate physical properties but is slow-setting, needing many hours for adequate strength, with high initial solubility in oral fluids; original RMGIC had significant delayed expansion that could cause root fracture, which the current generation has overcome.
- [BOOK: ProblemSolving | FILE: 14_Chapter_14_Problem_Solving_Techniques_for_Revision_of_Previo.md | PDF p.291 | ANCHOR: "will not be effective if composite resins, amalgam, or glass ionomer"] Retreatment: ultrasonic post removal ineffective if composite, amalgam or GIC remain around post base; reduce with burs first.
- [BOOK: ProblemSolving | FILE: 17_Chapter_17_Problem_Solving_in_the_Management_of_Teeth_With_S.md | PDF p.389 | ANCHOR: "Glass ionomer restorations are not recommended, owing to their tendency to"] Subgingival resorptive defect repair: glass ionomer restorations not recommended because they degrade subgingivally and are cytotoxic (author's statement); composite/compomer or resin ionomer used instead.
- [BOOK: ProblemSolving | FILE: 20_Chapter_20_Problem_Solving_the_Challenges_Faced_in_the_Resto.md | PDF p.458 | ANCHOR: "even a glass ionomer would be appropriate as a core buildup"] Core: GI acceptable only if final preparation is in sound dentin, lacks strength as free-standing restoration; when more than half crown missing, bonded amalgam, composite or core paste preferred as glass ionomers not strong enough.
- [BOOK: ProblemSolving | FILE: 20_Chapter_20_Problem_Solving_the_Challenges_Faced_in_the_Resto.md | PDF p.462 | ANCHOR: "one caution, however, with glass ionomer cement is the setting time,"] Post luting: zinc phosphate and glass ionomer used for years with good success; GIC may take days to set fully and further buildup/preparation same day can permanently weaken it; trend toward RMGI and resin cements which give higher initial retention and less leakage but are more technique sensitive.
- [BOOK: Prognosis | FILE: 10_10_Restoration_of_Endodontically_Treated_Teeth.md | PDF p.188 | ANCHOR: "Glass ionomers are markedly inferior to all other restorative options when"] Glass ionomers markedly inferior to other restorative options for endodontically treated teeth; should only be used as liners or bases, not as core buildup or coronal restorations.
