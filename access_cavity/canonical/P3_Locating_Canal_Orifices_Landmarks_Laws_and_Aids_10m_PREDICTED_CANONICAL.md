## 1. Landmarks

### Explanation

Krasner and Rankow, in a study of 500 pulp chambers, found the CEJ the most important landmark for locating the chamber and orifices (Gopikrishna, 2021; Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC]. Setzer and Walsch say that developmental lines on the floor connect all canal orifices, and that the colour difference between bright walls and a darker floor helps most in finding calcified orifices; the line between MB1 and MB2 ends at the MB2 orifice (Setzer and Walsch, 2025) [SRC]. Sikri adds that a canal in the centre of the floor suggests only one canal (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Landmarks**
- **CEJ**: most consistent landmark for the position of the chamber (Gopikrishna, 2021; Gutmann and Fan, 2021).
- **Floor colour**: darker than the walls; developmental grooves lighter (Gutmann and Fan, 2021; Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
- **Developmental lines** connect the orifices; the MB1–MB2 line ends at MB2 (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
- **Radiographs** from two angles; SLOB rule for canal position (Sikri, 2019).
- A canal in the centre of the floor suggests one canal only (Sikri, 2019).
- A sudden narrowing or disappearance of the canal radiolucency suggests a bifurcation (the "fast break"); an unusual root outline suggests an extra canal (Sikri, 2019).

**Where to look in molars** (Sikri, 2019)
- *Maxillary first molar*: MB orifice under the MB cusp; DB orifice 2.0–3.0 mm distal and slightly palatal to it.
- *Mandibular first molar*: ML orifice under the mesial pit; a middle mesial canal may lie between MB and ML; explore the distal root for a second canal.

## 2. Laws

### Explanation

Grossman lists nine laws (Gopikrishna, 2021) and Sikri seven (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]; they are set out in C1. Sikri notes that the laws of orifice location are especially useful for finding a third or fourth canal in premolars and MB2 in maxillary first molars (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Krasner and Rankow laws** (Gopikrishna, 2021)
- **Centrality**: floor in the centre of the tooth at the CEJ.
- **Concentricity**: walls concentric to the external surface at the CEJ.
- **CEJ**: the crown-to-chamber distance is equal all round the CEJ.
- **Symmetry 1 and 2**: orifices equidistant from, and on a line perpendicular to, the mesiodistal line (not maxillary molars).
- **Colour change**: floor darker than walls.
- **Orifice location 1, 2, 3**: orifices at the wall–floor junction, at its angles, and at the ends of the root fusion lines.

Uses: orifice-location laws help find extra canals in premolars and MB2 in maxillary first molars (Sikri, 2019).

## 3. Aids

### Explanation

Cohen's aids: fibre-optic light through the CEJ, 1% methylene blue, the sodium hypochlorite "champagne bubble" test and bleeding points, all enhanced by magnification (Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC]. Sikri names magnification and illumination as required for an ideal access, ultrasonic tips for troughing, and warns that under-extension gives a "mouse hole" effect (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Setzer and Walsch's guideline is complete removal of the roof of the chamber (Setzer and Walsch, 2025) [SRC], and Sikri says a white roof indicates a shallow cavity (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Aids** (Gutmann and Fan, 2021; Sikri, 2019)
- Magnification and illumination; fibre-optic transillumination.
- 1% methylene blue; champagne bubble test; bleeding points.
- Ultrasonic troughing of dentin.
- Remove the entire roof; a white roof means the cavity is too shallow (Setzer and Walsch, 2025; Sikri, 2019).
- Avoid "mouse hole" orifices by extending the axial walls (Sikri, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- CEJ landmark; darker floor; developmental lines.
- Laws of symmetry exclude maxillary molars.
- MB2: apply the location laws and follow the groove from MB1.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
- Mandibular molar floor with the mesiodistal line, symmetric orifices and the groove joining MB1 and MB2.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Listing the laws without examples or exceptions.
- Forgetting the aids (methylene blue, champagne bubble, ultrasonics).
