## 1. Why access morphology matters

### Explanation

The shape of the access cavity should correspond to the shape of the roof of the pulp chamber (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Setzer and Walsch say that, as a rule of thumb, the horizontal outline of the chamber follows the cross-section of the tooth at the CEJ (Setzer and Walsch, 2025) [SRC]. Cohen warns against relying on occlusal anatomy alone because it changes as the crown is destroyed and restored (Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC]. Baumann and Beer note that an incorrect estimate of the crown–root angle makes orifices harder to find (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Sikri lists the errors that follow from ignoring morphology: gouging the labial wall in maxillary anteriors when the linguoaxial inclination is missed, perforation (the maxillary first premolar is the most susceptible tooth), and furcation perforation in mandibular molars from a long-shank bur (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Morphology** means the outline, depth and angulation of the cavity as dictated by the chamber and canals. It matters because:
- the cavity shape should follow the roof of the chamber (Baumann and Beer, 2010);
- the chamber outline follows the CEJ cross-section (Setzer and Walsch, 2025);
- a wrong crown–root angle hides the orifices (Baumann and Beer, 2010), and wrong morphology gives gouging, perforation and missed canals (Sikri, 2019).

## 2. Cavity shape by tooth group

### Explanation

Baumann and Beer and Sikri agree on the broad shapes: triangular in maxillary incisors, oval in premolars, and trapezoidal in mandibular molars (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. For maxillary molars Baumann and Beer start in the central fossa with the bur towards the mesiopalatal cusp, while Sikri gives a quadrilateral shape kept mesial to the oblique ridge, since all orifices lie in the mesial three-fifths of the crown (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. For anteriors Sikri prefers the lingual approach to keep the labial surface intact (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

| Tooth | Shape and key point |
|---|---|
| Maxillary incisors | Triangular, centred on the palatal surface (Baumann and Beer, 2010); lingual approach keeps labial surface intact (Sikri, 2019) |
| Mandibular incisors | Ovoid, funnel-shaped, wider labiolingually to find an extra canal (Sikri, 2019) |
| Premolars | Oval, long axis buccolingual (Baumann and Beer, 2010); buccal canal under buccal cusp, palatal under palatal cusp; look for a third canal in the maxillary first premolar (Sikri, 2019) |

| Mandibular molars | Trapezoidal, confined to the mesial half of the crown (Sikri, 2019) |

## 3. Guidelines for molar access cavity

### Explanation

Setzer and Walsch describe an ideal molar access as giving good visualisation and identification of all orifices without excessive weakening, removing "as little as possible, but only as much as necessary" (Setzer and Walsch, 2025) [SRC]. They note that developmental lines connect all canal orifices, and that the isthmus between MB1 and MB2 appears as one developmental line that ends at the MB2 orifice (Setzer and Walsch, 2025) [SRC]. Coronal straight-line access is achieved by relocating orifices into the corners of the access (Setzer and Walsch, 2025) [SRC].

In the maxillary first molar the mesiobuccal orifice lies under the mesiobuccal cusp, and the distobuccal orifice is found 2.0–3.0 mm distal and slightly palatal to it (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. In the mandibular first molar the distal orifice lies under the central pit, the mesiolingual canal under the mesial pit, and a middle mesial canal may lie between the mesial canals (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Sikri advises exploring the distal root for an extra canal (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Molar guidelines**
1. Study angled radiographs; align the bur with the tooth's long axis (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
2. Outline follows the CEJ cross-section (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
3. Open after caries removal (Setzer and Walsch, 2025); the guideline is to remove the entire roof (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
4. Follow the developmental lines between orifices; the line to MB2 ends at the MB2 orifice (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
5. Maxillary first molar: MB orifice under the MB cusp; DB orifice 2.0–3.0 mm distal and slightly palatal to MB; palatal orifice is the largest (Sikri, 2019).
6. Mandibular first molar: distal orifice under the central pit, ML under the mesial pit; look for a middle mesial canal and a second distal canal (Sikri, 2019).
7. Relocate orifices into the cavity corners for straight-line access (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Maxillary molar: quadrilateral, mesial to the oblique ridge; mandibular molar: trapezoid, mesial half.
- Maxillary first premolar is the tooth most prone to perforation.
- MB2 lies on the developmental line from MB1.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
- Occlusal view of five outlines (triangle, oval, quadrilateral, trapezoid) with orifices marked.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Giving shapes without the reason (shape follows the chamber roof).
- Sacrificing the oblique ridge in the maxillary molar (Sikri says it is unnecessary).
- Forgetting the extra canals: MB2, middle mesial, second distal.
