## 1. Problem and detection

### Explanation

Sikri says pulp calcifications pose a problem in locating canals, and that tertiary dentin from caries or leakage shrinks the chamber and flattens the floor (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Setzer and Walsch note that in older patients the drop into the chamber may not be evident because of mineralisation or tertiary dentin (Setzer and Walsch, 2025) [SRC]. Cohen says the search for orifices should begin only after the chamber is fully prepared, cleaned and dried (Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Problem.** Calcified chambers and roots make it hard to locate, penetrate and negotiate the pulp space (Gutmann and Fan, 2021). Tertiary dentin from caries or leakage reduces the chamber volume, flattens the floor and narrows the entrances (Sikri, 2019); pulp stones may be loose or attached to the floor (Baumann and Beer, 2010). The "drop" into the chamber may be absent (Sikri, 2019; Setzer and Walsch, 2025).

**Before starting.** Take angled radiographs, use magnification, and take extra precautions when the radiograph shows hard tissue in the chamber, to avoid damaging the floor (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).

## 2. Locating the canal

### Explanation

Cohen lists several aids: the floor is darker than the walls and developmental grooves are lighter; fibre-optic light through the CEJ reveals landmarks; staining the floor with 1% methylene blue; the sodium hypochlorite "champagne bubble" test; and canal bleeding points, all enhanced by magnification (Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC]. Sikri adds long-shank burs or ultrasonics to remove tertiary dentin and a slow-speed handpiece where tactile awareness is needed (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Baumann and Beer recommend loupes, the microscope and ultrasonic tips because they protect the floor and reduce perforation risk (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Aids to find the orifice**
- Complete the chamber preparation first; clean and dry the floor, where 95% denatured ethanol may help drying and visibility, and only then search for orifices (Gutmann and Fan, 2021).
- Colour: floor darker than the walls, grooves lighter (Gutmann and Fan, 2021).
- Fibre-optic transillumination through the CEJ (Gutmann and Fan, 2021).
- 1% methylene blue stain; sodium hypochlorite "champagne bubble" test; bleeding points (Gutmann and Fan, 2021).
- Long-shank burs or ultrasonic tips to remove tertiary dentin; a slow-speed handpiece for feel (Sikri, 2019).
- Loupes or microscope; ultrasonic tips protect the floor and reduce the risk of perforation (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- Remove pulp stones before searching, because they block direct access and form niches for bacteria (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

## 3. Negotiation and guided alternative

### Explanation

Once found, Cohen says a small K-file coated with chelating agent is introduced to determine patency (Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC]. Sikri recommends rigid C-Pilot files for sclerosed canals and periodic radiographs to confirm file orientation (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Cohen advocates guided access for partially or completely calcified chambers (Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC], and Nair et al describe the technology as currently used for calcified canals (see C5) (Nair et al, 2021) [SRC]. Cohen lists the risks of poor access: mistaken identification of a located canal, failure to find a canal, gouging, and file separation while searching for an orifice (Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Negotiation.** Small K-file with chelating agent to confirm patency, in short up-and-down strokes with selective circumferential filing and pressure directed away from the furcation; this enlarges the coronal canal safely and moves it laterally, avoiding thinning of the dentin next to the furcation (Gutmann and Fan, 2021). Rigid C-Pilot files for sclerosed canals; take periodic radiographs to confirm orientation (Sikri, 2019).

**Guided access.** Where the freehand search fails, CBCT-based guides are advocated for partially or completely calcified chambers (Gutmann and Fan, 2021; Nair et al, 2021; see C5).

**Errors.** Mistaken canal identity, missed canals, gouging, file separation when searching (Gutmann and Fan, 2021). Take an angled radiograph with a file in the first canal found.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Search for orifices only after the floor is cleaned and dried.
- Colour change, transillumination, methylene blue, champagne bubble, bleeding points.
- Ultrasonic tips and long-shank burs protect the floor.
- Guided access is an option for severe calcification.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
- Flowchart: radiograph, magnification, clean/dry floor, colour and stain, ultrasonic troughing, K-file patency, guided access if still unfound.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Searching deep with a long bur without a radiograph check.
- Forgetting patency confirmation with chelator.
- Not mentioning guided access.
