## 1. The triad

### Explanation

Sikri states that successful endodontics is based on a triad: correct diagnosis, adequate cleaning and shaping, and three-dimensional obturation, and that root canal treatment usually fails when this standard protocol is not followed (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Only one book among those searched names it this way, so the tripartite framing should be treated as Sikri's teaching, not a universally standardised definition [Likely]. A second popular formulation (Schilder's three principles of cleaning, shaping and filling in three dimensions) is [RECALL] and is not attributed here.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Endodontic triad** (Sikri, 2019): the three steps on which successful endodontic treatment is based:
1. **Correct diagnosis**: pulpal and periapical diagnosis, plus the tooth's restorability.
2. **Adequate cleaning and shaping**: removal of tissue, infected dentin and microbes, and a tapering preparation.
3. **Three-dimensional obturation**: sealing the prepared space, apically and coronally.

Failure usually follows when this protocol is not followed (Sikri, 2019).

## 2. Where access cavity fits

### Explanation

Access is the physical step that makes the second and third legs of the triad possible. Sikri calls access the "gateway to success", requires direct access to the apical foramen and not merely to the orifices, and states that access must permit removal of pulp tissue from coronal and radicular spaces (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Cohen says straight-line access gives the best chance of débriding the whole canal space (Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC]. Baumann and Beer say that lack of straight-line access leads to ledging and lateral perforation, and that rotary NiTi instruments need straight-line access to mid-root to avoid cyclic fatigue (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Setzer and Walsch add that length determination should be done only after straight-line access is achieved (Setzer and Walsch, 2025) [SRC]. Obturation depends on preparation, so a poor access carries through to the third leg. Excessive access, however, weakens the tooth (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Importance of access in the triad**
- **Diagnosis**: access gives direct sight of the chamber floor, pulp state, cracks and extra canals; rubber dam isolation is mandatory (Sikri, 2019).
- **Cleaning and shaping**: straight-line access gives the best chance of débriding the whole canal (Gutmann and Fan, 2021); poor access leads to ledging, perforation and instrument fatigue (Baumann and Beer, 2010); length is measured after it is achieved (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
- **Obturation**: a canal that cannot be reached cannot be shaped or filled; access also determines whether an interim and final coronal seal can be built.
- **Limit**: over-preparation weakens the tooth, so the cavity should be only as large as needed (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

"Gateway to success" (Sikri, 2019).

**Alternative meaning to state if the examiner intends it:** the three constituents of treatment success may also be given as cleaning, shaping and obturation [RECALL].

## 3. Access objectives that protect the triad

### Explanation

The six objectives in Cohen's chapter (remove caries, conserve sound structure, unroof the chamber, remove coronal pulp, locate all orifices, straight-line access) each protect a leg of the triad (Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC]. Removing caries and defective restorations before entering the chamber reveals hidden caries or fractures and lets restorability be judged, which belongs to diagnosis (Gutmann and Fan, 2021) [SRC], and opening the chamber only after decay is removed avoids contaminating the canals (Setzer and Walsch, 2025) [SRC]. Baumann and Beer add that only complete removal of an old restoration shows the extent of caries and poorly sealed areas (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Retained pulp debris discolours the crown (Sikri, 2019) [SRC], and Sikri prefers a wider cavity to find extra canals rather than ignoring them for the sake of a conservative preparation (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**How the objectives serve the triad** (Gutmann and Fan, 2021)
- *Diagnosis*: remove caries and old restorations to find cracks and judge restorability (Gutmann and Fan, 2021; Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- *Cleaning and shaping*: unroof the chamber, remove all coronal pulp and find every orifice; a missed canal is an uncleaned canal (Sikri, 2019).
- *Obturation and seal*: open the chamber only after decay is removed to avoid contaminating the canals (Setzer and Walsch, 2025), and conserve sound structure so the tooth can be restored.
- Remnants of pulp left in the chamber discolour the crown (Sikri, 2019).
- A wider cavity to find extra canals is better than ignoring them for the sake of a conservative preparation (Sikri, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Triad (Sikri): diagnosis, cleaning and shaping, 3-D obturation.
- Access is the gateway; it serves the second and third legs.
- Straight-line access: less ledging, perforation and NiTi fatigue.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
- A triangle with the three legs and "access cavity" as the base or gateway beneath it. Labels: diagnosis, cleaning/shaping, obturation, access. Earns marks for showing dependence.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Writing only "cleaning, shaping, obturation" when the question asks for the triad and its link to access (state both meanings).
- Not linking access to each leg.
- Forgetting the limit: over-preparation.
