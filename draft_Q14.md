## STUDY MODE

### S1. Idea and rationale
The sandwich technique places GIC as the dentine replacement and composite as the enamel replacement, so each material is used where it is best [SRC:B50] [SRC:A392]. Composite shrinks and its dentine bond is hard to keep, while GIC bonds to dentine by ion exchange and to composite after cut-back and etching [SRC:A386] [SRC:A387].

### S2. Two kinds
Open sandwich: GIC forms part of the axial or gingival wall and is exposed to the mouth. Closed sandwich: composite covers the GIC completely [SRC:B316]. Summitt reserves the open type for Class II and V restorations extending below the cemento-enamel junction, with a resin-modified GIC covered by composite [SRC:C164].

### S3. Cautions
Pickard says closed sandwich restorations are more prone to long-term failure at interfaces [SRC:C2121]; etching GIC too early or too long damages it [SRC:B315].

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 8 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition
Restoration in which GIC replaces dentine and composite replaces enamel [SRC:B50] [SRC:A392].

### 2. Types
- **Open:** GIC exposed at the gingival or axial wall [SRC:B316]; used where the margin lies on cementum or dentine [SRC:C164].
- **Closed:** GIC completely covered by composite [SRC:B316].

### 3. Rationale
Composite shrinks 1-5%; the dentine bond is hard to maintain; GIC bonds to dentine, releases fluoride, and reduces the composite needed [SRC:A386] [SRC:A394].

### 4. Technique
1. Condition dentine 10 seconds and place the strongest radiopaque GIC [SRC:A395].
2. After setting, cut back about 2 mm to expose enamel margins [SRC:A415].
3. Etch enamel and auto-cure GIC 15 seconds (resin-modified GIC: enamel only) [SRC:A396] [SRC:A397].
4. Apply enamel bond; place composite in increments [SRC:A396].

### 4A. Indications
Subgingival Class II and Class V margins on dentine or cementum, erosive lesions, and deep proximal amalgam restorations with GIC in the gingival half [SRC:A393] [SRC:C164] [SRC:A391]. Leave GIC exposed only at a subgingival margin without enamel [SRC:A395].

### 5. Advantages and limits
Fluoride exposure, less shrinkage, tissue-tolerant GIC at the margin [SRC:A384]; closed type may fail at interfaces [SRC:C2121].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- GIC = dentine replacement; composite = enamel replacement [SRC:B50].
- Open (GIC exposed) against closed (fully covered) [SRC:B316].
- Cover all tubules, at least 1 mm [SRC:A385].
- Etch GIC 15 s only [SRC:A396].
