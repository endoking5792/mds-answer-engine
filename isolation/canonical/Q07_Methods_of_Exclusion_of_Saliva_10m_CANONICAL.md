## 1. Sources of saliva

### Explanation

Saliva has to be removed by evacuating it, by the patient's own swallowing, or by chemically reducing its secretion (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; in the field it impairs vision, materials and medicaments and is sprayed into the air by rotary instruments (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. The parotid duct opens at the parotid papilla between the upper first and second molars (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; the submandibular and sublingual ducts open at the sublingual caruncles (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; minor glands are scattered throughout the mouth (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Pickard's places cotton rolls accordingly: upper buccal sulci for the parotid, lingual sulci for the submandibular and sublingual ducts (Banerjee and Watson, 2011) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Why exclude saliva:** it obscures vision, weakens or prevents bonding and amalgam properties, dilutes medicaments and carries infection into the aerosol (Marzouk et al, undated).

**Sources** (Borges et al, 2020): parotid duct (parotid papilla, opposite upper first-second molars); submandibular and sublingual ducts (sublingual caruncles, floor of the mouth); minor glands everywhere.

**Methods:** mechanical barrier, absorption, evacuation, combined devices, pharmacological (Marzouk et al, undated).

## 2. Barrier: rubber dam

### Explanation

The rubber dam is the most complete method of isolation (Summitt, 2013) [SRC] and the best control of moisture (Banerjee and Watson, 2011) [SRC]. Its inverted edge acts as a valve that pressure under the dam only closes more tightly (Summitt, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**1. Rubber dam:** most complete exclusion of saliva (Summitt, 2013); the dam must be inverted into the sulcus, since an everted edge acts as a valve that lets saliva into the field (Summitt, 2013). A saliva ejector under the dam removes saliva pooling in the floor of the mouth (Ritter et al, 2019).

## 3. Absorbents

### Explanation

Cotton rolls are the alternative when a dam is impractical (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], acceptable for most procedures under profound anaesthesia (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. They must block the duct openings (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Maxillary anterior: a roll on each side of the labial frenum (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; mandibular anterior: add rolls each side of the lingual frenum (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; maxillary posterior: a roll in the posterior vestibule blocking the parotid duct (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; mandibular posterior is the most demanding region (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], needing a parotid roll, a vestibular roll and one or two under the tongue (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Cotton roll holders also retract cheek and tongue (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; a saturated roll can be suctioned in place (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; rolls are moistened before removal to protect the epithelium (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Cellulose wafers (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], parotid shields such as Dri-Aid (Summitt, 2013) [SRC], gauze (Summitt, 2013) [SRC] and adhesive pads (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], some with acrylate gel (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], serve the same purpose.

#### ✎ Exam core

**2. Absorbents** (Borges et al, 2020; Ritter et al, 2019)
- **Cotton rolls** over duct openings: maxillary anterior, one each side of the labial frenum; mandibular anterior, plus each side of the lingual frenum; maxillary posterior, buccal vestibule over the parotid duct; mandibular posterior, parotid roll + vestibular roll + one or two under the tongue.
- Holders retract cheek and tongue; moisten dry rolls before removal.
- **Cellulose wafers/parotid shields (Dri-Aid), gauze, adhesive absorbent pads** (Summitt, 2013).

## 4. Evacuation and combined devices

### Explanation

The HVE is preferred because a saliva ejector removes water slowly and few solids (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; it should clear a 150-mL cup in about a second (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], with the tip just distal to the tooth (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. The saliva ejector removes saliva pooling in the floor of the mouth (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; under profound anaesthesia it is often unnecessary (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. The Svedopter (Summitt, 2013) [SRC] and gentler Hygoformic ejector (Summitt, 2013) [SRC], placed on the working side (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], add tongue retraction; Isolite (Summitt, 2013) [SRC] and Vac-Ejector (Summitt, 2013) [SRC] combine suction with retraction and a bite block. All these isolate less well than a dam (Summitt, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**3. Evacuation**
- **High-volume evacuator:** fast removal of water, saliva and debris; tip distal to the tooth (Ritter et al, 2019).
- **Saliva ejector:** removes pooled saliva from the floor of the mouth; slow (Ritter et al, 2019).
- **Svedopter, Hygoformic ejector:** suction with tongue retraction for mandibular posterior teeth (Summitt, 2013).

**4. Combined devices:** Isolite, Vac-Ejector (suction, tongue/cheek retraction, bite block) (Summitt, 2013). Absorbents and suction devices isolate less completely than the dam (Summitt, 2013).

**Other measures:** when no dam is used, keep procedures short and work four-handed, replacing saturated rolls promptly (Borges et al, 2020).

## 5. Pharmacological control

### Explanation

Local anaesthesia reduces salivary flow (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Drugs to control salivation are rarely indicated and generally limited to atropine (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], contraindicated in nursing mothers and glaucoma (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Other anticholinergics (propantheline, glycopyrrolate) are named in older exam notes [RECALL] but were not found in any textbook read, so no doses are given.

#### ✎ Exam core

**5. Pharmacological**
- Profound local anaesthesia reduces salivary flow (Ritter et al, 2019).
- **Antisialagogues:** rarely needed; atropine; contraindicated in glaucoma and nursing mothers (Ritter et al, 2019).

**Conclusion:** the rubber dam excludes saliva most completely; absorbents with high-volume suction suffice for short procedures under profound anaesthesia (Summitt, 2013; Ritter et al, 2019).
