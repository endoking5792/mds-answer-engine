## 1. Why newer isolation methods developed

### Explanation

Kunzelmann notes the widest gap in dentistry between what is recommended and what is done for the rubber dam (Kunzelmann, 2001) [SRC], and Sturdevant's that dam use is low among private practitioners (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Dentists give lack of evidence, time and patient unwillingness as reasons (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Kunzelmann adds that hydrophilic bonding systems and a compomer marketed as not needing a dam reopened the question of whether the dam is essential for adhesion (Kunzelmann, 2001) [SRC]. Newer devices therefore aim at faster, more comfortable isolation, latex-free materials and better illumination, while trying to keep the dam's protective function.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Rubber dam remains the most complete method, but its use in practice is low because of time, patient objection and limited evidence (Ritter et al, 2019; Borges et al, 2020). Recent advances aim at: latex-free materials, quicker frameless placement, better retention, isolation of difficult cases, combined suction-retraction-illumination, comfort, and aerosol reduction.

**Classification of recent advances** (own grouping)
1. Advances in dam materials
2. Pre-framed and frameless dams; lip-cheek retractors
3. Newer retainers and clamps
4. Modified dam techniques and liquid (light-cured) dams
5. Combined suction-illumination-retraction systems
6. Newer absorbents and accessories
7. Aerosol control

## 2. Advances in rubber dam materials

### Explanation

Latex-free sheets are made of silicone or nitrile (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], and Summitt's notes that their elastic properties are now very similar to latex (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]; with a latex-allergy prevalence of 3.7% in one survey (Summitt, 2013) [SRC], this matters. Silicone dams need larger holes because the membrane is less elastic (Kunzelmann, 2001) [SRC]. Because the separating powder used in manufacture also triggers reactions (Kunzelmann, 2001) [SRC], powder-free dam has been produced. Colour has become a selection tool: blue, complementary to the yellow of teeth, is the usual choice (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; gray suits layered aesthetic composites (Kunzelmann, 2001) [SRC]; beige is translucent and suits endodontics (Kunzelmann, 2001) [SRC]. Pre-stamped dams and rubber stamps speed hole marking (Summitt, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**1. Dam materials**
- **Non-latex dams** (nitrile, silicone) for latex allergy (3.7% in one survey); elasticity now close to latex; silicone needs larger holes (Summitt, 2013; Kunzelmann, 2001; Borges et al, 2020).
- **Powder-free latex** to reduce reactions (Kunzelmann, 2001).
- **Colour selection:** blue (contrast), gray (layered composites), beige (endodontics) (Kunzelmann, 2001; Borges et al, 2020).
- **Pre-stamped/pre-punched dams** and rubber stamps for hole marking save chairside time (Summitt, 2013).
- **Latex-allergic patient:** take a history of reactions to gloves and other latex products; use a non-latex dam and non-latex gloves for that patient, or for all patients in a latex-free practice (Summitt, 2013).

## 3. Pre-framed and frameless dams; retractors

### Explanation

HandiDam comes with a built-in frame (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. OptraDam Plus has an integral frame that fits inside the mouth and retracts the cheeks (Summitt, 2013) [SRC] and can often be used without a clamp (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]; Modern Operative Dentistry says such flexible frames may reduce the need for clamps (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. OptraGate retracts lips and cheeks for anterior work, though it is not strictly a dam (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]; lip balm is needed before such retractors (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Silicone bite blocks keep the mouth open, and the more posterior they sit, the wider the anterior opening (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**2. Pre-framed and frameless systems**
- **HandiDam:** dam with built-in frame (Summitt, 2013).
- **OptraDam Plus:** three-dimensional dam with flexible intraoral frame; retracts cheeks; often no clamp needed (Summitt, 2013; Borges et al, 2020).
- **OptraGate:** flexible lip-cheek retractor for anterior work (Summitt, 2013).
- Silicone bite blocks with floss tie; the more posterior the block, the wider the anterior opening (Borges et al, 2020).
- **Advantages:** quick placement, cheek and lip retraction built in, often clampless. **Limitation:** in a 2025 crossover trial in 94 students, OptraDam Plus took longer and gave poorer isolation quality than a conventional dam (BaHammam et al, 2025).

## 4. Newer retainers and clamps

### Explanation

Retracting clamps 212R and 212L let two adjacent cervical lesions be restored together (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; asymmetric clamps (12A/13B) with a larger buccal jaw (Kunzelmann, 2001) [SRC] fit tapered maxillary molars (Kunzelmann, 2001) [SRC]; cervical clamps with movable jaws can be adjusted after placement (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. Clampless retention uses elastic cords such as Wedjets (Summitt, 2013) [SRC] or latex stabilising cords, which can replace clamps in the anterior and premolar regions (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], and orthodontic O-rings (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; running-knot ligatures are more stable than double knots (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**3. Retainers**
- 212R/212L for adjacent Class V lesions; asymmetric 12A/13B for tapered molars; movable-jaw cervical clamps (Borges et al, 2020; Kunzelmann, 2001; Marzouk et al, undated).
- **Clampless retention:** stabilising cords (Wedjets), orthodontic O-rings, elastic ligatures, running-knot floss ligatures (Summitt, 2013; Borges et al, 2020).

## 5. Modified dam techniques and light-cured ("liquid") dams

### Explanation

The sleeve (split) technique, with the dam cut in an arch rather than punched (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], is now the easiest approach for bridges, orthodontic retainers and deep subgingival margins (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; saliva can still seep in (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], so a light-cured gingival barrier at its margins helps (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. These barriers are pigmented flowable resins that stay slightly flexible after curing (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; they protect gingiva from in-office bleaching agents (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC] and seal small flaws in a dam (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Cavit seals the gap over a root concavity (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. In a 2026 RCT in 39 children receiving silver diamine fluoride, OpalDam (a light-cured barrier) gave less pain and better comfort ratings than OptraDam, and both than cotton rolls (Naik et al, 2026) [LIT].

#### ✎ Exam core

**4. Modified techniques**
- **Split/sleeve dam:** dam slit, not punched; for bridges, orthodontic retainers, veneers, deep margins (Borges et al, 2020).
- **Light-cured gingival barriers ("liquid dam", e.g. OpalDam):** pigmented flowable resin, cured on the gingiva; protects against bleaching agents, seals leaks and the margins of a split dam (Borges et al, 2020). Better comfort than cotton rolls in a 2026 paediatric RCT (Naik et al, 2026).
- Cavit to seal root concavities (Summitt, 2013).
- **Technique of a light-cured barrier:** syringe the resin over the dried gingival margin and papillae, light-cure, check for gaps; it remains flexible after curing, so it is easy to remove (Borges et al, 2020).

## 6. Combined suction-illumination-retraction systems

### Explanation

The Isolite gives illumination, suction, tongue and cheek retraction and an integrated bite block (Summitt, 2013) [SRC], with single-use mouthpieces in six sizes (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]; Summitt's is clear that isolation is less complete than with a dam (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. Sturdevant's reports shorter sealant placement time with it (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. The Vac-Ejector combines bite block, tongue retractor and high-speed suction (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]; the Ez-Dam adds cheek retraction to suction, tongue retractor and bite block (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; the Nola dry field system is a cheek-tongue retractor with saliva ejector (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Older devices in the same line are the Svedopter and the gentler Hygoformic ejector (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. A 2025 systematic review of five RCTs (170 patients, low-quality evidence) found Isolite about as effective as dam or cotton rolls, with less chair time, less gagging and less taste perception than cotton rolls (Siddalingappa et al, 2025) [LIT]. DryShield, a similar device, shortened chair time and gave better behaviour and less behavioural pain than the dam in children in a 2024 RCT, but was noisier, pressed on soft tissue and provoked gagging (Bagher et al, 2024) [LIT].

#### ✎ Exam core

**5. Combined systems**
- **Isolite:** illuminated mouthpiece with suction, tongue/cheek retraction and bite block; six sizes (Summitt, 2013). Shorter sealant time (Ritter et al, 2019); as effective as dam or rolls in a low-quality 2025 systematic review (Siddalingappa et al, 2025).
- **DryShield:** similar; faster and better tolerated than dam in children, but gag and soft-tissue pressure (Bagher et al, 2024).
- **Vac-Ejector, Ez-Dam, Nola dry field:** suction with tongue and cheek retraction and bite block (Summitt, 2013; Borges et al, 2020).
- Limitation: isolation is less complete than a rubber dam (Summitt, 2013).

## 7. Newer absorbents and accessories

### Explanation

Absorbent pads stick to the cheek over the parotid duct (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; some are cellulose, others contain acrylate polymers that turn moisture into gel (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Paper rolls with a non-woven covering do not stick to mucosa even when dry (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Disposable saliva ejectors with a bendable metal rod (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC] and Hygoformic ejectors with five small holes (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC] are standard. For unassisted work, a saliva ejector can be hooked into the clamp hole (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. Knitted and braided retraction cords are easier to use than twisted ones (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**6. Newer absorbents and accessories**
- Adhesive absorbent pads (cellulose or acrylate gel, some reflective) over the parotid duct; non-sticking paper rolls (Borges et al, 2020).
- Flexible disposable saliva ejectors, Hygoformic ejectors; ejector hooked in the clamp for unassisted work (Summitt, 2013; Borges et al, 2020).
- Knitted/braided retraction cords (Borges et al, 2020).

## 8. Isolation and aerosol control

### Explanation

Sturdevant's calls the dam an effective infection-control barrier (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. The 2020 Cochrane review of aerosol-reducing interventions (16 small studies, very low-certainty evidence, bacterial colony counts only, no infection outcomes) found that HVE may reduce contamination within about 30 cm, rubber dam may reduce colony counts at 1-2 m, and an Isolite-type combination system made no difference compared with a saliva ejector in one RCT (Kumbargere Nagraj et al, 2020) [LIT].

#### ✎ Exam core

**7. Aerosol control (post-COVID emphasis):** rubber dam and HVE may reduce bacterial aerosol, but the 2020 Cochrane evidence is very low certainty and measures colony counts, not infection (Kumbargere Nagraj et al, 2020).

**Evidence summary**
- Rubber dam vs cotton rolls: low-certainty evidence of better 6-month survival of cervical composites (OR 2.29); very uncertain later (Miao et al, 2021; Wang et al, 2016).
- Isolite: comparable to dam or rolls for sealants; less chair time, less gagging; low-quality evidence (Siddalingappa et al, 2025; Mattar et al, 2022).
- DryShield: faster and better behaviour in children than dam (Bagher et al, 2024).
- OptraDam Plus: not easier than a conventional dam for novices (BaHammam et al, 2025).
- Textbooks: evidence not enough to change practice, but consensus on a clean field for bonding (Borges et al, 2020).

**Conclusion:** newer systems make isolation quicker and more comfortable, but none matches the completeness of a well-placed rubber dam; selection depends on the procedure and the patient (Summitt, 2013; Borges et al, 2020).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

**Claim: combined systems such as Isolite can replace the rubber dam.** Summitt's states that their isolation is not as complete as the dam's (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. The clinical evidence uses mostly sealant outcomes in children: an RCT found no difference in sealant retention among Isolite, dam and cotton rolls over 12-22 months (Mattar et al, 2022) [LIT], and the same group found no difference in chair time, though children preferred cotton rolls and disliked the dam (Mattar et al, 2021) [LIT]; the 2025 systematic review rated the evidence low quality (Siddalingappa et al, 2025) [LIT]. The textbooks themselves find the evidence for the dam weak (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], consistent with the Cochrane reviews (Wang et al, 2016; Miao et al, 2021) [LIT]. What can be concluded: for short, non-invasive procedures such as sealants these devices perform comparably; what cannot: that they protect the airway or the pulp as well as a dam during bonding or deep caries work, which no study read here tested [Likely].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

- Non-latex dams: nitrile or silicone; silicone needs larger holes.
- OptraDam Plus: flexible intraoral frame, often no clamp.
- Sleeve (split) dam plus light-cured barrier for bridges and orthodontic retainers.
- Light-cured gingival barrier = "liquid dam" (e.g. OpalDam): bleaching protection and leak sealing.
- Isolite: light + suction + retraction + bite block; less complete than a dam.
- Evidence for all devices, including the dam, is low certainty.
- 212R/212L allow adjacent Class V lesions under one dam.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

- Listing only brand names; group them by what they do.
- Claiming newer devices are "better than rubber dam"; the evidence is low quality and mostly on sealants in children.
- Leaving out non-latex dams and light-cured barriers, which are the most widely used advances.
- Inventing device specifications or study figures from memory.
- Forgetting the aerosol-control angle in post-2020 papers.
