## 1. Definition and history

### Explanation

S.C. Barnum, a New York City dentist, introduced the rubber dam in 1864 (Ritter et al, 2019; Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. It isolates one or more teeth from the oral environment (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; Pickard's calls it a thin perforated latex (or equivalent) sheet giving the best control of moisture (Banerjee and Watson, 2011) [SRC], and Summitt's the most complete method of field isolation (Summitt, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** A thin perforated sheet of latex (or non-latex) material that isolates one or more teeth from the oral environment and gives the best control of moisture (Ritter et al, 2019; Banerjee and Watson, 2011). Introduced by S.C. Barnum, 1864 (Ritter et al, 2019).

## 2. Armamentarium

### Explanation

Sheets are 12.5 or 15 cm square (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], from thin (0.15 mm) to extra heavy (0.30 mm) (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; heavy and extra heavy are advised for operative work (Summitt, 2013) [SRC] because they seal and retract better (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. Dark colours contrast with teeth (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC] and make torn fragments easy to find (Banerjee and Watson, 2011) [SRC]; ivory dam is not recommended for operative work (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. Nitrile and silicone sheets serve latex-allergic patients (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. The Young frame is U-shaped (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; strap holders retract most but are least comfortable (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. The punch has a rotating table of graded holes (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. The clamp has four prongs and two jaws joined by a bow (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], contacting the four line angles (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], tied with about 30 cm of floss (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. A napkin goes between dam and skin (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. Lubricant must be water-soluble (Summitt, 2013) [SRC] because petroleum products impede bonding (Summitt, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Armamentarium** (Ritter et al, 2019; Summitt, 2013)
- Dam sheet: 12.5/15 cm; thin 0.15 to extra heavy 0.30 mm; dark colour; non-latex option.
- Frame: Young (U-shaped), Nygaard-Ostby, plastic (radiolucent); strap holders.
- Punch (Ainsworth, Ivory) with graded holes; largest hole for the clamped tooth.
- Clamps (bow, two jaws, four prongs; four-point contact at the line angles; tied with floss) and clamp forceps.
- Napkin between dam and skin; waxed floss; water-soluble lubricant (petroleum jelly impedes bonding); scissors; template or stamp.

## 3. Advantages and applications

### Explanation

Sturdevant's advantages: a dry, clean field (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], especially for deep caries near the pulp (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; access and visibility (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; material properties, as amalgam is weaker when wet (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC] and bonding fails on contaminated tooth (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; protection from aspiration (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC] and from medicaments (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC] such as hypochlorite (Banerjee and Watson, 2011) [SRC]; infection control (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; and efficiency (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], since the patient cannot talk or spit (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. Applications include single-tooth isolation for Class I and V, sealants, endodontics and internal bleaching (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], amalgam removal (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC] and in-office bleaching with heavy dam (Kunzelmann, 2001) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Advantages** (Ritter et al, 2019)
1. Dry, clean field: needed for bonding, amalgam, deep caries excavation.
2. Access and visibility: retracts soft tissues, dark contrasting background.
3. Protection: against aspiration/swallowing of instruments and against etchants and hypochlorite.
4. Infection-control barrier.
5. Efficiency: patient cannot talk or expectorate.

**Applications:** adhesive restorations, pulp capping, endodontics, amalgam removal, bleaching, sealants (Borges et al, 2020; Kunzelmann, 2001).

## 4. Limitations and contraindications

### Explanation

Time and patient objection are the usual complaints, though placement usually takes under 5 minutes (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. It may not be possible on insufficiently erupted teeth, some third molars and extremely malposed teeth (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], or with difficult nasal breathing (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], psychological intolerance or latex allergy (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC], quoted at 3.7% of patients in one survey (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. Pickard's adds claustrophobia (Banerjee and Watson, 2011) [SRC], clamp pain for some hours (Banerjee and Watson, 2011) [SRC], chipping of ceramic crowns by poorly fitted clamps (Banerjee and Watson, 2011) [SRC] and harder communication (Banerjee and Watson, 2011) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Limitations:** time, patient objection, difficult communication, claustrophobia, clamp pain, chipping of ceramic crowns, latex allergy (Ritter et al, 2019; Banerjee and Watson, 2011).

**Contraindications:** partially erupted teeth, some third molars, extremely malposed teeth, nasal obstruction or mouth breathing, psychological intolerance (Ritter et al, 2019).

## 5. Key points of technique

### Explanation

The dam edge works as a valve: if it is not inverted, pressure from the tongue pushes saliva into the field (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. After removal the dam is laid flat and checked for missing pieces (Summitt, 2013) [SRC], since a retained fragment can cause a gingival abscess (Summitt, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Technique (outline):** anaesthesia; floss contacts; punch holes (at least three teeth; spacing equal to tooth centre distance); test a floss-tied clamp on the anchor tooth; place dam; pass septa through contacts with floss; invert into the sulcus, because an everted edge acts as a valve that lets saliva into the field; fit frame and napkin. On removal stretch and cut the septa, remove the clamp and check the dam for missing fragments, which can cause a gingival abscess (Summitt, 2013).

**Conclusion:** the rubber dam is the most complete method of field isolation; its limitations are mostly overcome by training and non-latex materials (Summitt, 2013).
