## Explanation

**Definition — three wordings, one idea.** Marzouk's: the thickness of the dentin bridge between the floor of the cavity and the roof of the pulp chamber, called the effective depth; more precisely, the bridge of dentin and enamel from the floor at its greatest depth to the pulp contents at their highest location (Marzouk et al, 1985) [SRC]. Sikri's: the area of minimum thickness of sound dentin separating the pulpal tissue from the carious lesion (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Marzouk measures to the cavity floor; Sikri measures sound dentin under the lesion — the second is the more useful clinically because infected dentin is no barrier [Likely].

**Why thickness matters.** Marzouk calls cavity depth the most detrimental irritating factor; the less the effective depth, the more severe the pulp-dentin reaction (Marzouk et al, 1985) [SRC]. Permeability depends mainly on remaining dentin thickness (tubule length) and tubule diameter, so deep dentin is a poorer barrier than superficial dentin (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; greater RDT lengthens the diffusion path (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013) [SRC]. Every mm² of cut dentin exposes 30,000-45,000 tubules (Marzouk et al, 1985) [SRC]. Odontoblast processes reach only the inner third of adult dentin, about 1.0 mm (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013) [SRC]. Deep, wide preparations such as full crowns open more tubules (Haug, Kim and Heyeraas, 2019) [SRC].

**Thresholds — the books disagree; shown side by side, not ranked.**
- Marzouk's (amalgam, sound teeth, no base): ≥2.5 mm healthy reparative reaction; 1-2 mm unhealthy reparative reaction; <1 mm destruction (Marzouk et al, 1985) [SRC].
- Sikri's, direct gold: 3.0-3.5 mm for a healthy reparative action, <1.0 mm destruction (Sikri, 2020) [SRC].
- Sikri's, under caries: >1.0 mm no significant disturbance; 0.25-0.5 mm maximal reparative dentin and remineralization; ≤0.25 mm bacterial growth in the pulp (Sikri, 2020) [SRC].
- Sikri's and Summitt's: 2.0 mm is an adequate barrier against almost all techniques and materials (Sikri, 2020; Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013) [SRC]; 0.5 mm reduces toxicity to 75% and 1.0 mm by over 90% (Sikri, 2020) [SRC].
The spread (1, 2, 2.5 or 3-3.5 mm) reflects different irritants — amalgam, gold condensation, caries — so quote each with its context [Likely].

**Clinical use — liners and bases (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC].** Sturdevant's links liner/base choice to RDT: ≥2 mm (shallow) needs no pulpal protection beyond chemical protection (sealer or bonding agent); 0.5-2 mm (moderately deep) a light-cured RMGI base where needed; <0.5 mm (very deep) calcium hydroxide (0.5-1 mm set thickness) to stimulate reparative dentin, then RMGI. Marzouk's: varnish on all walls at ≥2 mm; burs are biologically acceptable over effective depths of 2 mm and more; excessive instrument or condensation pressure in shallow effective depths aspirates odontoblasts (Marzouk et al, 1985) [SRC]. Even 2 mm does not protect against a crown preparation cut without coolant (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Remaining dentin thickness (RDT), Marzouk's "effective depth", is the thickness of the dentin bridge between the deepest point of the cavity floor and the pulp (Marzouk et al, 1985); Sikri defines it as the minimum thickness of sound dentin between the lesion and the pulp (Sikri, 2020).

**Why it matters**
1. Permeability depends mainly on RDT (tubule length) and tubule diameter; deep dentin has more, wider, shorter tubules — a poorer barrier (Ritter et al, 2019).
2. Cavity depth is the most damaging irritant; less RDT means a more severe pulpal reaction (Marzouk et al, 1985).
3. Dentin dilutes toxins: 0.5 mm cuts toxicity to 75%, 1 mm by over 90% (Sikri, 2020).
4. Each mm² of cut dentin exposes 30,000-45,000 tubules, so a thin floor brings millions of odontoblasts within reach of the irritant (Marzouk et al, 1985).
5. Odontoblast processes occupy only the inner third of adult dentin (about 1 mm); preparations confined to outer dentin do not sever the cells directly (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013).

**Thresholds (sources differ)**
| Source | Healthy | Unhealthy | Destruction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marzouk (amalgam) | ≥2.5 mm | 1-2 mm | <1 mm |
| Sikri (direct gold) | 3.0-3.5 mm | — | <1.0 mm |
| Sikri (caries) | >1.0 mm no disturbance; 0.25-0.5 mm maximal reparative dentin | — | ≤0.25 mm |
| Summitt, Sikri | 2.0 mm adequate barrier | | |

**Clinical significance — Sturdevant's protocol (Ritter et al, 2019)**
- RDT ≥2 mm: no pulpal protection beyond sealer or bonding agent.
- 0.5-2 mm: light-cured RMGI base at the deep site.
- <0.5 mm: calcium hydroxide liner (0.5-1 mm), then RMGI base.

**Operative precautions:** burs acceptable over 2 mm effective depth with coolant; avoid heavy instrument and condensation pressure in shallow effective depths (odontoblast aspiration) (Marzouk et al, 1985); crowns open many tubules (Haug, Kim and Heyeraas, 2019).

**Conclusion.** RDT is the single best predictor of pulpal response and decides liner, base and restorative technique.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- 2 mm is the figure most books treat as a safe barrier; Marzouk asks 2.5 mm, Sikri 3.0-3.5 mm under direct gold.
- <0.5 mm RDT = very deep: calcium hydroxide + RMGI.
- 0.5 mm dentin cuts toxicity to 75%; 1 mm by >90%.
- RDT is measured in sound dentin, not soft carious dentin.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Measuring from the cavity floor through soft carious dentin.
- Quoting a single "safe" figure without its source.
- Forgetting that crown preparations without coolant damage the pulp even with good RDT.

## MNEMONIC
**"2-1-½"** — 2 mm safe, 1 mm danger begins, ½ mm calcium hydroxide.
