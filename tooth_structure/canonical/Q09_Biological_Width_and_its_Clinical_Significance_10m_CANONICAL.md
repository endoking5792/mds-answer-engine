## Explanation

**Definition.** The combined vertical dimension of the junctional epithelium and the supra-alveolar connective tissue (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; also called the buffer zone, soft tissue cuff or subcrevicular attachment complex (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. It is a seal around the cervical tooth with self-restoring capacity: epithelium separated by probing reattaches in about 5 days (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC].

**Dimensions — two cadaver studies.** Gargiulo et al (1961): average 2.04 mm; Vacek et al (1994): 1.91 mm (1.75 mm anterior, 2.08 mm posterior) (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013) [SRC], made up of junctional epithelium 1.14 mm and connective tissue 0.77 mm, with a mean sulcus depth of 1.32 mm (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. The connective tissue is the most consistent component and the junctional epithelium the most variable; biologic width is greater in posterior teeth and the junctional epithelium is longer in restored teeth (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Sikri's gives epithelial and connective tissue attachment as "0.97 mm and 1.7 mm" with a total of "approximately 2.0 mm" (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]; 0.97 + 1.7 is 2.67 mm, so "1.7" is a misprint for Gargiulo's 1.07 mm [Likely]. Elsewhere Sikri's gives 2.04 mm and Kois's "biologic zone" of 3.0 mm (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Working rule: 2.0 mm biologic width + 1.0 mm sulcus = 3.0 mm (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013) [SRC].

**Violation.** A margin within or apical to the junctional epithelium disturbs the seal; bacteria cause inflammation, attachment loss, recession or pocketing; the width is restored only by apical re-establishment of the supracrestal connective tissue (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Placing a margin more than 1 mm into the sulcus is potentially disruptive (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; Summitt's advises no more than 0.5 mm into the sulcus, no closer than 2.5 mm to the crest (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013) [SRC].

**Assessment — bone sounding (Kois).** Under anaesthesia the probe is pushed through the sulcus to the osseous crest. In 85% of subjects a midfacial reading of 3.0 mm confirms optimum biologic width, and the margin may go 0.5 mm below the gingival margin; more than 3.0 mm means recession may follow restoration; less than 3.0 mm suggests compromised width and the need for crown lengthening; proximally the attachment may be scalloped 3.5 mm incisal to the facial CEJ (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013) [SRC]. With 5.0 mm from crest to contact, 98% of papillae fill the embrasure (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013) [SRC].

**Correction.** Crown lengthening — removal of bone with removal or repositioning of soft tissue — to leave about 2 mm for attachment, 1 mm for sulcus and 0.5-1 mm more as a safety margin; 3-4 mm from gingival margin to crest; 3 mm is the minimum, and Wagenberg suggests aiming for 5 mm (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Alternatively orthodontic forced eruption with surgical crown lengthening (Rose, Haveman and Davis, 2013) [SRC]. Wait at least 6 weeks after surgery before final restoration (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Biologic width is the combined vertical dimension of the junctional epithelium and the supra-alveolar connective tissue attachment coronal to the alveolar crest (Ritter et al, 2019); also called the buffer zone or soft-tissue cuff (Sikri, 2020).

**Dimensions**
| Study | Junctional epithelium | Connective tissue | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gargiulo et al (1961) | — | — | 2.04 mm |
| Vacek et al (1994) | 1.14 mm | 0.77 mm | 1.91 mm (1.75 anterior, 2.08 posterior) |
Mean sulcus depth 1.32 mm (Ritter et al, 2019; Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013). Connective tissue is the constant component; junctional epithelium varies. Working rule: 2 mm + 1 mm sulcus = 3 mm from gingival margin to crest. Kois calls this 3 mm the "biologic zone" (Sikri, 2020).

**Significance in restorative dentistry**
1. **Margin placement:** prefer supragingival margins; subgingival margins no deeper than 0.5 mm into the sulcus (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013), never beyond 1 mm (Ritter et al, 2019).
2. **Violation:** inflammation, attachment loss, recession or pocket formation, bone loss; the body re-creates the width by moving the attachment apically (Ritter et al, 2019).
3. **Follow the CEJ scallop** proximally — up to 3.5 mm incisal to the facial CEJ in maxillary incisors (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013).
4. **Self-repair:** epithelium detached by probing or flossing reattaches in about 5 days, but a restoration margin in the attachment is a permanent irritant (Ritter et al, 2019).

**Assessment — bone sounding:** probe to the crest under anaesthesia (Kois). 3.0 mm = normal (85%); >3.0 mm = risk of recession; <3.0 mm = compromised, crown lengthening indicated (Fruits, Khajotia and Nicholson, 2013).

**Correction of violation**
- Surgical crown lengthening: 3-4 mm crest to gingival margin, 3 mm minimum; aim 5 mm crest to margin (Wagenberg) (Ritter et al, 2019).
- Orthodontic forced eruption ± surgery (Rose, Haveman and Davis, 2013).
- Restore after at least 6 weeks of healing (Ritter et al, 2019).

**Conclusion.** Respecting about 3 mm from margin to bone keeps subgingival restorations periodontally healthy.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Gargiulo 2.04 mm; Vacek 1.91 mm (JE 1.14 + CT 0.77); sulcus 1.32 mm.
- 2 + 1 = 3 mm rule; Kois biologic zone 3.0 mm.
- Margin ≤0.5 mm into sulcus; crown lengthening to 3-4 mm; wait 6 weeks.
- Sikri's "1.7 mm" connective tissue is a misprint for 1.07 mm.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
1. Dentogingival junction: alveolar crest, connective tissue (CT), junctional epithelium (JE), sulcus, gingival margin, with measurements.
2. Bone sounding: probe through sulcus to crest, 3 mm reading.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Including sulcus depth in biologic width.
- Quoting 2.04 mm as Vacek's (it is Gargiulo's).
- Forgetting the 6-week wait after crown lengthening.

## MNEMONIC
**"1-2-3"** — 1 mm sulcus + 2 mm attachment = 3 mm to bone.
