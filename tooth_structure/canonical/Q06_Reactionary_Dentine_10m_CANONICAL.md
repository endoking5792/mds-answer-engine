## Explanation

**Place in the classification.** Kuttler proposed tertiary dentin, formed in response to injury; it ranges from a regular tubular matrix that differs little from primary and secondary dentin to a very dysplastic, even atubular matrix (Smith, 2012) [SRC]. It is subclassified as reactionary or reparative (Smith, 2012) [SRC].

**Definition.** Reactionary dentin is tertiary dentin secreted by surviving postmitotic (primary) odontoblasts in response to an appropriate, typically milder, stimulus (Smith, 2012) [SRC]. Sturdevant's: in mild injury such as a shallow caries lesion, primary odontoblasts increase dentin formation beneath the affected area; reactionary dentin is tubular and continuous with primary and secondary dentin (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Tubular continuity between secondary and tertiary matrix is the histological marker (Smith, 2012) [SRC].

**Contrast with reparative dentin.** More severe injury (deep caries) kills the primary odontoblasts; odontoblast-like cells differentiate from pulpal mesenchymal cells and lay down reparative dentin (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Reparative dentinogenesis often follows reactionary dentinogenesis, and both can occur in one lesion (Smith, 2012) [SRC].

**Mechanism (Smith, 2012) [SRC].** Focal upregulation of secretion by surviving odontoblasts, limited to cells in direct tubular communication with the stimulus — so under unetched cavities it can form fingerlike projections. The signals are released from the dentin matrix itself by the injurious agent: TGF-β family growth factors, plus IGF-I and II, BMPs and angiogenic factors; "the concept of dentin as an inert tissue must therefore be questioned". EDTA is the most effective etchant at releasing them; calcium hydroxide solubilizes TGF-β1 and non-collagenous proteins from dentin, and MTA appears similar. Sustained inflammation blocks regeneration.

**Factors.** Reactionary dentin forms under small, slowly progressing lesions (Smith, 2012) [SRC]. In human cavity studies, RDT was the most significant factor: area increased by 1.187 mm² for every 1 mm decrease in RDT; in 217 teeth maximum secretion (about fourfold) occurred at RDT 0.50-0.25 mm, falling below 0.25 mm as odontoblast survival dropped; materials ranked calcium hydroxide > composite > RMGI > ZOE, and ZOE appeared to have no effect (Smith, 2012) [SRC]. **Terminology difference:** Sikri's gives the same 0.25-0.5 mm range as producing "maximal reparative dentin formation" (Sikri, 2020) [SRC], whereas Smith calls the response at that depth reactionary — read Sikri's "reparative" in the older, general sense of tertiary dentin [Likely].

**Significance.** Tertiary dentin increases the thickness and effectiveness of dentin as a barrier for the pulp (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Reactionary dentin is a tertiary dentin matrix secreted by surviving postmitotic (primary) odontoblasts in response to a mild stimulus (Smith, 2012).

**Classification of dentin:** primary → secondary (physiological) → tertiary (in response to injury; Kuttler): reactionary and reparative (Smith, 2012).

**Reactionary vs reparative**
| Feature | Reactionary | Reparative |
|---|---|---|
| Stimulus | Mild, slow (shallow caries, attrition, shallow cavity) | Severe (deep caries, exposure) |
| Cells | Surviving primary odontoblasts | New odontoblast-like cells from pulpal progenitors |
| Structure | Tubular, continuous with secondary dentin | Irregular, few or no tubules |
| Site | Only beneath tubules communicating with the stimulus | Localized at the injury site |
(Smith, 2012; Ritter et al, 2019)

**Mechanism**
1. The irritant (bacterial acid, etchant, material) solubilizes bioactive molecules fossilized in dentin matrix — TGF-β family, IGF-I/II, BMPs, angiogenic factors.
2. These diffuse along tubules to the odontoblasts and upregulate their secretion.
3. EDTA, calcium hydroxide and MTA release these factors (Smith, 2012).

**Factors influencing it**
- **RDT** — the most important: area rises 1.187 mm² per 1 mm decrease; maximum (about fourfold) at RDT 0.25-0.50 mm; less below 0.25 mm because odontoblasts die (Smith, 2012).
- **Material:** calcium hydroxide > composite > RMGI > ZOE (Smith, 2012).
- Patient age, cavity floor area, restoration width; persistent inflammation blocks the response.

**Significance**
- Thickens the barrier and lowers permeability under caries and cavities (Ritter et al, 2019).
- Basis of indirect pulp capping and selective caries removal.
- Explains why dentin is not inert: conditioners and liners act biologically.
- Under an unetched cavity the response is patchy, with fingerlike projections, because some tubules are plugged and stimulate their odontoblasts less (Smith, 2012).
- Reparative dentinogenesis often follows, and both types may appear in the same lesion (Smith, 2012); eliminating bacteria and inflammation is essential for either to form.

**Conclusion.** Reactionary dentin is the pulp's first, cell-preserving line of repair; conserving RDT keeps odontoblasts alive to make it.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Reactionary = surviving primary odontoblasts; reparative = new odontoblast-like cells.
- Tubular continuity with secondary dentin identifies reactionary dentin.
- TGF-β released from dentin matrix is the main signal.
- Peak at RDT 0.25-0.5 mm; Ca(OH)₂ most effective, ZOE least.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW
1. Cavity over dentin: tubules running to odontoblasts, a band of tubular reactionary dentin beneath only the communicating tubules.
2. Side-by-side: reactionary (tubules continuous) vs reparative (atubular, new cells).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Using "reactionary" and "reparative" as synonyms.
- Saying reparative dentin is made by primary odontoblasts.
- Omitting the growth-factor (dentin matrix) mechanism.

## MNEMONIC
**"Reactionary Remains, Reparative Replaces"** — the original odontoblasts remain; reparative cells replace dead ones.
