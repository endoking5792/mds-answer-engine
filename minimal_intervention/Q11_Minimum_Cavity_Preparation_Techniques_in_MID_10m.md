## STUDY MODE

### S1. Principle [orientation]
Black's outline included extension for prevention; Sikri says that with adhesive materials this is no longer followed, and the conservative Class II has a narrow isthmus of one-quarter of the intercuspal distance against one-third in the conventional cavity (Sikri, 2020). Summitt says the adhesive Class 2 is shallower with a narrower outline and rounded internal angles, and extension through the fissures does not make the restoration more resistant to fracture (Hilton et al, 2013). Pickard's Guide limits excavation to unrepairable diseased enamel and dentine and a sound peripheral enamel margin (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### S2. Site and size [framework]
Mount et al describe Site 1 pits and fissures, Site 2 approximal surfaces and Site 3 cervical or root surfaces, and Sizes 0 to 4; they say the size of a restoration should be set by the size of the lesion and not by detection alone (Mount et al, 2016). Sikri calls it the basis for minimal cavity preparation but says advanced lesions do not fall under it (Sikri, 2020).

### S3. Designs [treatment]
- **Site 1, Size 1:** remove only the pits involved and seal the rest; leave the floor untouched (Mount et al, 2016). Summitt's preventive resin restoration limits preparation to carious pits and fissures (Hilton et al, 2013).
- **Tunnel:** Mount: lesion at least 3.00 mm apical to the ridge crest (Mount et al, 2016); Sikri: access from the occlusal surface preserving the ridge, indicated in small lesions, especially in deciduous teeth (Sikri, 2020); Pickard: tunnelling beneath the ridge is only successful clinically if the lesion is sufficiently small, with a high risk of fracture of the weakened ridge (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- **Slot, Mount's design:** lesion closer than 3.00 mm to the crest, access through the marginal ridge itself (Mount et al, 2016).
- **Facial or lingual slot (a different approach under a similar name):** Sturdevant: access from a facial or lingual direction rather than through the marginal ridge (Ritter et al, 2019); Singh: caries up to 0.5 mm into dentine, ridge intact with at least 2 mm of enamel (Singh, 2020).
- **Box-only and minibox:** Sturdevant: no bevel or secondary retention (Ritter et al, 2019); Sikri's minibox removes the ridge but not the fissures if caries removal there is unnecessary (Sikri, 2020).
- **Saucer:** Summitt, for enamel-limited Class 3 lesions, without undercuts (Hilton et al, 2013).

### S4. Where the books differ (side by side, not ranked)
Sikri says tunnel studies could not show promising results (Sikri, 2020); Pickard says tunnelling is only successful if the lesion is sufficiently small, with a high risk of ridge fracture (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); Summitt cites one study in which 21% of 33 tunnels failed in 7 years against none for slots, and concludes that tunnel restorations are not indicated and should be avoided (Hilton et al, 2013); Sturdevant does not support the technique, because trials with glass ionomer found it inadequate for definitive long-term restorations and composite is unknown (Ritter et al, 2019); Mount et al illustrate one case satisfactory at 12 years (Mount et al, 2016).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 18 minutes.)*

### 1. Principle
Extension for prevention is no longer followed with adhesive materials (Sikri, 2020). Keep a sound peripheral enamel margin (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 2. Basis: site and size
Site 1 pits and fissures, Site 2 approximal, Site 3 cervical; Size 0 to 4; restoration size follows lesion size (Mount et al, 2016); not for advanced lesions (Sikri, 2020).

### 3. Designs
| Design | Key points |
|---|---|
| Site 1, Size 1 | Remove only the pits involved, seal the rest, leave the floor (Mount et al, 2016) |
| Preventive resin restoration | Carious pits only, then seal (Hilton et al, 2013) |
| Fissurotomy | Bur ten times smaller than #556; width 1/8 to 1/10 of intercuspal distance (Sikri, 2020) |
| Tunnel | Lesion at least 3.00 mm apical to ridge crest; enter medial to the ridge (Mount et al, 2016) |
| Slot (Mount) | Lesion nearer than 3.00 mm; access through the ridge (Mount et al, 2016) |
| Facial or lingual slot | Access from facial or lingual side, ridge intact (Ritter et al, 2019; Singh, 2020) |
| Box-only, minibox | Proximal only, no bevel or secondary retention (Ritter et al, 2019); minibox spares fissures (Sikri, 2020) |
| Saucer | Enamel-limited Class 3, no undercuts (Hilton et al, 2013) |
| Conservative Class II | Isthmus one-quarter of intercuspal distance; narrow proximal box (Sikri, 2020) |

### 4. Common rules
- For Site 2, Size 1 designs: no retention design is needed with glass ionomer (Mount et al, 2016).
- Tunnel cavity: do not extend to remove plaque retention (Mount et al, 2016).
- Excavate unrepairable, diseased enamel and dentine only (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); never leave infected dentine at the junction (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 5. Books differ; not found
Tunnel: not promising, small lesions (Sikri, 2020); small lesions only, ridge fracture risk (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); one study 21% failed, "should be avoided" (Hilton et al, 2013); not supported (Ritter et al, 2019); one case satisfactory at 12 years (Mount et al, 2016). Pickard: no slot, box-only or saucer design found.

### 6. Conclusion
Cut only what the caries needs; choose design by site and size (Mount et al, 2016).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Site and size: three sites, five sizes (Mount et al, 2016).
- Tunnel when the lesion is at least 3.00 mm from the ridge crest; slot when nearer (Mount et al, 2016).
- Conservative Class II isthmus one-quarter of intercuspal distance (Sikri, 2020).
