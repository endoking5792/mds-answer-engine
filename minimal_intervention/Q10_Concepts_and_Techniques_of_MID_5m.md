## STUDY MODE

### S1. Concepts [orientation]
Sikri says minimal intervention dentistry deals with the cause of dental disease and is based on prevention and control, and minimal invasive dentistry is its conservative restorative component (Sikri, 2020). The four core principles are to recognise risk factors, minimise them, remineralise incipient lesions with fluorides or casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate, and repair rather than replace (Sikri, 2020).

### S2. Techniques [treatment]
- **Non-operative:** remineralisation, 38% silver diamine fluoride twice yearly (which might arrest lesions; Sikri, 2020), and resin infiltration without drilling (Sikri, 2020). Sturdevant reports that up to 50% of arrested lesions were reactivated by 24 months after one application (Ritter et al, 2019).
- **Operative, from Sikri's list:** hand instruments (ART), rotary methods (tunnel, fissurotomy, minibox), air abrasion, chemo-mechanical excavation, ultrasonic excavation, fluorescence UV light and laser preparation (Sikri, 2020).
- **Hall technique:** a preformed metal crown cemented with glass-ionomer luting cement, with no preparation or excavation (Sikri, 2020). Evidence level: one textbook description, no outcome data read; Kidd and Fejerskov also describe it (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).
- **Rule for removal:** caries-infected dentine is never retained at the enamel-dentine junction (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### S3. Limit [prognosis]
Sikri says studies of tunnel restorations could not show promising results, and that tunnels are indicated in small lesions, especially in deciduous teeth (Sikri, 2020). Other books differ; see Q11.

### 1. Concept
Minimal intervention dentistry treats the cause of dental disease, based on prevention and control; minimal invasive dentistry is its conservative restorative part (Sikri, 2020). Core principles: recognise risk factors, minimise them, remineralise incipient lesions, repair rather than replace (Sikri, 2020).

### 2. Techniques
| Type | Technique |
|---|---|
| Non-operative | Fluoride and casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate; 38% silver diamine fluoride (might arrest; up to 50% reactivated by 24 months, Ritter et al, 2019); resin infiltration (Sikri, 2020) |
| Hand | Atraumatic restorative treatment, with glass ionomer (Sikri, 2020) |
| Rotary | Tunnel (small lesions only), fissurotomy, minibox (Sikri, 2020) |
| Others | Air abrasion, chemo-mechanical gel, ultrasonic, laser (Sikri, 2020) |
| Crown | Hall technique (primary molars); one textbook, no outcome data (Sikri, 2020) |

Infected dentine is never left at the enamel-dentine junction (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 3. When not to restore
Avoid restoring non-cavitated white spot lesions, marginal secondary caries and accessible cavitated root lesions (Sikri, 2020).

### 4. Conclusion
Prevent disease, keep tooth structure, repair rather than replace (Sikri, 2020).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Four core principles: recognise, minimise, remineralise, repair (Sikri, 2020).
- Techniques: ART, tunnel, fissurotomy, minibox, air abrasion, chemo-mechanical (Sikri, 2020).
