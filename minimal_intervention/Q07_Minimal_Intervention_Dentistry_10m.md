## STUDY MODE

### S1. What it is [definition]
MID is used here for minimally invasive / minimal intervention dentistry. Sikri says minimal intervention is patient care that deals with the cause of dental disease and not just the symptom, based entirely on prevention and control of oral disease; minimal invasive dentistry embraces conservative restorative procedures and, in his words, "is merely a component" of that care plan (Sikri, 2020). Banerjee and Watson define minimum intervention oral healthcare as team care, led by the dentist, that gives individualised patient-centred care and encourages the patient to take responsibility for own oral health (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). They define minimally invasive operative dentistry as direct repair or restoration of damaged tooth structure to maintain pulp vitality, function and aesthetics (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### S2. Concept [orientation]
Extension for prevention has changed to prevention for extension (Sikri, 2020). Restoring a lesion is only defect repair (Mount et al, 2016). Kidd and Fejerskov give the aim as healthy teeth for life with minimal need for operative intervention (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).

### S3. Principles [framework]
Sikri lists four core principles: recognise risk factors, minimise them, remineralise incipient lesions, and repair (Sikri, 2020). He gives the FDI principles as patient education and motivation, modification of oral micro-organisms, remineralisation of early lesions, minimal tooth preparation for cavitated lesions and repair of defective restorations (Sikri, 2020).

### S4. Procedures [treatment]
Banerjee and Watson describe four phases: identify, control, restore minimally, recall (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- **Identify:** early diagnosis with activity monitored over time (Sikri, 2020); Sikri groups risk as low, moderate or high (Sikri, 2020); Pickard's Guide says a low or high division is usually more than sufficient (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- **Control:** fluorides, casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate, and 38% silver diamine fluoride twice yearly, which might be able to arrest cavitated lesions (Sikri, 2020); resin infiltration arrests early lesions without drilling (Sikri, 2020).
- **Restore:** caries-infected dentine is never retained at the enamel-dentine junction (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); Sikri lists ART, tunnel, fissurotomy, minibox, air abrasion, chemo-mechanical, ultrasonic and laser methods (Sikri, 2020), and the Hall technique, which needs no preparation or excavation (Sikri, 2020).
- **Repair and recall:** repair defective restorations instead of replacing them (Sikri, 2020).

### S5. Limits [prognosis]
Sikri states that infection control with remineralising agents can reduce restoration placement to approximately 50%, without citing a study in the text (Sikri, 2020). Kidd and Fejerskov warn that minimal intervention easily becomes just a new buzzword for drilling and filling (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 18 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition
MID here means minimally invasive / minimal intervention dentistry. Sikri says minimal intervention is patient care that treats the cause of dental disease, not only the symptom, and rests on prevention and control of oral disease, and that minimal invasive dentistry "is merely a component" of that care plan (Sikri, 2020). Pickard's Guide adds individualised patient-centred team care with patient responsibility (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 2. Concept
- Extension for prevention has changed to prevention for extension (Sikri, 2020).
- A filling is defect repair, not caries treatment (Mount et al, 2016); drilling and filling does not cure caries (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Ultraconservative and microdentistry are synonyms of minimal invasive dentistry in Sikri's text and imply magnification and devices such as air abrasion, chemo-mechanical agents and lasers (Sikri, 2020).
- Adhesive materials need no mechanical retention (Sikri, 2020).

### 3. Principles (Sikri heads them "minimal invasive")
1. Recognise risk factors (Sikri, 2020).
2. Minimise them: diet, saliva, pH (Sikri, 2020).
3. Remineralise incipient lesions with fluoride and casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate (Sikri, 2020).
4. Repair: minimum tooth sacrificed; repair, not replace (Sikri, 2020).

FDI principles, headed "minimal intervention" by Sikri: patient education and motivation, modifying oral micro-organisms, remineralising early lesions, minimal preparation of cavitated lesions, repairing defective restorations (Sikri, 2020).

### 4. Procedures: the care cycle
| Phase | What is done |
|---|---|
| Identify | Early diagnosis, activity monitoring (Sikri, 2020); risk low, moderate, high (Sikri, 2020), or low or high only (Banerjee and Watson, 2015) |
| Control | Fluoride, casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate, 38% silver diamine fluoride, resin infiltration (Sikri, 2020) |
| Restore minimally | Excavate unrepairable, diseased enamel and dentine only (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); adhesive materials; ART, tunnel, fissurotomy, minibox, air abrasion, chemo-mechanical, laser; Hall technique in primary molars (Sikri, 2020) |
| Recall | Review according to caries susceptibility (Banerjee and Watson, 2015) |
| Repair | Repair defective restorations rather than replace them (Sikri, 2020) |

Caries-infected dentine is never left at the enamel-dentine junction (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 5. Benefit and caution
Sikri: restoration placement can be reduced to approximately 50% (no study cited in the text) (Sikri, 2020). MID can become a new buzzword for drilling and filling (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).

### 6. Conclusion
MID, as Sikri frames it, is disease control first and conservative restoration second (Sikri, 2020).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Sikri: minimal intervention is cause-based care; minimal invasive dentistry is "merely a component" of it (Sikri, 2020).
- Four core principles: recognise, minimise, remineralise, repair (Sikri, 2020).
- Care cycle: identify, control, restore, recall (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Drilling and filling does not cure caries (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
