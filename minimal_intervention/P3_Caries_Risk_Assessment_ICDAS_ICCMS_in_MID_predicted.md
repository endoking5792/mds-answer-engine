## STUDY MODE

### S1. Definitions [definition]
- Caries risk assessment relates the patient's risk status on presentation to the activity state of the lesions (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- ICDAS was developed in 2002 according to Mount; the ICDAS Foundation was convened in 2004 according to Banerjee and Watson (Mount et al, 2016; Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- ICCMS merges the seven ICDAS codes into four: sound, initial decay, moderate decay and severe decay (Mount et al, 2016).

### S2. Risk factors and categories [mechanism]
- Past caries experience is the best predictor of future caries activity (Ritter et al, 2019). Dietary and clinical analysis form the foundation of assessment (Ritter et al, 2019).
- High-risk thresholds differ: two or more new, progressing or restored lesions in 2 years (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); two or more lesions in the last year (Sikri, 2020); in the CAMBRA system, recent caries activity indicators (new lesions during the past three years) place the patient in the high risk category (Mount et al, 2016). Pickard says a simpler division into low or high risk is usually more than sufficient (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### S3. Pathway as the books give it [technique]
- Pickard's Guide: identify, control and prevent, restore, recall (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Summitt: preventive and noninvasive options, operative and minimally invasive options, then maintenance care and recall, enabled by ICDAS and integrated into CAMBRA (Hilton et al, 2013).
- ICCMS key elements: classification by severity and activity, a preventive care plan matched to risk, and a risk-based recall interval (Caneppele et al, 2020).

### S4. Management by lesion and risk [treatment relevance]
Pickard's Guide care plan table gives recall ranges from 2-6 to 12-18 months by risk (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); its column layout is garbled in the text read, so the match of each care step to lesion type is not stated here. Arrested lesions need no intervention except for aesthetics, function or pulp risk; most cavitated lesions are non-cleansable and in general need restoring (Fontana and Huysmans, 2019). Mount says brownish or black lesions, even when cavitated, may not be active and may not require surgical intervention (Mount et al, 2016).

### S5. Differences among books on ICDAS [prognosis]
Mount lists the surface condition code first; Ritter et al list severity first (Mount et al, 2016; Ritter et al, 2019). Kidd and Fejerskov find ICDAS difficult to chart in the clinic and prefer Nyvad criteria (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).

### S6. Evidence and limits [prognosis]
Abstract only: Pitts and Ekstrand describe staging of the caries process by extent and activity, with ICCMS integrating tooth and patient information including risk (Pitts and Ekstrand, 2013). Salivary test predictive value is not fully demonstrated (Ritter et al, 2019). Hilton et al call assessments of lesion activity "best guesses", and say risk is difficult to predict accurately because caries is a multifactorial disease (Hilton et al, 2013).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 18 minutes.)*

### 1. Definitions
- **Caries risk assessment:** relating the patient's risk status to the activity of the lesions (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- **ICDAS:** visual coded system for caries stage (Mount et al, 2016).
- **ICCMS:** merges seven ICDAS codes into four and was developed to improve decision-making (Mount et al, 2016); elements: severity and activity classification, risk-based plan and recall (Caneppele et al, 2020).

### 2. Pathway
Identify (examination, ICDAS, risk), control and prevent, restore minimally invasively, recall (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). Summitt: preventive and noninvasive options, operative options, then maintenance care (Hilton et al, 2013). The books do not use "detect, decide, do".

### 2a. Detection and risk factors
Teeth are cleaned and examined both wet and dry; sharp probes are contraindicated (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). Pickard uses a modified ICDAS scored 0-4 (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). Five common factors: microbial mix, sugar frequency, fluoride, stimulated salivary flow, recent caries experience (Mount et al, 2016).

### 3. ICDAS caries codes
| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | sound |
| 1 | first change in enamel |
| 2 | distinct visual change |
| 3 | enamel breakdown |
| 4 | dentin shadow |
| 5 | distinct cavity, dentin visible |
| 6 | extensive cavity |
(Hilton et al, 2013; Ritter et al, 2019). ICCS groups these as 0, 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6 (Hilton et al, 2013).

### 4. Risk categories (books differ)
Low, moderate, high (Sikri, 2020). High risk: two or more new lesions in 2 years (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); two or more in the last year (Sikri, 2020); CAMBRA adds extremely high and counts recent activity indicators (Mount et al, 2016).

### 5. Management
Recall ranges from 2-6 to 12-18 months by risk (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). Cavitated lesions in general need restoring (Fontana and Huysmans, 2019); brown or black ones may be inactive (Mount et al, 2016).

### 6. Limits
ICDAS is visual and gives no decision guidance (Mount et al, 2016); salivary tests are not fully proven (Ritter et al, 2019); activity judgements are "best guesses" and risk is hard to predict (Hilton et al, 2013).

### 7. Conclusion
Risk and activity decide whether a lesion is managed non-operatively or restored minimally invasively (Fontana and Huysmans, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- ICDAS codes 0-6; ICCMS four stages; ICCS 0, 1-2, 3-4, 5-6 (Mount et al, 2016; Hilton et al, 2013).
- High-risk threshold: two or more lesions in 2 years (Banerjee and Watson, 2015) or in 1 year (Sikri, 2020).
- Sharp probes are not used for detection (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
