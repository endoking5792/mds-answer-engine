## STUDY MODE

### S1. Guiding idea [orientation]
Sikri says adhesive dental materials conserve tooth structure because they do not need mechanical retention features, and gives glass-ionomer cements, resin-modified GICs, composite with dentine bonding agents and bonded amalgam as examples (Sikri, 2020).

### S2. Remineralising and arresting materials [treatment]
Sikri lists fluorides, CPP-ACP, tricalcium phosphate and NovaMin bioactive glass for enamel remineralisation (Sikri, 2020). CPP-ACP is an adjunct in high-risk patients and for white spot lesions (Sikri, 2020); most CPP-ACP studies have not looked at its effect independent of fluoride (Ritter et al, 2019). Silver diamine fluoride at 38% is applied twice yearly to cavitated lesions and might arrest the process (Sikri, 2020); Sturdevant reports that up to 50% of arrested lesions were reactivated by 24 months after one application (Ritter et al, 2019). The resin infiltration kit has an acid gel with 15% hydrochloric acid, an ethanol drying agent and a TEGDMA-based infiltrant (Sikri, 2020).

### S3. Restorative materials [treatment]
GIC has adhesion and fluoride release, performs well in low-stress areas, is rechargeable, but is technique sensitive and opaque; adding resin gives easier, light-cured, more aesthetic resin-modified GIC (Sikri, 2020). Flowable composites have a low modulus and higher polymerisation shrinkage and are not used routinely (Sikri, 2020). Bonded amalgam improves bonding but proper strength may not be achieved; Sikri also lists checked marginal leakage and a conservative approach as advantages (Sikri, 2020). Pickard's Guide says amalgam retention needs undercuts and slots, which is not particularly minimally invasive (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). Sturdevant says there are likely no indications for compomers in contemporary dentistry (Ritter et al, 2019).

### S4. Special materials [treatment]
The Hall technique cements a preformed metal crown with a glass-ionomer luting cement over a carious primary molar without preparation or excavation (Sikri, 2020). Evidence level: one textbook description (Sikri, 2020); Kidd and Fejerskov also describe it (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016); no outcome figures were read for this answer. For caries removal Sikri describes Carisolv, pepsin and Papacarie papain gels (Sikri, 2020). Pickard's Guide calls Biodentine a dentine replacement material and bioactive, and cannot suggest any positive indications for calcium hydroxide beneath adhesive restorations as an indirect pulp cap, while calcium hydroxide still has a use as a direct pulp cap (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### S5. Where the books differ, and single-source cautions (side by side, not ranked)
- **Silver diamine fluoride strength:** Sikri names a 38% solution (Sikri, 2020); Sturdevant also names 38% (Ritter et al, 2019); Craig's describes a solution of about 30% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- **ART glass ionomer:** Sikri presents ART as a technique for managing caries (Sikri, 2020); Sturdevant says the technique and material is not recommended in routine clinical use and is restricted to areas with limited access to treatment (Ritter et al, 2019).
- **Sealants (single source, Pickard):** systematic reviews found no clinically significant advantage of one sealant type over another, although resin sealants show increased longevity and better wear resistance, and GIC sealants may still be recommended where moisture control is difficult (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- **GIC in a high caries rate:** Mount lists GIC as ideal in a high caries rate, but contraindicated under heavy occlusal load and in xerostomia, and uses the same phrase for amalgam, "Excellent in the presence of a high caries rate" (Mount et al, 2016).

## EXAM MODE

### 1. Introduction
Adhesive materials make it possible to conserve tooth structure because they need no mechanical retention (Sikri, 2020). Materials are grouped by job; GIC types and ART are in the GIC answers.

### 2. Materials by function
| Group | Materials and use |
|---|---|
| Remineralising | Fluoride, CPP-ACP, tricalcium phosphate, NovaMin bioactive glass (Sikri, 2020); CPP-ACP gum: 18% less caries progression at 24 months in one trial (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); effect apart from fluoride not studied (Ritter et al, 2019) |
| Arresting | 38% silver diamine fluoride twice yearly might arrest lesions (Sikri, 2020); stains black; up to 50% of arrested lesions reactivated by 24 months (Ritter et al, 2019) |
| Sealants | Resin or GIC; reviews found no clinically significant advantage of one type, though resin shows longer longevity (Banerjee and Watson, 2015) |
| Microinvasive | Resin infiltrant: 15% hydrochloric acid gel, ethanol, TEGDMA resin (Sikri, 2020) |
| Restorative | GIC: adhesion, fluoride release, low-stress sites, technique sensitive; resin-modified GIC light-cured (Sikri, 2020) |
| Restorative | Composite with bonding; flowable has higher shrinkage (Sikri, 2020) |
| Restorative | Bonded amalgam: leakage checked, but strength may be inadequate (Sikri, 2020) |
| ART | Hand excavation, then GIC (Sikri, 2020) |
| Crown | Preformed metal crown cemented with GIC: Hall technique; one textbook description, no outcome data read (Sikri, 2020) |
| Pulp | Biodentine, a bioactive dentine replacement material (Banerjee and Watson, 2015) |
| Caries removal | Carisolv gel, papain gel (Sikri, 2020) |

### 3. Choosing the material
GIC is bioactive and needs no mechanical interlocks; Mount lists it as ideal in a high caries rate, but contraindicated under heavy occlusal load and in xerostomia, and uses the same phrase for amalgam (Mount et al, 2016). Amalgam retention needs undercuts and slots, which is not particularly minimally invasive (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 4. Where books differ
Silver diamine fluoride: 38% (Sikri, 2020; Ritter et al, 2019); about 30% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). ART glass ionomer: a caries technique (Sikri, 2020); not for routine use (Ritter et al, 2019).

### 5. Conclusion
Each material is matched to a job: remineralise, arrest, seal, restore or protect the pulp (synthesis, not a quoted statement).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

- Silver diamine fluoride: 38% (Sikri, 2020); about 30% (Sakaguchi et al, 2019).
- Resin infiltrant kit: acid gel, drying agent, infiltrant (Sikri, 2020).
