## STUDY MODE

### S1. Merits [treatment relevance]
Smaller cavities are easier to fill without voids, give better moisture control and seal, leave a stronger crown and are simpler to maintain (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). Where a peripheral seal is achieved and pulp symptoms are not present, caries-affected dentine left over the pulp gives better indirect pulp protection than any artificial restorative material (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). Summitt cites two systematic reviews in which partial removal reduced pulp exposure risk by 98% compared with complete removal (Hilton et al, 2013); in the "complete" removal control groups of the trials Kidd and Fejerskov review, the pulp was exposed in up to 53% of cases (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). Sikri states that restoration placement "can be reduced to approximately 50%", without a cited study (Sikri, 2020). ART needs no electricity, causes minimal discomfort and costs little (Sikri, 2020).

### S2. Demerits [prognosis]
Minimally invasive operative dentistry alone does not cure dental disease (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). The term can become a new buzzword for drilling and filling, and at the time of going to press there were no realistic fees in England and Wales for caries control in the Health Service (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). Residual caries shows as a dark shadow on a later radiograph, and another dentist might remove the filling (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). ART has marked operator effects and single-surface restorations did better than multisurface ones (Sikri, 2020); in deciduous teeth approximal ART restorations are prone to loss or fracture, and loss is not necessarily a failure (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). Small early lesions in a high-risk patient with failed prevention may need operative care (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); caries risk assessment is inevitably a best guess (Hilton et al, 2013).

### S3. Where the books differ (side by side, not ranked)
- **Tunnel preparation:** Sikri: studies could not show promising results, ridge strength is not maintained, indicated in small lesions especially in deciduous teeth (Sikri, 2020). Summitt: 21% failed in 7 years against none for slots; 46% against 76% survival in another study; "not indicated and should be avoided" (Hilton et al, 2013). Sturdevant: not supported (Ritter et al, 2019). Mount et al: a design for Site 2, Size 1, one glass-ionomer case satisfactory at 12 years (Mount et al, 2016). Pickard: only successful if the lesion is sufficiently small, high risk of ridge fracture (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- **Silver diamine fluoride:** Sikri: 38% twice yearly "might be able to arrest" caries (Sikri, 2020). Craig's: about 30% solution, growing evidence of arrest, stains tooth structure (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Sturdevant: black staining; up to 50% of arrested lesions reactivated by 24 months (Ritter et al, 2019).

### 1. Introduction
MID here means minimally invasive / minimal intervention dentistry: Pickard's Guide excavates "unrepairable, diseased enamel and dentine only" (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 2. Merits
| Merit | Source |
|---|---|
| Smaller cavities: easier void-free filling, better moisture control and seal, stronger crown, simpler maintenance | Banerjee and Watson, 2015 |
| Fewer pulp exposures: with a peripheral seal and no pulp symptoms, affected dentine over the pulp protects it better than any artificial material; two systematic reviews cited by Summitt: partial removal cut exposure risk by 98% | Banerjee and Watson, 2015; Hilton et al, 2013 |
| Fewer restorations: placement "can be reduced to approximately 50%" (no study cited in the text) | Sikri, 2020 |
| ART: no electricity, minimal discomfort, low cost | Sikri, 2020 |
| Resin infiltration: arrests early lesions without drilling; repair rather than replace saves tooth | Sikri, 2020 |

### 3. Demerits
| Demerit | Source |
|---|---|
| Does not cure disease alone; needs the patient's responsibility | Banerjee and Watson, 2015 |
| Can become a buzzword; no realistic fees in England and Wales (Health Service) for caries control at the time of going to press | Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016 |
| Residual caries shows as a dark shadow on radiographs; another dentist might remove the filling | Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016 |
| Stepwise re-entry: "no definitive answer"; the authors would not re-enter | Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016 |
| ART: marked operator effects; single-surface did better than multisurface (Sikri); in deciduous teeth approximal restorations are prone to loss or fracture (Kidd) | Sikri, 2020; Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016 |

### 4. Where the books differ (side by side, not ranked)
- **Tunnel:** Sikri, ridge strength not maintained, indicated in small lesions (Sikri, 2020); Mount et al, one case satisfactory at 12 years (Mount et al, 2016); Summitt, 21% failed in 7 years against none of the slots, "not indicated and should be avoided" (Hilton et al, 2013); Sturdevant, not supported (Ritter et al, 2019); Pickard, only if the lesion is small (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- **Silver diamine fluoride:** Sikri, 38% twice yearly, "might be able to arrest" caries (Sikri, 2020); Craig's, about 30% solution, growing evidence of arrest, stains (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); Sturdevant, black staining, up to 50% of arrested lesions reactivated by 24 months (Ritter et al, 2019).

### 5. Conclusion
The gains are tooth and pulp preservation; the limits include patient dependence (Banerjee and Watson, 2015) and operator effects in ART (Sikri, 2020).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Merit: smaller cavity, better seal, stronger crown (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Merit: fewer pulp exposures with partial removal (Hilton et al, 2013).
- Demerit: restoration alone does not cure disease (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Demerit: tunnel results differ between books (Sikri, 2020; Hilton et al, 2013; Mount et al, 2016).
