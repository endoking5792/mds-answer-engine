## STUDY MODE

### S1. Definition and terms [orientation]
- MID is used here for minimally invasive / minimal intervention dentistry. Sikri says the two terms are used interchangeably but describe different concepts; minimal intervention deals with the cause of disease and rests on prevention and control, and minimal invasive dentistry embraces conservative restorative procedures and, in his words, "is merely a component" of that care plan (Sikri, 2020).
- Pickard's Guide: minimally invasive operative dentistry repairs or restores damaged tooth structure directly to maintain pulp vitality, function and aesthetics (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). Sikri adds that "ultraconservative" and "microdentistry" are synonyms that imply magnification and devices such as chemo-mechanical, air abrasive and laser systems (Sikri, 2020).

### S2. Principle and components [basic science]
- The aim is to move the focus from restoring teeth damaged by caries to preventing disease at its early stages, and to keep the treatment of an existing lesion to a minimum involvement of healthy tooth (Sikri, 2020).
- The components are early diagnosis, caries risk assessment, arresting existing lesions, remineralisation, minimal invasive restoration, resin infiltration, and repair rather than replacement (Sikri, 2020).

### S3. Techniques [treatment]
- Detection: ICDAS II scores 0 (sound after prolonged air drying) to 6 (extensive cavity) (Sikri, 2020); Sikri grades risk low, moderate or high, with two or more lesions in the last year among the high-risk features (Sikri, 2020), while Pickard's Guide defines high risk as two or more new, progressing or restored lesions in 2 years and says a low or high division is usually more than sufficient (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); sharp probes are contraindicated for caries detection (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Remineralisation and arrest: fluorides and CPP-ACP (Sikri, 2020); 38% silver diamine fluoride twice yearly "might be able to arrest" caries (Sikri, 2020); resin infiltration treats early lesions without drilling (Sikri, 2020).
- Cavity: ART with hand instruments and glass ionomer (Sikri, 2020); tunnel, minibox and fissurotomy (Sikri, 2020); selective removal that leaves affected dentine over the pulp (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Other: air abrasion was developed in 1945 by R. B. Black (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); with Carisolv local anaesthesia is not required but can be given if the patient is apprehensive (Sikri, 2020), and patients do not always require it (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); Er:YAG and Nd:YAG lasers can cut tooth (Sikri, 2020).

### S4. Materials [treatment]
Adhesive materials need no mechanical retention features: glass ionomer, resin-modified glass ionomer, composite with bonding agent and bonded amalgam (Sikri, 2020). Set glass ionomer is rechargeable with fluoride (Sikri, 2020).

### S5. Repair [treatment]
Each replacement loses more tooth (Sikri, 2020). The 5 Rs are review, refurbish, reseal, repair and replace (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### S6. Limits [prognosis]
- Tunnel, side by side: Sikri, ridge strength not maintained, indicated in small lesions especially in deciduous teeth (Sikri, 2020); Mount et al, a tunnel (internal occlusal fossa) design for Site 2, Size 1, one case satisfactory at 12 years (Mount et al, 2016); Summitt, 21% failed in 7 years against none of the slot restorations, "not indicated and should be avoided" (Hilton et al, 2013); Sturdevant, not supported (Ritter et al, 2019); Pickard, only if the lesion is sufficiently small, with a high risk of ridge fracture (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Operative minimal invasion alone does not cure disease and needs patient responsibility (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 27 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition
- MID is used here for minimally invasive / minimal intervention dentistry. Sikri says minimal intervention "deals with the cause of dental disease", based on prevention and control, and that minimal invasive dentistry "is merely a component" of that care plan (Sikri, 2020).
- Minimally invasive operative dentistry repairs or restores damaged tooth structure directly, to maintain pulp vitality, function and aesthetics (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 2. Principle
- The focus moves from restoring teeth damaged by caries to preventing disease at its early stages (Sikri, 2020).
- "Extension for prevention" has changed to "prevention for extension" (Sikri, 2020).
- Black's classification described the final restoration location, not the lesion (Mount et al, 2016). Restoration of a lesion is not caries treatment; it is defect repair (Mount et al, 2016).
- "Drilling and filling teeth does not cure caries" (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 4. Techniques
- **Detection and risk:** ICDAS II scores 0 to 6 (Sikri, 2020); Sikri: risk low, moderate or high, 2 or more lesions in the last year among high-risk features (Sikri, 2020); Pickard: high risk is 2 or more new, progressing or restored lesions in 2 years, low or high usually sufficient (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); sharp probe contraindicated (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- **Remineralise and arrest:** fluorides, CPP-ACP, 38% silver diamine fluoride twice yearly, resin infiltration without drilling (Sikri, 2020).
- **ART:** hand instruments and glass ionomer, first tried in the mid 1980s (Sikri, 2020).
- **Selective removal:** affected dentine may stay over the pulp; infected dentine never at the junction (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- **Rotary:** tunnel, minibox, fissurotomy; the fissurotomy bur tip is ten times smaller than a #556 carbide bur (Sikri, 2020).
- **Air abrasion, Carisolv, laser:** non-rotary and other options (Banerjee and Watson, 2015; Sikri, 2020).

### 4A. In relation to conservative dentistry
| Point | Conventional teaching | MID concept |
|---|---|---|
| Extension | extension for prevention (Mount et al, 2016) | prevention for extension (Sikri, 2020) |
| Cavity size | prescribed forms larger than necessary (Mount et al, 2016) | restoration size follows lesion size (Mount et al, 2016) |

### 5. Materials
Glass ionomer, resin-modified glass ionomer, composite with bonding agent and bonded amalgam need no mechanical retention (Sikri, 2020). Set glass ionomer is rechargeable with fluoride (Sikri, 2020).

### 6. Repair
Each replacement loses more tooth, so repair is preferred; the 5 Rs are review, refurbish, reseal, repair, replace (Sikri, 2020; Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 7. Advantages
Smaller cavities allow easier void-free placement, better moisture control and seal, a stronger crown and simpler maintenance (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 8. Limitations
- Operative care alone does not cure disease and needs patient responsibility (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Tunnel, side by side: Sikri, ridge strength not maintained, indicated in small lesions (Sikri, 2020); Mount et al, one case satisfactory at 12 years (Mount et al, 2016); Summitt, 21% failed in 7 years against none of the slots, "not indicated and should be avoided" (Hilton et al, 2013); Sturdevant, not supported (Ritter et al, 2019); Pickard, small lesions only (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

### 9. Conclusion
MID, as Sikri frames it, treats the disease first (minimal intervention), removes the least tooth (minimal invasive) and repairs rather than replaces (Sikri, 2020; Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- MID covers both wordings; Sikri calls minimal invasive dentistry "merely a component" of minimal intervention care (Sikri, 2020).
- Seven components, from early diagnosis to repair (Sikri, 2020).
- "Prevention for extension" replaces "extension for prevention" (Sikri, 2020).
- Infected dentine never left at the junction (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Treating minimal intervention and minimal invasive dentistry as identical without noting that Sikri separates them (Sikri, 2020).
2. Listing only techniques and leaving out risk assessment and remineralisation.
3. Presenting tunnel preparation as proven or rejected; the books differ (Sikri, 2020; Hilton et al, 2013; Mount et al, 2016).
