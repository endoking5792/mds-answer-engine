## STUDY MODE

### S1. Names for the same procedures [definition]
- Torres and Schwendicke: non-selective removal to hard dentin was formerly "complete removal"; selective removal to soft dentin was formerly "partial or incomplete removal"; stepwise excavation was formerly "two-step excavation" (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).
- Kidd and Fejerskov: excavation to hard dentine is "complete" and superficial excavation is "partial" (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). Hilton et al call the procedure indirect pulp capping or indirect pulp treatment (Hilton et al, 2013).
- Banerjee and Watson say the labels stepwise and ART are becoming redundant (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
- Sikri defines indirect pulp capping as a two-appointment procedure and indirect pulp treatment as a single-appointment procedure, and describes stepwise excavation and a one-appointment method with no subsequent re-entry (Sikri, 2020); Hilton et al use the two names for the same procedure (Hilton et al, 2013).

### S2. Biological basis [mechanism]
- Mount describes an infected layer that cannot remineralise and an affected layer that can; healing the affected layer is called a cornerstone of minimal intervention dentistry (Mount et al, 2016).

### S3. Endpoints and steps [technique]
- The periphery ends on hard enamel and dentin; the pulpal endpoint is firm dentin in shallow or moderate lesions and leathery or soft dentin in deep lesions in vital painless teeth (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).
- Ritter et al remove caries to within approximately 1 mm of the pulp, then place a glass ionomer or definitive restoration (Ritter et al, 2019).

### S4. Where the books differ [prognosis]
- Re-entry: 6 to 9 months (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); 3 weeks minimum, with 6 months as the life of the temporary restoration (Mount et al, 2016); 6 to 12 months (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020); 4 to 12 months in the studies reviewed, and definitive indirect restorations delayed 4 to 8 months (Hilton et al, 2013); 25 days to 2 years, with the pulp exposed in 17% on re-entry (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016). Mount's second alternative leaves the bulk of the transitional restoration as a base and laminates over it (Mount et al, 2016). Ritter et al say re-entry does not improve outcomes (Ritter et al, 2019); Sikri says re-entry should only be carried out if the patient is asymptomatic (Sikri, 2020).
- Liner: Hilton et al place a calcium hydroxide liner over the remaining demineralized dentin, then resin-modified glass ionomer, and say the type of liner is less important than the seal (Hilton et al, 2013); Ritter et al say calcium hydroxide does not appear to improve outcomes (Ritter et al, 2019); Mount names glass-ionomer cement as the material of choice (Mount et al, 2016).
- Dyes: Hilton et al say to use a caries-disclosing dye if necessary for peripheral caries (Hilton et al, 2013); Banerjee and Watson say dyes stain deeper collagen, often leading to cavity over-preparation (Banerjee and Watson, 2015); Torres and Schwendicke say dyes are not at all recommended in pulpal areas (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020); Ritter et al say their use is no longer recommended (Ritter et al, 2019); Mount says they disclose bacteria rather than demineralized dentine (Mount et al, 2016).

### S5. Evidence and limits [prognosis]
- In teeth with deep caries lesions, partial removal reduced pulp exposure by 98% against complete excavation (two systematic reviews) (Hilton et al, 2013); complete removal in control groups exposed the pulp in up to 53% (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).
- 2016 consensus, abstract only: selective removal to firm dentine for shallow or moderately deep lesions, to soft dentine for deep lesions; non-selective removal to hard dentine no longer recommended (Schwendicke et al, 2016).
- Leathery dentin may look like residual caries on radiographs; consent is needed; full-coverage teeth are not suited (Ritter et al, 2019).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 18 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition
Complete removal excavates to hard dentin everywhere; selective removal uses a different pulpal endpoint; stepwise removal is two stages with a sealed interval (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).

### 2. Terminology (books differ)
| Book | Term |
|---|---|
| Torres and Schwendicke, 2020 | non-selective; selective; stepwise |
| Ritter et al, 2019 | removal to firm dentin; SCR to soft dentin |
| Hilton et al, 2013 | partial removal; indirect pulp capping |
| Mount et al, 2016 | infected layer only; indirect pulp therapy |
| Banerjee and Watson, 2015 | partial or incomplete; stepwise; ART |
| Sikri, 2020 | indirect pulp capping (two appointments); indirect pulp treatment (one appointment) |

### 3. Comparison
| Feature | Complete | Selective | Stepwise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Endpoint | hard dentin everywhere (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020) | periphery hard; pulpal firm or soft (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020) | soft, then firm 6 to 12 months later (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020) |
| Pulp exposure | up to 53% in control groups (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016) | 98% fewer than complete in deep lesions (Hilton et al, 2013) | more than one-stage (Banerjee and Watson, 2015) |

### 4. Steps
1. Isolate; clear the periphery to sound enamel and dentin (Hilton et al, 2013; Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
2. Leave firm, leathery or soft dentin over the pulp by depth (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).
3. Seal with glass ionomer or resin-modified glass ionomer (Mount et al, 2016; Hilton et al, 2013).
4. Restore definitively at once or after re-entry (Ritter et al, 2019; Mount et al, 2016). Calcium hydroxide liner: used by Hilton et al; no benefit per Ritter et al (Hilton et al, 2013; Ritter et al, 2019).

### 5. Indications
Vital tooth, no spontaneous pain, no periapical lesion (Hilton et al, 2013; Ritter et al, 2019).

### 6. Limits
Leathery dentin may mimic caries on radiographs; consent is needed (Ritter et al, 2019). Value of re-entry is not shown (Ritter et al, 2019; Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016); intervals differ (Mount et al, 2016; Hilton et al, 2013).

### 7. Conclusion
Selective removal spares the pulp; removal to hard dentin is not recommended any longer in deep lesions (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Selective removal to soft dentin was formerly "partial or incomplete" removal (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).
- In deep lesions, partial removal reduced pulp exposure by 98% (Hilton et al, 2013).
- Complete removal exposed the pulp in up to 53% in control groups (Kidd and Fejerskov, 2016).
- Re-entry intervals differ among books (Mount et al, 2016; Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Using "complete" and "partial" without stating the pulpal and peripheral endpoints (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).
2. Leaving infected dentine at the enamel-dentine junction; Banerjee and Watson state it should not be retained there in any clinical situation (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
3. Giving one re-entry interval as the only figure (Mount et al, 2016; Banerjee and Watson, 2015).
4. Using the selective method in a symptomatic tooth (Ritter et al, 2019; Hilton et al, 2013).
