## STUDY MODE

### S1. Why curvature matters [significance]
Nearly all canals are curved in the apical third, mainly faciolingually, which standard radiographs do not show (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Harty says all canals are curved in one or more planes (Harty, 2017); Walton and Torabinejad say assume a canal is curved (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002). Schafer et al measured 1163 canals: 0 to 75 degrees in the clinical view, 0 to 69 in the proximal view, and an S-shaped secondary curve in 12.3% of maxillary and 23.3% of mandibular teeth (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Grossman: 20 degrees in a narrow canal may be difficult, 30 degrees in a wide canal may be negotiable (Grossman, 2021).

### S2. Types [classification]
Torabinejad et al: gradual curve of the whole canal, sharp curve near the apex, or gradual curve with a straight apical ending (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Grossman adds minimal apical curvature (Grossman, 2021). Sikri: uniform, abrupt near the apical half, or S-shaped, and the letters I, J, C and S (one book) (Sikri, 2019).

### S3. Detecting curvature [method]
- Angled radiographs are needed for presence, direction and severity (Torabinejad et al, 2021); buccal curves move opposite to the beam and lingual curves with it (Basrani, 2012).
- A radiograph shows only two planes of a three-dimensional curve and, in general, proximal curvatures cannot be detected on two-dimensional films (Ingle, year not printed). Curves in the beam plane are estimated by tactile sense and initial files (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014); examine the file after removal (Grossman, 2021). CBCT shows buccolingual curvature better and its software can measure the radius (one book) (Sikri, 2019).

### S4. Estimating curvature [methods, side by side]
- Schneider: Torabinejad et al describe a line parallel to the canal axis and a second line from the foramen to where the canal leaves the axis, classed straight (5 degrees or less), moderate (10 to 20) and severe (25 to 70) (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Sikri describes points A, B and C with lines AB and BC and prints straight "less than 50", moderate 5 to 20, severe "more than 20" degrees (Sikri, 2019).
- Weine: two straight lines; Torabinejad et al say it reflects curvature more accurately, especially apically (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Sikri takes the coronal canal axis and the axis from apex to deviation (Sikri, 2019). Weine's own text cites Jungman et al (one book) and gives mesiodistal curvature only (Weine, 2004).
- Pruett et al: angle plus radius; Torabinejad et al say it is geometrically equivalent to Weine's but adds the radius by a circular arc (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Basrani: a shorter radius means a more abrupt curve (Basrani, 2012).
- Lutein four-point method and CBCT radius classes (more than 8 mm mild, 4 to 8 mm moderate, less than 4 mm severe): one book, Sikri (Sikri, 2019).

### S5. Management of curved canals [management]
Straight-line access should extend to the beginning of the curve (Ingle, year not printed), Coronal flaring reduces the angle and increases the radius (Ingle, year not printed). Weine enters canals only with precurved files (Weine, 2004); Grossman says always precurve stainless steel files (Grossman, 2021). Harty and Ingle say NiTi keeps the original curve better than stainless steel (Harty, 2017; Ingle, year not printed); Torabinejad et al say zips, ledges and perforations are no longer a problem except separation (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Gulabivala and Ng say that for double curves in one plane, rotary NiTi is only possible if the curves are gentle and the canal already wide (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014); Ingle says a great angle with an abrupt short curve can hardly be enlarged without transportation, with rotary NiTi or stainless steel (Ingle, year not printed).

### S6. Management of calcified canals [management]
Gutmann: calcification progresses from crown to apex, so failure to find an orifice does not rule out apical canal space; use a No. 8 K-file or a stiffer C+ file, teased in with irrigant, and avoid NiTi for first negotiation (Gutmann, 2011). Cohen lists methylene blue staining, the bubble test, and a K-file 6, 8 or 10, preferably C or C+, coated with a chelating agent (Cohen, 2021). Harty starts with an ISO 06 file after removing irritation dentine by ultrasonic or long-shank bur (Harty, 2017); Grossman uses C+ or C-Pilot files (Grossman, 2021). Chelators: Weine says best after the apex is reached with a fine instrument (Weine, 2004); Gutmann says a chelating agent can be used but will not dissolve loose debris, and advises chelating pastes or solutions to assist penetration (Gutmann, 2011); Harty names a chelating lubricant (Harty, 2017); Grossman, EDTA gel (Grossman, 2021). Harty and Gutmann accept no treatment or filling to the negotiated level when symptom-free (Harty, 2017; Gutmann, 2011).

### S7. Critical evidence: is the Schneider angle enough? [critical evidence]
Torabinejad et al call Schneider's method the most widely used but say the angle with radius and length may be more precise (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Ingle states that only two planes are visible, and Weine that the estimate is mesiodistal only (Ingle, year not printed; Weine, 2004). No study data or head-to-head test is printed; the accuracy statements are from Torabinejad et al only, so the books are shown side by side without a verdict.
## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 43 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition and importance
Nearly all canals are curved apically, mainly faciolingually, and this is not evident on a standard radiograph (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Treatment is more complex as curvature increases (Torabinejad et al, 2021, review-question answer); Weine marks 30 degrees or more as markedly more complex (Weine, 2004).

### 2. Types of curvature
Gradual curve of the whole canal, sharp curve near the apex, or gradual curve with a straight apical ending (Torabinejad et al, 2021); Grossman adds minimal apical curvature (Grossman, 2021); S-shaped (bayonet) canals involve at least two curves (Gutmann, 2011; Ingle, year not printed).

### 3. Detecting curvature
| Method | Point | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Angled radiographs | Presence, direction, severity (Torabinejad et al, 2021) | Only two planes seen (Ingle, year not printed) |
| Length film or file in canal | Severity evident (Ingle, year not printed; Basrani, 2012) | Proximal curves in general not detected (Ingle, year not printed) |
| Removed file | Shows curves not on film (Grossman, 2021) | Known only after entry |
| Tactile sense | Curves parallel to beam (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) | Operator dependent |
| CBCT | Buccolingual curve, radius (Sikri, 2019) | One book for the radius classes |

### 4. Estimating the angle and radius
| Method | Construction | Printed grading |
|---|---|---|
| Schneider | Line along canal axis; second line from foramen to where canal leaves axis (Torabinejad et al, 2021) | Straight 5 degrees or less, moderate 10 to 20, severe 25 to 70 (Torabinejad et al, 2021) |
| Schneider (Sikri) | Points A orifice, B deviation, C foramen (Sikri, 2019) | Straight "less than 50", moderate 5 to 20, severe more than 20 (Sikri, 2019) |
| Weine | Axis of coronal canal and axis from apex to deviation (Sikri, 2019) | Reflects apical curve more accurately (Torabinejad et al, 2021; one book) |
| Pruett et al | Weine-equivalent angle plus radius (Torabinejad et al, 2021) | Shorter radius, more abrupt (Basrani, 2012) |
| Lutein (one book) | Four points (Sikri, 2019) | As Schneider (Sikri, 2019) |
| CBCT radius (one book) | Software (Sikri, 2019) | More than 8 mm mild, less than 4 mm severe (Sikri, 2019) |

Weine canal classification: see A2.

### 5. Limits of the estimate
Two-dimensional estimate gives mesiodistal curvature only (Weine, 2004); double curves often lie in the beam plane and are not radiographically visible (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Underestimating curvature has caused severe straightening and apical transportation (Ingle, year not printed). Take angled films, examine the first file removed and consider CBCT (Torabinejad et al, 2021; Grossman, 2021; Sikri, 2019).

### 6. Management of curved canals: sequence
1. Estimate curvature; take angled films (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
2. Straight-line access to the first curve and coronal flaring (Ingle, year not printed); extend the mesial wall 1 to 2 mm in molars (Gutmann, 2011).
3. Negotiate with small precurved stainless steel K-files (Weine, 2004; Ingle, year not printed); 06, 08, 10 for narrow and calcified canals (Ingle, year not printed); 10 or 8, rarely 6, for glide path (Grossman, 2021); patency (Cohen, 2021).
4. Shape with a chosen technique (section 7), irrigating and recapitulating; keep patency with a small K-file passed passively through the foramen (Cohen, 2021).
5. Recheck working length as the canal shortens (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).
6. Obturate with a thermoplastic technique where canals are straightened (Ingle, year not printed).

### 7. Techniques as printed
| Technique | Book statement |
|---|---|
| Step-back | Designed to avoid errors in curved canals (Cohen, 2021) |
| Crown-down | Less straightening, friction and extrusion (Ingle, year not printed) |
| Balanced force | Ingle: 180 degrees clockwise, 120 counterclockwise; Grossman: 90 clockwise, 180 to 270 counterclockwise (Ingle, year not printed; Grossman, 2021) |
| Anticurvature filing | Protects the furcal wall of mesial molar roots (Grossman, 2021) |
| NiTi | Keeps curve better than stainless steel (Harty, 2017); zips, ledges, perforations no longer a problem except separation (Torabinejad et al, 2021); double curves in one plane: only if gentle and canal wide (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014); great angle with abrupt short curve: transportation hard to avoid (Ingle, year not printed) |

S-shaped canals: prepare the first curve before the second, with small flexible instruments (Ingle, year not printed; Gutmann, 2011).

### 8. Procedural errors
Ledge: bypass with a file precurved over the apical 3 to 4 mm (Grossman, 2021). Zip and elbow: from straightening. Weine, in his opinion, finds flute removal mandatory at 30 degrees and more, though tapered files lessened the need (one book) (Weine, 2004). Transportation: removal of outer canal wall in the apical half (Cohen, 2021). Strip perforation: use anticurvature filing (Ingle, year not printed).

### 9. Management of calcified canals
1. Radiographs from different angles; locate the orifice (Grossman, 2021; Gutmann, 2011).
2. Magnification, dyes and the bubble test (Cohen, 2021).
3. Ultrasonic probe tip to enlarge the orifice (Gutmann, 2011); Harty: ultrasonic or long-shank bur for irritation dentine (Harty, 2017).
4. Gutmann: No. 8 K-file or C+ file, precurved at 1 mm, teased in with NaOCl; never dry; NiTi contraindicated for first negotiation (Gutmann, 2011). Cohen: K-file 6, 8 or 10, preferably C or C+, with chelating agent, filing away from the furcation (Cohen, 2021).
5. Advance in 1 to 2 mm steps (Gutmann, 2011). Chelator: Gutmann allows it and advises chelating paste or solution to assist penetration (Gutmann, 2011); Cohen, Harty, Grossman: chelating agent, lubricant, EDTA gel (Cohen, 2021; Harty, 2017; Grossman, 2021); Weine: best after the apex is reached (Weine, 2004).
6. If blocked and symptom-free, fill to the level reached and review (Gutmann, 2011).

**Conclusion:** estimate by angle and radius, remember the film under-reads, and shape from straight-line access with precurved instruments (Torabinejad et al, 2021; Ingle, year not printed).
