## STUDY MODE

### S1. Name [terminology]
The MB2 orifice lies palatal and mesial to MB1 (Cohen, 2021); Weine prefers "mesiolingual" to "MB-2" (Weine, 2004).

### S2. Prevalence, side by side [prevalence]
Two canals in the MB root of the maxillary first molar, Ingle 59.4% (laboratory 64.0%, clinical 56.8%) (Ingle, year not printed); Sikri 55% (Sikri, 2019); Torabinejad et al 60.4% (Torabinejad et al, 2021); Gulabivala and Ng 71% (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014); Weine about 50%, separate or merging (Weine, 2004); Grossman 84% where two orifices are traced (Grossman, 2021); Harty up to 90% (Harty, 2017); Paqué roughly 60% pooled, laboratory range 25% to 96% (Paqué, 2025). Second molar MB root, two canals: Torabinejad et al 33.7% (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Extra canals elsewhere: two canals in the distobuccal root 1.9% to 9.5%; three or more in the MB root 1.3% to 2.4% (Ingle, year not printed).

### S3. Orifice distance from MB1 toward the palatal orifice [location]
Grossman 2-3 mm (Grossman, 2021); Sikri 1.0-2.0 mm (Sikri, 2019); Weine 2 to 5 mm (Weine, 2004); Gutmann 0.5 to 5 mm, most 1 to 3 mm (Gutmann, 2011); Setzer and Walsch about 1.5-2.0 mm (Setzer and Walsch, 2025); Paqué 3.5 mm palatally and 2 mm mesially (Paqué, 2025); Gulabivala and Ng 1.82 mm (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).

### S4. Identification [methods]
- Both MB canals overlap radiographically (Harty, 2017); a distal-to-mesial cone angle shows the extra canal (Weine, 2004).
- Access becomes rhomboid (Grossman, 2021) or quadrilateral, allowing troughing 2-3 mm lingual to MB-1 (Ingle, year not printed); a dentine shelf hides the orifice (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).
- Follow the floor dark lines with an explorer and routinely seek a fourth orifice (Grossman, 2021); a floor groove may indicate it (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014); the white line test helps (Ingle, year not printed); ultrasonic tips trough the groove (Cohen, 2021).
- Microscope 71.1%, loupes 62.5%, none 17.2% (Ingle, year not printed).
- CBCT detection rose from 60.1% to 93.3% with smaller voxels (Ingle, year not printed); sensitivity 96% (Barnett, 2017).

### S5. Preparation [preparation]
Prepare MB1 first; MB2 is the smallest, sharply curved canal (Setzer and Walsch, 2025). Gates-Glidden use on its curvature before scouting often leads to ledges, so orifice modifiers are advised (Peters and Arias, 2025). The distal concavity is thin (Sikri, 2019); strip perforation is a risk (Paqué, 2025). Prepare and fill it even if it joins MB1 (Weine, 2004); about 16% cannot be instrumented and sealed (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

### 1. Definition
MB2 is the second canal in the mesiobuccal root of a maxillary molar, palatal and mesial to MB1 (Cohen, 2021). Weine prefers "mesiolingual" (Weine, 2004).

### 4. Identification
1. Radiograph with distal-to-mesial cone angle (Weine, 2004).
2. Rhomboid or quadrilateral access; remove the dentine shelf (Grossman, 2021; Ingle, year not printed).
3. Trough the groove with ultrasonic tips (Cohen, 2021).
4. Follow floor dark lines with an explorer (Grossman, 2021); groove (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014); white line test (Ingle, year not printed).
5. Magnification: microscope 71.1% against 17.2% without (Ingle, year not printed).
6. Grossman's figure traces the canal with a size 8 K-file (Grossman, 2021).
7. CBCT when needed (Barnett, 2017).

### 5. Preparation
- Prepare MB1 first (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
- Orifice modifier rather than Gates-Glidden before scouting the curved MB2 (Peters and Arias, 2025); Cohen's figure shows an orifice shaper after a K-file glide path (Cohen, 2021).
- Avoid the thin distal concavity; strip perforation risk (Sikri, 2019; Paqué, 2025).
- Prepare and fill it even if it joins MB1 (Weine, 2004).
- Do not search at all costs (Weine, 2004).

### 6. Conclusion
In Weine's cases a sinus tract healed only after the missed canal was prepared (Weine, 2004). Seek it with access, light, magnification and imaging; prepare after MB1 (Setzer and Walsch, 2025). Elsewhere: two canals in the DB root 1.9% to 9.5% (Ingle, year not printed).
## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Prevalence differs by source and method; quote author and edition (Ingle, year not printed; Sikri, 2019; Grossman, 2021).
- Orifice 2-3 mm palatal to MB1 (Grossman, 2021); Sikri 1.0-2.0 mm (Sikri, 2019).
- Rhomboid or quadrilateral access (Grossman, 2021).
- Microscope 71.1%, loupes 62.5%, none 17.2% (Ingle, year not printed).
- MB1 first, then MB2 with orifice modifiers (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Writing one prevalence figure as if all books agreed.
- Placing MB2 buccal or distal to MB1 instead of palatal and mesial (Cohen, 2021).
- Managing the MB2 curvature with Gates-Glidden drills before scouting it (Peters and Arias, 2025).
- Forgetting that MB2 may join MB1 and still needs preparation (Weine, 2004).
