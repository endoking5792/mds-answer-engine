## STUDY MODE

### S1. Middle mesial canal: definition and origin [definition]
A middle mesial canal is a third canal between the mesiobuccal and mesiolingual canals (Grossman, 2021). It lies in the developmental groove and may be only a wide anastomosis, or a separate canal from orifice to apex (Cohen, 2021). Ingle credits the term "mid-mesial canal" to Weine (Ingle, year not printed). Pomeranz et al (1981) are given in a search snippet as describing fin, confluent and independent types (Pomeranz et al, 1981, snippet only).

### S2. Middle mesial canal: prevalence as each book prints it [disagreement]
- Ingle: 1.1% of mesial roots (Table 1-41), and elsewhere up to 36% of cases, 20% with an independent apical foramen (Ingle, year not printed).
- Paqué: 1 to 15%; up to 22% in one study using the microscope and troughing; 33%, 24% and 4% in patients aged 20 or under, 21 to 40 and over 40 (one study, Nosrat et al); 22% Brazilian against 15% Turkish in one micro-CT study of extracted teeth; 4.4% (first molar) and 1.3% (second molar) on CBCT meta-analysis (Paqué, 2025).
- Setzer and Walsch: approximately 5% of clinical cases (Setzer and Walsch, 2025). Sikri: 5% in the access chapter; 13% of mesial roots in one review (Navarro et al); 1.0 to 15% in earlier studies (Sikri, 2019).
- Grossman: the text says three mesial canals occur "in rare cases"; its Table 13.12 (partly garbled) prints three mesial canals as 1.0% (Vertucci) and 57.0% (Pineda and Kuttler) (Grossman, 2021).

### S3. Distal canal variations [classification]
- One distal canal: about 70% (Weine, 2004); 70% with 15%, 8%, 5% and 2% for the other patterns in the Grossman text, while its own Table 13.12 (Vertucci row) prints 70.0, 8.0, 15.0 and 7.0, the last being two canals with two foramina (Grossman, 2021); 62.7% (Sikri, 2019).
- Two distal canals: 29.56% (Torabinejad et al, 2021); nearly one third (Ingle, year not printed); more than 25% (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014); 38% (Harty, 2017); about 40% (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
- A third (middle distal) canal: 0.2 to 3% (Paqué, 2025). Radix entomolaris is in A9.
- Second molar, one mesial canal (figures from different study types): 18.1% (Ingle, year not printed); 12.5% (Torabinejad et al, 2021); 4.0% in one report by Weine, Pasiewicz and Rice (Weine, 2004).

### S4. Clinical identification [management]
- Trough the groove between the mesial orifices, with the risk of perforation or weakening (Cohen, 2021). Open the isthmus, as a middle mesial canal may appear only at a deeper level (Setzer and Walsch, 2025). It lies closer to the mesiobuccal canal (Ingle, year not printed).
- Microscope or CBCT find accessory mesial canals better than radiographs and inspection alone (Paqué, 2025).
- A single distal canal is well centred in its root; a distal canal not admitting a size 25 file at first entry suggests Type II or III (Weine, 2004).

### 1. Middle mesial canal
- A third canal between the mesiobuccal and mesiolingual canals, in the developmental groove; it may be a wide anastomosis or a separate canal to the apex (Cohen, 2021).
- Prevalence, as printed:

| Book | Middle mesial canal |
|---|---|
| Ingle (year not printed) | 1.1% of mesial roots; up to 36% elsewhere |
| Paqué (2025) | 1 to 15%; 22% in one study with troughing; 4.4% first molar, 1.3% second molar on CBCT |

| Grossman (2021) | text "rare"; Table 13.12 (partly garbled) 1.0% (Vertucci), 57.0% (Pineda and Kuttler) |

- Detection: troughing the groove (Cohen, 2021), opening the isthmus (Setzer and Walsch, 2025), microscope or CBCT (Paqué, 2025).

### 2. Distal canal variations
| Book | One distal canal | Two distal canals |
|---|---|---|
| Weine (2004) | about 70% | Type II or III about 30% |
| Grossman (2021) | 70% | text: 15% coalescing, 5% two foramina, 8% bifurcating; Table 13.12: 7.0 two foramina |
| Torabinejad et al (2021) | 70.3% | 29.56% |
| Harty (2017) | not given | 38% |
| Setzer and Walsch (2025) | about 60% | about 40% |

- Single distal canal: large, oval or kidney-shaped; trapezoidal access (Weine, 2004).
- Rare: a third distal canal, 0.2 to 3% (Paqué, 2025); a distolingual root (radix entomolaris), see A9.

### 3. Identification of the second distal canal
A single distal canal is well centred in its root; a wide distal root is suspicious of two canals (Weine, 2004). A canal not admitting a size 25 file at first entry suggests Type II or III (Weine, 2004). Locate the distal canal first (Weine, 2004).

### 4. Second molar
One mesial canal in 18.1% (Ingle, year not printed), 12.5% (Torabinejad et al, 2021) or 4.0% in one report (Weine, 2004); different study types.

### 5. Conclusion
The middle mesial canal is given as 1 to 15% (Paqué, 2025) or about 5% (Setzer and Walsch, 2025), and the second distal canal as 38% (Harty, 2017) or about 40% (Setzer and Walsch, 2025); the books differ.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Middle mesial canal: between MB and ML, in the groove; may be only an anastomosis (Cohen, 2021).
- Ingle prints 1.1% of mesial roots and up to 36%; Paqué prints 1 to 15%; Setzer and Walsch print about 5%; Grossman Table 13.12 prints 1.0% and 57.0% (Ingle, year not printed; Paqué, 2025; Setzer and Walsch, 2025; Grossman, 2021).
- CBCT meta-analysis: 4.4% first and 1.3% second molars (Paqué, 2025).
- Distal canal: one canal in about 70% (Weine, 2004); two in 29.56% (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Middle distal canal: 0.2 to 3% (Paqué, 2025).
- Locate the distal canal first, then ML, then MB (Weine, 2004).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Writing one prevalence figure for the middle mesial canal as settled; the books print different figures (Paqué, 2025; Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
2. Forgetting that the middle mesial canal may be only an anastomosis (Cohen, 2021).
3. Giving the distal root as single canal only; two canals occur in about one third or more in the books (Weine, 2004; Ingle, year not printed).
4. Using a triangular access; the books describe a trapezoid, or a rectangle if a second distal canal is present (Weine, 2004; Grossman, 2021).
