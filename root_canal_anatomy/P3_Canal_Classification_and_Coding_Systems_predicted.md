## STUDY MODE

### S1. Why classify [definition]
Weine and Vertucci are the two most used classifications (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### S2. Weine and Vertucci as printed [classification]
- Weine: Type I (1-1), II (2-1), III (2-2), IV (1-2) (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Torabinejad et al say the initial classification had three types from the mesiobuccal root of maxillary first molars and type IV was added later (Torabinejad et al, 2021); Weine's own book lists four (Weine, 2004); Paqué says the original four were expanded to five (Paqué, 2025).
- Vertucci: Types I to VIII, with III as 1-2-1, VI as 2-1-2, VII as 1-2-1-2 and VIII as 3-3 (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Baumann and Beer count eight types (Baumann and Beer, 2010); Weine writes that Vertucci used seven major types (Weine, 2004).
- Torabinejad et al base Vertucci on 200 maxillary second premolars (Torabinejad et al, 2021); the 1984 abstract reports 2400 teeth (Vertucci, 1984, abstract only).

### S3. Extensions of Vertucci [classification]
The books name the extra types differently. Gulabivala and Ng: Gulabivala et al added a number of further groups (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Paqué: Gulabivala et al added nine types in the text, while the figure caption says seven types (Paqué, 2025). Ingle: Sert and Bayirli reported 14 additional types, drawn as Types IX to XXIII (Ingle, year not printed). Sikri (one book): types IX to XV as Gulabivala, XVI and XVII as Sert, XVIII and XIX as Peiris, and XX to XXIII as Al-Qudah and Awandeh (Sikri, 2019).

### S4. Why a new system [reasoning]
Micro-CT has found up to 37 configurations (Paqué, 2025), which neither Weine nor Vertucci describes (Torabinejad et al, 2021). In one study of 20 maxillary first molar mesiobuccal roots (Verma and Love 2011), 40% of roots could not be classified by Weine and 30% by Vertucci (Versiani et al, 2015).

### S5. Ahmed et al coding system [coding]
- Paqué: three components, tooth number (any numbering system, such as FDI), number of roots (superscript) and canal configuration O-C-F from orifice to foramen; aims to be simple, accurate and flexible (Paqué, 2025). Limitation: no overarching class for whole tooth groups (Paqué, 2025).
- Torabinejad et al: root name and canal numbers, with the Universal Tooth Number system (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Sikri: tooth, roots and canals, for example 215B1P2 for a maxillary second premolar with two roots, one buccal and two palatal canals (Sikri, 2019).
- Cohen: three systems cover anomalies, root and canal configurations, and accessory canal morphology (Cohen, 2021); its reference list prints the root and canal system as Ahmed et al, 2016 (Cohen, 2021).
- Year and authors as each source gives them: Sikri, Ahmed and Dummer (2017) (Sikri, 2019); Cohen, Ahmed et al 2016 (Cohen, 2021); Paqué, figure caption Ahmed et al (2024) (Paqué, 2025). Web: a code-based scheme (Ahmed et al, 2017, snippet only).

### S6. Briseno-Marroquin [micro-CT coding]
Briseno-Marroquin et al (2015): 179 maxillary first molars, micro-CT, a coding method proposed (abstract only; four-digit form snippet only); not in the books read.

### 1. Need
Classifications standardise description of canal systems; Weine and Vertucci are the most used (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Weine's code gives canals at the chamber floor, then at the apex; Vertucci's sequence follows the canals along the root, with three or four numbers for types III, VI and VII (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### 2. Classical systems
| System | Types |
|---|---|
| Weine | I (1-1), II (2-1), III (2-2), IV (1-2) (Torabinejad et al, 2021) |
| Vertucci | I 1-1, II 2-1, III 1-2-1, IV 2-2, V 1-2, VI 2-1-2, VII 1-2-1-2, VIII 3-3 (Torabinejad et al, 2021) |
- Books differ: Weine three types then a fourth (Torabinejad et al, 2021) or four (Weine, 2004); Vertucci eight (Baumann and Beer, 2010) or seven major types (Weine, 2004).
- Extensions: Gulabivala et al, nine types in text or seven in caption (Paqué, 2025); Ingle: Sert and Bayirli, 14 types IX to XXIII (Ingle, year not printed); Sikri (one book) splits IX to XXIII among five author groups (Sikri, 2019).

### 3. Why newer systems
Micro-CT found up to 37 configurations that Weine and Vertucci cannot describe (Torabinejad et al, 2021); in one study of 20 maxillary first molar roots, 40% were unclassifiable by Weine, 30% by Vertucci (Versiani et al, 2015).

### 4. Ahmed et al coding
- Three parts: tooth number, number of roots as a superscript, canal configuration orifice-canal-foramen (Paqué, 2025).
- Aims: simple, accurate and flexible, as new variations can be added (Paqué, 2025).
- Sikri (one book) attributes a layout to Ahmed and Dummer (2017): 215B1P2 = maxillary second premolar (15), two roots (215), one buccal and two palatal canals (Sikri, 2019).
- Limit: no overarching class for tooth groups (Paqué, 2025).
- Web: code-based system (Ahmed et al, 2017, snippet only).

### 5. Briseno-Marroquin (2015)
Micro-CT of 179 maxillary first molars, proposing a coding method (Briseno-Marroquin et al, 2015, abstract only); a maxillary, not mandibular, study; not in the books read.

### 6. Ahmed code as printed in three books
| Book | Layout |
|---|---|
| Paqué (2025) | tooth, root superscript, O-C-F |
| Torabinejad et al (2021) | root name, canal numbers, Universal Tooth Number |
| Sikri (2019) | tooth, roots, canals, e.g. 215B1P2 |
