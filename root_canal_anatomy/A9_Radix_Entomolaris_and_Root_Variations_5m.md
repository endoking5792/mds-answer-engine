## STUDY MODE

### S1. Definition [definition]
Radix entomolaris: supernumerary distolingual root of a mandibular molar; radix paramolaris: extra mesiobuccal root (Torabinejad et al, 2021; Cohen, 2021).

### S2. Prevalence, side by side [prevalence]
Torabinejad et al: radix entomolaris 0.2% to 32% (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Grossman: third root, distal or mesial, 5.3% of first molars (Grossman, 2021). Harty: 6% to 44% (Harty, 2017). Paqué: 14.4% (Abella) (Paqué, 2025). Ingle ch 1: three-rooted forms (not radix entomolaris alone) 14.1% (Ingle, year not printed). Ingle ch 26: above 40% in Mongoloid-trait groups, below 5% in Europeans and Africans (Ingle, year not printed). Radix paramolaris: 0%, 0.5%, 2.0% (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### S3. Types [classification, one book]
Carlsen and Alexandersen types A, B, C and AC by the cervical part, and De Moor types I to III by curvature, are printed in Sikri only (Sikri, 2019).

### S4. Clinical features [clinical]
Smaller, lingual, more curved, with an apical hook toward the buccal (one book: Sikri, 2019). It overlaps the DB root on a 2-D radiograph (Ingle, year not printed). Use angled radiographs or CBCT (Torabinejad et al, 2021). The orifice lies disto- to mesiolingually from the main distal canal or canals (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Access becomes trapezoidal (Paqué, 2025).

### 1. Definition
Radix entomolaris: supernumerary distolingual root of a mandibular molar; radix paramolaris: extra mesiobuccal root (Torabinejad et al, 2021; Cohen, 2021).

### 2. Prevalence (as printed)

| Ingle (year not printed) | three-rooted forms 14.1%; above 40% in Mongoloid groups |
| Paqué, 2025 | 14.4% (Abella) |

### 3. Features and diagnosis
- Smaller, curved root, apical hook to the buccal (one book: Sikri, 2019).
- Overlaps the DB root on a 2-D radiograph (Ingle, year not printed).
- Angled radiographs or CBCT (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### 4. Management
- Orifice disto- to mesiolingual to the main distal canal or canals (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Access modified from triangular to trapezoidal (Paqué, 2025).

### 5. Other variations
Radix paramolaris is rarer: 0%, 0.5% and 2.0% in first, second and third molars (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Four-rooted maxillary molars occur in 0.4% to 1.4% (Ingle, year not printed).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Distolingual root of the mandibular molar (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Prevalence differs between sources and populations (Grossman, 2021; Harty, 2017).
- Trapezoidal access (Paqué, 2025).
- Types A, B, C and AC are in Sikri only (Sikri, 2019).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Calling the mesiobuccal extra root a radix entomolaris; Cohen and Torabinejad call it paramolaris; Grossman applies entomolaris to a distal or mesial third root (Cohen, 2021; Torabinejad et al, 2021; Grossman, 2021).
- Quoting one prevalence figure for all populations (Ingle, year not printed).
- Missing it because it overlaps the DB root radiographically (Ingle, year not printed).
