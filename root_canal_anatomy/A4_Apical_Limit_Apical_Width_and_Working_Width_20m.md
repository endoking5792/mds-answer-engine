## STUDY MODE

### S1. Terms [definitions]
Working length is the distance from a coronal reference point to where preparation and obturation should terminate (Grossman, 2021). Sikri defines working width as the horizontal dimensions of the root canal system at the apical constriction and other levels (Sikri, 2019). Weine: the master apical file is three sizes larger than the initial apical file that first binds (Weine, 2004).

### S2. Landmark positions
Landmark positions are set out in A3.

### S3. Where to end [side by side, no ranking]
Cohen: vital cases 2 to 3 mm short of the radiographic apex; success was better at or within 2 mm and fell by 20% when short of 2 mm or past the apex; the most favourable prognosis was at the AC; some studies support termination at or beyond the apex (chapter 7). Necrotic cases 0.5 to 1 mm short and irreversible pulpitis 1 to 2 mm short, with no definitive validation although follow-up studies seem to support it (chapter 8). The 1 mm guideline remains rational with radiographs, but the termination point remains empirical (chapter 9) (Cohen, 2021). Ingle: Ricucci and Langeland say the limit should be the apical constriction, not the radiographic terminus, CDJ or 1 mm short; others cited by Ingle say the 1 mm rule should not be accepted (Ingle, year not printed). Grossman: 0.5 to 1.0 mm short; Weine's modification 1.5 mm with bone loss, 2 mm with root resorption (Grossman, 2021). Walton and Torabinejad: 1 mm short, 1.5 mm with bone resorption, 2 mm with bone and root resorption (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002). Sikri: the most clinically relevant landmark is the apical constriction (Sikri, 2019). Gulabivala and Ng: 1 mm short is a widely accepted radiographic estimate of where the canal ends, inaccurate by up to 3 mm; files are set 0.25 mm short with good control, up to 1 mm short otherwise (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Torabinejad et al: just short of the electronically measured length (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Harty: at the constriction (Harty, 2017). Weine: at the minor diameter, building an apical dentin matrix (Weine, 2004). Barnett: short of the foramen in vital pulps, closer to the radiographic apex in necrotic pulps with apical periodontitis; each millimetre lost raised failure odds by about 14% and 12% (Barnett, 2017). Peters and Arias: infected systems healed best at 0 to 0.5 mm from the radiographic tip (Peters and Arias, 2025).

### S4. Apex locators [cross-reference]
Ingle: locators indicate the end of the canal (the foramen), not the CDJ or constriction (Ingle, year not printed). Sikri: they locate the apical constriction or vital periodontal tissue (Sikri, 2019). Barnett: readings were within 0.5 mm of the minor foramen 100% of the time (Barnett, 2017).

### S5. Apical width [definition and size by book]
Sikri: the first binding file does not accurately reflect the apical diameter, and enlargement depends on the amount of necrotic dentin, with no fixed size (Sikri, 2019). Weine: each canal's optimal enlargement is calculated separately (Weine, 2004). Ingle: traditionally at least three sizes beyond the first binding file, ISO 25 to 35 or larger recommended (Ingle, year not printed). Cohen: final size remains much disputed (Cohen, 2021). Grossman: width cannot be standardised, and the three-sizes rule is no longer recommended (Grossman, 2021). Gulabivala and Ng: optimal apical size 30, but 20 or 25 may suffice with a wide 0.06 plus taper (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Barnett: in molars the constriction corresponded to size 30 (Barnett, 2017).

### S6. Critical evidence note [one claim: a larger apical preparation improves healing]
Ingle: larger apical sizes seem to give increased success in necrotic teeth with periapical lesions, and master files below size 35 seem linked to longer healing; it also reports no difference in bacterial reduction between final sizes 35 and 20, or in debris between sizes 25 and 40 (Ingle, year not printed). Cohen: size 20 left more apical debris than size 40, but size 25 versus 40 and enlargement to 35 versus none showed no difference in bacteria (Cohen, 2021). Sikri: a minimal preparation reduces errors, a larger one aids bacterial elimination (Sikri, 2019).

### 1. Definitions
Working length is the distance from a coronal reference point to where preparation and obturation should end (Grossman, 2021). Landmark terms are as in A3.

### 2. Apical limit as printed (no author preferred)
| Author, edition | Where to end |
|---|---|
| Cohen, 2021 | AC; vital 2 to 3 mm short; best at or within 2 mm; necrotic 0.5 to 1 mm, irreversible pulpitis 1 to 2 mm (no definitive validation); 1 mm rational on radiographs, point empirical |
| Ingle, year not printed | AC, not radiographic terminus, CDJ or 1 mm short (Ricucci and Langeland); others cited by Ingle: 1 mm rule not accepted |
| Grossman, 2021 | 0.5 to 1.0 mm short of radiographic apex; 1.5 mm with bone loss, 2 mm with root resorption (Weine) |

| Gulabivala and Ng, 2014 | files 0.25 mm short with good control, up to 1 mm otherwise; 1 mm short is a radiographic estimate of canal end, up to 3 mm error |
| Peters and Arias, 2025 | infected systems: 0 to 0.5 mm from radiographic tip |

| Torabinejad et al, 2021 | just short of electronic length |
| Harty, 2017 | at the constriction |
| Barnett, 2017 | vital: short of foramen; necrotic with apical periodontitis: closer to radiographic apex |

Sikri: if working length is 1.0 mm short, many lateral canals may remain unsealed; a filling that reaches the radiographic apex is in fact extruding; the constriction gives an apical dentinal matrix but is hard to recognise (Sikri, 2019).

Reasons given: the constriction is a natural barrier, and going beyond it can cause periapical inflammation (Sikri, 2019). Apex locators: Ingle says they indicate the foramen, not the CDJ or constriction (Ingle, year not printed); Sikri says they locate the constriction or vital periodontal tissue (Sikri, 2019). Barnett reports that each millimetre lost from the radiographic apex raised the odds of failure by about 14% and 12% in two studies (Barnett, 2017).

### 3. Working-length methods (note; cross-reference)
Gulabivala and Ng list radiography, electronic apex locators, tactile sense and paper points (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Weine lists four major methods (Weine, 2004). Details are in the separate working-length answer.

### 4. Apical width and working width
Sikri defines working width as the horizontal dimensions of the canal at the apical constriction and other levels (Sikri, 2019). Apical size by book:
| Author, edition | Statement |
|---|---|
| Weine, 2004 | master file three sizes larger than initial file; each canal calculated separately |
| Ingle, year not printed | traditionally at least three sizes beyond first binding file; ISO 25 to 35 or higher recommended; larger sizes seem to raise success |
| Cohen, 2021 | final size much disputed |
| Grossman, 2021 | cannot be standardised; three-sizes rule not recommended |
| Gulabivala and Ng, 2014 | optimal size 30; 20 or 25 may suffice with a 0.06 plus taper |
| Sikri, 2019 | keep minimum, or larger for irrigant penetration; enlarge by necrotic dentin, no fixed size |

Sikri lists canal shape, length, taper, curvature and obstructions as factors in working width (Sikri, 2019). Baumann and Beer, citing Kerekes and Tronstad, note considerable variation in canal width even 1 mm from the tip (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

### 5. Significance of width
Sikri: overpreparation may unnecessarily remove sound tooth structure (Sikri, 2019). Grossman: overzealous preparation causes weakening, perforation and spatial movement of the foramen (Grossman, 2021). Weine: the far walls (poles) of oval canals may be completely untouched (Weine, 2004). Walton and Torabinejad: non-round canals cannot be enlarged to a round shape without perforating or weakening the root (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

### 6. Critical point
Ingle: larger apical sizes seem to raise success in necrotic teeth with periapical lesions, yet also reports no difference in bacterial reduction or in debris between final sizes (Ingle, year not printed). Cohen: size 20 left more apical debris than size 40, but no difference in bacterial growth between sizes 25 and 40 (Cohen, 2021).

### 7. Conclusion
Depending on the book, the limit is the constriction or lies 0 to 3 mm short of the radiographic apex, and apical sizes differ (Cohen, 2021; Peters and Arias, 2025; Grossman, 2021; Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Working length: distance from reference point to where preparation ends (Grossman, 2021).
- Working width: horizontal dimension of the canal; defined in one book (Sikri, 2019).
- 1 mm short of the apex: Cohen calls it rational on radiographs (Cohen, 2021); others cited by Ingle say it should not be accepted (Ingle, year not printed).
- Reduce length with bone or root resorption (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).
- Each canal has its own optimal size (Weine, 2004).
