## STUDY MODE

### S1. Why 2D films are not enough [definition]
Superimposition, distortion and the lack of cross-sectional views limit periapical films in molars (Spin-Neto et al, 2025). Changing the horizontal angulation by 20 to 40 degrees detected root number accurately in molars, yet 2D imaging often fails to give accurate information on canal anatomy compared with CBCT (Barnett, 2017).

### S2. Principle [mechanism]
CBCT uses a cone-shaped beam from a point source and a planar digital sensor, which rotate around a stationary patient (Lam, 2012). Anatomy is rebuilt as voxels and reviewed in axial, coronal and sagittal planes (Lam, 2012). Small field is under 8 cm; small-field pixels are as low as 0.076 mm, large-field 0.20 to 0.40 mm (Lam, 2012). For endodontic tasks Lam says smaller field, higher resolution machines "may be of greater value" (Lam, 2012), while Boveda says small or limited field units are preferred, with resolution under 200 micrometres (Boveda, 2012). Baumann and Beer print digital volume tomography at 125 to 300 micrometres, depending on the image area (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

### S3. What CBCT shows of canal anatomy [uses]
- Number, position, size, extent and curvature of roots and canals, and canal shape at any level (Boveda, 2012). Accessory canals, isthmuses and C-shaped canals missed on 2D images (Spin-Neto et al, 2025). Missed canals, additional roots and dilacerations (Ingle, year not printed).
- In one study cited in Boveda (Micheti et al, 2010), small-field reconstructions correlated strongly to very strongly with histological sections (Boveda, 2012). CBCT is rapidly becoming the "gold standard" in clinical anatomy studies (Ingle, year not printed), while micro-CT is called the most important and accurate research tool for root canal anatomy (Versiani et al, 2015). Against micro-CT as reference, CBCT detected a missed MB2 with sensitivity 96%, specificity 100% and accuracy 98% in one study (Barnett, 2017). Digital radiographs failed to find at least one canal in 40% of teeth compared with CBCT (Barnett, 2017).
- A middle mesial canal was found in 4.4% of first and 1.3% of second mandibular molars in a CBCT meta-analysis (Paqué, 2025).
- CBCT allows in vivo studies of the full dentition of large patient samples (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### S4. Limits [limitations]
Artefacts can hide accessory canals; only hard tissue is seen; false-positive periapical diagnoses are reported (Spin-Neto et al, 2025). In one study MB2 detection rose from 60.1% at a 0.4-mm voxel size to 93.3% at 0.125 mm (Ingle, year not printed).
Dose, as each book prints it: CBCT presents higher radiation doses than radiographs, and high-resolution protocols can raise exposure up to 15-fold (Spin-Neto et al, 2025). Lam prints effective doses of 5.3 to 38.3 microSv for small-volume CBCT, 74 to 1073 for large-field CBCT, and 388 for a full-mouth intraoral series with D-speed film and round collimation (Lam, 2012). Baumann and Beer say some digital volume tomography images use doses similar to panoramic radiographs (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

### S5. Versus micro-CT and when to use [comparison]
Micro-CT cannot be used in vivo, despite being called the most important and accurate research tool for root canal anatomy (Versiani et al, 2015). CBCT is used when 2D fails or findings are contradictory (Spin-Neto et al, 2025); Harty states it is not a standard assessment (Harty, 2017).

## EXAM MODE

### 1. Need
Periapical films suffer superimposition, distortion and no cross-sectional view (Spin-Neto et al, 2025); angled films detected root number, but 2D imaging often fails on canal anatomy compared with CBCT (Barnett, 2017).

### 2. Principle
Cone-shaped beam, point source and flat sensor rotating round the patient; data in voxels, viewed in axial, coronal and sagittal planes (Lam, 2012). Small field (under 8 cm): "may be of greater value" (Lam, 2012); "preferred" for endodontics (Boveda, 2012).

### 3. Uses in canal anatomy
- Number, position, size, extent and curvature of roots and canals; canal shape at any level (Boveda, 2012).
- Accessory canals, isthmuses, C-shaped canals, complex curvatures (Spin-Neto et al, 2025).
- Extra canals: MB2 sensitivity 96%, specificity 100% against micro-CT (Barnett, 2017); middle mesial canal 4.4% (first molar) and 1.3% (second molar) (Paqué, 2025).
- Epidemiology: in vivo studies of large populations (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### 4. Comparison
| Method | Point |
|---|---|
| Periapical film | superimposed, 2D (Spin-Neto et al, 2025) |
| CBCT | in vivo, 3D; pixels 0.076 mm small field (Lam, 2012) |
| Micro-CT | ex vivo only; "most important and accurate research tool" (Versiani et al, 2015) |

### 5. Limitations
Artefacts, hard tissue only, false-positive periapical diagnoses; dose higher than radiographs (Spin-Neto et al, 2025). Lam prints 5.3 to 38.3 microSv small-volume, 74 to 1073 large-field, 388 full-mouth intraoral series with D-speed film (Lam, 2012). MB2 detection rose from 60.1% at a 0.4-mm voxel to 93.3% at 0.125 mm in one study (Ingle, year not printed).

### 6. Selection and resolution
Use when 2D is inconclusive (Spin-Neto et al, 2025); Harty says it is not a standard assessment, and each case needs justification (Harty, 2017). Resolution is printed differently: under 200 micrometres (Boveda, 2012), 125 to 300 micrometres for digital volume tomography (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

### 7. Conclusion
CBCT shows canal anatomy in three planes in vivo, but its use must be justified because of dose and artefacts (Spin-Neto et al, 2025).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Cone beam: first described for dentistry in 1998; small field under 8 cm (Lam, 2012).
- Small-field pixel 0.076 mm; large-field 0.20 to 0.40 mm (Lam, 2012).
- MB2 on CBCT: sensitivity 96%, specificity 100%, accuracy 98% (Barnett, 2017).
- Middle mesial canal on CBCT: 4.4% and 1.3% (Paqué, 2025).
- Micro-CT is not for living patients (Versiani et al, 2015).
- Main CBCT limitation is artefacts (Spin-Neto et al, 2025).
