## STUDY MODE

### S1. Definition and cause [definition]
A continuous slit connects some or all canals (Ingle, year not printed); the horizontal section looks like the letter C (Weine, 2004). Cause: Hertwig's sheath fails to fuse on the buccal or lingual root surface (Ingle, year not printed; Cohen, 2021). Cooke and Cox, 1979 (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### S2. Site and prevalence, side by side [prevalence]
- Most often the mandibular second molar (Cohen, 2021); also first molars, premolars and maxillary molars (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- Ingle: 2.7% to 44.5%, higher in Asian groups (Ingle, year not printed). Torabinejad et al: Saudi Arabians 10.6%, Lebanese 19.14%, Chinese 31.5%, Koreans 44.5% (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Cooke and Cox 8%, confirmed by Weine in 811 teeth (Weine, 2004; Baumann and Beer, 2010). Baumann and Beer: Chinese populations 33% to 52% (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- Paqué: about 14% to 50% in Asian mandibular second molars (Paqué, 2025).
- Separate item, root fusion: Harty, up to 52% in Chinese mandibular second molars (Harty, 2017).

### S3. Classification [classification]
- Melton: category I continuous C, II semicolon, III two or more discrete canals; Fan et al add IV, one round or oval canal, and V, no lumen near the apex (Ingle, year not printed).
- Three-dimensional types: merging, symmetrical, asymmetrical (Cohen, 2021; Grossman, 2021).

### S4. Diagnosis [diagnosis]
A file may appear to perforate the furcation; Simon: conical root like one root with a furcation (Ingle, year not printed). The apex locator does not fire (Coil, 2012). The floor shows a C-shaped trough (Weine, 2004) or an arc of 180 degrees or more (Ingle, year not printed). Closure of the C is more commonly lingual (Weine, 2004). Orifices poorly predict canal form along the root (Cohen, 2021).

### S5. Management, side by side [management]
- Pre-curved file, microscope; ultrasonics supplement (Ingle, year not printed). Instrument main canals first (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
- Circumferential filing, 3% to 6% NaOCl; avoid overpreparation (Gutmann, 2011). Groove and thin isthmi are prone to perforation (Ingle, year not printed). Instrumentation leaves canal area untouched (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Obturation: thermoplasticised gutta-percha preferred over cold lateral condensation (Ingle, year not printed); cold, warm or thermoplasticised gutta-percha, core-carriers effective (Gutmann, 2011); no canal appears well condensed despite deep spreader penetration (Weine, 2004).
- Prognosis: equal to other teeth (Ingle, year not printed); questionable, consider referral (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); for such fused-root teeth, depends on root surface in contact with bone (Cohen, 2021).

### 1. Definition and cause
Continuous slit joining some or all canals; section is a letter C (Ingle, year not printed; Weine, 2004). Cause: failure of Hertwig's sheath to fuse on the buccal or lingual surface (Cohen, 2021). First reported by Cooke and Cox, 1979 (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### 2. Site and prevalence (as printed)
| Source | Figure |
|---|---|
| Ingle (year not printed) | 2.7% to 44.5%, higher in Asians |
| Torabinejad et al, 2021 | Saudi 10.6%, Lebanese 19.14%, Chinese 31.5%, Korean 44.5% |
| Weine, 2004 | 8% (Cooke and Cox) |
| Baumann and Beer, 2010 | Chinese populations 33% to 52% |
| Paqué, 2025 | about 14% to 50% in Asian mandibular second molars |
Most often in the mandibular second molar, also other teeth (Cohen, 2021; Baumann and Beer, 2010).

### 3. Classification
| Melton (Ingle, year not printed) | Feature |
|---|---|
| I | continuous C |
| II | semicolon |
| III | two or more discrete canals |
| Fan et al IV, V | single round canal; no lumen near the apex |
Three-dimensional: merging, symmetrical, asymmetrical (Cohen, 2021; Grossman, 2021).

### 4. Diagnosis
- Radiograph: file seems to perforate the furcation (Ingle, year not printed).
- Floor: C-shaped trough or 180 degree arc (Weine, 2004; Ingle, year not printed).
- CBCT may detect it (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### 5. Management
1. Microscope, small pre-curved file (Ingle, year not printed).
2. Main canals first, then the isthmus with ultrasonics (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
3. Circumferential filing, 3% to 6% NaOCl; avoid overpreparation (Gutmann, 2011).
4. Obturation: thermoplasticised gutta-percha preferred over cold lateral condensation (Ingle, year not printed); cold, warm or thermoplasticised gutta-percha (Gutmann, 2011).
5. Prognosis: Ingle, equal; Walton and Torabinejad, questionable; Cohen, for such fused-root teeth depends on root surface in bone.

### 6. Conclusion
C-shaped canals occur most often in the mandibular second molar; the orifices do not predict the canal form, so they need floor recognition, copious irrigation and careful instrumentation that avoids overpreparation (Cohen, 2021; Gutmann, 2011).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Melton I, II, III; Fan IV, V (Ingle, year not printed).
- Merging, symmetrical, asymmetrical types (Cohen, 2021).
- Mandibular second molar; Asian groups (Cohen, 2021; Ingle, year not printed).
- Cooke and Cox, 1979 (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Avoid overpreparation (Gutmann, 2011).
