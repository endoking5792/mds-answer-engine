## STUDY MODE

### S1. Landmarks [definitions]
The apical foramen is an aperture at or near the apex of a root through which pulp vessels and nerves pass (Grossman, 2021). Three landmarks: apical constriction (AC), cementodentinal junction (CDJ) and apical foramen (AF) (Cohen, 2021; Sikri, 2019). The AAE Glossary defines the apical constriction as the part of the canal with the narrowest diameter (AAE, 2012).

### S2. Structure [shape]
Weine, citing Kuttler: major diameter about twice the minor; minor-to-major distance 0.524 mm at 18 to 25 years, 0.659 mm at 55 and older (Weine, 2004).

### S3. Constriction position [side by side, no ranking]
Cohen: AC generally 0.5 to 1.5 mm coronal to the AF in chapter 7; Kuttler, cited in chapter 9, average foramen-to-constriction distance 0.5 mm (Cohen, 2021). Sikri: 0.5 to 1.5 mm short of the foramen (Sikri, 2019). Torabinejad et al: 0.5 to 1.5 mm from the foramen centre (Torabinejad et al, 2021). AAE Glossary: usually 0.5 to 1.0 mm (AAE, 2012). Harty: 1.0 to 1.5 mm from the foramen on one page, about 0.5 to 1.0 mm on the next (Harty, 2017). Gulabivala and Ng: 0 to 1.5 mm (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Baumann and Beer, quoting Kuttler: about 1 mm proximal to the foramen (Baumann and Beer, 2010). Ingle: up to 1 mm or more from the foramen (chapter 19), as far as 3.8 mm from the anatomic apex (chapter 22) (Ingle, year not printed). Grossman: 0.5 to 1.0 mm from the root apex; in chapter 1 the minor diameter lies on average 0.5 mm (young) or 0.75 mm (mature teeth) from the cemental surface (Grossman, 2021). Barnett: micro-CT of molars found the AC about 0.2 mm from the foramen (Barnett, 2017).

### S4. Foramen position [side by side, no ranking]
Cohen: offset 0.5 to 3 mm, coinciding with the apical vertex in 17% to 46%; Kuttler, cited in chapter 9, up to 2.5 mm (Cohen, 2021). Grossman: 17% to 46%, average 0.4 to 0.7 mm away, in a few cases 2 to 3 mm (Grossman, 2021). Torabinejad et al: 6.7% to 46%, 0.2 to 3.8 mm from the apex (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Sikri: 0.2 to 3.8 mm short of the apex, buccal or lingual deviation twice as often as mesial or distal (chapter 15); mostly 0.5 to 1.0 mm offset from the centre, mean major-to-minor distance 0.5 mm (young) and 0.65 mm (older teeth) (chapter 13) (Sikri, 2019). Harty: 0.2 to 2.0 mm (Harty, 2017). Gulabivala and Ng: 0 to 3 mm (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Walton and Torabinejad: about 0.5 mm, seldom over 1.0 mm (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002). Weine: 1 to 3 mm (Weine, 2004). Barnett: not at the anatomic apex in about 60 to 80% of teeth (Barnett, 2017). Ingle, citing Mizutani et al: coincidence in about one in six maxillary central incisors, 6.7% of lateral incisors and 16.7% of canines; citing Green: 0.3 mm from the apex in maxillary central incisors (Ingle, year not printed).

### S5. CDJ [side by side, no ranking]
Ingle: Grove (1929) held that obturation must stop at the CDJ, the maximal apical constriction, while Coolidge (1929) held the site too variable; chapter 19 calls it often the narrowest point forming the constriction (Ingle, year not printed). Cohen: generally not in the same area as the AC, about 1 mm from the AF (Cohen, 2021). Sikri: accepted clinically to almost coincide with the AC, 1.0 mm short of the foramen (chapter 13), but highly irregular and not coinciding with it (chapter 15) (Sikri, 2019). Grossman: does not always coincide with the constriction (Grossman, 2021). Harty: most endodontists place the constriction in the region of the dentine-cementum junction; one page earlier it lies within dentine just before the first cementum (Harty, 2017). Walton and Torabinejad: the idea that the CDJ forms the constriction is incorrect (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

### S6. Variations and apical third [features]
Dummer constriction shapes are listed in section 4 of Exam Mode (Sikri, 2019; Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Apical delta is a morphology in which the main canal divides into multiple accessory canals at or near the apex (AAE, 2012). Torabinejad et al: ramification in 1% to 15.1% of maxillary and 5% to 14% of mandibular teeth; 1 to 16 foramina per root, citing a previous study; nearly all canals curved in the apical third (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Cohen lists apical-third accessory canals, resorption, pulp stones and reparative dentin (Cohen, 2021).

### S7. Significance [clinical]
Instrumentation beyond the junction can cause periapical inflammation (Sikri, 2019). Ingle lists that only major apical foramina can be treated with the patency technique (Ingle, year not printed). In the mesial roots of molars the canal lies nearer the furcal wall (danger zone) than the outer wall (Torabinejad et al, 2021; one book).

### S8. Critical evidence note [one claim: a constriction is present]
Baumann and Beer: present in only about 50% of teeth (Baumann and Beer, 2010). Barnett: identified in less than 50% of teeth in one study, but in all canals on micro-CT of molars (Barnett, 2017). Walton and Torabinejad: presence unpredictable (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002). Gulabivala and Ng: not all teeth have a constriction, because of apical resorption caused by apical periodontitis (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). A different claim: Cohen reports classic Kuttler anatomy in 46% of teeth in one study (Cohen, 2021).
## EXAM MODE

### 1. Definition and landmarks
The apical foramen is an opening at or near the root apex through which pulp vessels and nerves pass (Grossman, 2021). Landmarks: apical constriction, cementodentinal junction (CDJ), apical foramen (Cohen, 2021; Sikri, 2019).

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anatomic apex | tip of the root, morphologic (AAE, 2012) |
| Radiographic apex | tip as seen on the radiograph; may differ from the anatomic apex (AAE, 2012) |
| Apical foramen (major diameter) | main opening of the canal (Grossman, 2021) |
| Apical constriction (minor diameter) | narrowest part of the canal (AAE, 2012) |
| CDJ | where pulp ends and periodontal tissue begins (Torabinejad et al, 2021) |

### 2. Structure
The canal tapers to the constriction, then widens to the foramen in a funnel or morning-glory shape (Cohen, 2021; Weine, 2004). The major diameter is about twice the minor (Weine, 2004). The minor diameter faces the pulp (Grossman, 2021).

A micro-CT study of molars found it 0.2 mm from the foramen (Barnett, 2017). Sikri says the CDJ may be up to 3.0 mm higher on one wall than the opposite wall (Sikri, 2019).

### 4. Features
- Dummer constriction shapes: Sikri prints five (typical single, tapering, multiconstricted, long parallel, blocked); Gulabivala and Ng print four (traditional single, tapering, parallel, multiconstricted) (Sikri, 2019; Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).
- Apical delta: main canal divides into multiple accessory canals at or near the apex (AAE, 2012); frequency 1 to 15.1% maxillary, 5 to 14% mandibular; foramina per root 1 to 16, citing a previous study (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Apical third complexities: accessory canals, resorption, pulp stones, reparative dentin, varying diameters (Cohen, 2021); nearly all canals curved (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Shape: apical third round or slightly oval (Torabinejad et al, 2021); long oval or ribbon-shaped (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).
- Age: cementum deposition narrows the minor and widens the major diameter, and deviates the foramen (Grossman, 2021); the apex curves more distally with time (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

### 5. Significance in treatment
1. Apical limit: the constriction is the natural barrier; going beyond it can cause periapical inflammation (Sikri, 2019). The limit itself is in A4.
2. Radiograph: files flush with the radiographic tip may pass the foramen (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).
3. Patency: only major apical foramina can be treated with the patency technique (Ingle, year not printed).
4. Apical dentin matrix: preparation ending at the minor diameter gives an apical dentin matrix for packing gutta-percha (Weine, 2004).
5. Shape: oval or ribbon canals are not made round without weakening (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).
6. Danger zone: in the mesial roots of molars the furcal wall is nearer the canal than the bulky outer wall (safety zone), and the furcal region at the curvature is a common site of stripping perforation (Torabinejad et al, 2021; one book).
7. Apical delta: several foramina may exist and a file enters only one (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014); aberrations of the apical canal are neither well debrided nor obturated (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

### 6. Critical point
Whether a constriction exists is disputed: about 50% of teeth (Baumann and Beer, 2010), under 50% in some studies but all molar canals on micro-CT (Barnett, 2017), unpredictable (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002), absent in some teeth with apical resorption (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).

### 7. Conclusion
The apex is an inconsistent region whose landmarks are placed differently by each book (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Three landmarks: constriction, CDJ, foramen (Cohen, 2021; Sikri, 2019).
- Foramen coincides with the anatomic apex in about 17 to 46% (Cohen, 2021; Grossman, 2021); 6.7 to 46% (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Major diameter about twice the minor (Weine, 2004).
- Constriction is minor diameter, foramen is major diameter (Grossman, 2021).
- Constriction may be absent (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Barnett, 2017).
- Cementum deposition with age moves the foramen away from the apex (Grossman, 2021).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Giving one distance for the constriction as if every book agreed; the books differ (Cohen, 2021; Harty, 2017; Grossman, 2021).
2. Stating that the CDJ is, or is not, the constriction as if books agreed: Walton and Torabinejad call it incorrect (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) and Cohen says generally not the same area (Cohen, 2021), while Sikri accepts near-coincidence clinically (Sikri, 2019) and Harty places the constriction in the region of the CDJ (Harty, 2017).
3. Saying the foramen is at the apex; it coincides in a minority (Grossman, 2021).
4. Writing significance as "working length" only and omitting delta, shape and danger zone (Ingle, year not printed; Torabinejad et al, 2021).
