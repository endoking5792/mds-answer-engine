## STUDY MODE

### S1. Definition and parts [definition]
The pulp cavity has the pulp chamber, in or just below the crown, and the root canal(s) in the root (Cohen, 2021). The canal ends at a foramen at or within 3 mm of the centre of the apex (Cohen, 2021).

### S2. Laws of pulp chamber anatomy [laws]
Krasner and Rankow studied 500 pulp chambers (Grossman, 2021) and found the CEJ the most important landmark (Cohen, 2021). Grossman, Torabinejad et al, Harty (Table 4-1) and Ingle ch 20 print nine laws: centrality, concentricity, CEJ, symmetry 1 and 2, colour change, orifice location 1 to 3; symmetry 1 and 2 exclude maxillary molars (Grossman, 2021; Torabinejad et al, 2021; Harty, 2017; Ingle, year not printed). Wording differs: the floor is darker than "the walls" in Grossman and Ingle, but darker than "the roof and walls", "invariably", in Harty (Grossman, 2021; Ingle, year not printed; Harty, 2017). Sikri prints a shorter set (Sikri, 2019); one book. Johnson prints three rules (Johnson, 2025); one book.

### S3. Anterior teeth [tooth by tooth]
- Maxillary central incisor: three horns correspond to the mamelons in a young tooth (Grossman, 2021); one canal in 99.2% (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Maxillary lateral incisor: two or no horns (Cohen, 2021).
- Maxillary canine: pulp horns one in Grossman and Harty, none in Cohen and Sikri (Grossman, 2021; Harty, 2017; Cohen, 2021; Sikri, 2019).
- Mandibular incisors: a lingual shoulder may conceal the orifice of a second canal, if present (Cohen, 2021). Two canals: Ingle ch 1 18.8% central, 24.6% lateral (Ingle, year not printed); Ingle ch 20 about 40% (Ingle, year not printed); Torabinejad et al 14.4% central, 20.2% lateral (Torabinejad et al, 2021); Gulabivala and Ng over 40% (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014); Baumann and Beer text 25% to 41.4% (Baumann and Beer, 2010), figure caption about 20% of all mandibular teeth (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

### S4. Premolars [tooth by tooth]
- Maxillary first: two canals regardless of root number (Cohen, 2021). Two canals 77.3% in Torabinejad et al (Torabinejad et al, 2021); "mostly two canals (80%)" in Sikri (Sikri, 2019); two separate canals 69% in Baumann and Beer (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- Maxillary second: one to three canals (Cohen, 2021). Single canal 51.8% in Ingle (Ingle, year not printed); 42.7% in Torabinejad et al (Torabinejad et al, 2021); type I 48% in Baumann and Beer (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- Mandibular first: transitional tooth (Grossman, 2021). Single canal 77.3% in Ingle (Ingle, year not printed); 71.3% in Torabinejad et al (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Mandibular second: lingual horn more prominent (Grossman, 2021). Single canal 91.3% in Ingle (Ingle, year not printed); 84.7% in Torabinejad et al (Torabinejad et al, 2021). Grossman's text gives one canal at one foramen 97.5%, the same as Vertucci's row in its Table 13.11, where four investigators range from 87.9 to 98.8 (Grossman, 2021).

### S5. Maxillary molars [tooth by tooth]
- First molar: largest chamber in the arch, four horns (Grossman, 2021). Floor: triangular in Grossman (Grossman, 2021); a quadrilateral with four unequal sides in Weine (Weine, 2004); triangular with three canals and rhomboidal with four or more in Sikri (Sikri, 2019); chamber outline rhomboid in Cohen (Cohen, 2021). Weine's quadrilateral with rounded corners is a separate statement, his access outline (Weine, 2004).
- The three orifices form the molar triangle (Cohen, 2021).
- Second molar: floor an obtuse triangle (Grossman, 2021); chamber usually triangular (Sikri, 2019).

### S6. Mandibular molars and pulpal floor morphology [tooth by tooth; floor]
- First molar: roof often rectangular, floor rhomboidal, four horns; three orifices MB, ML, distal (Grossman, 2021). Harty says the chamber may have five horns (Harty, 2017); one book each.
- A groove connects MB and ML orifices (Grossman, 2021); mesial orifices are usually well separated (Cohen, 2021).
- Danger zone: the distal wall of the mesial root is thin (Paqué, 2025).
- Floor in general: darker than the walls, with lighter grooves joining orifices (Cohen, 2021); developmental lines connect all orifices (Setzer and Walsch, 2025). Cohen's figure caption lists floor types of C-shaped mandibular second molars: bell-shaped, C-shaped, rounded mass, floor with two holes (Cohen, 2021); one book, figure caption only.

### S7. Average length of the maxillary central incisor [disagreement]
Ingle and Grossman 23.5 mm; Cohen 22.5 mm; Harty 23 mm; Sikri 23.3 mm; Torabinejad et al 23.6 mm (Ingle, year not printed; Grossman, 2021; Cohen, 2021; Harty, 2017; Sikri, 2019; Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### S8. Significance in endodontics [significance]
- Lack of anatomy knowledge ranks second only to errors in diagnosis and treatment planning as a cause of failure (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); missed canals could be the major cause of post-treatment disease (Barnett, 2017).
- Missed canals, isthmuses and lateral canals leave bacteria (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
- Periapical films are somewhat overrated for anatomy (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); it is prudent to assume a root may contain more than one canal system until proved otherwise (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### S9. Critical evidence: the laws of chamber anatomy [evidence]
The laws rest on a study of 500 pulp chambers (Grossman, 2021); Johnson describes them as derived from sectioning and observing 500 extracted teeth (Johnson, 2025). Barnett reports they occurred in 95% of the teeth examined (Barnett, 2017). The sets differ between books (see S2).

## EXAM MODE

### 1. Definition and parts
The pulp space is the cavity enclosed by dentin except at the apical foramen. It has the pulp chamber in the crown and the root canal(s) in the root (Grossman, 2021; Cohen, 2021). Features: pulp horns, canal orifices, accessory, lateral and furcation canals, isthmus, fins, apical delta, apical foramen (Cohen, 2021). The canal ends at a foramen within 3 mm of the apex centre (Cohen, 2021). Outline follows the external tooth form (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### 2. Laws of pulp chamber anatomy (Krasner and Rankow, 500 teeth)
| Law | Statement |
|---|---|
| Centrality | Floor lies in the centre of the tooth at CEJ level |
| Concentricity | Chamber walls are concentric to the outer surface at the CEJ |
| CEJ | Distance from outer crown surface to chamber wall is the same all round at the CEJ; CEJ is the most consistent landmark |
| Symmetry 1 | Except maxillary molars, orifices are equidistant from a mesiodistal line through the floor |
| Symmetry 2 | Except maxillary molars, orifices lie on a line perpendicular to that line |
| Colour change | Floor is darker than the walls (Grossman, Ingle) or than the roof and walls (Harty) |
| Orifice location 1, 2, 3 | Orifices lie at the wall-floor junction; at the angles of that junction; at the ends of the root fusion lines |

(Grossman, 2021; Torabinejad et al, 2021; Ingle, year not printed; Harty, 2017). Sikri prints a shorter set (Sikri, 2019).

### 3. Anterior and premolar teeth
| Tooth | Chamber and canals | Point |
|---|---|---|

| Maxillary lateral incisor | 2 or no horns (Cohen, 2021); tip curves distally or lingually (Ingle, year not printed) | Among the most variable teeth (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) |
| Maxillary canine | Longest tooth, large chamber (Grossman, 2021); horns: one in Grossman and Harty, none in Cohen and Sikri | Canal oval until the apical third (Harty, 2017) |

### 4. Maxillary molars
First molar: largest chamber in the arch, four horns (MB, DB, mesiopalatal, distopalatal), rhomboidal roof (Grossman, 2021). Palatal orifice largest; MB under the MB cusp; DB slightly distal and palatal to MB (Grossman, 2021). The three orifices form the molar triangle (Cohen, 2021). Floor: triangular (Grossman, 2021); quadrilateral with four unequal sides (Weine, 2004); chamber outline rhomboid (Cohen, 2021). Palatal root curves buccally in the apical third (Cohen, 2021). Second molar: roots closer, sometimes fused (Cohen, 2021); floor an obtuse triangle (Grossman, 2021); chamber usually triangular (Sikri, 2019). Third molar: highly variable (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).

### 5. Mandibular molars
First molar: roof often rectangular, floor rhomboidal, four horns (Grossman, 2021). Three orifices, MB, ML and distal; a groove joins MB and ML (Grossman, 2021). A single distal orifice is oval buccolingually (Cohen, 2021). The distal wall of the mesial root is thin, the danger zone (Paqué, 2025); the furcal side of both roots is concave (Ingle, year not printed). Horns: four in Grossman, up to five in Harty (Grossman, 2021; Harty, 2017). Access is trapezoid or rhomboid (Cohen, 2021). Second molar: roots and orifices closer, fusion or C-shape possible (Ingle, year not printed). Third molar: many anomalous configurations (Grossman, 2021).

### 6. Pulpal floor morphology
- Floor darker than walls; grooves joining orifices lighter (Cohen, 2021).
- Orifices at floor-wall junction and angles (Grossman, 2021).
- Developmental lines connect all orifices (Setzer and Walsch, 2025).
- Floor types of C-shaped mandibular second molars: bell-shaped, C-shaped, rounded mass, two holes; one book, figure caption only (Cohen, 2021).
- Age makes the molar chamber disc-like (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); taurodont floor lies apically (Torabinejad et al, 2021).

### 7. Significance in endodontics
1. Lack of anatomy knowledge ranks second only to errors in diagnosis and treatment planning as a cause of failure (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).
2. Missed canals could be the major cause of post-treatment disease (Barnett, 2017).
3. Laws locate orifices and calcified canals (Grossman, 2021).
4. Conventional periapical films are somewhat overrated for pulp anatomy (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); it is prudent to assume a root may contain more than one canal system until proved otherwise (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
5. Missed canals, isthmuses and lateral canals retain bacteria (Torabinejad et al, 2021).
6. Anatomy varies with age and ethnicity (Ingle, year not printed).

### 8. Conclusion
Chamber and canal anatomy follows the external form and the laws help locate the chamber and canal entrances (Torabinejad et al, 2021); it varies by tooth, age and population (Ingle, year not printed).
