## STUDY MODE

### S1. When a GIC restoration is the right choice [clinical decision-making]
Indications: Class V and Class III cavities, abrasion and erosion lesions, root caries, Class I in patients with high caries indices, deciduous teeth, and transitional restorations [SRC:G11] [SRC:G49] [SRC:G52]. GIC is the material of choice for lesions not under heavy occlusal load [SRC:A337]. Where load is heavy, use a laminate with composite or amalgam [SRC:A336].

### S2. Steps [treatment]
Isolate (rubber dam or cord), prepare conservatively, condition 10-20 seconds, mix to wet the powder only, place under a matrix, protect with resin, and finish after 24 hours [SRC:G41] [SRC:G42] [SRC:G43] [SRC:G45] [SRC:G46] [SRC:G48] [SRC:G47]. Class III is best restored with light-cure GIC for aesthetics, using a T-shaped Mylar strip [SRC:G51].

### S3. Why GIC restorations fail [prognosis]
Failure comes from dissolution of immature cement (first 24 hours), long-term erosion, abrasion loss, and fracture from flexure in cervical areas, often cohesive within the cement [SRC:G55]. An unprotected surface cracks [SRC:G48]. Average replacement age is about 3-5 years, though not all GICs fail after five years [SRC:C2133]. In Class V, one study showed 80% retention at 10 years and another 96.4% retention of a resin-modified GIC at 5 years [SRC:C1104] [SRC:C1106].

### S4. Advantages, limits and repair
Advantages are adhesion without undercuts, fluoride release, biocompatibility and low solubility after the full set; limits are opacity, brittleness and technique sensitivity [SRC:G14]. A fractured GIC can be repaired by conditioning with 10% polyacrylic acid for 10 seconds, washing, blotting dry and packing new GIC [SRC:C2134].

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
A glass ionomer restoration uses a glass polyalkenoate cement as a direct tooth-coloured filling that bonds chemically to tooth and releases fluoride [SRC:G4].

### 2. Indications
- Class V and Class III cavities; abrasion, erosion and abfraction lesions [SRC:G49] [SRC:G51].
- Root caries and high-risk or xerostomic patients [SRC:C1118].
- Class I in high caries-index patients; deciduous teeth; transitional, ART and interim therapeutic restorations [SRC:G52] [SRC:C1134].
- As part of a sandwich restoration [SRC:G54].

### 3. Contraindications
Heavy occlusal load in permanent teeth, large multi-surface restorations, incisal corners, cores without two walls, and where moisture control is impossible [SRC:C128] [SRC:C2154] [SRC:G41].

### 4. Cavity preparation
Minimal; no undercuts needed; outline follows the lesion; retention grooves only for wide lesions; do not break proximal contacts [SRC:G42] [SRC:B253]. No bevel; use a butt joint [SRC:C1102].

### 5. Technique
1. Isolate [SRC:G41].
2. Condition 10-20 seconds and wash [SRC:G43].
3. Mix quickly; plastic spatula [SRC:G45].
4. Place under a matrix, avoiding voids [SRC:G46].
5. Seal with resin at once [SRC:G48].
6. Finish after 24 hours in moist conditions [SRC:G47].

### 5A. Technique points by cavity class
- **Class III:** T-shaped Mylar strip; light-cure 20 seconds through the matrix, remove it, then cure further [SRC:G51].
- **Class V:** retraction cord for isolation; examine other teeth for further root lesions, since they rarely occur alone [SRC:G50].
- **Cervical Site 3, Size 2:** resin-modified or auto-cure GIC depending on light access, always conditioned with polyacrylic acid [SRC:A378].

### 6. Advantages
Chemical adhesion, fluoride release, biocompatibility, thermal expansion near dentine, low solubility once set, minimal preparation [SRC:G14] [SRC:G17].

### 7. Disadvantages
Brittle, opaque, moisture sensitive, high early solubility, low wear resistance [SRC:G14] [SRC:C125].

### 8. Failures and repair
Dissolution of immature cement, erosion, abrasion, fracture from flexure; repair with polyacrylic acid conditioning and new GIC [SRC:G55] [SRC:C2134].

### 8A. Maintenance
Old GIC must be protected from dehydration during prolonged isolation, especially under rubber dam [SRC:B256]; acidulated phosphate fluoride products can erode GIC and are avoided [SRC:A145]; fluoride toothpaste and gels recharge it [SRC:A144].

### 9. Prognosis
Average replacement age about 3-5 years; Class V retention of 80% at 10 years in one study [SRC:C2133] [SRC:C1104].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Best for Class V, Class III, root caries, high-risk and deciduous teeth [SRC:G49] [SRC:C1118].
- No bevel; condition 10-20 s; seal at once; finish at 24 h [SRC:C1102] [SRC:G43] [SRC:G48].
- Failures: early dissolution, erosion, abrasion, flexural fracture [SRC:G55].
