## STUDY MODE

### S1. Idea [basic science]
A tri-cure cement sets by three processes: the acid-base reaction, light polymerisation and a chemical redox "dark cure" (Sikri, 2020). The third mechanism addresses the main weakness of a light-cure material, which is that light cures only to a limited depth (Mount et al, 2016; Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Terminology differs between books. Sikri (2020), Bhat et al (2019), Bonsor and Pearson (2013) and Sakaguchi et al (2019) define dual-cure as acid-base plus light and tri-cure as those two plus a redox dark cure. Mount et al (2016) describe an incorporated redox auto-cure as part of resin-modified GIC in general (any resin not set by light sets by auto-cure, so the material sets completely without light), and in a lining table use "dual cure" for "auto cure plus light assisted cure"; they do not use the word tri-cure.

### S2. Composition and setting
Bhat's tri-cure powder is an ion-leachable fluoride glass with a BisGMA resin matrix, organosilane coupling agent and light and chemical initiators (benzoyl peroxide and camphoroquinone with an amine accelerator) (Bhat et al, 2019). The liquid is 30-50% aqueous polyacrylic acid with carboxyl groups modified with methyl methacrylate and HEMA (Bhat et al, 2019). Sikri says the redox reaction between initiator and catalyst runs after mixing so that, with time, the whole restoration cures with no free resin left (Sikri, 2020).

### S3. Properties and uses [treatment relevance]
Bhat states that the additional chemical cure makes bulk placement possible, assuring optimum cure even when the light does not reach the entire bulk, so no incremental placement is needed (example: Vitremer) (Bhat et al, 2019); Bonsor and Pearson report that areas set by dark cure have mechanical properties reduced by about 25-30% (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013). Reported properties: fluoride release, mild pulp response, compressive strength 105 MPa, tensile 20 MPa, hardness 40 KHN, chemical adhesion, but greater polymerisation shrinkage, and water uptake with reduced translucency (Bhat et al, 2019). Uses: liner under composite, core build-up, fissure sealant, cement base, orthodontic band cementation (Bhat et al, 2019).

### S4. Limits
Greater shrinkage and dehydration sensitivity are the costs of the added resin (Bhat et al, 2019). Resin-modified materials in general take up water over 5-7 days (Mount et al, 2016). Sturdevant 2nd South Asia ed says some resin-modified restorative materials have an additional chemical-cure reaction and can set in the absence of light, without using the term tri-cure (Ritter et al, 2019). Brambilla and Ionescu (2021) state that resin-modified GIC releases less fluoride than conventional GIC.

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Tri-cure GIC is a resin-modified glass ionomer that hardens by three mechanisms, intended to give cure even when light does not reach the whole bulk (Sikri, 2020; Bhat et al, 2019).

### 2. The three setting mechanisms
1. **Acid-base reaction** between glass powder and polyacrylic acid (the conventional GIC reaction) (Sikri, 2020).
2. **Light cure:** polymerisation of methacrylate groups under visible light (camphoroquinone) (Sikri, 2020; Bhat et al, 2019).
3. **Chemical (redox) dark cure:** reaction between initiator and catalyst once powder and liquid meet; benzoyl peroxide with an amine accelerator (Sikri, 2020; Bhat et al, 2019).

Terminology: dual-cure is acid-base plus light and tri-cure adds redox (Sikri, 2020; Bhat et al, 2019); Mount et al (2016) describe redox auto-cure within resin-modified GIC generally, call "auto cure plus light assisted cure" dual cure, and do not use the term tri-cure.

### 3. Composition
- **Powder:** fluoride glass, BisGMA resin matrix, organosilane coupling agent, light and chemical initiators (Bhat et al, 2019).
- **Liquid:** 30-50% aqueous polyacrylic acid with carboxyl groups modified by methyl methacrylate and HEMA, plus activators (Bhat et al, 2019).
- **Presentation:** often capsules (Bhat et al, 2019).

### 4. Properties
| Property | Value or effect |
|---|---|
| Compressive strength | about 105 MPa (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Tensile strength | about 20 MPa (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Hardness | about 40 KHN (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Fluoride | released; mild pulp response (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Adhesion | chemical (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Shrinkage | greater polymerisation shrinkage (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Water | takes up water, dimensional change, reduced translucency (Bhat et al, 2019) |

### 4A. Comparison with the other forms
| Feature | Conventional GIC | Dual-cure (resin-modified) | Tri-cure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanisms | acid-base only (Sikri, 2020) | acid-base plus light (Sikri, 2020) | acid-base plus light plus redox (Sikri, 2020) |
| Command set | no | yes (Sikri, 2020) | yes (Bhat et al, 2019) |
| Depth of cure | full, slow | light-cured part limited by penetration, over 0.5 mm not adequately polymerised (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013); Mount et al (2016) say the auto-cure component completes the set in the long run | full by chemical cure (Bhat et al, 2019); dark-cured areas about 25-30% weaker (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013) |
| Placement | bulk, sealed at once | increments | bulk possible (Bhat et al, 2019) |

### 4B. Depth of cure in practice
In a deep cavity (about 8 mm at its deepest) a dual-cure cement was light-activated for 40 seconds from the occlusal surface, with light penetration expected to about 4 mm and the remaining cement setting by its own chemistry (Mount, 1994). A tri-cure material adds a chemical resin cure for exactly this situation (Sikri, 2020). Light activation hardens the resin to the depth of penetration, and the auto-cure component then completes the set (Mount et al, 2016).

### 5. Advantages
Bulk placement with cure assured even without full light penetration; no incremental technique (Bhat et al, 2019); no free resin left with time (Sikri, 2020).

### 6. Disadvantages
Greater polymerisation shrinkage (3-4% in Bonsor and Pearson, 2013); water uptake and loss of translucency; sensitivity to dehydration (Bhat et al, 2019); heat up to 12 C (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013).

### 7. Uses
Liner under composite, core build-up, fissure sealant, cement base, orthodontic band cementation (Bhat et al, 2019); deep restorations where light penetration may be insufficient (Sikri, 2020).

### 8. Conclusion
Tri-cure adds a chemical cure to resin-modified GIC, giving a dependable set in depth at the cost of more shrinkage and water uptake.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Three cures: acid-base, light, redox (Sikri, 2020).
- Bulk placement, no incremental technique (Bhat et al, 2019).
- Example: Vitremer (Bhat et al, 2019).
