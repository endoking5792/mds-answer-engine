# "Relevance of single visit endodontics." (canonical answer)

## 1. Definition and why the question is asked now

**Explanation.** "Relevance" asks why single-visit treatment matters in today's practice, not merely what it is. Grossman's answer is historical: single-visit treatment is almost a century old, gave way to multiple visits as procedures became more demanding, and has regained acceptance for most cases with rotary nickel-titanium instruments (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Accurate working length, which single-visit treatment depends on, is now established by combining radiographs, an electronic apex locator and clinical judgement (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Practice has followed: in Cohen's surveys the share of clinicians completing vital cases in one visit rose to 79% (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core
**Definition.** Single-visit endodontics means cleaning, shaping, disinfecting and obturating the root canal system in one appointment, with no interappointment dressing (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

**Why it is relevant today.**
1. **Technology:** rotary NiTi instruments have made single visit the accepted choice for most cases (Gopikrishna, 2021); working length is confirmed with an apex locator, radiographs and clinical judgement (Gopikrishna, 2021).
2. **Changing practice:** clinicians completing vital cases in one visit rose to 79% in surveys (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021).

---

## 2. Biological and clinical relevance

**Explanation.** The biological case is strongest for the vital tooth, whose radicular pulp is not infected; finishing in one visit avoids contaminating it between appointments (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC]. The clinical case rests on outcome evidence: the Cochrane review found no difference in radiological failure between one and several visits (Mergoni et al, 2022) [LIT]. Practical gains follow, though Sikri notes that fee and time arguments are often assumptions rather than facts (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core
3. **Biology:** in vital teeth the radicular pulp is inflamed but not infected; one visit avoids contaminating it through a leaking temporary filling (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017).
4. **Evidence:** randomised trials pooled in the Cochrane review show no difference in radiographic failure between single and multiple visits (Mergoni et al, 2022).
5. **Patient factors:** anxiety and discomfort limited to one appointment, immediate final restoration that protects against fracture and restores appearance (Sikri, 2019).
6. **Practice factors:** saves chair time otherwise lost re-seating, re-isolating and re-irrigating; patients will pay for time saved (Sikri, 2019).
7. **Completion:** fewer abandoned treatments by patients who stop attending once pain settles (Sikri, 2019).
8. **Technical:** canal anatomy and working length are fresh in mind, reference points are not lost, and no medicament residue interferes with sealer flow; the access is sealed at once, avoiding interappointment leakage (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Gopikrishna, 2021).

---

## 3. Where it is relevant, and its limits

**Explanation.** Relevance is case-dependent. Grossman's indications and contraindications define the boundaries (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], and the 2022 Cochrane review is the main caution: patients more often report pain within the first week after one visit, a moderate-certainty finding seen in small subgroups of vital teeth and mechanical instrumentation, while pain at the one-week time point itself did not differ (Mergoni et al, 2022) [LIT].

#### ✎ Exam core
**Most relevant in:** vital teeth; fractured anteriors needing immediate aesthetics; physically or medically compromised patients; apprehensive but cooperative patients; patients needing sedation; non-vital teeth with a sinus tract (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Less relevant or contraindicated:** TMJ disorders or limited opening; limited access; nonsurgical retreatment (Gopikrishna, 2021); severe pain or swelling (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021); canals that cannot be dried (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

**Limits.** More patients report pain in the first week after a single visit, in small subgroups (vital teeth, mechanical instrumentation) (Mergoni et al, 2022); no canal access if a flare-up occurs (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

**Conclusion.** Single-visit endodontics is relevant because technology, biology and evidence now support it in well-selected cases. As Cohen's puts it, what can be done and what should be done are different decisions (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
1. Rotary NiTi drove the return of single visit (Grossman).
2. Vital tooth: radicular pulp inflamed, not infected.
3. Cochrane 2022: no difference in radiological failure; more first-week pain after one visit (small subgroups).
4. 79% of surveyed clinicians complete vital cases in one visit.
5. Retreatment is a contraindication in Grossman.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Rewriting a full single-visit essay (history, full protocol, all trials) instead of arguing relevance.
2. Omitting the limits; "relevance" needs both sides.
3. Claiming single visit is relevant for every case, including symptomatic retreatment.
