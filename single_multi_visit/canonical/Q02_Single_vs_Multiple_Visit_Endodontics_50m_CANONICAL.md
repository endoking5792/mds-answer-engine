# "Single visit endodontics Vs multiple visit endodontics." (canonical answer)

## 1. Introduction and definitions

**Explanation.** A comparison question is marked on whether the two options are set side by side on the same criteria, so the definitions must make the one real difference explicit: what happens to the canal between preparation and filling. In single-visit treatment nothing happens, because the canal is filled at once; in multiple-visit treatment the canal is dressed and sealed and filled at a later visit (Mergoni et al, 2022) [LIT]. Grossman's notes that completing treatment in one visit is not new: reviews of it go back almost a century; multiple visits became popular as procedures grew more demanding, and rotary nickel-titanium instruments have now made single visit acceptable as the treatment of choice for most cases (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Ingle's describes the two extreme camps: some clinicians treat every case in one visit regardless of symptoms or complexity, while others confine single visits to vital teeth (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) [SRC]. Q1 section 1 explains why the dispute is about infected teeth, not vital ones.

#### ✎ Exam core
**Single-visit endodontics (SVE):** chemomechanical preparation, disinfection and obturation of the root canal system completed at one appointment, the canal being filled directly after instrumentation and irrigation (Mergoni et al, 2022).

**Multiple-visit endodontics (MVE):** treatment spread over two or more appointments; obturated at the last visit (Mergoni et al, 2022); between visits the canal is usually dressed with an intracanal medicament such as calcium hydroxide and temporarily sealed (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

**Historical shift.** One-visit treatment was described almost a century ago; multiple visits then became the norm as treatment grew more exacting; with rotary nickel-titanium instruments, single visit has again become accepted for most cases (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**The point of comparison.** Both aim at the same goal: elimination of root canal infection and prevention of reinfection (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022). They differ only in whether a medicament and time are added between cleaning and filling.

## 2. Rationale of each approach

**Explanation.** Each approach rests on a different assumption about residual bacteria, explained in detail in Q1 section 2. The single-visit argument is that what instruments and irrigants leave behind is killed or entombed by the filling. The multiple-visit argument is that some bacteria almost always survive, that a medicament and time are needed to kill them, and that the second visit allows the clinician to judge the tissue response before filling (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Rosenberg and Malek, 2021) [SRC]. Ingle's adds a clinical reason for two visits in the emergency patient: the second visit shows the effect of treatment on inflamed and infected tissues (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) [SRC]. Siqueira and Rôças add the strongest modern statement for MVE: there is no shortcut to disinfection, and even the most potent antimicrobial needs time to kill bacteria (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core
| | Single visit | Multiple visit |
|---|---|---|
| Core assumption | Instruments and irrigants remove most bacteria; survivors are entombed and starved by the filling | Some bacteria survive preparation; a medicament and time are needed to kill them |
| Role of time | Not needed; delay only risks contamination through the temporary | Antimicrobials need time to act; "no shortcut to effective disinfection" (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) |
| Role of the second visit | None | Re-irrigation, assessment of symptoms and periapical response before filling (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021) |
| Theoretical risk | Entombment fails above a threshold bacterial load (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) | Interappointment leakage and secondary infection |

*(Baumann and Beer, 2010; Sikri, 2019)*

## 3. Microbiological basis of the choice

**Explanation.** The pulp diagnosis decides which rationale applies (Q1 section 3). In vital teeth the radicular pulp is not infected, so there is no residual flora for a medicament to act on, and delay only adds risk; Ingle's reports vital pulpectomy success above 94%, with many practitioners carrying it out in one visit (Goldberg, 2019) [SRC]. In necrotic teeth microorganisms colonise the canal system and penetrate deep into dentinal tubules, so Ingle's says the treatment must be more thorough and may need more than one visit with a dressing (Goldberg, 2019) [SRC]. Sikri's figure that only a third of canals are bacteria-free after the first appointment, and the doubts about calcium hydroxide, are discussed in Q1.

#### ✎ Exam core
**Vital pulp (irreversible pulpitis).**
- The radicular pulp is inflamed but not infected (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017).
- Nothing is gained by a medicament; delay only risks contaminating a clean canal. Single visit is favoured (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017; Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- Success of vital pulpectomy exceeds 94% (Goldberg, 2019).

**Necrotic pulp with apical periodontitis.**
- Bacteria colonise the canal system, isthmuses and dentinal tubules (Goldberg, 2019).
- Preparation and irrigation leave residual bacteria in many canals (Sikri, 2019; Gulabivala and Ng, 2022).
- Calcium hydroxide adds disinfection and inactivates endotoxin, but its antibacterial effect in human canals is limited (Sikri, 2019; Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- This is the group in which MVE has a biological rationale and where the debate lies (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

## 4. Indications and contraindications of each

**Explanation.** Grossman's gives the most structured criteria: a box of favourable and unfavourable factors, and the statement that the clinician's experience and ability is the most important factor; an experienced clinician can treat most cases in one visit (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. (Grossman's text then says the list helps a clinician moving "from single-visit endodontics to multiple-visit endodontics"; from context this appears to be a slip for the reverse direction [Likely].) Ingle's is explicit about when MVE is preferred: intracanal bleeding, persistent exudate, severe preoperative pain and acute symptoms (Goldberg, 2019) [SRC]. Note that Grossman's lists the presence of a periradicular lesion as unfavourable, while Sikri lists non-vital teeth with a sinus tract as an indication; both are reconciled by reading "lesion" as a chronic lesion in a case of doubtful disinfection [Likely].

#### ✎ Exam core
**Favourable factors for SVE** (Gopikrishna, 2021):
- Experienced operator (the most important factor).
- Cooperative patient; good access and mouth opening.
- Normal anatomy; patent canals; easily negotiable curvatures.
- No periradicular lesion; profound pulpal anaesthesia easily achieved.
- Primary (not retreatment) case.

**Indications for SVE** (Gopikrishna, 2021; Sikri, 2019; Weine, 2004):
- Vital teeth.
- Fractured anterior teeth where aesthetics is a concern.
- Physically or medically compromised patients (for example those needing antibiotic prophylaxis or a change in medication for each visit).
- Patients who need sedation or treatment in an operating room.
- Apprehensive but cooperative patients.
- Non-vital teeth with a sinus tract.

**Indications for MVE (contraindications to SVE)** (Gopikrishna, 2021; Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019; Goldberg, 2019; Rosenberg and Malek, 2021):
- Severe preoperative pain, acute symptoms, swelling, "hot tooth".
- Intracanal bleeding or persistent exudate (canal cannot be dried).
- Anatomical variation: extra roots or canals, calcification, ledges, obstructions, acute curvatures.
- Non-surgical retreatment.
- Poor access, TMJ disorder, inability to keep the mouth open; non-cooperative patient.
- Relatively inexperienced operator.
- Insufficient appointment time.

## 5. Procedural comparison

**Explanation.** The procedures share every step up to the end of shaping; Q1 section 5 gives the full single-visit protocol. What differs is the step after final irrigation. Ingle's gives the scheduling detail for MVE in vital cases: wait 5 to 7 days between instrumentation and obturation to let the periradicular tissues recover, which prevents filling while tenderness persists; for a single visit, enough time must be booked, including the 15 to 20 minutes that profound inferior alveolar anaesthesia can take (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) [SRC]. The 2022 Cochrane review treated rubber dam and sodium hypochlorite irrigation as the minimum standard of care for either approach (Mergoni et al, 2022) [LIT].

#### ✎ Exam core
| Step | Single visit | Multiple visit |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis, anaesthesia, rubber dam, access | Same | Same |
| Working length, cleaning and shaping, sodium hypochlorite irrigation | Completed in one session | May be split across visits |
| After final irrigation | Dry canal, immediate obturation | Calcium hydroxide dressing, sterile pellet, temporary seal |
| Interval | None | About 5-7 days in vital cases; at least a week for calcium hydroxide in infected cases (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019; Baumann and Beer, 2010) |
| Next visit | Not needed (restoration only) | Remove temporary and dressing, re-irrigate, confirm dry and asymptomatic, obturate |
| Coronal restoration | Immediate | After obturation |
| Appointment booking | One long appointment, allowing 15-20 min for IANB anaesthesia (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) | Two or more shorter appointments |

## 5A. The interappointment period: what MVE adds and what it risks

**Explanation.** The interval is the whole of the difference, so it deserves its own section. On the benefit side, calcium hydroxide dissolves tissue, kills bacteria, detoxifies antigens and releases slowly because of its low solubility (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021) [SRC]; Siqueira and Rôças favour more potent pastes (with camphorated paramonochlorophenol or chlorhexidine) and report a 5% failure rate in their own two-visit protocol (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) [SRC]. On the risk side, Harty's notes that between visits the canal can be contaminated through microleakage or breakage of the temporary restoration, which is why a medicament is placed even in vital teeth that could not be finished (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC]; and both Siqueira and Rôças chapters warn that more than two visits predisposes to secondary infection and reduced survival (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017; Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) [SRC]. The coronal seal matters after obturation too: Grossman's cites an in vitro study in which a canal without coronal protection was reinfected with staphylococci 19 days after obturation (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], which applies to both approaches.

#### ✎ Exam core
**What the interval adds (MVE).**
- Calcium hydroxide: dissolves organic tissue, kills bacteria, detoxifies antigenic material, slow release because of low solubility (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021).
- Inactivation of endotoxin (lipopolysaccharide), which irrigants do not achieve (Sikri, 2019).
- More potent pastes (calcium hydroxide with camphorated paramonochlorophenol or chlorhexidine) give predictable disinfection; a 5% failure rate is reported for one two-visit protocol (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- Time to observe symptoms and the periapical response before filling (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021).

**What the interval risks (MVE).**
- Contamination through microleakage or a broken or lost temporary restoration (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017).
- Secondary infection and reduced survival if treatment runs beyond two visits (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- Medicament residue on canal walls affecting sealer adaptation (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- Patient failing to return once pain settles (Sikri, 2019).

**Shared requirement.** A prompt, sound coronal seal after obturation; in one in vitro study an unprotected canal was reinfected by staphylococci 19 days after filling (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## 6. Head-to-head comparison

**Explanation.** These tables gather the advantages and disadvantages discussed in Q1 section 6 under shared headings, which is how examiners expect a "versus" question to be answered. The entries on outcomes come from section 7.

#### ✎ Exam core
**A. Biological and technical factors**
| Factor | Single visit | Multiple visit |
|---|---|---|
| Interappointment contamination | None | Possible through leaking or lost temporary |
| Use of medicament | Not possible | Calcium hydroxide disinfects and inactivates endotoxin |
| Residual bacteria | Entombed by the filling | Further reduced before filling |
| Familiarity with canal anatomy and length | Fresh at obturation | Reference points may change |
| Medicament residue | None to impair sealer flow | Residue may affect sealer and dentine |
| Reassessment of missed canals and symptoms | Not possible | Possible |
| Access for drainage if flare-up | Difficult (canal filled) | Easy (canal open under temporary) |

**B. Clinical outcomes**
| Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|
| Pain up to 72 hours, flare-up | No significant difference (Mergoni et al, 2022) |
| Pain within the first week | More patients with pain after SVE (RR 1.55), seen in small subgroups of vital teeth and mechanical instrumentation; pain at the one-week time point itself did not differ (Mergoni et al, 2022) |
| Flare-up incidence | 1.4-19% overall; highest in retreatment (Gopikrishna, 2021). SVE vs MVE: RR 2.13 against SVE on very weak evidence (Schwendicke and Göstemeyer, 2017) versus no difference (Mergoni et al, 2022) |
| Radiographic healing | No significant difference (Mergoni et al, 2022; Goldberg, 2019) |
| Tooth extraction | No difference; very little data (Mergoni et al, 2022) |

**C. Patient and operator factors**
| Factor | Single visit | Multiple visit |
|---|---|---|
| Number of injections, isolations | One | Repeated |
| Appointment length | Long: patient and operator fatigue, TMJ strain | Shorter, easier to tolerate |
| Anxiety | Limited to one day | Repeated |
| Risk of patient not returning | Nil | Treatment may be abandoned once pain subsides |
| Operator skill needed | High; not for beginners (Sikri, 2019) | Less critical |

**D. Time and cost**
| Factor | Single visit | Multiple visit |
|---|---|---|
| Total chair time | Less (no repeated set-up) | More |
| Cost | Lower overall | Higher |
| Final restoration | Immediate; reduced fracture risk | Delayed |
| Scheduling flexibility | Less | More |

*(Sikri, 2019; Baumann and Beer, 2010; Gopikrishna, 2021)*

## 7. Evidence

**Explanation.** The individual studies and reviews are set out in Q1 section 7. Ingle's adds one figure not in Q1: a systematic review found single-visit treatment slightly more effective, with a healing rate 6.3% higher, but not statistically significant (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) [SRC]. This is a useful counterweight to Siqueira and Rôças's 10-20% advantage for two visits in other studies (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) [SRC]: the direction of the difference changes from study to study, which is what one expects when the true difference is small and the trials are underpowered [Likely]. Ingle's (Ch.34) states the working conclusion: if the treatment is properly done, no significant difference can be assumed (Goldberg, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core
**Postoperative pain and flare-up.** Most trials and reviews show no difference in post-obturation pain or flare-up (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019; Baumann and Beer, 2010). The Cochrane review (2022; 47 trials) found more patients with pain in the first week after SVE (RR 1.55, moderate certainty), seen in small subgroups of vital teeth and mechanical instrumentation; pain at the one-week time point itself did not differ (Mergoni et al, 2022). Schwendicke and Göstemeyer (2017) found a higher flare-up risk after SVE (RR 2.13) on very weak evidence.

**Healing.**
- Favouring neither: Cochrane 2022, radiological failure RR 0.93 (moderate certainty) (Mergoni et al, 2022); overview of systematic reviews, similar success regardless of pulpal or periapical status (Moreira et al, 2017).
- Favouring SVE: one systematic review, 6.3% higher healing, not significant (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019).
- Favouring MVE: some trials 10-20% higher success with calcium hydroxide; a 5-year single-operator cohort, animal histology and a CBCT-assessed trial (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

**Key comparative trials** (as reported by Baumann and Beer, 2010, and Sikri, 2019):
| Trial | Teeth | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Trope et al (1999) | about 100 canals with apical periodontitis | Two visits without medicament healed worst; one visit equal to two visits with calcium hydroxide |
| Weiger et al (2000) | 76 patients | No significant difference |
| Peters and Wesselink (2002) | 39-42 teeth, 4.5-5 years | No significant difference; bacteria increased during a 4-week dressing |
| Molander et al (2007) | 101 teeth | No significant difference in healing |
| Penesis et al (2008) | 63 patients, 1 year | No significant difference |
| Risso et al (2008) | 118 necrotic molars | Pain 10.5% after one visit vs 23% after multiple |
| Paredes-Vieyra and Enriquez (2012) | 282 teeth, 2 years | No significant difference |

The striking point is that in Trope et al (1999) two visits **without** a medicament gave the poorest healing: the benefit of MVE, if any, comes from the medicament, not from the extra appointment (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

**Quality of evidence.** Trials are small, many at high or unclear risk of bias (Mergoni et al, 2022), with radiographic follow-up of 1-2 years; all lack robust statistical power (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021; Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

**Working conclusion.** When treatment is properly done, no significant difference in long-term success can be assumed (Goldberg, 2019).

## 8. Decision algorithm

**Explanation.** Cohen's and Ingle's both make the same point: most cases can be completed in one visit, but "what can be done and what should be done" are different decisions (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021; Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) [SRC]. The algorithm below turns the criteria into a sequence a candidate can draw.

#### ✎ Exam core
1. **Pulp diagnosis.**
   - Vital (irreversible pulpitis) → single visit if time, skill and anatomy allow.
   - Necrotic → step 2.
2. **Symptoms.** Severe pain, swelling or acute abscess → relieve pain, dress with calcium hydroxide, obturate at a later visit (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019).
3. **Case complexity.** Retreatment, calcification, extra canals, severe curvature → multiple visits (Gopikrishna, 2021).
4. **Patient and operator.** Poor cooperation, limited opening, inexperienced operator, limited time → multiple visits.
5. **Intraoperative check.** Canal dries, no bleeding or exudate, all canals located and prepared → obturate (single visit). Otherwise → calcium hydroxide and reappoint (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Goldberg, 2019).
6. **Limit.** Do not extend beyond two visits: risk of secondary infection and reduced survival (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

## 8A. Current trends

**Explanation.** Practice has moved towards single visit, especially for vital pulps: Cohen's cites practitioner surveys in which completion of vital cases in one visit rose from not being asked about in 1977 to 79% in the latest survey (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) [SRC]. Grossman's ties this to rotary nickel-titanium instruments (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. At the same time, Siqueira and Rôças report an ongoing search for supplementary protocols that could make disinfection fast enough for one visit in infected teeth (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) [SRC]; Q1 section 8 discusses this.

#### ✎ Exam core
- **Shift in practice:** surveys show completion of vital cases in one visit rising to 79% of practitioners (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021).
- **Technology:** with rotary nickel-titanium instruments, single visit has gained acceptance as the treatment of choice for most cases (Gopikrishna, 2021); apex locators and magnification also shorten treatment [RECALL].
- **Disinfection research:** intravisit supplementary steps, such as ultrasonic or mechanical activation of hypochlorite or a final 2% chlorhexidine rinse, are used to improve disinfection before immediate filling (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- **Outcome measurement:** CBCT is more sensitive than periapical radiographs; one CBCT-assessed trial favoured two visits (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022), so future trials may change the picture.
- **Need:** large, well-powered randomised trials with long follow-up (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021).

## 9. Conclusion

#### ✎ Exam core
Single- and multiple-visit endodontics share one aim, the control of root canal infection, and differ only in whether time and a medicament are added before filling. For vital teeth single visit is biologically sound and preferred. For necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis, current trials show no significant difference in healing, a little more first-week pain after single visit, and evidence of limited quality (Mergoni et al, 2022). The number of visits is therefore a decision of case selection, not of principle: single visit for simple, asymptomatic, dry cases treated by an experienced operator; two visits with calcium hydroxide for symptomatic, exudative, complex and retreatment cases (Gopikrishna, 2021; Goldberg, 2019). Regardless of the number of appointments, effective disinfection of the root canal system is critical (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

**Claim 1: "There is no difference in healing between single and multiple visits."**
- *Textbooks:* Ingle's states that properly done treatment shows no significant difference and cites a review with a non-significant 6.3% advantage for SVE (Goldberg, 2019; Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) [SRC]; Siqueira and Rôças cite studies with a 10-20% advantage for two visits and favour two visits for infected teeth (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) [SRC].
- *Retrieved evidence:* Cochrane 2022, RR 0.93 for radiological failure, moderate certainty (Mergoni et al, 2022) [LIT]; trial sequential analysis did not reach firm evidence for any outcome (Schwendicke and Göstemeyer, 2017) [LIT].
- *Agreement or challenge:* The literature agrees with Ingle's that no difference has been detected; it does not establish equivalence. Siqueira and Rôças's opposing data come from a single-operator cohort, animal histology and one CBCT trial [Certain].
- *What can be concluded:* any real difference is likely small; its direction is not established [Likely].
- *Clinical implication:* case selection, not dogma, should decide the number of visits.

**Claim 2: "Single visit is more painful."**
- *Textbooks:* Ingle's calls the evidence contradictory, with systematic reviews finding similar post-obturation discomfort (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) [SRC].
- *Retrieved evidence:* Cochrane 2022 found no difference up to 72 hours but more patients with pain in the first week after SVE, seen in small subgroups of vital teeth and mechanical instrumentation, while pain at the one-week time point itself did not differ (Mergoni et al, 2022) [LIT].
- *Refinement:* the textbook statement holds for immediate pain; the first-week difference is a refinement, of moderate certainty and modest size [Likely].
- *Implication:* warn the patient and prescribe analgesics after single visit.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
1. The only procedural difference: immediate obturation versus a medicament interval.
2. Vital teeth: single visit (radicular pulp not infected); vital pulpectomy success above 94% (Goldberg, 2019).
3. Grossman's: operator experience is the most important single factor.
4. MVE preferred for bleeding, exudate, severe pain, acute symptoms, retreatment (Goldberg, 2019; Gopikrishna, 2021).
5. Cochrane 2022: healing RR 0.93; more first-week pain after SVE (RR 1.55).
6. One review: SVE 6.3% better (not significant); others: MVE 10-20% better. Direction unsettled.
7. MVE in vital cases: 5-7 days between instrumentation and obturation (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019).
8. Never more than two visits.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Writing two separate essays (one on SVE, one on MVE) instead of comparing on shared headings.
2. Calling single visit "contraindicated" in all non-vital teeth: Grossman's and Sikri list non-vital teeth with a sinus tract as an indication.
3. Presenting healing differences as settled in either direction.
4. Omitting the intraoperative check (dry canal, all canals located) that can convert a planned single visit into two.
5. Forgetting cost, time and patient factors, which are part of any "versus" answer.
