## 1. Definition

**Explanation.** See Q1 section 1. The only point to add here is that "success" in a single-visit question means two things that must be kept apart: short-term comfort (pain, flare-up) and long-term healing or survival.

#### ✎ Exam core
Single visit endodontics is completion of root canal treatment, that is cleaning, shaping, disinfection and obturation of the canal system (and removal of an old filling in retreatment), in one appointment (Baumann and Beer, 2010). It is generally accepted for vital teeth, where only the coronal pulp is superficially infected; its use in necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis is debated (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

---

## 2. Criteria of success

**Explanation.** Outcome can be judged in four dimensions: the physical (health or disease, pain, function), tooth survival, the economic, and the patient's quality of life (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021) [SRC]. The classic definition of success and failure is Strindberg's (1956), which combines clinical and radiographic findings; Cohen's tabulates it beside Bender et al and Friedman and Mor, and notes that Friedman and Mor's "healed" corresponds to Strindberg's success while their "healing" corresponds to Bender's success (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021) [SRC]. The definitive measure, with absence of signs and symptoms, is periapical healing, because treatment aims to resolve periapical disease (Gulabivala and Ng, 2019) [SRC]. Friedman and Mor regarded "success" and "failure" as ambiguous and proposed healed, healing and disease; the AAE terms are healed, healing and non-healed (Goldberg, 2019) [SRC]. "Functional retention" is Friedman's term, not an AAE category: a tooth retained without signs or symptoms regardless of a radiographic lesion (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021; Goldberg, 2019) [SRC]. Note that the AAE "healing" category requires periradicular pathosis still to be present; a "reduced" lesion is Friedman and Mor's definition of healing (Goldberg, 2019; Ng and Gulabivala, 2021) [SRC]. Most lesions heal within a year, but healing can continue for up to 4 years or longer (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021) [SRC], which is why a single-visit outcome cannot be judged at the next appointment.

#### ✎ Exam core
**Clinical:** no pain; no swelling, mobility or sinus tract (Goldberg, 2019).
**Radiographic:** normal periodontal ligament width, continuous lamina dura, recovered cancellous bone, no root resorption (Goldberg, 2019).

**Classic criteria** (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021):
| Strindberg (1956) | Bender et al (1966) | Friedman and Mor (2004) |
|---|---|---|
| Success: no symptoms; normal periodontal contours, width and structure | Success: no pain, swelling or fistula; rarefaction eliminated or arrested at 6 months-2 years | Healed: normal clinically and radiographically |
| Failure: symptoms; or rarefaction decreased, unchanged, new or enlarged | Failure: lesion emerged or persisted, or symptoms | Diseased |
| Uncertain: ambiguous radiographs | — | Healing: normal clinically, lesion reduced |

**AAE categories** (Goldberg, 2019):
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Healed | Functional, asymptomatic, minimal or no periradicular pathosis |
| Healing | Asymptomatic and functional with radiographic pathosis, or symptomatic with unaffected function |
| Non-healed | Nonfunctional, symptomatic, with or without a lesion |

Functional retention (Friedman): tooth retained without signs or symptoms regardless of a lesion (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021). Healing may take 4 years or more; success needs long-term follow-up (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021; Goldberg, 2019).

## 3. Success of single visit endodontics

**Explanation.** The figures come in two kinds. Single-arm series show what one visit achieves alone: Pekruhn's 925 teeth reached 95% overall success at one year, with failure higher in retreatments and teeth with apical periodontitis; Field et al reported 89.2% in 223 teeth, anterior teeth doing better than posterior (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Comparative evidence answers whether one visit does worse than two. The 2022 Cochrane review found no difference in radiological failure or extraction, but more patients reporting pain within the first week after one visit (Mergoni et al, 2022) [LIT]. For orientation, nonsurgical and surgical treatment and retreatment overall succeed in about 85% of cases, rising above 90% when judged by functional retention (Goldberg, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core
| Study | Teeth | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Pekruhn (1986) | 925, one operator, 1 year | 95% success; failures higher in retreatment and apical periodontitis |
| Field et al (2004) | 223 | 89.2% success; anteriors better than posteriors |
| Trope (1991) | 226 | No flare-ups without preoperative symptoms |
| Peters and Wesselink (2002); Molander et al (2007); Paredes-Vieyra and Enriquez (2012) | Comparative trials | Healing equal to two visits |

*(Sikri, 2019)*

- Systematic reviews: no significant difference in healing between one and two visits (Baumann and Beer, 2010); Cochrane 2022, radiological failure RR 0.93, moderate certainty (Mergoni et al, 2022).
- Vital pulpectomy, often done in one visit, succeeds in more than 94% (Goldberg, 2019).

## 4. Factors affecting success

**Explanation.** Ingle's groups the factors into treatment, patient and practitioner factors: correct diagnosis and plan, anatomical complexity, severity of disease, host response and the operator's experience (Goldberg, 2019) [SRC]. For single visit the two that dominate are preoperative pulp-periapical status and case selection (Q1 section 4).

#### ✎ Exam core
1. **Pulp and periapical status:** vital pulpectomy exceeds 94% success (Goldberg, 2019); failure is higher with apical periodontitis and in retreatment (Sikri, 2019).
2. **Tooth type and anatomy:** anterior teeth do better than posterior (Sikri, 2019; Weine, 2004).
3. **Operator experience:** the most important single factor (Gopikrishna, 2021).
4. **Quality of disinfection:** bacteria remaining at obturation lower healing, 68% versus 94% (Sjögren et al 1997, in Baumann and Beer, 2010).
5. **Apical control:** no overinstrumentation or extrusion (Weine, 2004).
6. **Coronal seal:** immediate core build-up prevents reinfection (Gopikrishna, 2021).
7. **Host response** (Goldberg, 2019).

## 5. Failures of single visit endodontics

**Explanation.** Failures split into early (flare-up, postoperative pain) and late (non-healing lesion). The early failures are the ones peculiar to one visit, because the canal is already sealed. Grossman defines a flare-up as an acute exacerbation after starting or continuing treatment, gives an incidence of 1.4-19%, and lists preoperative pain, abscess, overinstrumentation, step-back shaping and hypochlorite extrusion as causes (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Late failure reflects residual infection: above a threshold, obturation cannot entomb surviving bacteria (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core
**A. Early failure: flare-up and pain.**
- Flare-up: acute exacerbation of an asymptomatic pulp or periapical pathosis after treatment starts; incidence 1.4-19% (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Causes: overinstrumentation, extrusion of debris or irrigant; preoperative pain, swelling or abscess; retreatment (Gopikrishna, 2021; Sikri, 2019).
- Single visit: more patients with pain within one week (RR 1.55; small subgroups), but no difference in flare-up (Mergoni et al, 2022).

**B. Late failure: persistent or new periapical lesion.**
- Residual bacteria not entombed by the filling (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- Missed canals and complex anatomy: one visit gives no chance to re-evaluate additional canals (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
- Coronal leakage before the final restoration (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Extruded filling material can delay healing (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021).

**C. Failures of case selection.** Weeping canals, symptomatic teeth or retreatments forced into one visit (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Gopikrishna, 2021).

---

## 6. Management of failures

**Explanation.** A flare-up is managed symptomatically (Q1 section 6A). A non-healed tooth with a persisting or enlarging lesion and symptoms needs nonsurgical or surgical retreatment or extraction, chosen on local and general factors (Goldberg, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core
- **Flare-up:** NSAIDs with acetaminophen, long-acting local anaesthetic, occlusal relief, anxiety reduction (Gopikrishna, 2021); antibiotics only with systemic signs (Goldberg, 2019); do not leave the tooth open (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021).
- **Overinstrumentation found during the visit:** do not complete; place a corticosteroid-antibiotic dressing and finish later (Weine, 2004).
- **Non-healed tooth:** nonsurgical or surgical retreatment, or extraction (Goldberg, 2019).
- **Persisting asymptomatic radiolucency:** continue observation (Goldberg, 2019).

**Success and failure compared with multiple visits.**
| Outcome | Single visit vs multiple visits |
|---|---|
| Radiographic healing | No difference (RR 0.93) (Mergoni et al, 2022) |
| Tooth extraction | No difference; very low certainty (Mergoni et al, 2022) |
| Pain within one week | More patients after single visit (RR 1.55), seen in small subgroups of vital teeth and mechanical instrumentation; pain at one week itself did not differ (Mergoni et al, 2022) |
| Flare-up | No difference in pooled trials (Mergoni et al, 2022) |
| Necrotic teeth with lesions | Some studies 10-20% better with two visits (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) |
| Managing a flare-up | Harder after one visit: canal already filled (Baumann and Beer, 2010) |

**Conclusion.** With good case selection, single visit gives success equal to multiple visits; its typical failure is short-term pain, not poorer healing (Mergoni et al, 2022; Sikri, 2019).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim: "Single visit fails more often than multiple visits."** Textbook series report 89-95% single-visit success (Sikri, 2019) [SRC], and the 2022 Cochrane review finds no difference in radiological failure (RR 0.93, moderate certainty) but more patients with pain within the first week after one visit, seen in small subgroups (Mergoni et al, 2022) [LIT]. Siqueira and Rôças counter that the trials are small and of low-to-medium quality, and that some studies show 10-20% better outcomes with two visits (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) [SRC]. The single-arm series lack a control group and the trials lack power, so the evidence shows no detected difference rather than proven equivalence [Certain]. Clinically, one visit is not a higher-failure option for well-selected cases; failure risk is driven by preoperative periapical status and retreatment [Likely].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
1. Single-visit success: 95% (Pekruhn), 89.2% (Field).
2. Cochrane 2022: radiological failure RR 0.93; first-week pain RR 1.55.
3. Strindberg: success, failure, uncertain. AAE: healed, healing (pathosis still present), non-healed. Functional retention is Friedman's term.
4. Healing may take up to 4 years.
5. Flare-up incidence 1.4-19%.
6. Non-healed: retreat (nonsurgical or surgical) or extract.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Judging success at one week: postoperative pain is a short-term outcome, not failure of healing.
2. Quoting single-arm success rates as proof that one visit beats two.
5. Calling "functional retention" an AAE category, or defining AAE "healing" as a reducing lesion.
3. Using only "success/failure" without Strindberg's uncertain category or the AAE healed/healing/non-healed terms.
4. Listing failures without separating early (flare-up) from late (persistent lesion).
