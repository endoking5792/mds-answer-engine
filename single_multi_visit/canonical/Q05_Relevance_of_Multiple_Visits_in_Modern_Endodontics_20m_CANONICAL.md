## 1. Introduction: why the question is asked

**Explanation.** "Modern" in the question is the hinge. Rotary nickel-titanium instruments have made single-visit treatment acceptable for most cases (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], so the examiner wants to know whether multiple visits still have a biological justification or are now habit. Siqueira and Rôças give the multiple-visit case its sharpest modern form: routine interappointment medication is indicated for infected teeth with apical periodontitis, primarily to eliminate residual biofilms that survived chemomechanical preparation, and secondarily to act as a barrier against reinfection between visits (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core
**Introduction.** Multiple-visit endodontics completes root canal treatment over two or more appointments, with obturation at the last; an intracanal medicament, usually calcium hydroxide, is placed between visits (Baumann and Beer, 2010). With rotary nickel-titanium instruments, single visit has become acceptable for most cases (Gopikrishna, 2021), so the relevance of multiple visits must now be justified biologically and clinically, not by habit or tradition.

---

## 2. Biological rationale for multiple visits

**Explanation.** The argument rests on three steps. First, instruments and irrigants leave bacteria behind: in Siqueira's specialist-performed series, 55% of canals in teeth with primary apical periodontitis were still culture-positive after chemomechanical preparation, falling to 9% after one week of calcium hydroxide/camphorated paramonochlorophenol/glycerin (CHPG) paste; the same chapter reports no significant decrease when calcium hydroxide was used in an inert vehicle such as glycerin (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b) [SRC]. The vehicle and additive matter, not calcium hydroxide alone. Second, residual bacteria matter: Sjögren et al's 5-year data showed 94% healing when cultures were negative before filling versus 68% when positive (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Third, entombment has a limit; above a bacterial threshold the filling cannot neutralise survivors (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022a) [SRC]. Each step is defensible, but the chain is weaker than it looks: step 1 is culture data, not healing data, and step 2 compared culture status, not visit number (see Q1, section 2).

#### ✎ Exam core
**Biological rationale.**
1. **Residual infection.** Adherent biofilm is not fully removed by instrumentation and irrigation (Gulabivala and Ng, 2022). In one specialist series, 55% of canals were culture-positive after preparation and 9% after one week of calcium hydroxide/camphorated paramonochlorophenol/glycerin (CHPG) paste; calcium hydroxide in plain glycerin gave no significant decrease (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b).
2. **Residual bacteria affect healing.** Sjögren et al (1997): 94% healing with negative cultures before filling versus 68% with positive cultures (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
3. **Medicament actions.** Calcium hydroxide dissolves tissue, kills bacteria, detoxifies antigenic material and releases slowly (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021); it inactivates endotoxin, which irrigants do not (Sikri, 2019).
4. **Limit of entombment.** Above a bacterial threshold the filling cannot entomb survivors (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022a).
5. **Diagnostic pause.** The interval lets the clinician gauge the periapical response before filling (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021) and re-check missed canals (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

## 3. Where multiple visits remain indicated today

**Explanation.** Even single-visit advocates keep a list of exceptions, and these are the strongest part of the modern case. Ingle's names intracanal bleeding, persistent exudate, severe preoperative pain and acute symptoms as situations needing a dressing (Goldberg, 2019) [SRC]; Cohen's says a patient in severe pain is not a prime candidate for single visit (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021) [SRC]; Grossman contraindicates single visit in nonsurgical retreatment and notes that one of the highest incidences of flare-ups occurs in retreatment (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Some procedures are multi-visit by design: regenerative endodontics usually needs two treatment visits, and calcium hydroxide apexification needs multiple observation visits (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021) [SRC]. The interval itself differs by purpose: Ingle's suggests 5-7 days between instrumentation and obturation in a vital case, to let periradicular tissues recover (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) [SRC], whereas Siqueira and Rôças schedule the second visit at least one week later in infected teeth, to give the medicament time to act (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core
**Current indications for multiple visits.**
- Intracanal bleeding, persistent exudate (weeping canal), severe preoperative pain, acute symptoms (Goldberg, 2019).
- Acute apical abscess or swelling: relieve pain first, obturate later (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021).
- Nonsurgical retreatment: single visit is contraindicated, and retreatment has one of the highest flare-up incidences (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Necrotic teeth with apical periodontitis, where two visits with medication give the most predictable infection control (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022a).
- Complex anatomy, doubt about missed canals, overinstrumentation, or insufficient time (Baumann and Beer, 2010; Weine, 2004).
- Procedures that are multi-visit by design: regenerative endodontics (two treatment visits) and calcium hydroxide apexification (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021).
- Inexperienced operators, uncooperative or limited-opening patients (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Practical advantages of multiple visits** (Baumann and Beer, 2010):
- Easy access to the apical canal if a flare-up occurs between visits.
- Opportunity to re-evaluate additional canals and the response of symptoms before filling.
- Shorter appointments: less patient fatigue, better cooperation and less operator fatigue.
- More flexible scheduling, and better control when retreatment time is hard to predict.
- Shorter appointments also suit patients with TMJ problems or limited mouth opening (Sikri, 2019).

**Protocol.** Calcium hydroxide paste placed to working length, access sealed with at least 3-5 mm of temporary cement (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b).

**Interval between visits.** At least one week in infected teeth, so the medicament can act (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b); 5-7 days in vital cases, so periradicular tissues recover before filling (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019).

## 4. Critical appraisal: what the evidence shows and the limits

**Explanation.** The clinical trials do not confirm the biological argument. The 2022 Cochrane review found no difference in radiological failure between single and multiple visits, with moderate certainty (Mergoni et al, 2022) [LIT]; Ingle's cites a review in which single visit was 6.3% better, not significantly (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) [SRC]. Against this, Siqueira and Rôças cite studies showing 10-20% better outcomes with two visits, a single-operator cohort favouring two visits in necrotic teeth, and judge the trial evidence low to medium in quality (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022a) [SRC]. Three weaknesses undercut the multiple-visit case: calcium hydroxide's antibacterial effect is limited in human canals (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]; temporaries can leak or break between visits (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC]; and more than two visits predisposes to secondary infection and reduced survival (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022a) [SRC]. Siqueira and Rôças also note that rapid single-visit disinfection methods have not yet been shown to reduce bacteria below conventional levels, so for now an interappointment medicament is still needed (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core
**Critical appraisal.**
| For multiple visits | Against / limitations |
|---|---|
| Bacteria survive preparation; CHPG paste reduces culture-positive canals (55% to 9%) (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b) | Culture reduction is not proof of better healing; plain calcium hydroxide in glycerin gave no significant decrease (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b) |
| Some studies: 10-20% better outcomes with two visits (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022a) | Cochrane 2022: no difference in radiological failure (Mergoni et al, 2022); one review 6.3% better with single visit, not significant (Rosenberg, Malek and Okamoto, 2019) |
| Rapid single-visit disinfection not yet proven equal (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b) | Calcium hydroxide has limited antibacterial effect in human canals (Baumann and Beer, 2010) |
| Interval allows reassessment and drainage | Temporaries may leak or break, contaminating the canal (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) |
| Fewer patients with pain in the first week (small subgroups; Mergoni et al, 2022) | More than two visits: secondary infection, reduced survival (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022a) |

**Appraisal.** The biological rationale is sound, but trials are underpowered and of low-to-medium quality, so neither superiority nor equivalence is proven (Ng and Gulabivala, 2021; Siqueira and Rôças, 2022a).

## 5. Conclusion

#### ✎ Exam core
Multiple visits remain relevant, but selectively. They are justified for infected teeth with apical periodontitis, symptomatic, bleeding or exudative canals, retreatment and multi-visit procedures such as regeneration and apexification. They are unnecessary for most vital teeth, and treatment should not exceed two visits (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022a). The decision depends on the tooth's infection status, case complexity and operator skill rather than routine. Their biological rationale is strong, but the clinical trials have not shown that it translates into better radiographic healing (Mergoni et al, 2022), and calcium hydroxide itself is less reliable than once believed (Baumann and Beer, 2010). Until single-visit disinfection methods are proven, a second visit with an effective medicament remains the cautious choice for infected teeth (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim: a calcium hydroxide dressing between visits improves healing.** Textbooks support it biologically (culture-positive canals fall from 55% to 9% with CHPG paste, but not significantly with calcium hydroxide in plain glycerin; Siqueira and Rôças, 2022b) [SRC], but the pooled RCTs show no difference in radiological failure (Mergoni et al, 2022) [LIT] and *Endodontology* reports limited antibacterial efficacy (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. The evidence is laboratory and culture-based on one side and underpowered radiographic trials on the other, so a modest benefit in necrotic teeth with lesions can neither be confirmed nor excluded [Likely]. Clinically, keep the second visit for infected, symptomatic or complex cases rather than for every tooth.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Writing only advantages of multiple visits: the question says "critically".
2. Presenting multiple visits as the default for vital teeth.
3. Omitting the "no more than two visits" rule.
4. Treating culture reduction as proof of better healing, or crediting plain calcium hydroxide with the CHPG result.
5. Forgetting multi-visit-by-design procedures (regeneration, apexification).
