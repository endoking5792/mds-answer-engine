## STUDY MODE

**Definition and composition.** Triple antibiotic paste (TAP) is a combination of three systemic antibiotics — **ciprofloxacin, metronidazole, and minocycline in a 1:1:1 ratio**, carried in a macrogol/propylene-glycol vehicle (Mathew and Nagendrababu, 2021) — placed as an intracanal medicament rather than given systemically.

**Rationale.** The three agents together cover the broad, mixed facultative-anaerobic/obligate-anaerobic flora typically found in the necrotic, infected, immature tooth, a situation where a single antibiotic's narrower spectrum is considered inadequate (Mathew and Nagendrababu, 2021; Berman and Hargreaves, 2021).

**Principal indication.** TAP is the standard intracanal medicament used in **regenerative endodontic procedures (REPs)** — revascularisation of immature, necrotic, infected permanent teeth with an open apex — where thorough disinfection without aggressive instrumentation (to preserve the stem-cell niche) is required before inducing bleeding and a blood-clot scaffold (Mathew and Nagendrababu, 2021).

**Limitations/criticisms.**
- **Tooth discoloration**: minocycline causes a well-documented grey/black crown discoloration, a significant cosmetic problem especially in anterior teeth (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021).
- **Stem-cell cytotoxicity**: the originally described thick paste concentration has been shown to be cytotoxic to stem cells of the apical papilla, which revascularisation depends on; current protocols favour a dilute mixture (roughly 1-5 mg/mL) rather than a thick paste (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021).
- **Bacterial resistance risk**: intracanal antibiotic use carries a theoretical risk of selecting resistant strains (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021).
- **Modification**: a **double antibiotic paste (DAP)** — dropping minocycline, retaining ciprofloxacin and metronidazole — is increasingly preferred specifically to avoid discoloration, with Ca(OH)₂ also considered as an alternative in some protocols (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021).

## EXAM MODE
*(estimated writing time: ~9 minutes)*

**Definition.** TAP = ciprofloxacin + metronidazole + minocycline (1:1:1) in a macrogol/propylene-glycol vehicle, used as an intracanal (not systemic) medicament.

**Indication.** Standard medicament for regenerative endodontic procedures in immature, necrotic, infected permanent teeth.

**Advantages.** Broad combined antibacterial spectrum covering mixed anaerobic endodontic flora of the necrotic immature tooth.

**Disadvantages.** Minocycline-related crown discoloration; cytotoxicity to apical-papilla stem cells at high/thick-paste concentration; resistance risk; evidence of efficacy against multispecies biofilms remains unclear.

**Modern modification.** Double antibiotic paste (drop minocycline) or a dilute TAP concentration (1-5 mg/mL) used instead of the original thick paste, to limit discoloration and cytotoxicity.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- TAP = ciprofloxacin + metronidazole + minocycline, 1:1:1.
- Standard medicament for regenerative endodontic procedures.
- Minocycline → discoloration; this is why DAP (drop minocycline) is now often preferred.
- Thick-paste concentration is cytotoxic to apical-papilla stem cells — dilute to 1-5 mg/mL in current protocols.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Confusing TAP's components with Ledermix's components (triamcinolone + demeclocycline) — different drug classes and purposes.
- Omitting the discoloration/cytotoxicity disadvantages, which are the most frequently tested part of this topic.
