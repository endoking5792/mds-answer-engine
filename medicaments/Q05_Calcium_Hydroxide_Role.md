## STUDY MODE

**1. Historical introduction.** Calcium hydroxide was introduced to dentistry by Hermann in 1920 (Mathew and Nagendrababu, 2021; Berman and Hargreaves, 2021) and remains, nearly a century later, the single most widely used intracanal medicament and pulp-therapy material in endodontics.

**2. Physical and chemical nature.** Ca(OH)₂ is a white, odourless powder, poorly soluble in water (<0.2% dissociates to Ca²⁺ and OH⁻ at body temperature) (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021). In air it slowly converts to calcium carbonate (CaCO₃), a reaction of little clinical consequence but which gives an aged paste a granular feel (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021). High alkalinity (pH ≈ 12.5) is the basis of virtually all its biological actions (Mathew and Nagendrababu, 2021).

**3. Mechanism of action.**
   - **Antibacterial**: the high pH damages the bacterial cytoplasmic membrane, denatures structural and enzymatic protein, and damages bacterial DNA (Mathew and Nagendrababu, 2021); it is a slow-acting antiseptic — in-vitro complete killing of enterococci needs roughly 24 hours of direct contact (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021).
   - **Lipopolysaccharide (LPS/endotoxin) hydrolysis**: Ca(OH)₂ hydrolyses the lipid moiety of bacterial LPS, inactivating its biological activity and reducing the inflammatory stimulus from dead cell-wall material that would otherwise persist in the canal (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021) — a genuinely distinct action from simple bacterial killing.
   - **Hard-tissue induction**: the alkaline environment is believed to stimulate alkaline-phosphatase activity and fibroblast/osteoblast-like differentiation at an exposed pulp or periapical wound surface, promoting mineralised-tissue (dentin bridge/cementum-like) deposition (classical rationale, Mathew and Nagendrababu, 2021).

**4. Clinical roles/uses in endodontics.**
   - **Interappointment intracanal medicament** in infected canals with apical periodontitis — the most common current use ([[Q01_Intracanal_Medicaments_General]]).
   - **Apexification** of an immature, necrotic, open-apex tooth — long-term Ca(OH)₂ dressing (historically up to 1-2 years) to induce a calcific apical barrier before obturation, now increasingly supplanted by single-visit MTA/bioceramic apexification (Spångberg and Haapasalo, 2019).
   - **Direct/indirect pulp capping and pulpotomy** in vital-pulp therapy, inducing a dentin bridge over an exposed or near-exposed pulp.
   - **Perforation repair and internal/external resorption management** — as a biologically compatible interim dressing.
   - **Combination vehicles**: mixed with camphorated paramonochlorophenol (CMCP) or 0.12% chlorhexidine for a broader/synergistic antimicrobial spectrum, or with a viscous vehicle (glycerine/polyethylene glycol/propylene glycol) for slower release and easier handling (Mathew and Nagendrababu, 2021).

**5. Clinical/handling protocol.** Mixed with sterile water or saline (aqueous vehicle preferred for maximal dissociation/antibacterial effect) and placed to full working length with a lentulo spiral, or delivered from a prefilled single-dose syringe system (Calasept/Calciject); a confirmatory radiograph verifies homogeneous fill (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021).

**6. Accidents and complications.** Because it is a strong base, periapical extrusion risks irreversible soft-tissue/nerve injury and, if it reaches the maxillary sinus or mandibular canal, serious complications; ocular contact can cause blindness (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021) — clinicians must take explicit precautions.

**7. Limitations — "critical appraisal."** 
   - Removal is frequently incomplete: residue covers 20-45% of canal-wall surface even after copious NaOCl/EDTA/saline irrigation, which can shorten ZOE-sealer setting time and compromise the final seal (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021).
   - It is NOT effective against several important endodontic pathogens, notably *E. faecalis* and *C. albicans* (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021).
   - Dentin itself can inactivate Ca(OH)₂'s antibacterial activity in vitro, and at least one clinical study found MORE bacteria-positive canals after Ca(OH)₂ medication than immediately after instrumentation (Berman and Hargreaves, 2021) — a genuinely counter-intuitive finding.
   - A systematic review/meta-analysis (Sathorn et al., cited in Haapasalo and Shen, 2019) concluded Ca(OH)₂ has only limited effectiveness in eliminating bacteria from human canals when assessed by culture technique.
   - Long-term apexification-duration Ca(OH)₂ was linked to weakened root dentin in some studies, though a more recent six-month study did not detect such weakening versus controls (Spångberg and Haapasalo, 2019) — a live, unresolved disagreement.

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

The textbook-era position (classical 1970s-80s Swedish studies, e.g., Byström, Claesson and Sundqvist, 1985) held that interappointment Ca(OH)₂ reliably renders canals bacteria-free (34/35 canals in one widely cited study). Later, methodologically similar but larger/more recent studies and a formal systematic review (Sathorn et al.) found this benefit to be smaller and less consistent — some reporting residual flora in 7-35% of canals, others reporting bacterial counts that actually rose after medication [Certain, both findings are directly sourced]. The two bodies of evidence are not strictly contradictory once study heterogeneity (sampling method, medication duration, baseline infection burden) is considered, but the clinical take-away has shifted: Ca(OH)₂ should be regarded as a USEFUL, not a GUARANTEED, disinfecting step, and complete bacterial elimination should not be assumed from its use alone [Likely].

## EXAM MODE
*(estimated writing time: ~32 minutes)*

**Introduction.** Calcium hydroxide (Hermann, 1920) is the most extensively used material across almost every branch of clinical endodontics, valued for its combined antibacterial and hard-tissue-inductive properties.

**Mechanism of action** (high pH 12.5): direct antibacterial action (membrane/protein/DNA damage); LPS/endotoxin hydrolysis; hard-tissue induction at a wound surface.

**Clinical uses — table:**

| Use | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Interappointment intracanal medicament | Sustained antibacterial action between visits in infected canals |
| Apexification | Induces calcific apical barrier in immature necrotic teeth |
| Pulp capping/pulpotomy | Induces dentin-bridge formation over exposed/near-exposed pulp |
| Perforation/resorption management | Biocompatible interim dressing, reduces inflammatory resorption |
| Combined with CMCP/CHX | Broadens/potentiates antimicrobial spectrum |

**Handling.** Aqueous vehicle (water/saline) for maximum dissociation; viscous vehicle (glycerine/PEG) for slower release; delivered via lentulo or prefilled syringe; confirm fill radiographically.

**Limitations (critical appraisal).** Incomplete removal (20-45% residue); ineffective vs *E. faecalis*/*C. albicans*; dentin can inactivate its antibacterial action; systematic-review evidence shows only limited added bacterial reduction over instrumentation alone; extrusion risk (nerve injury, sinus/mandibular-canal involvement, blindness on ocular contact); possible root-dentin weakening with prolonged use (contested).

**Conclusion.** Calcium hydroxide remains the benchmark intracanal medicament and vital-pulp-therapy agent for its unique combined antibacterial/hard-tissue-inductive action, but modern evidence tempers the classical claim of reliable canal sterilisation and demands awareness of its handling risks and real limitations against resistant pathogens.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Hermann, 1920 — always state the historical attribution.
- High pH (12.5) is the single mechanism underlying BOTH antibacterial action and hard-tissue induction — link the two rather than listing separately.
- LPS/endotoxin hydrolysis is a distinct, examinable action beyond simple bacterial killing.
- NOT effective against *E. faecalis*/*C. albicans* — a frequently tested limitation.
- 20-45% residue left on canal walls even after thorough irrigation — a specific, quotable figure.
- Extrusion complications: nerve injury, sinus/mandibular canal, blindness (eye contact) — list all three.
- Root-dentin-weakening with long-term use is a genuinely contested point — present both sides if asked to critically appraise.
