## STUDY MODE

Drug therapy in endodontics spans five functionally distinct categories, used at different stages of treatment:

**1. Local anaesthetics** — used to achieve pulpal/periradicular anaesthesia for instrumentation; lignocaine (lidocaine) 2% with 1:80,000 adrenaline, 2-4 mL, is the standard safe and effective agent (Harty's Ch.2); articaine and prilocaine are alternatives, the latter used with felypressin in true lidocaine allergy.

**2. Sedative/anxiolytic drugs** — inhaled nitrous oxide/oxygen or intravenous midazolam for anxious patients; rarely required for routine root canal treatment but more often used for periradicular surgery (Harty's Ch.2).

**3. Intracanal medicaments** — calcium hydroxide, chlorhexidine, antibiotic pastes, steroid-antibiotic combinations; placed within the canal between appointments (full classification in [[Q01_Intracanal_Medicaments_General]]).

**4. Systemic antibiotics** — reserved for spreading cellulitis or systemic signs of infection, NOT for pain control or routine irreversible pulpitis (evidence and regimens in [[Q07_Antibiotics_and_Analgesics_in_RCT]]).

**5. Analgesics** — NSAIDs (ibuprofen) first-line, paracetamol as alternative, opioids for severe pain; pre-emptive dosing reduces post-treatment pain (full detail in [[Q04_Analgesics_and_Hot_Tooth]]).

A sixth, narrower category worth naming is **drugs used for specific special situations** — corticosteroid-antibiotic pastes for acute inflammatory pain/trauma-resorption (see [[Q08_Corticosteroids_in_Endodontics]]), and antibiotic cocktails specifically for regenerative endodontic procedures in immature necrotic teeth (see [[Q09_Triple_Antibiotic_Paste]]).

## EXAM MODE
*(estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

**Classification of drugs used in endodontics — table:**

| Category | Example | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Local anaesthetic | 2% lignocaine + 1:80,000 adrenaline | Pulpal/periradicular anaesthesia for instrumentation |
| Sedative/anxiolytic | N₂O/O₂, IV midazolam | Anxiety control, mainly for surgery |
| Intracanal medicament | Ca(OH)₂, CHX, antibiotic paste | Interappointment disinfection |
| Systemic antibiotic | Amoxicillin, metronidazole | Spreading infection/systemic signs only |
| Analgesic | Ibuprofen, paracetamol | Pain control, pre-emptive and postoperative |
| Steroid-antibiotic combination | Ledermix, Odontopaste | Rapid anti-inflammatory relief in selected cases |

**Conclusion.** Drug therapy in endodontics is multi-stage and multi-purpose — from achieving anaesthesia before treatment begins, through intracanal disinfection during treatment, to systemic antimicrobial/analgesic support only where specifically indicated; no single drug category substitutes for correct mechanical/surgical management.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Six functional categories: local anaesthetic, sedative, intracanal medicament, systemic antibiotic, analgesic, steroid-antibiotic combination.
- Systemic antibiotics ≠ pain control; reserve for spreading infection/systemic signs.
- Lignocaine 2% + 1:80,000 adrenaline is the standard local anaesthetic regimen to quote.
- Cross-link each category to its specific clinical indication — examiners reward this linkage over a bare drug list.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Answering only about intracanal medicaments when the question asks about "drugs in endodontics" broadly.
- Omitting local anaesthetics and sedatives entirely.
- Blurring systemic antibiotics with intracanal antibiotic medicaments as if they were the same category.
