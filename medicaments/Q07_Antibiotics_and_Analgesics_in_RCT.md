## STUDY MODE

**1. Systemic antibiotics — general pharmacology.** Antibiotics are either bactericidal (kill susceptible bacteria) or bacteriostatic (arrest multiplication, relying on host defence to complete clearance) (Harty's Ch.2). Critically, antibiotics **do not relieve pain or reduce swelling** directly (Harty's Ch.2) — their role is purely antimicrobial.

**2. Evidence-based indications.** Given insufficient evidence of benefit, antibiotics **should not be used to treat irreversible pulpitis** (Harty's Ch.2, citing Cope et al. and related evidence review). Evidence is also only "very low quality" for systemic antibiotics in adults with symptomatic apical periodontitis or acute apical abscess without spreading signs (Harty's Ch.2) [Certain, directly sourced]. Antibiotics should therefore be **reserved specifically for: a spreading cellulitis, or systemic signs of infection** (fever, malaise, lymphadenopathy, trismus) — not for localised pain or an uncomplicated abscess that can be managed by drainage/pulp extirpation alone (Harty's Ch.2).

**3. Stewardship.** Overuse must be avoided to limit the rise of resistant organisms; the UK's Antibiotic Guardian campaign (Public Health England) and PHE/NICE antimicrobial-stewardship guidelines are cited as the drivers of this more restrictive, evidence-based prescribing culture (Harty's Ch.2) [SRC].

**4. Regimens** (Harty's Table 2-2, "Useful Antibacterial Drugs" for treatment of spreading infection): phenoxymethylpenicillin 250 mg, 1-2 tablets 6-hourly for 4-7 days; amoxicillin 250 mg, 1-2 capsules 8-hourly for 4-7 days; co-amoxiclav (Augmentin) 375 mg, 1-2 capsules 8-hourly for 4-7 days; metronidazole 200 mg, 1-2 tablets 8-hourly for 3-5 days (added for anaerobic cover, e.g., in combination with penicillin for a spreading odontogenic infection).

**5. Analgesics in root canal treatment (compressed; full detail in [[Q04_Analgesics_and_Hot_Tooth]]).** NSAID (ibuprofen) first-line for inflammatory pain; paracetamol if NSAID contraindicated (maximum 4 g/day — risk of fatal liver damage if exceeded, Harty's Ch.2); may combine NSAID + paracetamol; opioids reserved for severe, refractory pain.

**6. Combined clinical logic.** In a typical infected/symptomatic case: pulp extirpation or drainage is the definitive treatment; an NSAID/paracetamol is given routinely for pain; a systemic antibiotic is added ONLY if there is a spreading cellulitis or systemic involvement — never as a substitute for operative treatment and never for pain alone.

## EXAM MODE
*(estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

**Antibiotics in root canal treatment.**
- Mechanism: bactericidal or bacteriostatic; no direct analgesic/anti-inflammatory action.
- NOT indicated for irreversible pulpitis or for an uncomplicated, localised apical abscess/periodontitis — evidence is low-quality/insufficient for benefit in these.
- Indicated ONLY for spreading cellulitis or systemic signs of infection.
- Regimens: phenoxymethylpenicillin, amoxicillin, co-amoxiclav, metronidazole (as per Table 2-2 dosing above).
- Stewardship: avoid overuse to limit antibiotic resistance; follow PHE/NICE-type guidelines.

**Analgesics in root canal treatment.**
- NSAID (ibuprofen 600-800 mg) first-line; paracetamol (max 4 g/day) if contraindicated; combination use permitted; opioid for severe pain only.
- Pre-emptive dosing reduces postoperative pain.
- Never a substitute for definitive operative treatment.

**Conclusion.** Antibiotics and analgesics serve two different, non-interchangeable purposes in root canal treatment — antimicrobial control of spreading infection versus pain relief — and evidence-based practice restricts systemic antibiotics to a narrow, specific indication rather than routine use.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Antibiotics do NOT relieve pain — state this explicitly, it is the examiner's favourite single fact.
- NOT indicated for irreversible pulpitis (evidence-based, low/insufficient-quality data against routine use).
- Indicated only for spreading cellulitis/systemic signs.
- Know Table 2-2 regimens (names + dose + duration) verbatim.
- Paracetamol max 4 g/day — hepatotoxicity risk if exceeded.
- Antibiotic stewardship context (resistance) is worth one line for a "critically discuss" framing.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Recommending antibiotics for pain or for routine, uncomplicated apical periodontitis.
- Omitting the stewardship/resistance point.
- Giving analgesic detail without the antibiotic-indication detail, or vice versa — the question asks for both.
