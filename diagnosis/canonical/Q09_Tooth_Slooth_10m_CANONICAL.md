## 1. Definition and design

### Explanation

The Tooth Slooth (Professional Results, Laguna Niguel, CA) and the FracFinder are commercial bite-test devices (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]. Berman and Tamse describe a small pyramid with a flattened top placed on one cusp (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC]; Grossman's calls it a small, pyramid-shaped plastic bite block (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC] with a small concavity at the apex for the cusp (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Abbott: the opposite side is flat and may be serrated (Abbott, 2019) [SRC]; Berman and Rotstein call the tip a small cupped-out area (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]. The descriptions agree in substance: a concave tip on one cusp, a flat surface for the opposing teeth. The books differ on the FracFinder's maker: HuFriedy in Cohen's (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC], Denbur, Oak Brook in Harty's (Chandler and Chong, 2017) [SRC] and Ingle's (Bakland, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** The Tooth Slooth (Professional Results, Laguna Niguel, CA) is a plastic bite-test device that loads one cusp at a time to locate a cracked tooth or cusp (Berman and Rotstein, 2021).

**Design**
- Small pyramid with a concavity at its apex that seats on one cusp (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Flat (sometimes serrated) opposite surface on which the opposing teeth bite (Abbott, 2019).
- FracFinder is a similar device (Berman and Rotstein, 2021).

## 2. Indications and principle

### Explanation

Bite tests are indicated when the patient has pain on biting (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC], especially poorly localised biting pain with a suspected cusp, tooth or root fracture (Chandler and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. Apical periodontitis hurts wherever pressure is applied to the crown (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC], while a crack hurts only when one cusp or section is loaded in a certain direction (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]; the device places the force on one cusp at a time (Abbott, 2019) [SRC]. Biting separates the fractured segments temporarily (Chandler and Chong, 2017) [SRC]; Kahler et al, cited by Bakland, explain release pain by fluid movement as the crack rapidly closes (Bakland, 2019) [SRC]. Duncan, Smith and Cooper (Harty's) stress the pressure phase instead: biting wedges the crack and moves fluid in the crack and communicating tubules, provoking pain in an already inflamed pulp (Duncan, Smith and Cooper, 2017) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Indications:** pain on biting or chewing, poorly localised; suspected cracked tooth, fractured cusp or root fracture; to separate apical periodontitis from a crack (Chandler and Chong, 2017).

**Principle:** loading one cusp opens the crack (segments separate); on release the crack closes rapidly and dentinal fluid moves, causing sharp pain (Bakland, 2019).

## 3. Technique

### Explanation

Test adjacent and contralateral teeth first as controls (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC], so the patient knows what is normal (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC], and explain the press-versus-release difference beforehand (Witherspoon and Regan, 2025) [SRC]. Load each cusp in sequence (Witherspoon and Regan, 2025) [SRC]: the patient bites slowly to full closure (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC], holds firm pressure a few seconds and releases quickly (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]; note whether pain comes during pressure or on release (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]. Bakland: place it on the filling, then on the cusps, to separate a leaking restoration from a crack (Bakland, 2019) [SRC]. Abbott adds that cusps in the opposing arch should also be tested, as the pain may be referred (Abbott, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Technique**
1. Explain; test a contralateral and adjacent tooth first as control (Berman and Rotstein, 2021).
2. Seat the concave tip on one cusp; patient bites slowly on the flat side to full closure.
3. Hold firm pressure a few seconds; release quickly.
4. Record pain on pressure or on release; repeat on every cusp, then on the restoration (Bakland, 2019).

## 4. Interpretation

### Explanation

Pain on release is a common finding with a cracked tooth (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC], called "rebound" or "relief" pain (Bakland, 2019) [SRC]; Grossman's calls it a strong indication of a crown-originating fracture (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Sikri states sharp pain on release rather than biting indicates a crack (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Witherspoon and Regan also link release pain with a fracture (Witherspoon and Regan, 2025) [SRC], as do Baumann and Beer (Baumann and Beer, 2008) [SRC]. Berman and Tamse are less strict: sharp pain may occur on pressure or release (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], and it reproduces the chief complaint (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC]; Gulabivala and Ng agree it may be felt on pressure or release (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC]. Abbott disagrees with the release rule on data: citing Abbott and Leow, among patients with cracks in teeth with reversible pulpitis 68% had pain on application of pressure, 8% on release, 15% on both and 9% no pain on biting (Abbott, 2019) [SRC], so biting pain is not specific enough as a test on its own (Abbott, 2019) [SRC]. The PubMed abstract of Abbott and Leow confirms the population (100 consecutive teeth with reversible pulpitis from cracks) and that 80 needed no endodontic treatment under a conservative protocol, but it does not give the pressure and release percentages, which therefore rest on Abbott's chapter (Abbott and Leow, 2009) [LIT]. Release pain therefore supports a crack but its absence does not exclude one [Likely]. A non-vital cracked tooth gives a periodontal rather than dentinal sensation (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

| Finding | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Sharp pain on release from one cusp | Cracked tooth or cusp (Gopikrishna, 2021; Sikri, 2019) |
| Pain on loading every cusp, plus percussion tenderness | Apical periodontitis |
| Pain only on the restoration | Leaking restoration (Bakland, 2019) |
| Periodontal, not sharp dentinal, sensation | Crack in a non-vital tooth (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) |

Note: Berman and Tamse (2021) accept pain on pressure or release; Abbott and Leow, cited by Abbott (2019), found in cracked teeth with reversible pulpitis 68% pain on pressure, 8% on release, 15% both; absent release pain does not exclude a crack.

## 5. Comparison with other bite tests and crack adjuncts

### Explanation

Alternatives: cotton-tip applicators, toothpicks, orangewood sticks, rubber polishing wheels (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]; Burlew wheels and moist cotton rolls (Bakland, 2019) [SRC]; rolled rubber dam or an inlay seater (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC]. Gutmann and Lovdahl note most clinicians prefer semi-hard stopping or an orangewood stick (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC], whereas Abbott calls a purpose-made device such as the Tooth Slooth or FracFinder the most useful (Abbott, 2019) [SRC]. Limitation: repeatability is unpredictable because the load must align with the crack (Krishnan and Swain, 2025) [SRC]. Other adjuncts: history (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], transillumination, since fractures block light (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC], methylene blue (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]; in a study cited by Krishnan and Swain, none of transillumination, DIAGNOcam, microscope or macrophotography had high accuracy against micro-CT (Krishnan and Swain, 2025) [SRC], and a high-power microscope best confirms absence of a crack (Krishnan and Swain, 2025) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

| Device | Comment |
|---|---|
| Tooth Slooth / FracFinder | Loads a single cusp; most useful (Abbott, 2019) |
| Cotton roll, cotton-tip applicator | Loads whole tooth; less selective |
| Orangewood stick, stopping | Preferred by many clinicians (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) |
| Rubber or Burlew wheel, rolled rubber dam | Alternative cusp-loading aids (Bakland, 2019; Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) |

**Limitation:** load must align with the crack, so a negative test does not exclude it (Krishnan and Swain, 2025). Combine with transillumination, methylene blue, magnification and restoration removal.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Pyramid with concave tip on one cusp; flat side for opposing teeth.
- Test controls first; load each cusp; hold, then release quickly.
- Pain on release suggests a crack (most books), but in Abbott and Leow's data 68% hurt on pressure, 8% on release, 15% both; pain on every cusp = apical periodontitis.
- Negative test does not rule out a crack.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Writing that release pain is essential for a crack diagnosis: most cracked teeth in Abbott and Leow's data (cited by Abbott, 2019) hurt on pressure, not release.
- Omitting the control tooth.
- Not testing each cusp separately.
- Presenting the bite test as conclusive.

## MNEMONIC
**"Seat, Squeeze, Snap"**: seat on one cusp, squeeze to full closure, snap open and note release pain.
