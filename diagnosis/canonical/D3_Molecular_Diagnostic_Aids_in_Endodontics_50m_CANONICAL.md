## 1. Introduction: the diagnostic gap that molecular aids try to close

### Explanation

Galicia and Khan set out what the ideal pulp test would be: for conservative treatment of pulpitis "an accurate diagnostic method that is quick, non-invasive and quantifiable should be available for chairside use", able to stage inflammation and find the threshold between pulp that can and cannot be saved (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. Fouad and Levin put it bluntly: combined subjective and objective findings "are frequently insufficient in reaching definitive diagnosis", particularly in deciding whether vital inflamed pulp is reversibly or irreversibly inflamed (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC]. Present tests fall short in two ways.

- **They do not stage disease.** Thermal and electric tests detect vital tissue well, but "the lingering predicament with these tests is their inability to stage the severity of pulpal disease" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. EPT "cannot be used to detect the presence or extent of inflammation" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]; Fouad and Levin agree that "pulp testing cannot determine the degree of pulp inflammation" (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC].
- **They test nerves, not the tissue.** "Rather than measuring blood flow, which is the hallmark of tissue vitality, these tests stimulate nerves" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC].

Accuracy figures for vitality (not inflammation) are reasonable: cold had the highest accuracy among thermal tests (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]; Fouad and Levin quote about 82% to 84% for traditional testing (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC].

**The histology problem.** Histology is the gold standard, but attempts to classify pulp disease by histology and symptoms failed, and "the pulp must be removed in order to examine it histologically" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. Classic extraction studies found that "clinical symptoms generally did not correlate with histopathological findings" (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC]. Pain is an unreliable guide because painless pulpitis is common, but the books give different figures:

| Source | Painless pulpitis |
|---|---|
| Galicia and Khan, 2024 | "approximately 40% of cases" [SRC] |
| Fouad and Levin, 2021 | "about 40% to 60% of all pulpitis cases" (that lead to necrosis and asymptomatic apical periodontitis) [SRC] |

Seltzer and Bender give a molecular reason: "Not all cases of irreversible pulpitis are associated with pain", which may reflect neurotransmitter inhibitors, and opioid receptors have been identified in human dental pulp (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]; Fouad and Levin make the same point about peripheral opioid receptors (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC].

**Why it matters now.** Inflammation in carious teeth "may be locally limited to the pulpal tissue adjacent to the carious lesion", and new grading of pulpitis as mild, moderate and severe allows vital pulp therapy (VPT) for more teeth (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. A test that stages inflammation would decide between capping, pulpotomy and pulpectomy. Without it, "the resulting treatment becomes a defensive reaction to simply extirpate the dental pulp", worst in teeth with a full coverage restoration or recent trauma (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. Fransson and Bjørndal: "So far, no diagnostic tests, biomarkers or sets of symptoms give conclusive guidance" (Fransson and Bjørndal, 2024) [SRC].

Fouad and Levin link the two problems: the lack of correlation between histology and symptoms "may be explained by recent advances in the science of pulp biology", since many mediators "tend to reduce the pain threshold" and their nature and quantity "cannot be determined from histological analysis, without the use of very specialized staining techniques and molecular assays" (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC]. That is the case for molecular diagnosis.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Pulpal diagnosis today rests on history, thermal and electric tests and radiographs. These tests tell whether the pulp is vital, but they stimulate nerves rather than measure blood flow, and they cannot stage inflammation (Galicia and Khan, 2024; Fouad and Levin, 2021). Histology is the gold standard, but it needs the pulp removed, and classic studies showed that symptoms correlate poorly with histology (Fouad and Levin, 2021). Painless pulpitis is common: about 40% of cases (Galicia and Khan, 2024) or 40% to 60% (Fouad and Levin, 2021).

With vital pulp therapy now possible in "irreversible pulpitis" and newer grading into mild, moderate and severe pulpitis, the clinician needs to know how much of the pulp is inflamed (Galicia and Khan, 2024). The ideal test should be quick, non-invasive, quantifiable and chairside, and able to separate salvageable from unsalvageable pulp (Galicia and Khan, 2024). No current test, biomarker or set of symptoms gives conclusive guidance (Fransson and Bjørndal, 2024).

**Molecular diagnostic aids** are tests that measure host molecules (enzymes, cytokines, neuropeptides, lipid mediators, gene and microRNA expression) in pulpal blood, dentinal fluid, gingival crevicular fluid (GCF) or periapical exudate, or that identify microbes by their nucleic acids, to give an objective, biologically based diagnosis.

## 2. Biological basis

### Explanation

"The dental pulp is an immunocompetent tissue with cytokines, cell surface receptors and other protein markers", differentially expressed in inflamed and normal pulps (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. Dammaschke explains why they are useful early: even before the cellular immune response, cytokines (IL-1, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, TNF and others) are released, and "these molecules, expressed in the early stages of pulpal inflammation, can serve as markers" (Dammaschke, 2024) [SRC]. Inflammation "typically progresses from the coronal to the apical pulp", so a marker could in principle stage it (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC].

The pulp is not isolated: "a local neurogenic spread of the inflammatory response can occur from the pulp to the surrounding periodontal tissues" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. This is why GCF, sampled outside the tooth, can carry pulpal signals. In the periapex, inflammation and bone destruction occur "within the periradicular tissue prior to pulp tissue necrosis" (Murray, 2010) [SRC], and Fouad and Khan note "the pulp does not have to be totally necrotic for periapical bone resorption" (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Biological basis.** The pulp is immunocompetent; inflamed pulps express cytokines, receptors and protein markers differently from normal pulps (Galicia and Khan, 2024). Cytokines such as IL-1, IL-6, IL-8 and TNF are released early, before the cellular response, so they can act as markers (Dammaschke, 2024). Inflammation spreads from coronal to apical pulp, so marker levels could stage it, and neurogenic spread to the periodontium explains why GCF reflects pulpal disease (Galicia and Khan, 2024). Periapical inflammation starts before total pulp necrosis (Murray, 2010).

## 3. Diagnostic fluids (where the sample comes from)

### Explanation

The books name four sampling sites. Dammaschke lists them together: "elevated levels of biomarkers from dentine fluid, pulp blood, gingival crevicular fluid and periapical fluid correlate with different stages" of pulpal inflammation (Dammaschke, 2024) [SRC]. Fouad and Levin: "sampling can be made from crevicular fluid, dentinal fluid, or the pulp directly" (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC].

- **Pulpal blood.** "Some studies have evaluated the molecular markers found in the pulp blood in pathologic and normal conditions" (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]; Fouad and Khan say only "a limited number of clinical studies" have done this at protein level (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC]. Dammaschke's proposed use is to "immediately examine blood from the pulp exposure site and decide whether direct pulp capping", partial or complete pulpotomy or extirpation is indicated (Dammaschke, 2024) [SRC]. Pulp blood is available only after exposure.
- **Dentinal fluid (DF).** "As the dentinal fluid contents may be a reflex of the pulp conditions, it can be potentially used as a diagnosis mean" (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]. Access "through the dentine will be required to collect DF", and collection "is challenging due to the small volume of analyte available" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC].
- **Gingival crevicular fluid.** "GCF can be extracted non-invasively" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC], which makes it the most attractive preoperative fluid; its drawback is "contamination from periodontal fluids and blood" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC].
- **Periapical exudate and root canal fluid.** Cytokine expression "from root canal sampling relates to clinical" findings (Murray, 2010) [SRC]. Fouad and Khan suggest "one could potentially assay a periapical lesion (through the root canal of a necrotic pulp or directly)" to tell whether it is developing, stable or healing (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Diagnostic fluids** (Dammaschke, 2024; Fouad and Levin, 2021; Galicia and Khan, 2024):

| Fluid | How collected | Advantage | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pulpal blood | At exposure, from the pulp wound | Direct sample of pulp | Only after exposure; invasive |
| Dentinal fluid | Through cut dentine, paper point or capillary | Reflects pulp state | Invasive; very small volume |
| GCF | Paper strip in the sulcus | Non-invasive, preoperative | Contamination by periodontal fluid and blood |
| Periapical exudate / root canal fluid | Through the canal of a necrotic tooth | Reflects periapical activity | Only after access |

## 4. Candidate biomarkers of pulpal inflammation

### Explanation

**Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs).** MMPs are zinc-dependent enzymes; the balance between activated MMPs and TIMPs "controls the extent of extracellular matrix remodeling" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC].
- MMP-9: in symptomatic irreversible pulpitis "MMP-9 was significantly increased and correlated with an overall increase in gelatinolytic activity" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. Its diagnostic value was shown in dentinal fluid: "This molecule was found to be significantly elevated in dentinal fluid of patients with irreversible pulpitis", "although this finding was not universally present in all patients studied" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. Fouad and Khan describe the same study: "a clear increase in levels of MMP9 in the symptomatic teeth as compared to controls" (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC]. Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira: "MMP-9 is a proteolytic enzyme secreted by inflammatory cells, and the increase in its levels may be indicative of soft tissue destruction" (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]; Fouad and Khan say it is secreted by neutrophils and "may be a marker of tissue destruction" (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC]. Ingle's: "MMP-9 levels may aid in the diagnosis of irreversible pulpitis, or correlate with symptomatic apical periodontitis" (Fouad and Huang, 2019) [SRC]. The Karrar review found limited data that high active MMP-9 levels "correlate with poorer outcomes of full coronal pulpotomy" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC], so MMP-9 may be prognostic as well as diagnostic. MMP-9 is the example used in the proposed chairside test (section 7).
- MMP-8: "painful stimulation of teeth induces an increase in MMP-8, sampled from the crevicular fluid" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC].
- MMP-2 and MMP-3: conflicting, "upregulated in one study" but downregulated in another (Fouad, 2012) [SRC].

**Cytokines.**
- IL-1: "IL-1 activity was significantly higher in human dental pulps with symptomatic caries lesions" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]; but in a gene-expression study "IL-1α and IL-1β were not significantly increased" in inflamed symptomatic pulps (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. In the meta-analysis IL-1β and TNF-α had low diagnostic accuracy (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC].
- IL-6, IL-8, IL-18: "were all significantly increased in teeth with painful pulpitis" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. IL-6 in symptomatic carious pulp was far above normal pulp but "less than that in periradicular lesions" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. Patients with irreversible pulpitis "had an almost 23-fold increase in IL-8 levels in the pulp" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]; IL-8 expression "is increased in teeth with symptomatic pulpitis when compared with asymptomatic pulpitis or normal pulps" (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC].
- IL-2: earlier elevated in symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, but later "present at comparable levels in normal, inflamed, and necrotic pulps" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC].
- C-reactive protein: elevated in pulps with irreversible pulpitis, "a finding that did not correlate with the patients'" serum CRP (Fouad, 2012) [SRC], so serum is not a substitute.
- Innate receptors: NOD2 gene expression "was found to be significantly upregulated in patients with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC].

**Neuropeptides.** Painful irreversible pulpitis teeth "contained increased levels of substance P or its receptor, CGRP or its receptor, neurokinin A, and NPY" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. In GCF, "levels of SP and neurokinin A increased in the gingival crevicular fluid of teeth with painful irreversible pulpitis", and "the levels returned to normal 1 week after pulpectomy" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. Fouad and Levin report crevicular neuropeptides raised compared with contralateral teeth (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC]. The exploratory use of matrix metalloproteases, substance P, interleukin-8 and neurokinin-A to diagnose vitality and stage inflammation has been carried out but is limited in scope (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. Galicia and Khan quote Rechenberg's review: GCF biomarkers rose during tooth pain and showed "a marked decrease" after endodontic treatment (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. A caution: severe irritation "resulted in a drop of the overall pulpal levels of SP and CGRP", possibly by depletion (Fouad, 2012) [SRC], so the relation is not linear.

**Lipid mediators and kinins.** "Patients with severe pulpal pain had significantly greater pulpal levels of PGE2 and PGF2α" than those with nonpainful pulpitis or normal pulp, and it has been suggested that PG levels "could be measured to determine the degree of pulpal inflammation of patients undergoing pulpotomy procedures in primary teeth" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. Bradykinin, "measured directly using microdialysis", showed "a 13-fold increase in BK level in irreversible pulpitis", and a "17-fold increase versus a 3-fold increase" in patients with current versus past pain (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. In pulpal blood, immunoglobulins, elastase and PGE2 "are increased in the inflamed pulp in comparison with healthy pulps" (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022; Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC].

**Oxidative enzymes.** Catalase "increases significantly in reversibly inflamed pulp", "an increase that is moderated as the inflammation is diagnosed to be irreversible" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. This is one of the few markers the books describe as behaving differently between reversible and irreversible pulpitis [Likely, single study].

## 5. Periapical and systemic markers

### Explanation

High IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-8 have been found in root canals of teeth with periradicular disease, and "a trend toward raised IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-8 concentrations has been demonstrated in symptomatic teeth" (Murray, 2010) [SRC]. "Neutrophil elastase was increased in periapical lesions that were symptomatic", correlated with PGE2, but "did not change following root canal instrumentation" (Fouad and Huang, 2019) [SRC].

**Lesion activity.** Fouad and Khan report that active lesions (by a RANKL/OPG ratio) "were associated with upregulation of TNF" and CXCL11, while inactive lesions upregulated TIMP1, TGF-beta1 and others; in another study active lesions "were found to have upregulated MMP-2, MMP-7, and MMP-9, whereas inactive lesions had upregulated TIMP-1" (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC].

**Granuloma versus cyst: the books disagree.**

| Source | Statement |
|---|---|
| Fouad and Huang, 2019 | "MMP-9 and MMP-13 were shown to be more prevalent in periapical granulomas than cysts" [SRC] |
| Fouad and Khan, 2020 | Several studies comparing the cellular and molecular content of granulomas and cysts "have failed to show notable differences" [SRC] |

**Systemic markers.** Acute endodontic infection causes systemic inflammation "with elevation of CRP and SAA", but chronic lesions "do not appear to be associated with CRP or SAA" (Fouad and Huang, 2019) [SRC]. A meta-analysis of 31 systemic markers found CRP, IL-1, IL-2, IL-6, ADMA and immunoglobulins "were increased in patients with apical periodontitis compared to controls", yet "serum CRP, IgA, IgE, IgG, and IgM were not" significantly different before and after treatment (Fouad and Huang, 2019) [SRC]. So systemic markers show association, not a usable tooth-level test [Likely].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Periapical and systemic markers.** Root canal and periapical exudate samples show raised IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-8, higher in symptomatic teeth (Murray, 2010). Neutrophil elastase is raised in symptomatic periapical lesions (Fouad and Huang, 2019). Active lesions show TNF, MMP-2, -7 and -9; inactive lesions show TIMP-1 and TGF-β1, so canal sampling may one day show whether a lesion is enlarging or healing (Fouad and Khan, 2020). Books disagree on granuloma versus cyst: MMP-9 and MMP-13 are more prevalent in granulomas (Fouad and Huang, 2019), but other studies found no notable molecular difference (Fouad and Khan, 2020). Systemically, CRP and SAA rise only in acute infection; several serum markers are raised in apical periodontitis but do not fall after treatment (Fouad and Huang, 2019).

## 6. Gene expression, microRNA and epigenetic markers

### Explanation

"A microarray study showed an association between" over- or under-expressed genes and pulpal status in patients treated chairside (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. Cytokines and mediators are "upregulated underneath a carious lesion, in a manner that correlates with the depth of caries" (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC]. In a clinical study, immune and cytokine genes "were expressed at relatively higher levels in the pulpitis group as compared to normal pulps" (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC].

MicroRNAs: research shows "the differential expression of microRNAs (miRNAs) in healthy and diseased dental pulp" which "may eventually assist in clinical predictions of pulp status" (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC]; Fouad and Khan found "36 human miRs were dysregulated in inflamed pulps" (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC]. "The role of epigenetic modifications in pulpitis is only just beginning to be explored" (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Gene expression and microRNA.** Microarrays map hundreds of genes at once; immune and cytokine genes are higher in pulpitis than normal pulp, and expression rises with caries depth (Fouad and Khan, 2020; Fouad and Levin, 2021). Thirty-six microRNAs are dysregulated in inflamed pulps, and miRNAs may help predict pulp status (Fouad and Khan, 2020; Fouad and Levin, 2021). Epigenetic markers (DNA methylation, histone change) are at an early research stage (Fouad and Khan, 2020).

## 7. Diagnostic accuracy and the proposed chairside test

### Explanation

Galicia and Khan reproduce the GRADE summary of a systematic review and meta-analysis (Karrar et al., as cited by them): "IL-8 and IL-6 can discriminate with high accuracy between healthy pulps and that with irreversible pulpitis", "while the diagnostic accuracy of TNFα and IL-1β is low"; but "None, however, of the biomolecule's studies can differentiate between reversible and irreversible pulpitis, where the distinction is needed most" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. Certainty for every outcome was Low or Very low (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC].

The proposed workflow (their Fig. 3.1, from Duncan): sample "taken using paperpoint, microcapillary tube or other collection vessel"; chairside or adjacent-lab analysis for a marker such as MMP-9; "level of biomarker compared with established reference standard"; an objective measure of pulpitis to inform treatment (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. Dammaschke similarly calls for "a chairside immediate test for inflammatory mediators" on pulp-exposure blood (Dammaschke, 2024) [SRC]. Fouad and Levin: "more accurate chair-side diagnostic methods are potentially feasible to develop" (Fouad and Levin, 2021) [SRC]. No book describes such a test as commercially available; Galicia and Khan note "a distinct lack of commercial drive" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC].

Until then, direct observation is the working substitute: "direct observation of the dental pulp using magnification is recommended", and the colour and intensity of bleeding on exposure "may provide a crude measure or surrogate marker of inflammation" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. The ESE position quoted by them says routine CBCT is not justified for assessing pulpitis (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Diagnostic accuracy.** A meta-analysis summarised by Galicia and Khan (2024) found:
- IL-6 and IL-8 separate healthy pulp from irreversible pulpitis with high accuracy.
- TNF-α and IL-1β have low accuracy.
- No biomarker separates reversible from irreversible pulpitis, the distinction clinicians most need.
- Limited data link high active MMP-9 to failure of full pulpotomy.
- Certainty of evidence is low to very low throughout.

**Proposed chairside test (flowchart)** (Galicia and Khan, 2024; Dammaschke, 2024): sample pulp blood or dentinal fluid with a paper point or microcapillary tube → chairside or adjacent-lab assay of a marker such as MMP-9 → compare with a reference standard → objective grade of pulpitis → choose capping, partial or full pulpotomy, or pulpectomy. No such test is yet in routine use; until then, direct inspection of the exposed pulp under magnification and the control of bleeding act as a crude surrogate (Galicia and Khan, 2024).

## 8. Limitations of host biomarker diagnosis

### Explanation

Galicia and Khan list the problems: costly, slow clinical studies with drop-outs; "contamination from periodontal fluids and blood"; "challenges in identifying and collecting pulp tissue from appropriate control teeth"; the small volume of inflamed pulp; and a lack of commercial interest (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC]. Inconsistency between studies is visible in Seltzer and Bender (MMP-2/-3, IL-1, IL-2) (Fouad, 2012) [SRC], and the MMP-9 rise was not universal (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. Neuropeptides fall with severe injury (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]. Most studies compare painful pulpitis with normal pulps; the reference standard is usually a clinical diagnosis, itself unreliable [Likely].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Limitations**
1. Most studies compare painful irreversible pulpitis with normal pulp; none separate reversible from irreversible pulpitis (Galicia and Khan, 2024).
2. Inconsistent results: MMP-2/-3, IL-1 and IL-2 vary between studies; MMP-9 is not raised in every patient (Fouad, 2012).
3. Some markers fall with severe injury (SP, CGRP depletion), so levels are not linear with severity (Fouad, 2012).
4. Sampling: DF volume is tiny and invasive; GCF is contaminated by periodontal fluid and blood; suitable control teeth are hard to find (Galicia and Khan, 2024).
5. Small studies, low-certainty evidence, no commercial chairside kit (Galicia and Khan, 2024).

## 9. Molecular detection of endodontic microbes

### Explanation

Culture misses the as-yet-uncultivated majority; molecular methods identify organisms by their genes. "The gene encoding the 16S rRNA has been widely accepted and used for bacterial identification": conserved regions allow broad-range primers, "while the variable regions contain unique signatures of the genus and species" (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC].

- **PCR.** Conceived by Kary Mullis in 1983; "repetitive cycles of DNA melting, primer annealing, and extension steps" (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC]. It can detect "as few as 10 bacterial cells in a sample"; culture using nonselective media can detect only 10^4 to 10^5 cultivable cells, and PCR is "at least 10- to 100-fold more sensitive" than the next most sensitive method (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC]. Variants: species-specific; multiplex PCR permits "the concomitant detection of different species"; nested PCR "shows increased sensitivity when compared to single PCR"; RT-PCR amplifies RNA targets (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC].
- **Real-time (quantitative) PCR** quantifies "individual target species as well as total bacteria in clinical samples"; chemistries are SYBR-Green and TaqMan, and "the TaqMan method can be more specific" (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC]. It can measure the antibacterial effect of treatment before and after (Siqueira and Rôças, 2021) [SRC].
- **Broad-range PCR with cloning and sequencing** "can detect the unexpected" (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC].
- **Checkerboard DNA-DNA hybridisation**, introduced by Socransky et al., "permits the simultaneous determination of the presence of a multitude of bacterial species"; the reverse-capture version uses 16S oligonucleotide probes that detect cultivable and uncultivated bacteria, whereas whole genomic probes detect only cultivable species (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC]. DNA microarrays such as HOMIM extend this (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC].
- **FISH** identifies organisms while giving "information about morphology, number, community architecture, and spatial relationships" (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC].
- **Next-generation sequencing** performs "massively parallel sequencing of clonally PCR amplified products"; 454 pyrosequencing was most used but Roche discontinued it in 2016 (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC]; low-abundance members can be disclosed (Siqueira and Rôças, 2021) [SRC]. Metagenomics "is the only one that remains to be used in endodontic microbiology research" (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC].

**Limitations.** Most assays are qualitative or semiquantitative (exceptions real-time PCR and microarrays); detection of dead cells "can be at the same time an advantage and a limitation", addressed by RT-PCR or viability-PCR with propidium monoazide; only a small aliquot of the sample enters each reaction (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC].

**Impact, with a numerical conflict.**

| Source | Species detected | Molecular only | Culture only | Both |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Siqueira and Rôças, 2017 | about 500 | about 45% | 32% | 33% [SRC] |
| Siqueira and Rôças, 2021 | more than 400 | about 45% | 32% | 23% [SRC] |

The 2017 figures add to 110%, so its "33%" is plainly an error; the 2021 figure of 23% makes the three add to 100% [Certain on the arithmetic; which total species figure is current is not resolved]. Community profiling moved thinking towards a "community-as-pathogen" concept, and communities of symptomatic lesions "are more similar to each other" (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC], which links microbial profile to clinical presentation, though no book presents this as a chairside diagnostic.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Molecular detection of microbes.** Culture misses fastidious and uncultivated species; molecular methods detect organisms by DNA or RNA, mostly the 16S rRNA gene, whose conserved regions allow broad-range primers and variable regions identify species (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017).

| Method | Principle and use |
|---|---|
| PCR (Mullis, 1983) | Melting, annealing, extension cycles; detects about 10 cells, 10 to 100 times more sensitive than other methods |
| Species-specific, multiplex, nested PCR | One species; several species at once; two rounds for higher sensitivity |
| RT-PCR | RNA target; better for living cells |
| Real-time PCR (SYBR-Green, TaqMan) | Quantifies species and total bacteria; measures antibacterial effect of treatment |
| Broad-range PCR, cloning, sequencing | Detects unexpected and uncultivated species |
| Checkerboard DNA-DNA hybridisation (Socransky) | Many species in many samples on one membrane; reverse-capture detects uncultivated species |
| Microarray (HOMIM) | About 300 oral taxa at once |
| FISH | Fluorescent probes in situ; shows spatial arrangement in biofilm |
| Next-generation sequencing | Massively parallel; deep coverage of whole community |

(Siqueira and Rôças, 2017; Siqueira and Rôças, 2021)

**Limitations:** most assays qualitative; dead-cell DNA detected (overcome by RT-PCR or viability-PCR); bias in broad-range PCR; cost. **Impact:** about 45% of endodontic species were found only by molecular methods (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017, 2021); the "community-as-pathogen" concept replaced the single pathogen. These remain research tools, not chairside tests.

## 10. Conclusion

### Explanation

Seltzer and Bender's chapter closes that better understanding of mediators "may eventually lead to the development of better diagnostic tools of pulp conditions" (Fouad, 2012) [SRC]; Dammaschke says molecular-based diagnosis "may have the potential to improve clinical diagnosis of true pulp disease" (Dammaschke, 2024) [SRC]; Galicia and Khan call for pulp biology and molecular diagnostics to be used "for the development of novel chairside tests" (Galicia and Khan, 2024) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Conclusion.** Molecular diagnosis aims to replace subjective nerve-based tests with an objective measure of pulpal and periapical inflammation. MMP-9, IL-6, IL-8, neuropeptides and prostaglandins in pulp blood, dentinal fluid and GCF distinguish inflamed from healthy pulps, but none yet separates reversible from irreversible pulpitis, the evidence is low certainty, and no chairside kit is in routine use (Galicia and Khan, 2024). Molecular microbial methods have redefined the endodontic microbiome but remain research tools (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017). The future is a validated chairside biomarker test that guides vital pulp therapy (Dammaschke, 2024).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

**Claim 1: MMP-9 is a useful diagnostic marker of irreversible pulpitis.**
- Textbooks: Fouad (2012), Fouad and Khan (2020), Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira (2022) and Fouad and Huang (2019) all report raised MMP-9 in irreversible or symptomatic pulpitis, mostly from one dentinal-fluid study and pulp-tissue studies; Galicia and Khan (2024) add a single study linking high active MMP-9 to pulpotomy failure.
- Evidence: small observational studies (the dentinal fluid study compared irreversible pulpitis (n=19) with normal controls (n=12), as Fouad and Khan describe); the rise "was not universally present" (Fouad, 2012). The GRADE rating for MMP-9 and outcome is very low (Galicia and Khan, 2024).
- Interpretation: MMP-9 tracks tissue destruction and is a plausible candidate [Likely]. Limitation: it has been tested against normal teeth, not against reversible pulpitis, so it does not yet answer the clinical question.
- Clinical implication: no treatment decision should rest on MMP-9 now [Certain].

**Claim 2: Clinical diagnosis correlates poorly with pulp histology.**
- Textbooks diverge. Galicia and Khan (2024) and Fouad and Levin (2021) cite classic studies of poor correlation. Fouad and Levin (2021) and Fouad and Khan (2020) also cite a later histological study (Ricucci et al., as cited) in which clinical reversible pulpitis matched histology 96.6% and irreversible 84.4%; Fouad and Levin themselves note it was retrospective, used one dentist's unstandardised definitions and excluded asymptomatic irreversible pulpitis.
- Interpretation: the gap may be smaller for clear-cut cases than the classic studies suggest, but the newer data do not show that tests can stage inflammation [Likely]. Both positions are kept here; neither is ranked.
- Implication: the main need is staging inflammation within a vital pulp, which is exactly where biomarkers have not yet succeeded.

**Claim 3: Pooled biomarker accuracy.** IL-6 and IL-8 separate healthy from irreversibly inflamed pulps with high accuracy, but with low certainty, small numbers (for example IL-6: 4 studies) and no ability to separate reversible from irreversible pulpitis (Galicia and Khan, 2024). Diagnostic-accuracy data against a normal-pulp control overstate the performance expected in real borderline cases [Likely]. Not verified with live literature this session.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Current tests test nerves, not blood flow, and cannot stage inflammation.
- Four fluids: pulpal blood, dentinal fluid, GCF (non-invasive), periapical exudate.
- MMP-9 in dentinal fluid: raised in irreversible pulpitis, not in all patients; high active MMP-9 linked to pulpotomy failure.
- SP and NKA in GCF rise in painful pulpitis and normalise 1 week after pulpectomy.
- IL-6 and IL-8: best pooled accuracy; TNF-α and IL-1β low.
- No biomarker separates reversible from irreversible pulpitis; evidence low or very low certainty.
- Proposed chairside test: paper point or capillary sample, MMP-9 assay, compare with reference.
- Microbes: PCR detects about 10 cells; real-time PCR quantifies; checkerboard, FISH, NGS.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Writing only about PCR and microbes; the question is about molecular diagnostic aids, and host biomarkers of pulpitis are the core.
- Claiming a biomarker can already distinguish reversible from irreversible pulpitis; the pooled evidence says none can.
- Calling pulse oximetry or laser Doppler "molecular" aids; they measure blood flow, not molecules.
- Quoting MMP-9 as a validated chairside kit in routine use; it is a proposal.
- Giving culture-versus-molecular percentages that do not add up (the 45/32/33 error in one book).
