## Explanation

**What the word appears to mean.** The textbooks use "endogramme" only in the title of one study: "Analysis of the endogrammes developed during obturations on extracted teeth using System B" (Blum, Cathala, Machtou and Micallef, J Endod 2001), listed by Plotino, Venturi and Grande (2025) [SRC] and by Baumann and Beer (2010) [SRC]. The PubMed abstract of that paper settles the meaning: Blum and co-workers used a force analyser called the Endographe to analyse the forces and wedging effects developed in the root canal during obturation, and "Endogrammes showed the force application and wedging effect sequences" (Blum et al, 2001) [LIT]. Five endodontists performed 50 obturations of extracted teeth and 50 of the Endographe's "wedging effect" holder; with System B the mean vertical force, horizontal force and wedging effect were 16 ± 2 N, 5 ± 1 N and 9 ± 1 N (Blum et al, 2001) [LIT]. The authors conclude that endogrammes give a new approach to analysing obturation techniques and a means to demonstrate correct handling of the System B plugger (Blum et al, 2001) [LIT]. The earlier paper describes the device as a force analyser, "the modified Endographe with a new cupule", and calls its graphs a new approach to the analysis of obturation techniques (Blum, Machtou and Micallef, 1998) [LIT]. So an endogram is the graph of force application and wedging effect against time recorded by the Endographe during obturation. [Certain from the abstracts]

**What the force studies showed (as cited in the textbooks).** Clauder cites Blum et al (2001) for the statement that, for vertically applied forces during obturation, the continuous wave technique is one of the best techniques (Clauder, 2010) [SRC]. Cohen's, citing Blum's earlier force papers, says vertical root fracture is possible with warm vertical compaction too; the forces with warm vertical and lateral compaction were not significantly different, and mean wedging was 0.65 ± 0.07 kg for warm vertical against 0.8 ± 0.1 kg for lateral compaction (Aminoshariae et al, 2021) [SRC]. Lateral compaction needs relatively high spreader forces with some risk of root fracture (Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025) [SRC]; a force between 1.5 kg and 7.2 kg can fracture a root (Schäfer, 2010) [SRC]. Harty's, citing Blum, Machtou and Micallef (1998), notes that spreader forces can be considerable, that the force should be apical with no lateral action, and that finger rather than hand-held spreaders may be used to reduce the risk of root fracture from excessive condensation pressure (Chandler, 2017) [SRC]; Plotino and co-workers add that NiTi finger spreaders decrease stress on the root (Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025) [SRC]. Thermafil developed lower condensation forces than lateral condensation, vertical condensation or System B in Blum's work (Castellucci, 2019) [SRC].

**A conflict between the books on the same data.** Pontius, citing Blum et al (1998), says the wedge effect with warm vertical compaction is significantly lower than with cold lateral compaction (Pontius, 2010) [SRC]. Aminoshariae and co-workers report the forces of the two techniques as not significantly different (0.65 against 0.8 kg wedging) (Aminoshariae et al, 2021) [SRC]. The 1998 abstract gives the same means as Aminoshariae et al (wedging 0.65 ± 0.07 kg warm vertical, 0.8 ± 0.1 kg lateral, 0.6 ± 0.08 kg thermomechanical, 0.03 ± 0.01 kg Thermafil) but reports no significance test between techniques (Blum, Machtou and Micallef, 1998) [LIT]. So the abstract confirms the numbers, and lateral compaction is numerically higher, but it cannot settle whether the difference was significant; that needs the full text, which is not in PubMed Central. [Certain that the abstract is silent on significance]

**Other meanings.** None was found in any source. If the examiner intended something else (for example a set of endodontic radiographs), that reading has no textbook support and is not drafted. [Guessing]

## ✎ Exam core

**Meaning.** An endogram (endogramme) is the graph of force application and wedging effect recorded by a force analyser, the Endographe, while a canal is obturated (Blum, Machtou and Micallef, 1998; Blum et al, 2001). With System B: vertical 16 N, horizontal 5 N, wedging 9 N (Blum et al, 2001).

**Findings of these force studies**
- Wedging: lateral 0.8, warm vertical 0.65, Thermafil 0.03 kg (Blum, Machtou and Micallef, 1998); not significantly different (Aminoshariae et al, 2021) versus significantly lower with warm vertical (Pontius, 2010).
- Continuous wave (System B) is one of the best techniques for vertical forces (Clauder, 2010).
- Lateral compaction needs relatively high spreader forces (Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025); 1.5 to 7.2 kg can cause root fracture (Schäfer, 2010).

**Uses**
- Compare obturation techniques for force and root fracture risk; a new approach to analysing obturation (Blum, Machtou and Micallef, 1998).
- Demonstrate correct, optimal handling of the System B plugger (Blum et al, 2001).
- Teach controlled, apical compaction; finger spreaders lower fracture risk (Chandler, 2017).

**Note.** No alternative meaning was found in any source.
