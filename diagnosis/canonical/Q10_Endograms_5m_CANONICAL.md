## Explanation

**What the word appears to mean.** The textbooks use "endogramme" only in the title of one study: "Analysis of the endogrammes developed during obturations on extracted teeth using System B" (Blum, Cathala, Machtou and Micallef, J Endod 2001), listed by Plotino, Venturi and Grande (2025) [SRC] and by Baumann and Beer (2010) [SRC]. From the title, an endogram is a record made during obturation of extracted teeth. [Likely] It is a graph of the force applied to the root, plotted against time (and plugger movement), recorded while the canal is filled. The instrument used by Blum's group in Marseille is recalled as the "Endographe", a force sensor holding the tooth during compaction [RECALL]; no book names it. [Guessing] on the exact quantities plotted, since the paper was not opened.

**What the force studies showed (as cited in the textbooks).** Clauder cites Blum et al (2001) for the statement that, for vertically applied forces during obturation, the continuous wave technique is one of the best techniques (Clauder, 2010) [SRC]. Cohen's, citing Blum's earlier force papers, says vertical root fracture is possible with warm vertical compaction too; the forces with warm vertical and lateral compaction were not significantly different, and mean wedging was 0.65 ± 0.07 kg for warm vertical against 0.8 ± 0.1 kg for lateral compaction (Aminoshariae et al, 2021) [SRC]. Lateral compaction needs relatively high spreader forces with some risk of root fracture (Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025) [SRC]; a force between 1.5 kg and 7.2 kg can fracture a root (Schäfer, 2010) [SRC]. Harty's, citing Blum, Machtou and Micallef (1998), notes that spreader forces can be considerable, that the force should be apical with no lateral action, and that finger rather than hand-held spreaders may be used to reduce the risk of root fracture from excessive condensation pressure (Chandler, 2017) [SRC]; Plotino and co-workers add that NiTi finger spreaders decrease stress on the root (Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025) [SRC]. Thermafil developed lower condensation forces than lateral condensation, vertical condensation or System B in Blum's work (Castellucci, 2019) [SRC].

**A conflict between the books on the same data.** Pontius, citing Blum et al (1998), says the wedge effect with warm vertical compaction is significantly lower than with cold lateral compaction (Pontius, 2010) [SRC]. Aminoshariae and co-workers report the forces of the two techniques as not significantly different (0.65 against 0.8 kg wedging) (Aminoshariae et al, 2021) [SRC]. Both are shown side by side; the original papers were not opened, so which reading is right cannot be settled here. [Certain that the books differ]

**Other meanings.** None was found in any source. If the examiner intended something else (for example a set of endodontic radiographs), that reading has no textbook support and is not drafted. [Guessing]

## ✎ Exam core

**Meaning (uncertain).** No textbook defines "endogram". The term occurs only in Blum, Cathala, Machtou and Micallef, "Analysis of the endogrammes developed during obturations on extracted teeth using System B" (J Endod 2001; cited by Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025; Baumann and Beer, 2010). [Likely] An endogram is a graph of the force (and plugger movement) against time, recorded while a canal in an extracted tooth is obturated on a force-measuring device (the "Endographe" of Blum's group, [RECALL]).

**Findings of these force studies**
- Warm vertical versus lateral compaction: forces not significantly different, wedging 0.65 against 0.8 kg (Aminoshariae et al, 2021); wedging significantly lower with warm vertical (Pontius, 2010).
- Continuous wave (System B) is one of the best techniques for vertical forces (Clauder, 2010).
- Lateral compaction needs relatively high spreader forces (Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025); 1.5 to 7.2 kg can cause root fracture (Schäfer, 2010).

**Uses**
- Compare obturation techniques for root fracture risk.
- Teach controlled, apical compaction; finger spreaders lower fracture risk (Chandler, 2017).

**Note.** No alternative meaning was found in any source; any other reading (for example, endodontic radiographs) is unsupported. [Guessing]
