## Explanation

**Cause.** Apical periodontitis is an inflammatory disease caused by bacterial infection of the root canal system (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC]. In germ-free rats exposed pulps did not cause pulp necrosis or periradicular inflammation (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC], and Sundqvist found that the canals of traumatised teeth without apical lesions were aseptic (Sedgley, Silva and Fouad, 2021) [SRC]. Bacteria sit in a "strategic and privileged position" in the necrotic canal, out of reach of phagocytes (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]; they are usually in the canal, not in the lesion (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; Harty's adds that, apart from abscesses, it is still controversial whether asymptomatic lesions can harbour bacteria for long (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC].

**Timing.** Apical periodontitis can begin as a direct extension of apical pulpitis before total pulp necrosis (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; in rodents PMNs and monocytes enter the periapex 1 to 3 days after pulp exposure (Sasaki and Stashenko, 2012) [SRC]. Fouad and Khan conclude the pulp does not have to be totally necrotic (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC].

**Purpose of the lesion.** Sasaki and Stashenko call it a second line of defence that localises infection inside the root canal (Sasaki and Stashenko, 2012) [SRC]; Rôças and co-workers call it a defence barrier (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]; Fouad and Khan say it defends the body against advancing bacteria (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC]. The three agree. [Certain]

**Innate (acute) phase.** LPS is recognised by TLR4/CD14 and Gram-positive components by TLR2 (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. PMNs arrive first, peaking at 24 to 48 hours, macrophages as a second wave at 48 to 96 hours (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; PMNs also kill by neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]. Complement fragments (C3a, C5a) may attract PMNs, but C5-deficient animals form lesions of normal size, so complement probably does not play a major role (Sasaki and Stashenko, 2012) [SRC]. Much of the tissue damage comes from lysosomal enzymes and MMPs of disintegrating leukocytes rather than from bacteria (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Raised tissue hydrostatic pressure is "arguably the main cause of pain" (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]. Neurogenic inflammation adds CGRP-driven vasodilation and substance P extravasation (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

**Adaptive (chronic) phase.** Chronic antigenic stimulation drives asymptomatic apical periodontitis (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]; antigens reach regional lymph nodes for lymphocyte activation (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]. Rôças and co-workers name lymphocytes and plasma cells as predominant (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC], Lin and Huang lymphocytes and macrophages (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC], and Sasaki and Stashenko record continuing disagreement on this point (Sasaki and Stashenko, 2012) [SRC]; IgG cells are about 70% of plasma cells (Sasaki and Stashenko, 2012) [SRC]. B cells, not T cells, prevent dissemination in knockout mice (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC], and neutrophil suppression disrupts lesion formation (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC].

**Bone resorption.** Bacteria destroy bone indirectly through host mediators (Sasaki and Stashenko, 2012) [SRC]. OPG is a decoy receptor for RANKL (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; a raised RANKL/OPG ratio drives resorption (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC] and correlates with lesion expansion (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC]; IL-4 and IL-10 limit it (Sedgley, Silva and Fouad, 2021) [SRC]. M1 macrophages prevail in active lesions, M2 in stable ones, and lesions alternate between activity and stability (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022) [SRC], ending in an equilibrium between aggression and defence (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]. Bone resorption is the "hallmark" (Sedgley, Silva and Fouad, 2021) [SRC].

**Epithelium.** Epithelial rests proliferate in about 52% of lesions (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC], driven by EGF and KGF (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]; a minority become cysts.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Apical periodontitis is inflammation and destruction of the periapical tissues caused by bacterial infection of the root canal (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017).

**1. Aetiology.** Germ-free rats did not develop periapical lesions (Kakehashi, cited by Lin and Huang, 2021). Bacteria stay in a "strategic and privileged position" in the necrotic canal, beyond phagocytes (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022); LPS, LTA and enzymes diffuse out.

**2. Onset before total necrosis.** PMNs reach the periapex 1-3 days after exposure, while vital apical pulp remains (Sasaki and Stashenko, 2012; Lin and Huang, 2021).

**3. Innate (acute) phase**
- TLR4 recognises LPS, TLR2 Gram-positive wall components (Lin and Huang, 2021).
- Mast cells, histamine, complement (C3a, C5a), vasodilation, exudate.
- PMNs peak at 24-48 h; macrophages arrive at 48-96 h.
- Tissue damage mainly from lysosomal enzymes and MMPs.
- Pain: raised hydrostatic pressure on nerves (arguably the main cause), plus mediators and neuropeptides (CGRP, substance P) (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022).
- Clinical result: symptomatic apical periodontitis; abscess if pus forms.

**4. Adaptive (chronic) phase**
- Antigens carried to regional lymph nodes; effector cells return (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019).
- Lymphocytes, plasma cells (IgG about 70%), macrophages; which predominates is disputed (Sasaki and Stashenko, 2012).
- TH1 and TH17 drive progression; TH2, Tregs, IL-10 restrain.
- Antibodies prevent spread of infection.

**5. Bone resorption**
- Bacteria act indirectly via host cytokines (Sasaki and Stashenko, 2012).
- IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α, PGE2 make osteoblasts express RANKL; RANKL + RANK = osteoclasts; OPG is the decoy.
- High RANKL/OPG ratio means lesion expansion; M1 in active, M2 in stable lesions (Rôças, Ricucci and Siqueira, 2022).
- Granulomatous tissue replaces bone: radiolucency.

**6. Equilibrium and outcomes.** A balance between aggression and defence gives a stable granuloma (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019); epithelial rests may form a cyst; exacerbation gives an abscess. The lesion is a defence barrier, not the disease's cause (Fouad and Khan, 2020); removing canal infection lets it heal.

**Flow:** caries → pulp infection → canal biofilm → innate response (SAP/abscess) → adaptive response, RANKL-mediated bone loss → granuloma/cyst (AAP).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Cause is intracanal bacteria; lesion bacteria are usually absent.
- Starts before total pulp necrosis.
- Lesion = second line of defence.
- Pain = pressure on nerves more than mediators.
- RANKL/OPG ratio decides bone loss.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Saying bacteria invade and destroy the bone directly.
- Saying the pulp must be totally necrotic first.
- Omitting the adaptive phase and RANKL/OPG.

## MNEMONIC
**"Bugs Trigger Pus, Then Bone Goes"**: Bacteria, TLR/innate, PMN (acute), T and B cells (adaptive), Bone resorption via RANKL.
