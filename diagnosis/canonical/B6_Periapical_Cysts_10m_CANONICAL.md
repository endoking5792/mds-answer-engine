## 1. Definition and incidence

### Explanation

A cyst is an epithelium-lined pathologic cavity that may contain fluid, semi-solid material or cellular debris (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC]. The periradicular (apical, periapical, radicular) cyst is an odontogenic cyst associated with a tooth with a necrotic pulp that develops within a periradicular inflammatory lesion and takes its epithelium from the cell rests of Malassez (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC]; Grossman's defines it as an inflammatory lesion of endodontic origin with a closed cavity internally lined with epithelium (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Lin and Huang stress that the radicular cyst is unique because no cyst elsewhere in the body has a similar pathogenesis (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; it is lined by nonkeratinized stratified squamous epithelium (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC], is classified by WHO as inflammatory and not developmental or neoplastic (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC], and should not be considered a separate disease from chronic apical periodontitis (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC].

| Landwehr (Cohen's Ch.3) | 6% to 55% (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC] |
| Grossman's table | 6% to 55%; varies with the criteria for a cyst and with serial sectioning (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC] |

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** An inflammatory odontogenic cyst at the apex of a tooth with a necrotic pulp, arising within apical periodontitis, lined by nonkeratinised stratified squamous epithelium derived from the rests of Malassez (AAE, 2012; Lin and Huang, 2021). It is not a separate disease from chronic apical periodontitis.

**Incidence** varies with criteria and serial sectioning: 6% to 55% (Landwehr, 2021); 15% to 20% (Lin and Huang, 2021); Bhaskar 42%, Nair 15% (9% true, 6% pocket) (Ricucci et al, 2019; Estrela et al, 2012). About 75% occur in the maxilla (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## 2. Pathogenesis

### Explanation

The epithelial cell rests of Malassez are remnants of Hertwig's epithelial root sheath (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC], quiescent in the normal periodontal ligament (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; the cyst epithelium is accordingly described as derived from Hertwig's sheath (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019; Lam, 2012) [SRC]. Nair's account, as given by Grossman's: dormant rests proliferate under inflammatory cytokines and growth factors (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. A cyst arises within a granuloma, which forms part of its wall (Sedgley, Silva and Fouad, 2021) [SRC], as a late stage of the host defence against root canal infection (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]; not every epithelialised granuloma becomes a cyst: 52% showed epithelial proliferation, only 15% were cysts (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC].

Theories of cavity formation: (1) nutritional deficiency or breakdown: central cells of an epithelial island lose their nutrition and liquefy (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; (2) abscess theory: epithelium lines an abscess cavity because of its tendency to cover exposed connective tissue (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC], supported by Nair's rat tissue-cage experiment (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]; (3) immunologic theory: an immune response against activated rests that become antigenic (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]; (4) fusion or merging theory: strands merge into a three-dimensional sphere (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Three stages: rests start to proliferate (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]; an epithelium-lined cavity forms; the cyst expands at the expense of bone (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC].

> **SOURCE CONFLICT, expansion.** The osmotic theory is described as speculation that overlooks cell biology (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; they attribute growth to MMP degradation of the capsule (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC] and a bone-resorbing factor from the rests (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira keep osmotic pressure as a possible stage-3 mechanism but also name pro-resorptive mediators; they note a higher RANKL/OPG ratio in granulomas than in cysts (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Pathogenesis (three stages)**
1. Dormant rests of Malassez (remnants of Hertwig's sheath) proliferate under inflammatory cytokines and growth factors (Gopikrishna, 2021).
2. A cavity forms: nutritional deficiency (breakdown), abscess, immunologic or fusion theory (Ricucci et al, 2019; Lin and Huang, 2021).
3. Cyst enlarges at the expense of bone: osmotic pressure (disputed), MMPs and bone resorption (Lin and Huang, 2021).

Only a minority of epithelialised granulomas become cysts (52% epithelialised, 15% cysts) (Ricucci et al, 2019).

## 3. Types and histology

### Explanation

Pocket (bay) cyst: a sac-like, epithelium-lined cavity open to and continuous with the root canal (American Association of Endodontists, 2012; Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; originally called the bay cyst (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; an epithelial collar seals the infected canal and microabscess off from the periapex (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. True cyst: completely enclosed in epithelium with no communication to the canal (American Association of Endodontists, 2012; Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Telling them apart needs serial sections of the lesion attached to the root (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]. The lumen may hold exudate, cholesterol crystals, clear fluid or bacterial colonies (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; cholesterol crystallises as thin plates (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]; ciliated respiratory-type lining occurs occasionally (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC], in about 8% (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]. The lining shows no neoplastic features (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]. Actinomyces has been isolated from a cyst (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

> **Plainly wrong statement in Sikri.** Sikri calls the true periapical cyst "an acute inflammatory cyst" (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. The Glossary wording it follows is "apical inflammatory cyst" (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC], and Sikri's own key-features box calls it a chronic response; "acute" is an error [Certain].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Types (Nair; Simon's "bay" cyst)**
| Pocket (bay) cyst | True cyst |
|---|---|
| Lumen open to the root canal | Lumen fully enclosed by epithelium |
| Epithelial collar seals the apex | No communication with the canal |

Proved only by serial sections (Ricucci et al, 2019).

**Histology:** nonkeratinised stratified squamous lining infiltrated by inflammatory cells, fibrous capsule; lumen with fluid, cholesterol clefts, foam cells; occasionally ciliated lining (about 8%) (Lin and Huang, 2021; Ricucci et al, 2019).

## 4. Diagnosis (answers "Diagnosis of periapical cyst", 7m)

### Explanation

**Clinical.** No symptoms apart from those of pulp necrosis (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; a large cyst may spread the roots so crowns drift out of alignment (Gopikrishna, 2021; Sedgley, Silva and Fouad, 2021) [SRC]; the tooth does not respond to electric or thermal tests (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

**Radiograph.** Granuloma and cyst are radiographically indistinguishable (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]; neither size nor shape is definite (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. The historical rule that lesions over 1.5 cm and well demarcated were cysts (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC] has been dropped: there is no way to separate them on conventional imaging (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC]. Suggestive features only: large size, a corticated border, pushing neighbouring roots (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC]; cysts tend to be larger (Torneck, 2012) [SRC]; larger and older lesions more often contain cystic elements (Ørstavik, 2012) [SRC]; lesions over 200 square mm have a higher chance of being cystic (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) [SRC].

> **SOURCE CONFLICT, the corticated border.** Sikri: a hazy lesion is a granuloma and a well-circumscribed lesion with a sclerotic border is a cyst (Sikri, 2019) [SRC], and a radiographic band is "a diagnostic feature" (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Ørstavik: the belief that a radiopaque rim means a cyst has been challenged (Ørstavik, 2012) [SRC]; Torneck: cortication is a reactive response and not pathognomonic (Torneck, 2012) [SRC]. Sikri's next page itself concedes that radiographs cannot separate them, so his band rule should be read as suggestive only [Certain].

**Advanced imaging.** On CT a cyst reads darker than a granuloma, near background density (Trope et al, cited by Cotti and Campisi, 2012) [SRC]; CBCT results are controversial (Cotti and Campisi, 2012) [SRC]: Simon et al found pathologist and radiologist agreed in only 76% of cases (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC], and Rosenberg et al concluded biopsy remains the standard (Estrela, Bueno and Alencar, 2012) [SRC]. Multislice CT costs more radiation (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC]; ultrasound and MRI may help (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC], ultrasound showing a cyst as a well-contoured transonic, fluid-filled cavity (Cotti and Campisi, 2012) [SRC]. Fluid electrophoresis has also been tried (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]; aspirated cyst fluid carries more albumin and globulin than granuloma fluid (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) [SRC].

**Limits of non-histological diagnosis.** Histology remains the gold standard (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]; cysts cannot be diagnosed clinically, only after biopsy or extraction (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; the same in Essential Endodontology (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC]. Even histology is debated: criteria differ between pathologists (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC], and curetted tissue breaks up the lumen (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC].

**Differential diagnosis.** A cyst stays attached to the apex at any angulation, unlike the incisive foramen (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; an inflammatory lesion should be unilocular with a non-responding pulp (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC]; multilocular: OKC, ameloblastoma, central giant cell lesion (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC]; globulomaxillary cyst (not due to pulp death), traumatic bone cyst (not epithelium-lined), lateral periodontal cyst (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Of biopsied lesions, 0.7 to 5.0% prove to be something else (Gutmann, 2017) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Diagnosis**
- Clinical: usually symptomless; non-vital tooth; large cysts displace roots, tilt crowns, cause swelling (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Radiograph: cannot separate cyst from granuloma (Landwehr, 2021). Suggestive only: size over 200 sq mm (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019), corticated border, root displacement (Fouad and Khan, 2020). Sikri's "sclerotic band = cyst" is challenged (Ørstavik, 2012; Torneck, 2012).
- CT: cyst darker than granuloma (Cotti and Campisi, 2012). CBCT inconsistent, 76% agreement (Landwehr, 2021). Ultrasound: transonic fluid cavity.
- Aspirate: albumin and globulin high.
- Definitive: histology with serial sections (Ricucci et al, 2019).
- Differential: incisive canal, OKC, ameloblastoma, traumatic bone cyst, lateral periodontal cyst; vital pulp excludes radicular cyst.

## 5. Healing and management

### Explanation

Treatment of cysts and granulomas is identical: remove the stimulus by root canal treatment or extraction (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC]; nonsurgical treatment with observation is the treatment of choice (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Lesions resolve after root canal treatment in 80 to 98% (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], 78% (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; about 75% of large lesions thought to be cysts healed (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]. Cysts that resolve do so more slowly (Torneck, 2012) [SRC]. A pocket cyst may make the canal weep (Siqueira and Rôças, 2017) [SRC]; calcium hydroxide helps dry it (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]. Healing runs from periphery to centre (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; once irritation is removed the immune system removes the epithelium (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

> **SOURCE CONFLICT, do true cysts heal without surgery?** *Say no or doubtful:* Lin and Huang (outcomes paragraph): self-sustaining, surgery necessary (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; Landwehr: healing potential may be limited (Landwehr, 2021) [SRC]; Ørstavik: may be resistant and need extirpation (Ørstavik, 2012) [SRC]; Sikri: least potential to heal without surgery (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. *Say yes or unproven:* Lin and Huang (healing section): may regress by apoptosis if infection is controlled (Lin and Huang, 2021) [SRC]; Sedgley, Silva and Fouad: never any direct evidence (Sedgley, Silva and Fouad, 2021) [SRC], both heal equally well (Sedgley, Silva and Fouad, 2021) [SRC]; Fouad and Khan: hypotheses unproven (Fouad and Khan, 2020) [SRC]; Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira: the pocket cyst is the likelier problem (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC], and nonhealing is almost always infection (Ricucci, Rôças and Siqueira, 2019) [SRC]. Lin and Huang take both positions in one chapter. Not resolved.
>
> **Plainly wrong wording in Sikri.** "surgical removal of the cystic lesion (marsupialization)" (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]: marsupialisation exteriorises and decompresses the cyst (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC]; removal is enucleation.

Gutmann notes that "presence of a cyst" was a historical indication for surgery (Gutmann, 2017) [SRC] that has been eliminated (Gutmann, 2017) [SRC]. Surgery is for failure to resolve or symptoms (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Decompression and marsupialisation are used interchangeably (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) [SRC]; it disrupts the wall and lowers osmotic pressure (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) [SRC] and may avoid enucleation (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) [SRC]; best evidence is a 20-patient cohort (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) [SRC]; aspiration and irrigation is another option (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) [SRC]. Enucleation is surgical removal of the lesion (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC]. Large cysts risk pathological fracture, so healing must be followed (Torneck, 2012) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Management**
1. Nonsurgical root canal treatment first, with recall; cysts and granulomas are treated alike (Landwehr, 2021). Healing 80 to 98% (Gopikrishna, 2021), slower in cysts (Torneck, 2012).
2. Weeping canal (pocket cyst): calcium hydroxide (Ricucci et al, 2019).
3. Large lesions: decompression (tube or drain, irrigation) or aspiration and irrigation (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019).
4. Failure or symptoms: enucleation with apical surgery and biopsy (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**True cyst controversy:** may need surgery (Landwehr, 2021; Ørstavik, 2012) versus no direct evidence (Sedgley et al, 2021).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Epithelium: rests of Malassez (remnants of Hertwig's sheath).
- Nair 1996: 15% cysts (9% true, 6% pocket); textbook range 6% to 55%.
- Four theories: breakdown, abscess, immunologic, fusion; three stages.
- Pocket = open to canal; true = enclosed; serial sections needed.
- No reliable radiographic or CBCT differentiation; histology is the gold standard.
- Treat like a granuloma: root canal treatment first; surgery on failure.
