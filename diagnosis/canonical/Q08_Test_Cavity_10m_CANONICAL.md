## 1. Definition and indications

### Explanation

The test cavity is an invasive, irreversible pulp test and is not used routinely (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]; it is used only when all other tests are impossible or inconclusive (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]. The classic example is a tooth with a full-coverage crown (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]; Weine gives a periapical radiolucency near an incisor with a porcelain jacket crown (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; Walton and Torabinejad limit it to cases where a necrotic pulp is suspected (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]; Baumann and Beer add calcified pulps in elderly patients (Baumann and Beer, 2008) [SRC]. Grossman's, however, makes carbon dioxide snow the test of choice for crowned teeth (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], so the test cavity follows a failed cold test; Chandler and Chong likewise find carbon dioxide snow particularly useful for teeth with full-coverage restorations (Chandler and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. Weine's second example is an acute mandibular swelling where the teeth are splinted together with gold or bonded porcelain (Weine, 2004) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** A test cavity is a small cavity cut into dentin of an unanaesthetised tooth to see whether the pulp responds; it is an invasive, irreversible sensibility test used only when other tests are impossible or inconclusive (Berman and Rotstein, 2021).

**Indications**
- Full-coverage crown (PFM, all-ceramic, porcelain jacket) where electric pulp test cannot be bridged and cold (CO₂ snow) is inconclusive (Berman and Rotstein, 2021; Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Periapical radiolucency on a crowned tooth, or swelling among splinted teeth, to identify the necrotic one (Weine, 2004).
- Suspected necrosis in calcified (elderly) teeth (Baumann and Beer, 2008).

## 2. Technique

### Explanation

Explain the procedure and reassure the patient first (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]; no anaesthetic is given (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]. Cohen's: a class I preparation with a high-speed #1 or #2 round bur with air-water coolant (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]. Grossman's: drill through the enamel-dentin junction at high speed with water coolant (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Harty's: a small bur with copious irrigation into dentine (Chandler and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. **Books disagree on coolant.** Weine: cut dentin with an airotor without water spray to produce the most heat (Weine, 2004) [SRC], although an overlying restoration may be cut with water spray until dentin is reached (Weine, 2004) [SRC], and once a response is felt no further heat-producing cutting is done, lest the pulp be damaged (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; Sikri: without anaesthesia and without coolant (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Site: the position of the access cavity (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC], i.e. lingual or palatal on anteriors and occlusal on posteriors (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. Enamel or restoration can be cut with little discomfort (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]; pain while cutting a splint or bridge casting is meaningless because adjacent pulps may respond (Weine, 2004) [SRC]. On a response the procedure stops and the cavity is restored (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]: bonded restoration (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC], sedative cement (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC] or ZOE (Weine, 2004) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Technique**
1. Explain and obtain consent; no local anaesthetic (Berman and Rotstein, 2021).
2. Enamel or restoration is usually cut without much discomfort (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).
3. Site: as for access, lingual/palatal on anterior, occlusal on posterior teeth (Weine, 2004).
4. High-speed #1 or #2 round bur through enamel or crown to the DEJ and into dentin; patient signals any sensation.
5. Coolant: Cohen's, Grossman's and Harty's use water; Weine (in dentin) and Sikri cut dry to produce heat (Weine, 2004; Sikri, 2019).
6. Ignore pain while cutting metal of a splint or bridge (adjacent pulps) (Weine, 2004).

## 3. Interpretation and conversion to access

### Explanation

Pain on reaching sound dentin means only that some viable nerve tissue remains, not that the pulp is healthy (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]. No sensation in dentin is a good indication of necrosis and the need for root canal treatment (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) [SRC]; preparation continues to the pulp chamber (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], enlarged into a normal access opening (Weine, 2004) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Interpretation**
| Response | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp pain on reaching dentin | Vital nerve tissue present, not necessarily healthy pulp (Berman and Rotstein, 2021) | Stop; restore (bonded, sedative cement or ZOE) |
| No pain in dentin | Pulp probably necrotic | Continue into chamber; becomes the access cavity (Weine, 2004) |

## 4. Value and limitations

### Explanation

**Books disagree on its worth.** Weine calls it unquestionably the most accurate pulp vitality test (Weine, 2004) [SRC], yet still reserves it for use only as a last resort (Weine, 2004) [SRC]; Walton and Torabinejad call direct dentin stimulation probably the most accurate (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC], and the 6th edition treats a test cavity as an indicator of vitality (Blicher, Walton and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Gutmann and Lovdahl equate its value with an EPT (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]; Sikri calls it a subjective last resort (Sikri, 2019) [SRC], and Baumann and Beer the last diagnostic method of choice (Baumann and Beer, 2008) [SRC]; Chandler and Chong present it as a technique that was historically suggested (Chandler and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. Abbott (Ingle's) rejects it: a barbaric procedure (Abbott, 2019) [SRC] in which apprehension may make the patient report pain even when the pulp is necrotic (Abbott, 2019) [SRC], so false responses are very likely (Abbott, 2019) [SRC], not specific enough for a diagnosis (Abbott, 2019) [SRC], adding nothing to cold or EPT (Abbott, 2019) [SRC], with no place in modern dentistry (Abbott, 2019) [SRC]. False positives: A-delta fibres may persist in necrotic pulp (Chandler and Chong, 2017) [SRC]; vibration (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC]. False negatives: if the pulp has receded and much tertiary dentine has formed, cut tubules may not reach vital odontoblast processes (Chandler and Chong, 2017) [SRC]; calcified teeth may feel nothing until pulp exposure, committing the tooth to treatment (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) [SRC]. Grossman's prefers the anaesthetic test because iatrogenic damage is possible with a test cavity (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Limitations**
- Invasive and irreversible; damages crown or tooth; patient apprehension (Abbott, 2019).
- Positive shows only nerve response, not health; cannot separate reversible from irreversible pulpitis (Abbott, 2019).
- False positive: surviving A-delta fibres, vibration (Chandler and Chong, 2017; Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).
- False negative: receded pulp, tertiary dentine, calcified canals; a calcified tooth may feel nothing until exposure, committing it to root canal treatment (Chandler and Chong, 2017; Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011).
- Grossman's prefers the selective anaesthetic test, which avoids iatrogenic damage (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Opinions differ.** Weine (2004) calls it the most accurate vitality test, though still a last resort; Abbott (2019) calls it "barbaric" with no place in modern dentistry; Chandler and Chong (2017) treat it as historical. Most books keep it as a last resort.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- No anaesthesia; small round bur through enamel/crown into dentin.
- Indication: crowned tooth, other tests impossible or inconclusive.
- Pain = viable nerve, not healthy pulp; no pain = proceed to access.
- Coolant: wet (Cohen's, Grossman's, Harty's) vs dry for heat (Weine, Sikri).
- Ingle's (Abbott) rejects the test.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Saying a positive response proves a healthy pulp.
- Giving anaesthesia (this defeats the test).
- Omitting that the cavity becomes the access opening if there is no response.
- Ignoring false responses from adjacent abutments when cutting through a bridge.

## MNEMONIC
**"No LA, Small Bur, Stop or Go"**: no anaesthetic, small round bur, stop and restore on pain, go to access if none.
