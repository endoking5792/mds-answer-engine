## Explanation

**Case selection.** Grossman's lists the factors that influence outcome, ending with the operator's skill (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Jain reduces them to strategic value, periodontal, patient factors and restorability (Jain, 2025) [SRC]; Sikri's contraindications are a nonrestorable tooth, grade III mobility, vertical root fracture and no strategic value (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. The AAE form grades "teeth with extensive coronal destruction" as moderate difficulty (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

**Restorability first.** RCT on an unrestorable tooth is "as unethical as extraction of a sound tooth", and restorability needs removal of restorations and caries (Johnson, Abedi and Shabahang, 2021; Badr, Seong and Perdigão, 2016) [SRC]; assess it at the outset with supragingival margins as the aim (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC]. Harty's agrees: restorability is considered before treatment, the whole restoration is removed if in doubt, and the "Tooth Restorability Index" (McDonald and Setchell, cited there) grades remaining coronal tissue per sextant (Chandler and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. Endodontically treated teeth are more often extracted for restorative than for endodontic failure (Mannocci and Giovarruscio, 2017) [SRC]. **Ferrule:** a 360 degree collar of crown around parallel dentine walls (Sorensen and Engelman, cited by Barsness and Roach, 2016) [SRC]; Badr and co-workers advise that if even adjunct surgical or orthodontic procedures cannot provide a 2 mm-high ferrule, extraction "might be" the solution, and they proceed when the ferrule is at least 2 mm high and 1 mm thick (Badr, Seong and Perdigão, 2016) [SRC]; Jain contraindicates RCT if the crown cannot engage 1.5 to 2.0 mm (Jain, 2025) [SRC]; ferrule length matters more than post length, and an incomplete ferrule is better than none (Barsness and Roach, 2016) [SRC]. Harty's is more guarded: at least 2.0 mm is regarded as optimal, but the evidence for the ferrule effect is limited to laboratory studies, and one randomised trial in premolars restored with fibre posts and composite found that a ferrule did not improve survival (Mannocci and Giovarruscio, 2017) [SRC]. **Biologic width:** about 2 mm (Gargiulo, cited by Badr, Seong and Perdigão, 2016) [SRC]; at least 3 mm of supra-alveolar structure plus ferrule height (Barsness and Roach, 2016) [SRC].

**Adjuncts.** Subosseous caries may need crown lengthening before RCT (Rosenberg and Malek, 2021; Johnson, Abedi and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]. Crown lengthening can harm adjacent bone and crown-root ratio (Barsness and Roach, 2016) [SRC]; keep crown-root ratio at most 1:1, watch furcations and keep 2 to 3 mm attached gingiva (Badr, Seong and Perdigão, 2016) [SRC]. Orthodontic extrusion takes longer but had a better prognosis than surgical lengthening in Juloski's review (cited by Barsness and Roach, 2016) [SRC]. Patients must be told of these procedures, which add cost and risk (Blicher, Walton and Torabinejad, 2021; Jain, 2025) [SRC].

**Isolation and pre-endodontic build-up.** Treatment needs aseptic conditions; a stainless steel band or gingivectomy may help; do not start unless the crown can be restored (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; no dam, no RCT (Johnson, Abedi and Shabahang, 2021) [SRC]. Pre-endodontic restorations seal for disinfection and test restorability (Leprince, Hardy and Daher, 2025) [SRC], give a stable reference point for working length (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC], but must not override a subgingival unrestorable tooth (Foschi, 2025) [SRC]. Glass ionomer loses substance under load, so adhesive composite is often preferred (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]; bonding before RCT gives better bond strength (Leprince, Hardy and Daher, 2025) [SRC]. A long-term provisional can test tissue response (Badr, Seong and Perdigão, 2016) [SRC]. Where cusp coverage is hard to achieve with an interim plastic restoration, an orthodontic band prevents tooth or root fracture, and a combination of endodontic, periodontal and restorative treatment may be needed to rescue a tooth (Mannocci and Giovarruscio, 2017) [SRC].

**Periodontium and strategic value.** Class III mobility favours extraction (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; reduced but healthy support is acceptable (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC]; decide whether the tooth is strategic (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Jain gives second and third molars little strategic value unless they support a prosthesis (Jain, 2025) [SRC]. Only teeth with a favourable prognosis that can be restored belong in the plan (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]; compromised teeth may be kept up to 10 years with plastic restorations through informed consent (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**Case selection** decides whether a tooth suits endodontic treatment; its errors caused 22% of failures (Ingle and Beveridge, cited by Gopikrishna, 2021). Factors (Jain, 2025): strategic value, periodontal support, patient factors (health, motivation, cost) and restorability, plus anatomy and operator skill. **Contraindications:** nonrestorable tooth, grade III mobility, vertical root fracture, no strategic value, inability to isolate (Sikri, 2019; Johnson, Abedi and Shabahang, 2021).

**Note on grossly decayed teeth** (AAE: moderate difficulty or more)

1. **Restorability first:** remove all caries and old restorations, then judge sound structure (Johnson, Abedi and Shabahang, 2021; Chandler and Chong, 2017). Treating an unrestorable tooth is as unethical as extracting a sound one.
2. **Ferrule:** 360 degree collar of 1.5 to 2 mm height and about 1 mm thickness of sound dentine (Jain, 2025; Badr, Seong and Perdigão, 2016); incomplete ferrule is better than none (Barsness and Roach, 2016). Harty's: 2 mm is optimal but the evidence is laboratory-based, and one premolar trial showed no survival benefit (Mannocci and Giovarruscio, 2017).
3. **Biologic width:** about 2 mm (Badr, Seong and Perdigão, 2016); at least 3 mm of supra-alveolar tooth structure is needed in addition to ferrule height (Barsness and Roach, 2016).
4. **Crown lengthening:** for subgingival caries; limits: crown-root ratio at most 1:1, adjacent bone, furcation, aesthetics (Badr, Seong and Perdigão, 2016).
5. **Orthodontic extrusion:** spares bone and ratio; slower; better prognosis than surgical lengthening in one review (Barsness and Roach, 2016).
6. **Pre-endodontic build-up:** composite (or GIC) with matrix or band; gives dam retention, seal against saliva, a reference for working length, and confirms restorability (Leprince, Hardy and Daher, 2025; Baumann and Beer, 2010). It must not hide an unrestorable subgingival tooth (Foschi, 2025).
7. **Isolation:** dam mandatory; band, gingivectomy or crown lengthening (Gopikrishna, 2021).
8. **Periodontal status:** probe; class III mobility favours extraction (Gopikrishna, 2021).
9. **Strategic value:** abutment value, arch position, aesthetics, and the cost and complexity of RCT, lengthening, post and crown against an implant or bridge. Jain gives second and third molars little strategic value unless they support a prosthesis (Jain, 2025).
10. **Consent:** explain the extra procedures (build-up, crown lengthening, extrusion, post, crown), their cost and the guarded prognosis (Blicher, Walton and Torabinejad, 2021).

**Decision:** restorable with ferrule and periodontal support: build-up, RCT, post if needed, crown. Not restorable: extraction and replacement. Doubtful: provisional build-up and review with informed consent (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Restorability is judged after removing caries and restorations, before access.
- Ferrule 1.5 to 2 mm high, about 1 mm thick; biologic width about 2 mm, so at least 3 mm of tooth above bone plus the ferrule.
- Orthodontic extrusion protects crown-root ratio; crown lengthening may not.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Starting RCT before confirming restorability.
- Forgetting orthodontic extrusion as the alternative to crown lengthening.
