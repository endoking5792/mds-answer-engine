## 1. Composition and setting

### Explanation

Super EBA is the commercial alumina-reinforced zinc oxide cement in which much of the eugenol is replaced by an aromatic acid. The books do not agree on its exact make-up, so the versions are shown side by side.

| Source | Powder | Liquid |
|---|---|---|
| Craig and Powers, 2002 [SRC] | 70% zinc oxide, 30% alumina | 62.5% ortho-EBA, 37.5% eugenol |
| Sakaguchi et al, 2019 [SRC] | ZnO with 30% alumina | 2-ethoxybenzoic acid and eugenol about 2:1 |
| McCabe and Walls, 2008 [SRC] | About 60% ZnO, about 35% quartz | o-ethoxybenzoic acid and eugenol |
| Johnson et al, 2021 (Cohen's) [SRC] | 60% ZnO, 34% aluminium oxide, 6% natural resins | 37.5% eugenol, 62.5% o-methoxybenzoic acid |
| Parirokh and Shabahang, 2017 [SRC] | ZnO, aluminium oxide, natural resins | eugenol, o-methoxybenzoic acid |

The materials texts name the acid as ortho-ethoxybenzoic acid; the two endodontic texts read here name it o-methoxybenzoic acid. The discrepancy is recorded, not resolved.

**Setting.** EBA chelates zinc as eugenol does, and allows some crystalline zinc eugenolate to form, which adds strength (Craig and Powers, 2002) [SRC]. Bonsor and Pearson say this crystalline form is stronger and less soluble (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013) [SRC]. Craig's 14th edition locates the gain elsewhere: EBA permits a very high powder/liquid ratio of 6:1, which is what raises strength (Sakaguchi et al, 2019) [SRC]. Bonsor and Pearson allow up to 7:1 (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013) [SRC].

**Manipulation.** Mix on a glass slab (Craig and Powers, 2002) [SRC]: knead for 30 seconds, then strop for 60 seconds to a creamy consistency (Craig and Powers, 2002) [SRC]. Working time is about 22 minutes unless the slab is moist (Powers and Wataha, 2013) [SRC]. The product comes in fast-set and regular-set forms (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition:** an EBA-alumina reinforced zinc oxide-eugenol cement (Bosworth).

**Composition** (Craig and Powers, 2002)
- Powder: zinc oxide 60-70%, alumina about 30% (some list quartz), natural resin.
- Liquid: ortho-ethoxybenzoic acid 62.5%, eugenol 37.5%. Cohen's names the acid o-methoxybenzoic acid (Johnson et al, 2021).

**Setting:** chelation to zinc eugenolate and zinc ethoxybenzoate, partly crystalline; a high powder/liquid ratio (6:1) gives the strength (Sakaguchi et al, 2019). Mix on glass, strop 60 s; fast and regular set.

## 2. Properties

### Explanation

**Strength.** McCabe and Walls give about 85 MPa compressive strength (McCabe and Walls, 2008) [SRC]. Craig's 14th edition gives 72 MPa for EBA-eugenol against 26 MPa for plain ZOE (Sakaguchi et al, 2019) [SRC], yet calls reinforced ZOE the weakest of the permanent luting cements (Sakaguchi et al, 2019) [SRC].

**Film thickness.** 25 to 35 µm (Craig and Powers, 2002) [SRC].

**Solubility.** The books conflict, and the views are shown side by side:
- lower solubility than ZOE (McCabe and Walls, 2008; Bonsor and Pearson, 2013) [SRC];
- the zinc 2-ethoxybenzoate matrix is even more prone to hydrolysis than zinc eugenolate (Sakaguchi et al, 2019) [SRC];
- solubility and disintegration increase (Sikri, 2017) [SRC].

Cohen's adds that Super EBA disintegrates over time at acid pH (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Properties**
- Compressive strength 72-85 MPa, several times plain ZOE (Sakaguchi et al, 2019; McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Film thickness 25-35 µm (Craig and Powers, 2002).
- Radiopaque, biocompatible, tolerates a moist field (Castellucci, 2003).
- Solubility disputed: lower than ZOE (McCabe and Walls, 2008), but its matrix hydrolyses and disintegrates in acid pH (Sakaguchi et al, 2019; Johnson et al, 2021).

## 3. Uses and comparison

### Explanation

**Luting.** EBA-alumina ZOE has been clinically successful for permanent cementation of crowns and bridges (Craig and Powers, 2002) [SRC], with 92% success at 2.5 years in one study (Sakaguchi et al, 2019) [SRC]. As a provisional restoration it disintegrated and wore excessively (Craig and Powers, 2002) [SRC]. McCabe and Walls say it is used mainly as a luting cement (McCabe and Walls, 2008) [SRC], though it is strong enough to line a cavity for amalgam condensation (McCabe and Walls, 2008) [SRC].

**Root-end filling.** For root-end filling it is mixed to a putty, rolled into a narrow cone and carried to the retro-preparation (Castellucci, 2003) [SRC]. Castellucci lists its merits as easy handling, adequate working time, dimensional stability, biocompatibility, tolerance of moisture and radiopacity (Castellucci, 2003) [SRC]. It seals like IRM and leaks less than amalgam (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC], but does not regenerate cementum (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. Persistent disease after surgery with Super EBA ranged from 4% to 20% (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. In a survey of American Board diplomates, 34.3% preferred Super EBA against 61.4% for MTA (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2017) [SRC]. In vitro, MTA is superior in sealing and biocompatibility (Johnson et al, 2021) [SRC]. MTA is now the material of choice despite long setting time and difficult handling (Parirokh and Shabahang, 2017) [SRC], with IRM and Super EBA as accepted alternatives (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Uses:** root-end filling; permanent luting of crowns and bridges; base under amalgam (McCabe and Walls, 2008).

**Comparison** (Johnson et al, 2021; Parirokh and Shabahang, 2017)
- Versus IRM: similar seal; both leak less than amalgam.
- Versus MTA: MTA is superior in laboratory seal and biocompatibility and is now the material of choice; but MTA sets slowly and handles with difficulty.

## High-yield points
- Liquid 62.5% EBA and 37.5% eugenol; powder ZnO with about 30% alumina.
- Cohen's and Torabinejad's surgical text write "o-methoxybenzoic acid": know both names.
- Strength comes from the high powder/liquid ratio (6:1 to 7:1).
- Mix on glass, strop 60 s; working time about 22 min.
- Root-end filling: putty cone, condense, finish with a bur; alternative to MTA.

## Common exam mistakes
- Calling it a glass ionomer or resin cement.
- Writing "ethylenediamine" or "ethyl benzoate" for EBA.
- Claiming it regenerates cementum like MTA.
- Omitting its luting use and writing only root-end filling.
