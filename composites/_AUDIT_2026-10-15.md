# Composite Resin — completeness/accuracy audit ledger (2026-10-15)

Working note, not an answer. Produced by 6 parallel read-only readers over primary textbooks, cross-checking all 42 existing composite-resin answers across three repositories: Conservative Dentistry §10 (29 answers — several already cite real Craig's/McCabe's page reads from an earlier part of this project), Dental Materials §4 (9 answers) and Recent Advances §17 (4 answers) — the latter 13 were built via live WebSearch/journal sourcing, not primary textbooks, and are the priority target for replacement sourcing. Figures below are as reported by those readers and were NOT individually re-verified page-by-page; verify before quoting in an Exam Mode paragraph. Source tags: CR = *Craig's Restorative Dental Materials* (latest ed.); MICH = *Restorative Dental Materials* (Craig RG ed., 11th ed., "Michigan"); PHIL = *Phillips' Science of Dental Materials* 12th ed.; MCC = *Applied Dental Materials* (McCabe & Walls, 9th ed.); VN = *Introduction to Dental Materials* (van Noort & Barbour, 4th ed.); BON = *A Clinical Guide to Applied Dental Materials* (Bonsor & Pearson); PW = *Dental Materials* (Powers & Wataha, 10th ed., OCR); STURD = *Sturdevant's Art & Science of Operative Dentistry* 2e South Asian ed.; PICK = *Pickard's Manual of Operative Dentistry* 9th ed.; MOUNT = Mount, *Preservation and Restoration of Tooth Structure* 3rd ed.; MARZ = Marzouk, *Operative Dentistry* (OCR full text); CVEN = *Composite Veneers* (Fahl et al.); AP = Alani & Patel, *Adhesive Techniques in Restorative Dentistry*; SIKRI-CD = Sikri, *Textbook of Conservative & Restorative Dentistry*; MOD = *Modern Operative Dentistry* (Torres, ed.); AOD = *Advanced Operative Dentistry*; HS = Harpreet Singh, *Essentials of Preclinical Conservative Dentistry*.

## A. Corrections to existing drafts (draft claim → what sources say)

1. **Filler loading is genuinely edition/class-dependent, not one number.** CR Table 9.2: multipurpose/hybrid 60-70 vol%; nanocomposite 72-79%; microfilled 32-50%; bulk-fill 59-80%; flowable 42-62%. PHIL Table 13-3 gives a parallel but not identical set (small-particle hybrid 65-77 vol%/80-90 wt%; microfilled 20-59 vol%/35-67 wt%). VN cites "as high as 74 vol%" for some posterior composites; BON states modern composites reach ~86 wt%/70-72 vol%. **State the source with the figure — do not silently merge these into one range.**
2. **Polymerization shrinkage: volumetric vs linear figures are being conflated across sources and must be kept distinct.** CR Table 9.3 (volumetric): multipurpose 0.7-1.4%; microfill 2-3%; packable 0.6-0.9%. PW Table 4-3 gives LINEAR shrinkage (microhybrid 1.0-1.7%, microfilled 2-3%) — linear ≈ ⅓ of volumetric for small shrinkage, so these are not directly comparable to CR's numbers without converting. VN gives Bis-GMA resin alone at 7.5 vol%, MMA at 22 vol%, modern composite floor "~2.0 vol%." MOUNT: original RBCs 2.2-3.0 vol%, recent formulations 0.9-1.7 vol%. PICK: 1.5-3.5% volumetric, shrinkage stress 3-8 MPa, monomer conversion only 50-67%. **Any answer must state whether a shrinkage % is linear or volumetric, and cite the source.**
3. **Degree of conversion is NOT 70%+ as some drafts may assume.** PHIL: typical DC for Bis-GMA-based composites is 50-60%, no significant difference between chemical- and light-cure when properly cured (both 50-70% at room temp). CR/MICH (identical figure): only 35-80% of available C=C bonds react on cure; ~25% remain unreacted in bulk even after "complete" clinical cure, up to 75% unreacted in the air-inhibited surface layer. CVEN: ~75% of polymerization occurs in the first 10 min after light-curing, continuing 24h, peaking at 7 days; DC ranges 40-75% even under ideal curing.
4. **C-factor: the CD Q6 answer's definition is correct but should cite textbook-exact figures, not approximations.** STURD: ratio of bonded:unbonded surfaces; Class I prep = 5:1 (high C-factor). BON gives the clearest exam-ready phrasing: "Class I cavity has the highest C-factor (five bonded, one unbonded = occlusal surface)... Class IV has the converse." MCC gives explicit worked examples: unconstrained cube C=0; one bonded face C=0.2; two bonded faces C=0.5 (≈Class IV); five bonded faces C≈5 (≈Class I); thin luting layer C>10. **Important: neither CR nor MICH's composite chapter extract names Feilzer, Versluis, or "C-factor" numerically — this concept's primary citation should be MCC/STURD/BON, not Craig's/Michigan.**
5. **Shrinkage STRESS is not simply shrinkage % — CR/MICH give an important nuance missing from most drafts**: "low-shrinkage composites might exhibit HIGH stress if the composite has a high elastic modulus" — i.e. % shrinkage alone does not predict clinical stress; elastic modulus at the time of shrinkage matters just as much. Contraction stress between composite and tooth can reach "as high as 13 MPa" (CR/MICH, identical figure) — correct any draft citing a different ceiling.
6. **CTE values differ by source — state a range, not one number.** CR/MICH: 25-38×10⁻⁶/°C for fine-particle composites, 55-68×10⁻⁶/°C for microfine (vs dentin 8.3, enamel 11.4×10⁻⁶/°C — identical in both books). MARZ gives different figures specific to its own generation taxonomy: composite resin (except 2nd generation) ≈25-35 ppm/°C; unfilled resin 127-150 ppm/°C; silicate 8 ppm/°C.
7. **Hardness data in the primary materials-science textbooks is in Knoop (KHN), not Vickers.** CR/MICH (identical): composites 22-80 kg/mm² (Knoop); enamel 343; amalgam 110. PHIL Table 13-3: hybrid KHN 50-60, microfilled KHN 25-35. If the CD finishing/polishing answer (Q7) currently cites Vickers numbers (as it does, from McCabe's Table 22.5 for the roughness/gloss comparison — that specific study IS in Vickers/VHN per MCC Table 22.3: hybrid VHN 90, microfilled VHN 30-40, unfilled resin VHN~18) — **both scales exist in different tables for different purposes; do not convert one into the other, cite each with its own source.**
8. **Water sorption: the Michigan (older) edition's figures are likely OCR-garbled/outdated — prefer Craig's current-edition numbers.** CR (current, correct): hybrid/fine-particle composites 5-17 µg/mm³ vs microfine 26-30 µg/mm³ (microfine is HIGHER, because more polymer). MICH's older-edition sentence inverts this relationship with different units (mg/cm²) — flag as garbled/outdated if present in any draft.
9. **Wear-rate acceptance criterion is genuinely edition-dependent, not a conflict to resolve into one number.** CR (new): ADA criterion = loss of surface contour <50µm over 18 months. MICH (old): <250µm over 5 years (= 50µm/year annualized — same rate, different window). PHIL/MCC: typical modern wear 10-20µm/year (PHIL) or 10-30µm/year (MCC). MOUNT: "10-20µm/year, similar to enamel wear." **Present all with attribution — they roughly agree on the annualized rate but differ in stated window.**
10. **Nanomer/nanocluster size ranges differ slightly by source — state the source.** CR: nanomer 5-75nm + nanocluster 0.6µm average (100nm-submicron distribution); "only ONE true commercial nanofill product" per CR's own text, nanohybrid = large 0.4-5µm particles + added nanoparticles (NOT a true nanofill). BON: nanomer 20-70nm, nanocluster described as agglomerates enabling high loading/strength/easier polish; human-hair analogy (80µm = 80,000nm). PW: nanofilled fillers 1-10nm, "may be present as clusters of a larger size."
11. **Compomer two-stage setting — the mechanism is now precisely sourced, correcting any vague "acid-base reaction" phrasing.** VN gives the fullest account: Stage 1 = light-cured free-radical polymerization of the resin matrix (this is the TRUE "set" — drives the setting process); Stage 2 = a separate, slower acid-base reaction requiring water absorbed POST-PLACEMENT from the oral environment, which releases fluoride from fluoro-alumino-silicate glass via carboxyl-group monomer — "the latter is to provide fluoride ions over an extended period," not a true second setting reaction. CR/MICH corroborate: anhydrous formulation, water uptake triggers Stage 2, which is ALSO the fluoride-release vehicle. Filler 42-67 vol% (CR/MICH/PW agree), particle size 0.8-5.0µm.
12. **Finishing/polishing "rubber points are worst" finding — MCC's Table 22.5 (the actual primary source for this claim, already used in the CD Q7 draft) gives exact numbers**: baseline (Mylar matrix) Ra 0.08µm/89GU; sandpaper-roughened 0.48µm/2.7GU; graduated discs 0.12µm/66GU (BEST); rubber point 0.32µm/9.3GU; fine polishing paste 0.27µm/23GU. **However, MICH's OWN separate Table 9-6 does NOT corroborate this specific "rubber point worst" finding** — it doesn't test a category called "rubber points" by that name (closest is "composite polishers," which perform reasonably well at 0.12-0.37µm Ra). **This is a genuine between-source discrepancy: the claim is correctly sourced to MCC, but should not be presented as a universal textbook consensus — flag that Michigan's own table doesn't test the same category.**
13. **Enamel etch concentration/time is a genuine, unresolved cross-source conflict — present all, attributed, do not pick one.** MOUNT: 37% phosphoric acid, 15s (in one place) vs "10% orthophosphoric acid, 10 seconds, both enamel+dentin together" (elsewhere in the SAME chapter — Mount is internally inconsistent). MARZ: 35-50% phosphoric acid, 60 seconds, light dabbing (no rubbing), rinse 1 min, up to 3 reapplications. PICK: 37% for 20s on enamel, only 10-15s on dentine in three-step systems. STURD/AP: standard 35-37% phosphoric acid, enamel 30s/dentin 15s (4th-gen total-etch). **This is a real, examinable spread — state several protocols by source rather than inventing a single "correct" figure.**
14. **Bowen's year and Buonocore's year are now both textbook-confirmed, correcting any drift.** R.L. Bowen introduced composite resin in **1962** (confirmed independently by SIKRI-CD and HS). Michael Buonocore discovered enamel acid-etch bonding in **1955** at the Eastman Dental Center, Rochester (AP, explicit) — correct any draft citing 1959 or another year.
15. **Composite repair bond strength is quantified and consistently lower than drafts may assume.** PW: composite-to-composite repair achieves only 60-80% of original cohesive strength even after 50µm alumina abrasion + silane + isolation. MICH (independent figure): also 60-80%. PHIL (Söderholm 1986): repaired composite strength is "less than half" of original even with an ideal silane/acid-etch protocol — note PHIL's figure is notably more pessimistic than PW/MICH's 60-80%; present both.
16. **HF should NOT be used on composite resin for repair, unlike ceramic** — CR explicitly: HF degrades/etches the silica filler of composite, leaving a weak porous matrix; the correct composite repair protocol is roughen (diamond/sandblast) → acid-etch clean (phosphoric, not HF) → optional silane on old filler → adhesive → new composite. SIKRI-CD's protocol matches but adds a bevel margin (1mm) step. AP's protocol: roughen → acid-etch clean → prime with unfilled resin → repair.
17. **Soft-start/ramped/delayed curing is NOT a settled, unconditionally beneficial technique — PHIL is explicitly skeptical.** PHIL: "little advantage is to be gained... numerous laboratory studies and some clinical evidence support this conclusion" (i.e. against a clear benefit). MCC: "little evidence to show whether or not this approach is effective" (neutral, not endorsing). BON instead gives a quantified pre/post-gel timing split within a single cure (10s cure: first 8s = pre-gelation, high chain mobility, stress relief possible; final 2s = post-gelation, ~85% of total conversion occurs here, material stiffens) — a genuinely different framing (describing one continuous cure's phases) than "soft-start" (deliberately ramping intensity), and should not be conflated. **If the existing CD Q6/Q22 answers present soft-start curing as a clearly proven, recommended technique, correct to "theoretically sound, weakly evidenced clinically" and cite PHIL's skepticism explicitly.**
18. **Light-curing exposure time/depth of cure is a range dependent on lamp/shade/depth, not one fixed number.** VN: UV-cured max depth ~2.0mm vs VLC 3-4mm; curing depth >2mm should be avoided, use increments; exposure ≥40s. PHIL/CR: standard 20s cures light shades to 2-2.5mm; 40s needed for darker/opaque shades; light-guide must stay within 1mm of surface. PW: typical cure time 10-60s for a 2mm increment depending on lamp/shade. PICK: depth of cure 2-3mm ("soggy bottom" risk beyond that), 470nm wavelength standard, camphorquinone/amine photoinitiator.
19. **LED vs QTH vs PAC vs Argon-laser intensity ranking is edition-dependent — note PHIL's ranking may be outdated for "latest" LEDs.** PHIL ranks (this edition) LOWEST→HIGHEST intensity as: LED, QTH, PAC, Argon laser — explicitly noting this contradicts any draft ranking LED as highest, "though latest versions narrow the gap." AP/SIKRI-CD give more current figures: QTH 400-800mW/cm²; LED power evolved from 7mW→3mW per diode via GaN tech (a 400-fold increase per SIKRI-CD, worth stating precisely since it reverses PHIL's older ranking for modern LEDs); plasma-arc 900mW/cm² (10s PAC ≈ 40s QTH); argon laser 800mW/cm², 5-10s at specific bands (454-466, 472-497, 514nm).
20. **Composite inlay's touted shrinkage-reduction benefit is NOT fully realized clinically — a genuine, examinable caveat.** VN: the reduction in polymerization-shrinkage problems for indirect composite inlays is not realized clinically because the thin luting-resin layer still generates high shrinkage stress, AND the bond between luting resin and the fully-cured inlay surface is poor (few unreacted methacrylate groups remain on a lab-cured surface). This directly corrects an overly optimistic "indirect composite solves the shrinkage problem" framing if present in CD Q23/Q9 or DM Q5.
21. **Composite-core-material/bonding-agent compatibility warning (new, not in existing drafts)**: PW — a bonding agent must be from the SAME manufacturer as a self-cured composite core material, since some self-cured cores are chemically incompatible with some light-cured bonding agents (redox/amine interaction) — a real, examinable clinical-safety fact.
22. **Occlusal bevels and mechanical retention are NOT routine for Class I/II direct composite — correcting an amalgam-derived assumption some drafts may carry.** STURD, explicit: no bevel is placed on occlusal cavosurface margins of Class I/II composite preps (enamel rod ends already exposed; bevelling would enlarge the isthmus) — bevels are reserved for Class III/IV/V facial margins (45°, 0.5-2mm width). No mechanical retention form (grooves/coves/slots) is needed in Class I/II composite preps except for very large foundations/cusp-capping restorations — pure adhesive retention is the default.
23. **RMGI/flowable "stress-absorbing layer" claim is NOT strongly evidenced — a genuine correction if the draft states this as settled.** STURD, explicit: "those claims are not supported by current clinical evidence."
24. **Caries-control/stepwise-excavation (ICCC) framing may be outdated relative to a modern textbook's own view.** PICK explicitly states stepwise excavation and ART, while historically important, are now "rather redundant" given modern adhesive materials — a materially different framing than older ICCC teaching; present as a genuine textbook-sourced counterpoint to the existing CD Q28 answer, not as an outright replacement.

## B. Gaps filled (substantial dedicated textbook content, especially the WebSearch-sourced DM/RA 13 answers)

- **Light-curing lamp physics and depth-of-cure formula** (PHIL pp.288-290; MCC): quantified radiant-energy requirement ~16 J/cm² for a 2mm layer; worked example 40s×400mW/cm²=16,000mJ/cm²; Beer-Lambert-type depth-of-cure formula I_x = I_0·e^(−μx); camphorquinone peak absorption ~465-480nm, used at 0.1-1.0% (new ed.)/0.2-1.0% (old ed.).
- **MARC device** (PHIL): "Managing Accurate Resin Curing" — a training/monitoring system measuring real intraoral irradiance/energy via a spectroradiometer-fitted mannequin (Price et al. 2010-2011) — good "recent advances in curing technology" content for RA §17.
- **Named low-shrinkage/novel monomer chemistries, with mechanisms and commercial products** (PHIL pp.301-303; AP; PICK) — directly fills the DM §4 "newer materials"/RA "recent advances" gap: Silorane (Filtek LS, 3M ESPE — epoxy+siloxane ring-opening cationic cure; PICK gives exact figure 0.9% shrinkage, requires a dedicated silane-free "Silorane SE Bond" adhesive system); Ormocer (organically modified ceramic; Admira/Definite/Ceram-X — Si-O-Si inorganic network + methacrylate-grafted oligomer, 2-3nm particles; AP gives 1.9 vol% shrinkage, "lowest of resin composites"); POSS (polyhedral oligomeric silsesquioxane, 12-sided silicate cage, used in Artiste Nano-Hybrid); PCDMA/"Alert" (Pentron, polycarbonate dimethacrylate); DX-511/"Kalore" (GC, MW 895 vs Bis-GMA's 512); TCDDMA/"Venus Diamond" (bulky tricyclodecane, steric hindrance/delayed gelation); DDCDMA/"N'Durance" (phase-separating dicarbamate). SDR (Smart Dentin Replacement, Dentsply — VN): a bulk-fill "stress-decreasing resin" using a polymerization-modulator molecular "spring" chain that reduces shrinkage stress WITHOUT reducing cure rate/conversion, designed to fill to the DEJ with a conventional composite cap placed over it.
- **PRIMM and sonic bulk-fill mechanisms** (SIKRI-CD): PRIMM (polymer rigid inorganic matrix material) — a continuous ceramic-fibre scaffold (alumina+SiO2, fibre diameter <2µm) for packable composites, rather than ground particles; SonicFill (Kerr) — sonic vibration transiently lowers viscosity for placement, 1-2% shrinkage, 5mm depth.
- **Antibacterial composites** (SIKRI-CD, RA-relevant): six distinct agent classes — chlorhexidine, silver/oligodynamic action, MDPB, ZnO nanoparticles, antibacterial polycations, DHMAI/MPC.
- **ISO 4049 formal classification** (MCC pp.201-202; CR/MICH Table 9.4): Type 1 (occlusal-surface-rated) vs Type 2; Class 1 (self-cure)/Class 2 (external energy, Group 1=intraoral/Group 2=extraoral-inlay/onlay)/Class 3 (dual-cure); flexural strength minimums (Type1Class1=80MPa, Type1Class2Group2=100MPa, Type2=50MPa); solubility max 7.5µg/mm³, water sorption max 40µg/mm³, working time 90s, setting time max 5min(Class1)/10min(Class3), depth of cure min 1.0mm opaque/1.5mm other shades.
- **Full generational bonding-agent protocol tables** (AP Tables 2.6-2.9) — exact second-by-second steps (etch time, primer scrub time, adhesive scrub time, light-cure time) for 4th/5th/8th-generation ("Universal") agents in total-etch, selective-etch, and self-etch modes; the "bonding stack" concept (soft-tissue management → environment control → substrate management → adhesion → material placement → occlusal control — any single failed link collapses the whole stack); the composite-specific intaglio-surface-treatment protocol (steam clean → alumina blast → alcohol clean → adhesive → resin cement) distinct from ceramic/metal/zirconia protocols already covered by the ceramics audit.
- **Full illustrated step-by-step clinical protocols for every cavity class** (PICK Ch.7, ≥80 captioned photos) — fissure sealant, PRR, Class I/II/III/IV/V, bonded large amalgam, Nayyar core, fibre-post/composite core — each with exact timings (etch 15-20s, primer 5-10s, air-thin 5-10s, light-cure 20s). The DIRECT COMPOSITE VENEER (Class IV) protocol specifically: shade selection before dehydration, undulating/scalloped bevel to mask margin, custom lingual silicone-putty matrix, dentin-shade-first/enamel-shade-rolled-incisally-to-cervically layering, tints between "lobes" for opalescence — independently and near-identically corroborated by STURD (Fig 19.44), a strong two-source confirmation.
- **Matrix systems — named, with clinical tips** (PICK/STURD): Siqveland, Tofflemire, Automatrix, Omni-matrix, Palodent/Composi-Tight/V-Ring/Quickmat sectional systems; BiTine ring mechanics; prewedging rationale; burnishing metal bands before use; composite warmers (Calset, up to 68°C) as a viscosity-lowering adaptation aid.
- **The "direct-indirect" (semidirect) composite veneer technique — an entire paradigm likely missing from CD Q8** (CVEN, a dedicated monograph): full 10-step protocol (shade selection → sculpt directly on unprepared tooth → initial intraoral light cure → remove → supplemental extraoral light cure → heat tempering → extraoral+intraoral finishing → extraoral polishing → shade try-in with try-in pastes → adhesive luting); named advantages (knife-edge margins only achievable extraorally, color modulation via luting resin, no provisionalization needed); heat-tempering regimens table (microwave 450-500W×3-5min; electric oven 120°C×10min; pressure-polymerization unit 120°C×10min; autoclave dry-cycle 121-134°C×15min); the **0.5mm thickness threshold distinguishing a "contact lens" (0.2-0.5mm, monochromatic, shape-correction only) from a "veneer" (>0.5mm, layered dentin+enamel, corrects shape AND color)** — same A1 shade reads as A2 at 1.0mm, A3 at 1.5mm; a full Type 1/2/3 contact-lens and Type 1/2 veneer sub-classification by incisal-augmentation need and layer composition; veneer-preparation-design classification for discolored teeth (overlap-incisal-edge/palatal-chamfer/butt-joint vs no-overlap/window/feather-edge, with survival inversely related to preparation depth/extent into dentin); direct-indirect Class V inlay technique for NCCLs (no-prep vs beveled by lesion type, tooth-colored vs gingiva-colored composite choice).
- **Marzouk's six-generation composite-by-filler-reinforcement-type taxonomy, with six supply-system types** (MARZ, DM/RA-relevant, genuinely distinct from the usual macro/micro/hybrid/nanofill scheme): (A) 1st-gen macroceramic (highest lab mechanical properties, highest destructive wear clinically); (B) 2nd-gen colloidal/microceramic (best surface texture, poor CTE ~65ppm/°C); (C) 3rd-gen hybrid (75:25 macro:micro); (D) 4th-gen hybrid with heat-cured pre-polymerized macro-particles (highest shrinkage of all generations, very technique-sensitive); (E) 5th-gen spherical heat-cured macro-particles (improved wettability); (F) 6th-gen sintered agglomerated micro-ceramic particles (best mechanical properties, least shrinkage, "condensable").
- **Marzouk's five-type bevel/margin classification for composite cavity design** (MARZ, CD-relevant, genuinely novel vs the usual single "bevel" concept): (1) partial bevel, (2) long bevel, (3) hollow-ground bevel (gingival walls/inaccessible areas, direct-loading zones), (4) scalloped margin (increases retentive surface area, contraindicated at gingival/inaccessible margins), (5) skirting (wide shallow defects relying on enamel retention alone).
- **Pulp-dentin "effective depth" safety thresholds specific to composite** (MARZ, distinct from amalgam/silicate tables): healthy reparative reaction if remaining dentin thickness ≥3mm; unhealthy/possible pulpal reaction 1.5-3mm; always destruction if <1.5mm (assuming etch acid doesn't reach dentin directly).
- **Fibre-reinforced composite commercial products and clinical outcome data** (VN Table 2.2.5): Fibre-Kor, Stick, Vectris, Ribbond etc. by fibre type/form; FRC splints perform well to 4+ years, removable-denture FRC outcomes "more variable." **Confirmed, not fixable: NONE of the six sources name the Krenchel factor or give uniaxial/biaxial/random orientation-efficiency numbers** — this quantitative FRC theory (already in the existing CD Q21/RA-Q4 answers, correctly sourced to McCabe & Walls' §22.6 in an earlier part of this project) is apparently NOT in the composite-chapter extracts any of the six readers found this time, despite MCC being directly assigned — the existing citation to MCC §22.6 should be re-verified but is plausible since that specific subsection may sit just outside what this pass's readers paged through.
- **Core build-up composite specifics** (PW/AOD): composite favored over amalgam/GIC when >50% coronal structure lost; GIC core requires ≥2 intact walls + 1-2mm ferrule; composite core bonds to dentin, finishes immediately, high rigidity, better color under all-ceramic crowns.
- **Componeers** (SIKRI-CD): prefabricated composite veneer shell system — split rubber dam, contour-guide sizing, exact shell thicknesses (0.3mm cervical/0.5mm middle/0.7mm incisal).
- **Named composite-inlay-system generations with cure parameters** (SIKRI-CD Table 19.6): SR Isosit, Coltene Brilliant, Art Glass, Belleglass HP/NG, Tescera, Sculpture, Herculite, with exact temperatures/pressures/times.

## C. High-value new facts (by likely question/topic)

**History**: silicates introduced 1871 (obsolete by 1960s, dissolution/crazing); unfilled acrylics preceded composites (dimensional instability failure); Bowen 1962 (US Patent 3,006,112) is the actual milestone citation; Buonocore 1955 enamel acid-etch discovery preceded/enabled Bowen's clinical utility; GI developed 1960s, RMGI "late 1980s," compomers introduced 1995 (MICH, explicit date).

**Toughness/crack mechanics**: strong filler-matrix coupling → crack must detour around filler (toughening); weak coupling → "crack blunting" (a void opens ahead of the crack tip, perpendicular to propagation, blunting it) (PHIL Fig 13-16) — good mechanistic content for DM "fillers' effect on properties." Fracture toughness (MCC): conventional composite ≈1.2 MN·m⁻¹·⁵; unfilled acrylic ≈1.0; heavily filled hybrid ≈2.0 (context comparator: mild steel ≈100).

**Radiopacity**: PHIL gives 2-4mm Al equivalence for hybrids, 0.5-2mm Al for microfilled; radiopacity should not greatly exceed enamel's, or it masks secondary caries/gaps. MICH's OLDER figures: 2mm dentin ≈2.5mm aluminum; enamel ≈4mm aluminum; ISO minimum acceptable = 2mm Al equivalent; amalgam >10mm Al equivalent.

**Survival/outcome data (quantified, strong, largely missing from WebSearch-sourced drafts)**: Chadwick et al. 2001 — amalgam 7-yr survival 94.5% vs composite 67.4%. Opdam et al. 2007 — composite 5-yr 91.7%, 10-yr 82.2% (private practice). Wilder et al. 1999 — 76% at 17 years (UV-cured). da Rosa Rodolpho et al. 2006 — 65% at 17 years (hybrid light-cured). Manhart et al. 2004 — indirect composite inlay annual failure 1.9% (same as amalgam) vs 3.6% direct composite vs 1.4% cast gold inlay. Placement-time data (Chadwick 2001): Class I amalgam 11min vs composite 15min; Class II amalgam 15min vs composite 24min; relative lifetime cost ratio amalgam:composite:ceramic-inlay = 1:1.62:6.35 (5-yr).

**Biocompatibility/BPA**: Bis-GMA/TEGDMA/UDMA all cytotoxic in vitro as pure monomer; dentin barrier reduces (not eliminates) pulpal exposure; low-MW monomers (TEGDMA, MMA) leach more than high-MW (Bis-GMA, UDMA). BPA/BPA-dimethacrylate (BPA-DM) specifically is the estrogenic agent of concern per newer studies — "BPA-DM should be restricted... the effect of BPA [itself] is negligible" (PHIL); Olea et al. 1996 found BPA in saliva 1hr post-sealant placement; cured/commercial composite estrogenicity "not demonstrated," earlier pediatric-estrogenicity claims "largely discredited" (both CR and MICH, same conclusion).

**Ion leaching**: silicon leaches most (15-17µg/mL in first 30 days; microfilled composites leach more slowly, +100% to 14.2µg/mL in the second 30-day window); boron/barium/strontium leach 6-19µg/mL.

**Bond strengths**: enamel/dentin-to-composite 20-30MPa (CR/MICH, identical); restorative-material/cement interface 20-30MPa irrespective of material type via the double-bonding mechanism (AP); smear layer 1-2µm thick, etching removes it + demineralizes peritubular dentin to 5-8µm depth; enamel etch pattern Types I-III micro-porosities ~20 microns deep (AP). Etching changes enamel surface area up to 2000× original; valley depth ≈25 microns; etch should be perpendicular to prism heads, not parallel to prism sides (MARZ). Etched enamel must never contact saliva before bonding — instant salivary glycoprotein biofilm blocks further adhesion; re-etching possible after decontamination (MARZ).

**Wear mechanisms**: three-way classification — abrasive (2-body/3-body), fatigue, corrosive wear, with corrosive+stress combination causing "stress corrosion cracking" (VN).

**Shade/colour science for composite specifically**: value assessed first (rods >> cones in retina, brightness mismatches most noticeable), then chroma, then hue; clear/low-opacity try-in paste first, escalate only if value too high (CVEN). Prolonged exposure to high-intensity light degrades the clinician's own colour perception (VN) — a clinical pearl. Tints vs opaquers distinction with concrete recipes (CVEN: 1 part red : 5 parts yellow : 5 parts ochre for cervical caramel hue); opaquer ideal opacity 50-65% — over-opaquing causes a "lifeless, dull, high-value" look.
