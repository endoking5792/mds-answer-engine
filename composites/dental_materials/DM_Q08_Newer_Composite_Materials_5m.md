## EXAM MODE

**(Estimated writing time: ~8 min)**

### Answer
Newer composite materials include bulk-fill composites, which use modulated photoinitiator and shrinkage-stress-relieving chemistry to permit single increments up to 4mm deep rather than the traditional 2mm incremental technique; nanofill and nanohybrid composites, which use nanomer and nanocluster filler engineering to combine high filler loading with sub-wavelength optical properties for simultaneous polish and strength; bioactive, ion-releasing composites such as giomer-type materials incorporating surface pre-reacted glass-ionomer fillers for fluoride and multi-ion release without sacrificing composite-level esthetics; and, most recently, digitally fabricated composite materials — CAD/CAM milled resin composite blocks that are factory-polymerized under high pressure and temperature for improved conversion and strength over direct composite, and 3D-printed resin composites fabricated by vat-photopolymerization, both still being validated for long-term clinical performance.

- Bulk-fill, nanofill/nanohybrid, bioactive/giomer, CAD/CAM blocks, 3D-printed composite — the five-item "newer materials" checklist.
- Digital fabrication (CAD/CAM, 3D printing) is the genuinely newest direction, distinct from filler-chemistry advances.
