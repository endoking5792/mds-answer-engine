## EXAM MODE

**(Estimated writing time: ~8 min)**

### Answer
Newer composite materials include named low-shrinkage monomer systems — Silorane (Filtek LS), using ring-opening cationic polymerization to reach shrinkage as low as 0.9% but requiring its own dedicated adhesive; Ormocer (Admira, Ceram-X), building an inorganic Si-O-Si network backbone to reach a reported 1.9 vol% shrinkage; and SDR, a bulk-fill "stress-decreasing resin" using a polymerization-modulator spring-chain mechanism to reduce shrinkage stress without reducing cure; bulk-fill composites more broadly, which use modulated photoinitiator and shrinkage-stress-relieving chemistry to permit single increments up to 4mm deep rather than the traditional 2mm incremental technique; nanofill and nanohybrid composites, which use nanomer and nanocluster filler engineering to combine high filler loading with sub-wavelength optical properties for simultaneous polish and strength; bioactive, ion-releasing composites such as giomer-type materials incorporating surface pre-reacted glass-ionomer fillers for fluoride and multi-ion release, alongside a distinct family of antibacterial composites (chlorhexidine, silver, MDPB, ZnO nanoparticles, antibacterial polycations, DHMAI/MPC) without sacrificing composite-level esthetics; and, most recently, digitally fabricated composite materials — CAD/CAM milled resin composite blocks that are factory-polymerized under high pressure and temperature for improved conversion and strength over direct composite, and 3D-printed resin composites fabricated by vat-photopolymerization, both still being validated for long-term clinical performance. The common thread across the newest of these materials is a shift away from purely chairside, intraorally light-cured polymerization toward factory- or digitally-controlled curing, a genuinely different direction from the filler/matrix-chemistry refinements that defined earlier "newer materials" in this same topic.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Named monomer chemistries (short-note essentials): Silorane/Filtek LS (0.9% shrinkage, own adhesive), Ormocer/Admira-Ceram-X (1.9vol% shrinkage), SDR (stress-decreasing spring-chain).
- Bulk-fill, nanofill/nanohybrid, bioactive/giomer + antibacterial agents, CAD/CAM blocks, 3D-printed composite — the "newer materials" checklist.
- Digital fabrication (CAD/CAM, 3D printing) is the genuinely newest direction, distinct from filler-chemistry advances.
