## STUDY MODE
Flowable composites reduce filler loading relative to a conventional hybrid by the same resin chemistry, which lowers viscosity enough for syringe-tip delivery into fine or inaccessible areas without hand condensation, at the cost of higher polymerization shrinkage and lower strength/wear resistance than a packable/universal composite [SRC, condensed]. **Exact figures differ by source (new):** Powers & Wataha give flowable filler loading as 42-53 vol% with particle size 0.4-3.0µm; Craig's gives a wider 42-62 vol% range [PW/CR]. **Van Noort's named recent advance:** "Vertise Flow," a self-etching flowable composite that combines an etch/prime/bond step with the flowable material itself [VN].

## EXAM MODE

**(Estimated writing time: ~8 min)**

### Answer
Flowable composites are conventional dimethacrylate-resin composites reformulated with a reduced filler loading compared with a standard hybrid composite, which lowers their viscosity enough to be delivered directly from a fine syringe tip and flow passively into narrow or difficult-to-access areas without hand condensation. The exact loading figure differs by source: Powers & Wataha give flowable filler content as 42-53 vol% with a particle size of 0.4-3.0µm, while Craig's gives a somewhat wider 42-62 vol% range. This lower filler loading is a direct trade-off: flowable composites shrink more on polymerization and have lower strength and wear resistance than a conventional packable or universal hybrid composite of the same resin chemistry, so their accepted clinical use is confined to specific low-stress-bearing roles — lining the base of a Class I/II preparation to improve adaptation before a stronger composite is layered over it, sealing pits and fissures, repairing small defects, and filling the proximal box angle where hand-condensation access is poor — rather than as a bulk restorative material in load-bearing areas. A named recent advance worth citing is van Noort's "Vertise Flow," a self-etching flowable composite that combines the etch/prime/bond step with the flowable material itself, reducing the number of clinical steps for small, low-stress restorations.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Flowable = reduced filler → lower viscosity, syringe-tip delivery. PW: 42-53vol%/0.4-3.0µm particle size; CR: 42-62vol%.
- Trade-off: higher shrinkage, lower strength/wear resistance than hybrid/packable.
- Clinical use: cavity liner, pit/fissure sealant, small repairs, proximal box access — NOT bulk load-bearing restoration.
- Vertise Flow (VN): named self-etching flowable composite, a recent advance.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Omitting the shrinkage/strength trade-off that comes with reduced filler.
2. Listing flowable as a bulk restorative option rather than a liner/access/sealant material.

## MNEMONIC
**"Low fill, low fill"** — low FILLER, low FILL-strength: the two-word summary of flowable composite's entire trade-off.
