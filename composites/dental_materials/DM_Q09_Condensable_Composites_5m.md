## EXAM MODE

**(Estimated writing time: ~8 min)**

### Answer
Condensable, or packable, composites are a high-viscosity subtype of direct resin composite engineered primarily through modified filler particle geometry — fibrous or irregular, interlocking filler particles rather than the spherical particles used in conventional hybrids — which raises the material's stiffness and reduces its tendency to stick to hand instruments, allowing an amalgam-like condensation and contouring technique rather than the usual brush/instrument placement of conventional composite. Some packable products report filler content around 80% by volume, though Michigan gives a more specific figure of 66-70 vol% for packable fillers with an associated wear rate of approximately 3.5µm per year, described as similar to amalgam's own wear rate — a genuinely favourable, specific comparator worth citing. One named technology, PRIMM (polymer rigid inorganic matrix material), achieves its packable handling characteristics through a structurally different route from simple particle-geometry modification: a continuous ceramic-fibre scaffold of alumina and silica, with fibre diameter under 2µm, replaces the usual ground filler particles entirely. The important, examinable nuance is that this is primarily a HANDLING advantage rather than a clearly established materials advantage: laboratory comparisons found packable composites' mechanical properties falling between or below non-packable materials, with polymerization contraction similar to or even higher than conventional composite in some comparisons, and no material achieving adequate depth of cure in increments thicker than 2mm despite marketing suggesting otherwise. Condensable composites are therefore best understood as a handling-technique innovation targeted at posterior, heavy-occlusal-load restorations resembling amalgam placement, not as a composite sub-type with intrinsically superior mechanical or shrinkage properties over a well-placed conventional hybrid.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Condensable = modified (fibrous/irregular) filler geometry → higher viscosity, less instrument stickiness → amalgam-like condensation technique.
- PRIMM (SIKRI-CD): a continuous ceramic-fibre scaffold (alumina+SiO2, fibre diameter <2µm) replacing ground filler particles entirely.
- ~80% filler by volume in some products; Michigan's specific figure: 66-70vol% with wear rate ~3.5µm/year, similar to amalgam.
- CONTESTED: mechanical properties/shrinkage NOT consistently superior to non-packable composite in lab studies — a handling advantage, not a proven material advantage.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Claiming condensable composite has definitively lower shrinkage or superior strength — the evidence does not consistently support this.
2. Omitting that the main engineered change is filler PARTICLE GEOMETRY, not simply "more filler."

## MNEMONIC
**"Feels different, isn't always better"** — condensable composite's handling genuinely changes; its material properties don't reliably improve.
