## EXAM MODE

**(Estimated writing time: ~7-9 min)**

### Answer
Matrix selection matters more for composite restoration than for amalgam, because composite cannot be actively condensed against a matrix the way amalgam can — proximal contact quality therefore depends almost entirely on the matrix's own contour and position being correct before any material is placed. Several matrix systems are available, each with real trade-offs. Ultrathin metal bands suit general Class II use and resist deformation well. A standard Tofflemire retainer wraps both proximal surfaces and must be wedged to compensate for two band thicknesses at once, whereas a customised, compound-supported matrix for a single proximal box only needs to compensate for one band thickness, making good contact easier to achieve. Clear polyester strip matrices are recommended only for small Class II, III or IV preparations, since they contour and resist condensation-type force poorly with viscous composite. Sectional matrix systems with a separator ring simplify proximal-contact establishment and achieve genuinely greater marginal-ridge strength than a circumferential band, though careless use on a wide faciolingual preparation can deform the band and produce a poor contour.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Composite cannot be condensed against a matrix — contact depends almost entirely on matrix precision before placement.
- Tofflemire (2 thicknesses) vs. compound-supported (1 thickness, easier contact) vs. clear polyester (small preps only) vs. sectional+ring (better marginal-ridge strength, but can deform on wide preps).
