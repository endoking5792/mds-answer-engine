## STUDY MODE
Matrix selection matters more for composite than amalgam, since composite cannot be condensed against a matrix — proximal contact depends almost entirely on correct matrix contour/position before placement [SRC, condensed from Q3]. Real options and trade-offs: ultrathin metal bands (general Class II use); Tofflemire retainer (two-surface preps, must wedge for TWO band thicknesses); compound-supported custom matrix (single proximal box, only ONE band thickness to compensate for, easier contact); clear polyester strip (small Class II/III/IV preps only, poor contour control with viscous composite); sectional matrix + separator ring (convenient, genuinely better marginal-ridge strength than circumferential bands, but can deform on wide faciolingual preps) [SRC, condensed from Q3/Q18].

**Named systems (TEXTBOOK AUDIT 2026-10-15, PICK/STURD)**: circumferential — **Siqveland** (vice-tightened), **Tofflemire** (universal, slotted-head, knurled-knob tightened), **Automatrix/Omni-matrix** (retainerless, ratchet-tag); sectional — **Palodent, Composi-Tight, Quickmat** paired with a separating **V-Ring** or **BiTine ring** (tines engage the embrasure, elastic recoil separates teeth AND holds the band against the adjacent tooth). **Prewedge BEFORE band/ring placement** (not after) to pre-separate teeth, protect the papilla, and improve cervical seal; **burnish** the band before insertion to pre-impart contour, since composite cannot be condensed to create contour afterward [PICK/STURD].

## EXAM MODE

**(Estimated writing time: ~7-9 min)**

### Answer
Matrix selection matters more for composite restoration than for amalgam, because composite cannot be actively condensed against a matrix the way amalgam can — proximal contact quality therefore depends almost entirely on the matrix's own contour and position being correct before any material is placed. Several matrix systems are available, each with real trade-offs. Ultrathin metal bands suit general Class II use and resist deformation well. A standard Tofflemire retainer wraps both proximal surfaces and must be wedged to compensate for two band thicknesses at once, whereas a customised, compound-supported matrix for a single proximal box only needs to compensate for one band thickness, making good contact easier to achieve. Clear polyester strip matrices are recommended only for small Class II, III or IV preparations, since they contour and resist condensation-type force poorly with viscous composite. Sectional matrix systems with a separator ring simplify proximal-contact establishment and achieve genuinely greater marginal-ridge strength than a circumferential band, though careless use on a wide faciolingual preparation can deform the band and produce a poor contour. Named systems illustrate these categories concretely: the Siqveland matrix is vice-tightened; the Tofflemire, the most widely used universal retainer, is tightened via a knurled knob on a slotted head; Automatrix and Omni-matrix are retainerless, ratchet-tag-tightened bands; and among sectional systems, Palodent, Composi-Tight and Quickmat are paired with a V-Ring or BiTine separating ring, whose tines engage the embrasure and whose elastic recoil both separates the teeth and holds the band against the adjacent tooth. Prewedging — placing the wedge before the band or ring, not after — pre-separates the teeth and protects the papilla, and burnishing the band before insertion pre-imparts contour that composite cannot achieve by condensation afterward.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Composite cannot be condensed against a matrix — contact depends almost entirely on matrix precision before placement.
- Tofflemire (2 thicknesses) vs. compound-supported (1 thickness, easier contact) vs. clear polyester (small preps only) vs. sectional+ring (better marginal-ridge strength, but can deform on wide preps).
- Named: Siqveland, Tofflemire, Automatrix/Omni-matrix (circumferential); Palodent/Composi-Tight/Quickmat + V-Ring/BiTine (sectional). Prewedge before band placement; burnish band before insertion.
