## Explanation

Multinational working groups, including ANSI and ISO scientists, were formed "to develop standard ISO 10993, published in 1992" (Sakaguchi, Ferracane and Powers, 2019) [SRC]. "ISO 10993 is not restricted to dental materials"; "In 2010, ISO Standard 10993 consisted of 20 parts" — for example "Part 3 specifies tests for genotoxicity, carcinogenicity, and reproductive toxicity", "Part 4 describes tests for materials that interact with blood", and "Part 20 specifies the principles and methods for immunotoxicology testing"; "The standard identifies initial tests for cytotoxicity, sensitization, systemic toxicity, and supplementary tests for chronic toxicity, carcinogenicity, and biodegradation" (Anusavice, Shen and Rawls, 2013) [SRC]. Selection guidance is "based on how long the material will be present; whether it will contact body surface only, blood, or bone", but "the ultimate selection of tests for a specific material is left up to the manufacturer" (Sakaguchi, Ferracane and Powers, 2019) [SRC]. Its dental counterpart is ISO 7405:2008, "Preclinical evaluation of biocompatibility of medical devices used in dentistry" (Sakaguchi, Ferracane and Powers, 2019) [SRC], which describes "all usage tests" and dental cell tests "such as the dentin barrier test" (Anusavice, Shen and Rawls, 2013) [SRC]. ANSI/ADA Specification 41 "was further updated in 2005" to conform to ISO 10993 (Sakaguchi, Ferracane and Powers, 2019) [SRC]. Standards are slow to change: "they tend to be developed quite slowly" (Anusavice, Shen and Rawls, 2013) [SRC]. The 2002 edition of Craig's describes an earlier version that "contains 12 parts, each dealing with a different aspect of biological testing", where "part 2 addresses animal welfare requirements" (Craig and Powers, 2002) [SRC] — the standard has grown, not changed in principle.

## ✎ Exam core

**ISO 10993** ("Biological evaluation of medical devices") is the international standard for biocompatibility testing. It was published in 1992 and covers all medical devices, not only dental materials (Sakaguchi, Ferracane and Powers, 2019; Anusavice, Shen and Rawls, 2013).

**Structure:** 12 parts in 2002 (Part 2: animal welfare) and 20 parts by 2010 (Craig and Powers, 2002; Anusavice, Shen and Rawls, 2013), for example Part 3 (genotoxicity, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity), Part 4 (interaction with blood) and Part 20 (immunotoxicology).

**Tests**
- **Initial:** cytotoxicity, sensitization, systemic toxicity (in vitro or in animals).
- **Supplementary:** chronic toxicity, carcinogenicity, biodegradation (mostly in animals).

**Selection of tests (Part 1)** depends on how long the material stays in the body, whether it contacts the body surface, blood or bone, and whether it communicates externally. The final choice and legal responsibility rest with the manufacturer.

**ISO 7405:2008** is the dental companion: it describes the usage tests (pulp, bone, mucosa) and dental-specific tests such as the dentin barrier test. The American ANSI/ADA Specification 41 was revised in 2005 to conform to ISO 10993.

**Limitation:** standards are developed slowly by consensus and lag behind new science (Anusavice, Shen and Rawls, 2013).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- 1992; all medical devices; 20 parts (2010).
- Initial: cytotoxicity, sensitization, systemic toxicity.
- Dental: ISO 7405:2008.
