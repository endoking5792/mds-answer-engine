## STUDY MODE
A universal adhesive can be applied etch-and-rinse, self-etch or selective-etch, so the operator decides what happens to the smear layer. Etch-and-rinse removes it; self-etch modifies it (Hilton et al, 2013) [SRC]. Mild self-etch systems bond dentin well and enamel less well; aggressive ones the reverse (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. With mild and ultramild systems a thick layer lowers bond strength, so abrade walls with fine diamonds, scrub the adhesive and etch enamel selectively (Hilton et al, 2013) [SRC].

Recent in vitro data: in one 2026 study fine (600-grit) finishing gave the highest dentin shear bond for self-etch and universal adhesives, with 120-grit lowest (Verma et al, 2026) [LIT, abstract]; in a 2025 study of a universal adhesive on cut and uncut enamel, etch-and-rinse mode gave the highest bond (Jowkar et al, 2025) [LIT, abstract]; a 2026 narrative review found alumina air abrasion often lowered dentin bond with self-etch adhesives (Polak et al, 2026) [LIT, abstract]. All are laboratory studies; no clinical comparison was retrieved.

### Why the question arises
Universal (multimode) adhesives can be used etch-and-rinse, self-etch or selective-etch. The mode decides what happens to the smear layer.

### Smear layer in each mode
| Mode | Smear layer | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Etch-and-rinse | Removed; dentin demineralised | Keep dentin moist |
| Self-etch | Modified (fixed and infiltrated) | Strong systems dissolve it; mild and ultramild cannot |
| Selective enamel etch | Enamel etched with phosphoric acid, dentin self-etched | Common compromise |

### Rules for self-etch and universal adhesives
- Mild and ultramild: the thicker the layer, the lower the bond. Reduce thickness by finishing walls with fine-grit diamond burs; actively scrub the adhesive; allow longer application.
- At enamel a thick layer neutralises mild acidic monomers; selective etching with phosphoric acid should always be performed.
- Mild self-etch adhesives bond dentin well and enamel poorly; aggressive ones the reverse.
- The 10-MDP monomer bonds to hydroxyapatite left after self-etch and improves resistance to biodegradation.

### Recent in vitro data
- Fine (600-grit) finishing gave the highest dentin bond for self-etch and universal adhesives; coarse (120-grit) the lowest (2026).
- Etch-and-rinse mode gave the highest enamel bond for a universal adhesive on cut and uncut enamel (2025).
- Alumina air abrasion improved etch-and-rinse bonding but often reduced dentin bond with self-etch adhesives (2026 review).

### Limitation
All laboratory bench data; no clinical comparison retrieved.


### Alani and Patel
Universals are used in total-etch, selective-etch or self-etch mode, the etching being left to operator preference, but phosphoric acid on enamel is always advised whichever bonding agent is used; re-etch if the etched surface is contaminated.

### Conclusion
Choose the mode by substrate: selective enamel etching plus self-etch dentin, with fine-bur finishing, is a reasoned default; no mode is shown best clinically.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
Selective enamel etch; fine-diamond finishing; thick layer lowers mild self-etch bond.
