## Retrieval Report
Reuses Q01 retrieval; additional reads of the Sikri Ch.17 agent-by-agent section and Cohen's Ch.8 final-rinse section. "Novel" is taken to mean agents beyond EDTA/NaOCl: that reading is my inference [Guessing]; the 2015 sitting did not define novel. Journal items are abstract-level.

## STUDY MODE
The conventional pair is 17% EDTA to chelate the mineral and NaOCl to remove the organic part: EDTA alone cannot remove it (Berman et al, 2021) [SRC] and NaOCl cannot remove the inorganic part (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019) [SRC]. Newer agents try to combine chelation with disinfection, work in less time, spare dentin, or work apically where EDTA is weak (Berman et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Combination irrigants.** *MTAD* (3% doxycycline, 4.25% citric acid, 0.5% polysorbate; low pH 2.15) demineralised dentin faster than 17% EDTA in the studies Sikri (2019) reports [SRC]. Cohen's recommends 1.3% NaOCl during instrumentation, then 1 mL MTAD for 5 minutes and 4 mL as final rinse (Berman et al, 2021) [SRC]. Cohen's cites two studies finding MTAD and EDTA equally effective at smear layer removal [SRC]. *QMix* (bisguanide, polyaminocarboxylic acid, surfactant) is a one-step 60-90 second final rinse (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]; Cohen's reports it as effective as 17% EDTA in the studies it cites (Berman et al, 2021) [SRC]. *SmearClear* (17% EDTA plus two surfactants) was similar to EDTA coronally and better apically in studies cited by Sikri (2019) [SRC]; the added surfactants lower surface tension, but Cohen's says surfactants did not improve chelator action in other studies (Berman et al, 2021) [SRC]. Two sources disagree here; I present both [Certain].

**Natural and alternative agents** (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]: super-oxidised water (comparable to 17% EDTA, less erosion); chitosan 0.2-0.6% for 3 minutes (adequate removal, less erosion than EDTA); 10% citric acid sequenced with NaOCl; 40% polyacrylic acid for 30 seconds; 25% tannic acid (partial removal in the apical third); tetracycline family. Evidence is laboratory SEM.

**Mechanical and activation systems.** XP-endo Finisher reaches the 40-50% of the wall that rotary instruments do not touch (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Ultrasonic activation: effective at 3-5 minutes, ineffective at one (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]; mixed overall (Basrani, 2015) [SRC]. Lasers: Er:YAG removed more than Nd:YAG; difficulty in narrow canals and possible peritubular dentin damage (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]; laser-activated irrigation removes it in less time than PUI in the studies Cohen's cites (Berman et al, 2021) [SRC].

**Newest, preclinical.** 2026 systematic review of nanoparticle-assisted irrigation (12 extracted-tooth studies): promising antibiofilm and some smear layer removal, no clinical evidence (Hussein and Ali, 2026) [LIT, abstract]. 2026 review of GentleWave: more debris and smear removal in most studies but inconsistent superiority over passive ultrasonic irrigation (Usta et al, 2026) [LIT, abstract]. 2025 review: solution forms removed smear layer better than gel forms (Teja et al, 2025) [LIT, abstract].

**Costs.** Longer or repeated chelator exposure erodes dentin (Berman et al, 2021) [SRC]; NaOCl after chelators erodes exposed collagen, which is why combination rinses that avoid a second NaOCl are attractive (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019) [SRC].

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
*Claim: newer agents are superior to EDTA + NaOCl.* Sources find them equal (MTAD, QMix against EDTA) or better only in selected regions (SmearClear apically). No source shows better healing. All evidence is SEM or laboratory. Implication: choose for convenience (one-step rinse), apical performance or erosion risk, not for proven outcome [Likely].

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~34 minutes)*

### 1. Introduction
The smear layer is part mineral, part organic. The standard approach is 17% EDTA to chelate the mineral and NaOCl to dissolve the organic part. EDTA alone cannot remove it, and NaOCl cannot remove the inorganic part. "Novel" agents try to combine chelation with disinfection, work faster, spare dentin, or reach the apical third where EDTA is weak.

### 2. Reference: EDTA and NaOCl
17% EDTA decalcifies dentin to about 20-30 μm in 5 minutes; 1 minute with 10 mL is adequate and 10 minutes erodes peritubular and intratubular dentin. Action is weaker apically. NaOCl used after a chelator erodes exposed collagen and may weaken root dentin; limit it to under 30 seconds.

### 3. Combination irrigants
| Agent | Composition | Use and findings |
|---|---|---|
| **MTAD** | 3% doxycycline, 4.25% citric acid, 0.5% polysorbate (pH 2.15) | 1.3% NaOCl during instrumentation; 1 mL for 5 min then 4 mL final rinse; demineralised faster than 17% EDTA in some studies, equal to EDTA in two studies cited by Cohen's |
| **QMix** | Bisguanide (antimicrobial), polyaminocarboxylic acid (chelator), surfactant | Final rinse 60-90 s; saves sequential EDTA and chlorhexidine; equal to or better than 17% EDTA; low surface tension, effective on E. faecalis |
| **SmearClear** | 17% EDTA + two surfactants | Similar to EDTA coronally and middle; better apically in studies cited; effective in 1 minute. Cohen's reports surfactants did not enhance chelators in other studies (disagreement) |
| **Solvidont** | Bis-dequalinium acetate + EDTA | Effective even apically |
| **EDTAC** | EDTA + cetavlon | 15 minutes; negligible apically; more caustic |

### 4. Acids, natural and other agents
- **Citric acid 10%** (best of the organic acids), sequenced with NaOCl; **polyacrylic acid** 40% for 30 s; **lactic acid** 50% less effective (viscosity); **tannic acid** 25% partial removal apically; **tetracyclines** (doxycycline, minocycline) chelate calcium, low pH.
- **Super-oxidised water** (reactive oxygen, neutral pH): comparable to 17% EDTA with less erosion.
- **Chitosan** 0.2-0.6% for 3 minutes: chelating, biocompatible, less erosion than EDTA; may improve dentin resistance to degradation.

### 5. Devices and activation
- **XP-endo Finisher** (25/.00, changes shape in the canal): reaches the 40-50% of wall untouched by rotary files.
- **Canal brush:** little added benefit; irrigant does the work.
- **Passive ultrasonic irrigation:** effective at 3-5 minutes, not at 1 minute in one account; results mixed; best with EDTA/EDTAC plus NaOCl.
- **Lasers:** Nd:YAG (1064 nm) and Er:YAG (2940 nm); Er:YAG removes more because water absorbs its energy; CO2 also works. Limits: narrow canal access, large probes (300 μm flexible fibres now), possible damage to peritubular dentin.
- **Laser-activated irrigation:** promising, removes smear layer in less time than PUI in studies cited by Cohen's.

### 6. Recent, preclinical developments
- **Nanoparticle-assisted irrigation** (2026 systematic review, 12 extracted-tooth studies): promising antibiofilm and some smear removal; no clinical evidence.
- **GentleWave** (2026 review of 12 in vitro studies): generally enhanced debris and smear removal; superiority over PUI inconsistent.
- **Gels versus solutions** (2025 review): solutions removed smear layer better; gels left more debris and raised file separation risk.

### 7. Critical comment
Almost every claim for a newer agent rests on SEM in extracted teeth, graded by region. Most show equivalence to EDTA, with advantages mainly in convenience, apical performance, or reduced erosion. None has shown better healing.

### Additional laboratory findings
- QMix demonstrated better debridement of smear layer from the apical third than NaOCl, EDTA and chlorhexidine in one reported study; QMix must not meet NaOCl directly, so an intermediate saline rinse is needed to avoid precipitate.
- 17% EDTAC for 1 minute removes the layer without erosion, with no difference in dentin microhardness from EDTA after 1 minute.
- 2% chitosan removed more smear layer apically; MTAD removes it with less erosion than EDTA in one account yet was not as effective as QMix and EDTA in another (state both).
- Calcium removal from the canal was most efficient with SmearClear, then MTAD (one study).

### 8. Conclusion
Newer agents refine rather than replace the EDTA-NaOCl principle. Choose by convenience, apical performance and erosion risk, not on a claim of proven clinical superiority.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Mineral by chelator, organic by NaOCl.
- MTAD composition and 1.3% NaOCl protocol.
- QMix 60-90 s.
- Er:YAG better than Nd:YAG.
- Gel worse than solution for smear removal (2025 review).
