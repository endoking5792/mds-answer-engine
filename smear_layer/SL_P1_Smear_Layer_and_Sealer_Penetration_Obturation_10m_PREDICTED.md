## STUDY MODE
Sealer needs contact with dentin to penetrate and adhere. Evidence that sealer penetration into tubules does not occur when the smear layer is present (Berman et al, 2021) [SRC]; removal increased penetration depth by 10 to 80 μm in studies cited by Hilton et al (2013) [SRC]. Pooled leakage tests mostly show no difference or an advantage for removal (Shahravan et al, 2007) [LIT, abstract]. Thermoplasticised techniques produce deeper penetration and the smear layer may prevent gutta-percha penetration (Berman et al, 2021) [SRC]. Self-etching resin sealers are designed to etch through a layer left in the apical third, which lowers technique sensitivity (Berman et al, 2021) [SRC]. The effect on outcome is not shown.

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

### Principle
The smear layer lies between sealer and dentin, so it controls how far a sealer penetrates and how well it adheres.

### Effects of its presence
- Sealer penetration into dentinal tubules does not occur.
- It is weak and loosely attached; it can dissolve or be broken down by bacterial acids and enzymes, leaving a void around the filling and a leakage path.
- It may prevent gutta-percha penetration in thermoplastic techniques.
- It harbours bacteria that can persist and use it for growth.

### Effects of removal
- Sealer penetration depth increased by 10-80 μm in studies cited.
- Better adhesion and adaptation of gutta-percha and sealer; AH-26 studies showed higher bond strength and lower microleakage.
- A combination of removal, AH-26 and vertical compaction reduced leakage cumulatively in one study.
- Possible reinfection route if the coronal seal fails (one study found pre-obturation bacteria not viable afterwards).

### Resin sealers
Self-etching resin sealers are designed to etch through smear layers retained in the apical third, lowering technique sensitivity; conventional resin sealers need a clean wall.

### Evidence
Meta-analysis of 26 laboratory studies: 53.8% no difference, 41.5% for removal, 4.7% for retention. No clinical outcome trial. Majority opinion favours removal.

### Protocol
17% EDTA about 1 minute, NaOCl, activation, dry with paper points, obturate.

### Post space
Drilling creates a second smear layer with plasticised sealer and gutta-percha that lowers fibre-post bonding.


### Reasons for removal (Siqueira and Rocas)
Enhances diffusion of medicament into tubules; promotes adaptation and sealing of fillings; removes bacterial substrate and residual bacteria in the layer. Outcome evidence is scarce: one study found removal positively influenced retreatment outcome.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
10-80 μm deeper penetration; self-etching sealers; evidence laboratory only.
