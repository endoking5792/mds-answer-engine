### Definition
Film of cutting debris smeared on enamel, dentin or root canal wall by instruments. **AAE (endodontic):** surface film of debris retained on dentin after instrumentation, consisting of dentin particles, pulp remnants, bacterial components and irrigants.

### Structure
About 1-2.5 μm thick; plugs in tubules up to about 40 μm. Coronal film: hydroxyapatite and denatured collagen; canal film also has bacteria and pulp tissue.

### Significance
- **Restorative:** lowers permeability (about 90% in vitro) but weak (5-10 MPa); removed or modified before bonding.
- **Endodontic:** may harbour bacteria, block irrigants and sealer; most investigators favour removal; evidence is laboratory-based.

### Removal
- Restorative: phosphoric acid etch, self-etching primers.
- Endodontic: 17% EDTA about 1 minute alternating with NaOCl; MTAD; QMix; ultrasonic or laser activation.


**Note:** the layer re-forms after any further instrumentation, so a final EDTA then NaOCl rinse is repeated before filling.
