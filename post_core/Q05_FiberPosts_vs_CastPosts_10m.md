## STUDY MODE

### Why fiber posts were developed — the modulus-of-elasticity rationale
A rigid metal post and core provides high strength but carries real, significant drawbacks: it requires colour masking, can discolour the crown/gingiva over time, and shows through an all-ceramic restoration, blocking light transmission and compromising esthetics (Sikri, 2017).

**The unifying mechanical rationale across all fiber-post types**: their modulus of elasticity (rigidity) is similar to DENTIN's own modulus, meaning functional stresses are dissipated through the root rather than concentrated at a single rigid interface the way a much-stiffer metal post concentrates stress — directly reducing root-fracture risk, the real mechanical reasoning behind preferring a fiber post specifically in a root with thin remaining dentin (Sikri, 2017). This directly corroborates the rigid-vs-fiber stress-concentration mechanism established in Q3: rigid posts concentrate stress at the APEX (catastrophic, untreatable fracture); fiber posts shift it to the CERVICAL region — but this protection is **conditional on an adequate ferrule being present** (Cohen et al., 2021).

### Fiber-post families — real, distinct trade-offs
- **Carbon fiber posts** (e.g. Mirafit Carbon, Endopost): strength, stiffness, lightness, corrosion/fatigue resistance, dentin-like modulus — but **black and, in the original form, RADIOLUCENT**, genuinely difficult to evaluate radiographically and unesthetic under a ceramic crown. Radiopaque carbon posts now exist (barium sulphate added; only Composipost and Snowpost are uniformly radiopaque), and carbon posts are no longer recommended where esthetics matter (Baba, 2013; Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Quartz fiber posts** (e.g. DT Light Post) — the real, modified, improved version: **radiopaque** (postoperative radiographic evaluation possible), translucent enough to **transmit curing light** to the apical end (usable with light- or dual-cure cements) — this is the direct basis of "light-transmitting posts." Real quantified dimensions: 20mm length, three apical diameters (0.9/1.0/1.2mm), three tapers (0.06/0.08/0.10). A genuine practical advantage: complete, non-traumatic removal is possible with a dedicated retrieval kit — a real advantage over a cemented cast post if retreatment is later needed.
- **Glass fiber posts** (e.g. Tenax, Luscent Anchor, Fiberkov): a further-improved version with an even **lower** modulus of elasticity than carbon or quartz fibres — genuinely closer still to dentin's own modulus — while remaining esthetically white (Sikri, 2017). **Note — the modulus claim is source-dependent.** Sikri and Torabinejad describe glass or quartz posts as closer to dentine than carbon (Torabinejad et al, 2021); Harty says carbon, glass and quartz have "substantially similar" mechanical properties and that the modulus is 3-4 times that of dentine (Chong, 2017); and Galhano found flexural strength was similar across carbon, quartz and glass because the epoxy matrix is the same (Baba, 2013). So "lowest modulus" is one author's claim, not a consensus.

### Light-transmitting posts — mechanism and clinical significance
**DEFINITION — Light-transmitting post**: a translucent fiber post (typically quartz- or glass-fiber-based) engineered to conduct curing light along its length to the apical third of the post space, allowing a light- or dual-cure luting cement to polymerise more completely at depth than it otherwise would with an opaque post — directly addressing the known problem of incomplete cement cure in the apical portion of a deep post space with conventional (opaque) posts (Sikri, 2017).

### The "generation" classification — radiopacity and esthetics
A useful clinical classification groups fiber posts by "generation," based on radiopacity and esthetics, not chronology alone:
- **Generation I** (e.g. Composi Post, C Post): neither radiopaque nor esthetic.
- **Generation II** (e.g. Esthetic Post, Light Post, Fiber White, Fiberkov, Dentatus Luscent Anchor): esthetic but **NOT** radiopaque.
- **Generation III** (e.g. DT Light Post, DT Light Post Illusion, Snow Light, Rely-X Post, FRC Postec Plus): achieves **BOTH** esthetics and radiopacity — the real, practical improvement each generation solves is worth naming explicitly (Sikri, 2017).

### Clinical bonding technique (representative, generalisable across fiber systems)
Canal etched with 37% phosphoric acid for 10-15 seconds, washed/dried with paper points; primer applied to canal and post, light cured; dual-cure cement placed in canal, post seated under pressure; exposed post surface coated with bonding agent + composite to build initial core shape, cured 40 seconds, then trimmed/contoured (Sikri, 2017).

### Audit addendum (2026-10-14) — evidence, failure modes and retrieval
**What the clinical evidence actually shows.** There is no long-term head-to-head randomised trial of fibre versus cast posts in the textbooks audited. The Cochrane review found a single trial and weak evidence of longer survival for fibre posts (Bolla et al., 2007, cited in Chong, 2017); two premolar RCTs found higher survival with fibre posts (Ferrari et al., 2007; Cagidiaco et al., 2008, both in Chong, 2017); and a literature review found fibre posts outperform metal posts "in general" but "cannot be considered conclusive" (Banerjee and Watson, 2015). Against that, a longer-term pattern of loosening, post fracture and root fracture has been reported (Torabinejad et al, 2021), and Gulabivala and Ng state that the best clinical evidence is for custom gold posts, with evidence for carbon or glass fibre lacking (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014). Ingle's pooled failure data: cast posts 91% survival against 81% for threaded posts with resin core (Creugers); reported clinical failure ranges for carbon fibre 0% (2.7 years) to 35% (6.7 years), glass fibre 0% to 11.4% (up to 2 years), and zirconia 0% to 9% (up to 4.8 years); root fractures with fibre posts were 2/173, 1/106, 15/985 and 14/99 in different series, and post fractures 1/173 and 7/105 (Baba, 2013).

**Failure mode — contested.** Harty reports the commonest fibre-post failure as debonding/decementation (with caries) rather than root fracture, and Torabinejad lists loosening, post fracture and root fracture (Chong, 2017; Torabinejad et al, 2021). But a fibre post with little or no ferrule flexes, giving core micromovement, marginal leakage and undetected secondary caries (Baba, 2013); Cohen's regards fibre posts as "generally contraindicated" in structurally compromised teeth lacking cervical stiffness, preferring a rigid post or cast gold where the ferrule is limited (Berman et al, 2021); and Baba found that the premise that a failed fibre post leaves a restorable root is "not always true" (Baba, 2013). Cast post-and-cores had higher fracture resistance than fibre or prefabricated metal posts in one review (Jain, 2016), and ferruled cast posts have performed above 90% at 5 years (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Retrievability — also contested.** Fibre posts are generally easier to remove than cemented metal posts: bespoke drills get between the fibres, an ultrasonic tip without water is effective, and a bonded fibre post can be drilled out centrally without peripheral dentine loss (Banerjee and Watson, 2015; Berman et al, 2021). Carbon is easier than glass; glass fibre is "bonded and difficult to remove" and wears drills (Sikri, 2019); ceramic and zirconia posts are "virtually impossible" to retrieve (Ingle et al., 2019); an adhesively cemented metal post is nearly impossible to remove without damaging root dentine (Banerjee and Watson, 2015).

**Bonding to fibre posts — specifics.** Bonding to root dentine is "one of the most challenging situations"; self-etch adhesives are not indicated because their acidity impedes chemical activation of some dual-cure cements (Sakaguchi et al, 2019); self-adhesive resin cements gave promising laboratory results; the post surface is silanised and/or airborne-particle abraded (Sakaguchi et al, 2019; Baba, 2013). Resin-to-resin bonding is difficult because prefabricated posts lack an oxygen-inhibited layer (van Noort and Barbour, 2013). A reported bond strength of 14 MPa for silica-treated posts with 4-META cement is a rare quantified figure (Craig, 2002). Light intensity falls to under 40% through a root canal, which is why light-transmitting posts (iLumi transmits to 12 mm) were developed (Sikri, 2019). Fibre posts also lose flexural strength under wet cyclic loading and thermocycling (about 40% loss in a glass-fibre example) (Baba, 2013; Chong, 2017).

**Other post types worth a line.** Polyethylene-fibre ribbon (Ribbond) is placed without canal enlargement, so there is no perforation risk (Sikri, 2017). Zirconia posts have a modulus equal to metal, are brittle, hard to remove and cannot be etched, so bonding needs tribochemical silica coating (Baba, 2013). Fiberfill combines a fibre post with a gutta-percha-tipped obturator in one step (Sikri, 2019).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS (compressed, one claim — 10m, directly answering "advantages/disadvantages of cast post v/s fiber post")

**Claim — fiber posts and cast metal posts represent two genuinely different, not simply "better vs. worse," biomechanical philosophies.**

| Factor | Cast metal post | Fiber post |
|---|---|---|
| Retention/fit | Excellent for flared/irregular canals (custom-conforms) | Standardised sizes; less ideal for severely flared canals |
| Modulus of elasticity | High (rigid) — stress concentrates at apex | Low, dentin-like — stress concentrates cervically (ferrule-dependent) |
| Fracture pattern if failure occurs | Catastrophic, often untreatable (apical root fracture) in rigid posts | Often more restorable (debonding is the commonest failure); but loosening, post fracture and root fracture all occur and failure can occur within 1-3 years |
| Esthetics | Requires masking; shows through all-ceramic | Tooth-coloured/translucent options (Gen II/III) — ideal under ceramic |
| Retreatability | More difficult (cemented, rigid) | Quartz-fiber systems: non-traumatic removal possible with retrieval kit |
| Laboratory step | Required (custom cast) | Not required (chairside, prefabricated) |
| Cost/chair time | Higher (lab fabrication, 2+ appointments) | Lower (single-visit, direct bonding) |
| Corrosion/discoloration risk | Real (stainless steel/brass); titanium corrosion-free but radiolucent-confusable | None (non-metallic) |

EVIDENCE: the modulus-matching rationale and the stress-concentration-location mechanism are independently corroborated across Sikri's and Cohen's (Module 1/Module 2 study material). INTERPRETATION: fiber posts are widely preferred for esthetic zones and, by several authors, for thin-dentin cases because of a more favourable (cervical, treatable) failure mode — **a claim that the audit shows is contested, see the addendum** — but cast metal posts remain genuinely indicated for severely flared/irregular canals where a standardised prefabricated fiber post cannot achieve adequate fit. LIMITATION: the comparative fracture-pattern claim is a mechanistic inference from stress-distribution physics rather than a cited head-to-head clinical trial comparing actual fracture INCIDENCE between the two post types in the material retrieved this session — [Certain] on the mechanism; [Likely], not [Certain], on the magnitude of real-world outcome difference.

## EXAM MODE

**(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)**

### Why fiber posts were developed
Metal post drawbacks: colour masking needed, discoloration risk, blocks light transmission/shows through all-ceramic crowns. Fiber-post rationale: modulus of elasticity ≈ dentin's own → stress dissipated through root, not concentrated at one point.

### Fiber-post families

| Type | Radiopacity | Esthetics | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon fiber | Radiolucent (radiopaque versions exist) | Black, unesthetic | Dentin-like modulus, corrosion/fatigue resistant |
| Quartz fiber | Radiopaque | Esthetic | Light-transmitting (curing light to apex) |
| Glass fiber | Varies | Esthetic (white) | Lowest modulus, closest to dentin |

### Generation classification
Gen I: neither radiopaque nor esthetic. Gen II: esthetic, not radiopaque. Gen III: BOTH (modern standard, e.g. DT Light Post).

### Light-transmitting posts
Translucent (quartz/glass) posts conduct curing light to the apical third — ensures more complete polymerisation of light/dual-cure cement at depth, solving the classic incomplete-apical-cure problem of opaque posts.

### Cast post vs fiber post — advantages/disadvantages

| | Cast metal | Fiber |
|---|---|---|
| Fit in flared canals | Excellent (custom) | Limited (standardised) |
| Stress/failure pattern | Rigid post: apical concentration, root fracture | Cervical, ferrule-dependent; debonding commonest — contested (failures within 1-3 yr reported) |
| Esthetics | Needs masking | Tooth-coloured/translucent |
| Retreatment | Difficult (adhesively cemented ≈ cannot be removed without root damage) | Easier in general (carbon > glass; zirconia ≈ irretrievable) |
| Chair time/cost | Higher (lab + 2 visits) | Lower (chairside, 1 visit) |

### Bonding technique (fiber post)
37% phosphoric acid (10-15s) → primer + light cure → dual-cure cement → pressure seating → bonded composite core build-up.

### Conclusion
Fiber posts and cast metal posts differ fundamentally in modulus-driven stress-distribution behaviour and resulting failure mode, not merely in material cost or esthetics — fiber posts are now the evidence-preferred default for esthetic-zone and thin-dentin cases specifically because their cervical (ferrule-dependent) failure mode is more clinically manageable than the catastrophic apical fracture pattern of rigid metal posts, while cast posts remain indicated for severely flared or irregular canals.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Fiber post rationale: modulus ≈ dentin → stress distributed, not concentrated.
- Carbon fiber: black, unesthetic, originally radiolucent (radiopaque versions exist; no longer recommended for esthetics). Quartz fiber: radiopaque + light-transmitting. Glass fiber: lowest modulus, white/esthetic.
- Generation III (e.g. DT Light Post) = BOTH radiopaque AND esthetic — the modern standard.
- Light-transmitting = translucent post conducts curing light to apical cement for complete polymerisation.
- Evidence: no long-term head-to-head RCT; Cochrane found one trial; fibre posts "outperform in general but not conclusive"; failure modes and retrievability are contested — cite both sides.
- Rigid metal post → apical stress concentration → catastrophic fracture. Fiber post → cervical concentration → more treatable, BUT ferrule-dependent.
- Quartz-fiber posts: non-traumatic removal possible (dedicated retrieval kit) — genuine retreatability advantage over cast posts.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Treating all fiber posts as a single uniform category rather than distinguishing carbon/quartz/glass families and their generation-based radiopacity/esthetics trade-offs.
2. Describing fiber-post fracture protection as unconditional, omitting the ferrule-dependency.
3. Confusing "light-transmitting" with "radiopaque" — they are related but distinct properties (quartz fiber has both; not every radiopaque post is designed for light transmission).
4. Giving a one-sided (fiber-always-better) answer to the cast-vs-fiber comparison rather than noting cast posts' genuine fit advantage in flared canals.
