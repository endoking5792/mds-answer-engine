## 1. Introduction and digital workflow

### Explanation

3D printing (additive manufacturing) builds an object by sequential application of thin layers of material (Watanabe et al, 2022) [SRC], and can produce more complex geometries than milling while saving material (Watanabe et al, 2022) [SRC]. In endodontics it turns a CBCT volume into a physical object. For a guide, CBCT images are superimposed on an intraoral scan, a virtual sleeve is placed, and a 3D printed guide with a directed sleeve is produced (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021) [SRC]; implant planning software is most often used (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition:** 3D printing (additive manufacturing) builds an object layer by layer from a digital file, allowing complex, patient-specific shapes (Watanabe et al, 2022).

**Workflow:** CBCT + intraoral scan → superimposition in planning software → virtual design (guide, replica, model) → STL file → printing → post-curing → clinical use (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021).

## 2. Printing technologies

### Explanation

Watanabe et al describe the methods used in dentistry:
- **Vat photopolymerisation:** stereolithography (SLA), where a laser cures light-activated resin in cross-sectional layers (Watanabe et al, 2022) [SRC], and digital light processing (DLP), which cures each layer with a projected image.
- **Material jetting**, **material extrusion** (fused deposition modelling) and **DLP** are listed as other polymer methods (Watanabe et al, 2022) [SRC].
- **Powder bed fusion** for metals: selective laser sintering, selective laser melting and electron beam melting (Watanabe et al, 2022) [SRC]; printed metal tends to be porous and rough (Watanabe et al, 2022) [SRC].

Surgical guides are commonly printed in light-cured resin with metal sleeves added (Watanabe et al, 2022) [SRC], which is the same construction used for endodontic guides. In chairside resin printing each layer is cured at 50-100 µm (Renne and DeFee, 2023) [SRC], and post-curing in glycerin or nitrogen removes oxygen so polymerisation is complete (Renne and DeFee, 2023) [SRC]. The DLP description in the first bullet is [RECALL]; the article names DLP but does not describe it.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Technologies** (Watanabe et al, 2022)
1. Stereolithography (SLA): laser cures light-activated resin layer by layer; one of the commonest polymer printing methods.
2. Digital light processing (DLP): projector cures a whole layer at once.
3. Material jetting.
4. Material extrusion (fused deposition modelling).
5. Powder bed fusion (SLS, SLM, EBM): metals; porous, rough surfaces.

Resin layers are 50-100 µm and need post-curing (Renne and DeFee, 2023).

## 3. Endodontic applications

### Explanation

**a. Guided access to calcified canals (static guided endodontics).** Used for calcified canals and anatomical variations (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021) [SRC]. In one study of calcified teeth canals were found in 91.7% with guided access versus 41.7% freehand (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021) [SRC]. Cohen's adds that high-level evidence is lacking and the literature is in vitro studies and case reports (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021) [SRC]. A 2026 scoping review found 13 clinical studies (4 case series, 3 observational or validation studies, 1 retrospective, 3 prospective comparative studies and 2 RCTs), with inconsistent outcome definitions and limited follow-up (Alhawiti and Ali, 2026) [LIT].

**b. Guided endodontic microsurgery.** Templates make osteotomy and root-end resection more precise. Guides deviated 1.743 mm from the CBCT target against 2.638 mm for freehand surgery (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021) [SRC]. Targeted endodontic microsurgery (TEMS) with 3D-printed guides has been proposed to optimise resection volume and bevel angle (Hülsmann, Schäfer and Bürklein, 2022) [SRC].

**c. Autotransplantation.** A printed replica of the donor tooth is used to prepare the recipient socket before extraction. A systematic review reported shorter extraoral time and success of 80.0-91.1% with low-quality evidence (Verweij et al, 2017) [LIT]; a 2026 review found extra-alveolar time under one minute in eleven reports and better operative efficiency, but clinical superiority over the conventional technique unproven (Almutairi et al, 2026) [LIT].

**d. Education and research.** CT-based printed replicas have improved artificial teeth for teaching (Versiani, Cleghorn and Christie, 2021) [SRC]; they can be printed oversized, show rare anatomy, and supply many identical teeth for destructive analysis (Versiani, Cleghorn and Christie, 2021) [SRC], which standardises laboratory studies [Likely].

**e. Regenerative endodontics.** Scaffolds can be 3D printed in the shape of the canal for pulp regeneration (Cavalcanti and Chiego, 2024) [SRC]. A 2026 scoping review found 3D printing and bioprinting among scaffold fabrication methods, with evidence still mainly preclinical (Forte et al, 2026) [LIT].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Applications**
1. **Guided access (static guides):** for pulp canal obliteration and calcified or variant canals; sleeve directs the bur along the planned path. Canal detection 91.7% guided versus 41.7% freehand in one study (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021).
2. **Guided endodontic surgery:** osteotomy and root-end resection guides; deviation 1.743 mm versus 2.638 mm freehand (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021). Targeted endodontic microsurgery controls resection bevel (Hülsmann, Schäfer and Bürklein, 2022).
3. **Autotransplantation:** a printed donor-tooth replica is used to prepare and try in the recipient socket before extraction, so extraoral time falls (Verweij et al, 2017) and most donor teeth fit at the first attempt (Almutairi et al, 2026); evidence is low quality.
4. **Education and research:** replica teeth with real canal anatomy, enlarged models, standardised teeth for studies (Versiani, Cleghorn and Christie, 2021).
5. **Regenerative endodontics:** canal-shaped scaffolds for pulp regeneration (Cavalcanti and Chiego, 2024); still preclinical.
6. Patient education models and surgical planning.

## 4. Advantages and limitations

### Explanation

Guides improve precision and conserve tooth structure, but they need CBCT (radiation), planning time and cost, enough mouth opening and a straight path, and printing errors can accumulate through scanning, merging and printing [Likely]. Evidence is mostly in vitro and case reports (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Advantages:** precision, conservation of tooth structure, predictable surgery, shorter extraoral time in transplantation, reproducible training models.

**Limitations:** CBCT radiation, cost and planning time, mouth opening, curved canals beyond guide reach, cumulative scan/print errors; evidence mostly case reports and in vitro (Nair, Levin and Nair, 2021).

**Conclusion:** 3D printing makes endodontics more precise and conservative, but long-term clinical trials are needed.

## High-yield points
- Workflow: CBCT + intraoral scan → planning → printed guide with sleeve.
- Guided access: 91.7% versus 41.7% canal detection.
- Guided surgery deviation 1.743 mm versus 2.638 mm freehand.
- Autotransplant replicas shorten extraoral time.
- SLA/DLP for resin guides; powder bed fusion for metals.
- Evidence: case reports, in vitro, few RCTs.

## Common exam mistakes
- Writing only restorative uses (crowns, models) and missing guides and replicas.
- Calling guided access proven by RCTs; clinical evidence is heterogeneous, with only two RCTs.
- Confusing static guides with dynamic navigation (no printed guide).
- Listing printer brands instead of technology types.
