## 1. Rationale

### Explanation

Files leave large parts of the canal untouched (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]; Stabholz et al cite over 35% of canal surface unchanged after NiTi preparation (Stabholz et al, 2016) [SRC]. Laser light reaches fins, deltas and lateral canals that irrigants miss (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. The second argument is depth: NaOCl penetrates about 130 μm into tubules (Pandey, 2018) [SRC] while bacteria may lie 1100 μm from the lumen (Pandey, 2018) [SRC]. Lasers enter biomechanical preparation in three ways: shaping (ablation), smear layer removal, and disinfection (photothermal, photodynamic, and laser-activated irrigation) [Certain].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Rationale:** files leave over 35% of canal walls uninstrumented and NaOCl reaches only about 130 μm into tubules, while bacteria lie deeper (Stabholz et al, 2016; Pandey, 2018). Laser energy and activated fluids reach fins, isthmuses and tubules (George and Walsh, 2017).

## 2. Laser shaping of the canal (biomechanical preparation)

### Explanation

**What was tried.** Levy widened apical canals from ISO #20 to #35 with Nd:YAG and water spray (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. With Ho:YAG, Cohen et al widened ISO #25 canals to #40 (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. Sikri's technique: hand-instrument the apex to a #15 K-file, then insert a fibre at 150 mJ to working length and prepare circumferentially, third by third (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]; canals are clean but carbonisation is seen (Sikri, 2019) [SRC].

**Curvature limits, which differ by study:** Er,Cr:YSGG up to 10° curvature succeeded in one study (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]; above 5° led to zipping, ledges or perforation in another (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. Ali et al found the mechanical quality (smoothness, taper) worse with the laser (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC], and the Current Therapy chapter says Levy's laser caused more mishaps such as ledges (Alshahrani et al, 2016) [SRC].

**Why the fibre fails as a file.** A fibre cannot be precurved (Pandey, 2018) [SRC], needs a canal at least ISO #20 before insertion (Pandey, 2018) [SRC], and fires forward rather than onto the walls (Stabholz et al, 2016) [SRC]. George and Walsh conclude it is difficult to meet all mechanical objectives with conventional fibres (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC], and Pandey that traditional instrumentation is still required with erbium lasers (Pandey, 2018) [SRC]. Two genuine advantages remain: lasers do not transport the canal when used without ablating the walls (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC] and create no smear layer when cutting dentin (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Laser shaping:** Nd:YAG (Levy: #20 to #35), Ho:YAG, Er:YAG and Er,Cr:YSGG fibres have been used to widen canals, third by third after a #15 glide path (George and Walsh, 2017; Sikri, 2019).
**Problems:** straight, stiff fibres cannot be precurved, fire forward not laterally, and cause ledging, zipping, perforation and carbonisation; curvatures above 5-10° are unsafe; taper and smoothness are worse than with files (George and Walsh, 2017; Pandey, 2018). **Hence files remain essential; lasers are adjuncts after conventional preparation.**

## 3. Smear layer removal

### Explanation

The deep smear layer is packed into tubules up to 40 μm (Stabholz et al, 2016) [SRC]. Nd:YAG disinfects better but removes smear less well than erbium lasers (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]; in Takeda's study all lasers beat EDTA and Er:YAG was the most effective (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. Water-based irrigants must be used, never dry (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC], and laser activation enhances EDTA (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC].

**Tip design.** Stabholz's RCLase side-firing spiral tip emits laterally through spiral slits (Stabholz et al, 2016) [SRC], but such tips are too large and rigid for narrow curved canals (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. A radial-emitting Er,Cr:YSGG tip was introduced in 2007 (Pandey, 2018) [SRC]; Mortman gives its diameter as equivalent to a #20 file (Mortman, 2011) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Smear layer removal:** erbium lasers (Er:YAG best) with EDTA or water remove smear layer better than EDTA alone; Nd:YAG removes less and melts dentin (George and Walsh, 2017). Side-firing (RCLase spiral) and radial tips (Er,Cr:YSGG, #20 size) direct energy onto the walls (Stabholz et al, 2016; Mortman, 2011).

## 4. Photothermal disinfection

### Explanation

**Mechanism.** Erbium energy ruptures bacteria by expanding their water (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]; near-infrared light is absorbed by porphyrins, melanin and pigments (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. Near-infrared lasers pass through water and hydroxyapatite and act deeper (Pandey, 2018) [SRC]; erbium acts superficially (Pandey, 2018) [SRC].

**Depth, as each source states it:** near-infrared beyond 1 mm (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]; diode and Nd:YAG up to 500-1000 μm (Pandey, 2018) [SRC]; mid-infrared about 0.5 mm (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. George and Walsh prefer Nd:YAG, KTP and near-infrared diode for this purpose (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC].

**Laboratory kill figures (extracted teeth and dentin slices, not clinical):**
- Nd:YAG: three log steps at 1 mm (Pandey, 2018) [SRC];
- 810 nm diode, 0.6 W: 74% in a 500 μm slice (Pandey, 2018) [SRC];
- 980 nm diode: 66% at 2.3 W and 86% at 2.8 W (Pandey, 2018) [SRC];
- erbium (Moritz): 99.64% at 1.5 W, but no effect deep in lateral canals (Pandey, 2018) [SRC].
Sikri calls Nd:YAG significantly more bactericidal than other lasers (Sikri, 2019) [SRC]. Stabholz et al note kill depends on dose and energy (Stabholz et al, 2016) [SRC]. Mortman cites one in vitro study in which neither laser nor rotary preparation eliminated infection (Mortman, 2011) [SRC].

**Technique (Pandey).** Prepare apically to ISO 30/40 (Pandey, 2018) [SRC]; place a 200-300 μm fibre 1 mm from the apex and withdraw it coronally in a vertical or helical movement (Pandey, 2018) [SRC]. George and Walsh add tilting and rotating (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC] and constant motion (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. Pandey's Twinlight protocol uses Nd:YAG for deep thermal disinfection (Pandey, 2018) [SRC] at 1.5 W and 15 Hz through a 200 μm fibre (Pandey, 2018) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Photothermal disinfection** (after conventional preparation to ISO 30/40):
- Near-infrared (Nd:YAG 1064 nm, diode 810-980 nm): poorly absorbed by water, absorbed by bacterial pigments; reaches 500-1000 μm (Pandey, 2018) or beyond 1 mm (George and Walsh, 2017).
- Erbium: absorbed by water, kills by vaporisation; superficial (about 0.5 mm).
- In vitro: Nd:YAG 3-log reduction at 1 mm; 810 nm diode 74% in 500 μm dentin (Pandey, 2018).
- Technique: 200-300 μm fibre 1 mm short of apex, withdrawn helically, kept moving, repeated passes (Pandey, 2018; George and Walsh, 2017).
- **Photodynamic:** dye + red light (633-670 nm) → reactive oxygen species (George and Walsh, 2017).

## 5. Laser-activated irrigation (LAI) and PIPS

### Explanation

Er:YAG and Er,Cr:YSGG are ideally suited to activate fluids (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]; bubbles expand and implode, creating rapid fluid motion (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC], and the tip need not enter the apical third (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. Erbium cavitation takes microseconds, diode cavitation seconds (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]; diode agitation is weaker (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC] and can be boosted by 3% hydrogen peroxide (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. Banday et al describe diode lasers forming cavitation bubbles in irrigants (Banday et al, 2026) [SRC].

PIPS uses under 20 mJ and 50 μs Er:YAG pulses (Pandey, 2018) [SRC]; SWEEPS uses paired pulses (George and Walsh, 2023) [SRC]. LAI may reduce bacteria more than ultrasonic file agitation (George and Walsh, 2023) [SRC] and made 0.5% NaOCl as effective as 2.5% (George and Walsh, 2023) [SRC]. Extrusion with erbium fibres was no greater than with a 25-gauge needle (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. The PIPS evidence base is still in vitro proof of principle (Stabholz et al, 2016) [SRC]; a 2026 meta-analysis of RCTs found activated irrigation, including LAI, improved periapical healing at moderate certainty (Fernández-Moreno et al, 2026) [LIT].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Laser-activated irrigation:** pulsed erbium energy absorbed by NaOCl/EDTA creates vapour bubbles that expand and implode (cavitation) in microseconds, causing streaming and shock waves that remove smear layer and biofilm; tip need not reach the apical third (George and Walsh, 2017). PIPS: Er:YAG under 20 mJ, 50 μs pulses, tip in pulp chamber (Pandey, 2018); SWEEPS: paired pulses (George and Walsh, 2023). Diode lasers cavitate more slowly and weakly.

## 6. Limitations and safety

### Explanation

Forward emission (Stabholz et al, 2016) [SRC] prevents uniform 360° irradiation (Stabholz et al, 2016) [SRC] and risks energy beyond the apex near the mental foramen or mandibular nerve (Stabholz et al, 2016) [SRC]. Bacteria in tubules can survive below the surface (Goodis and Pashley, 2012) [SRC]. Thermal limits: 5.5-7 °C root-surface rise in laboratory studies (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC], 7 °C commonly accepted (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]; Nd:YAG can raise root-surface temperature 17-27 °C (Mortman, 2011) [SRC]. Dry lasing melts and overheats walls (Banday et al, 2026) [SRC]: near-infrared lasers close tubules by melting (Pandey, 2018) [SRC] and mid-infrared lasers can ledge, transport and perforate (Pandey, 2018) [SRC]. Refreshing irrigant between cycles controls temperature (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. Gopikrishna's conclusion is the right one: lasers are an adjunct to shaping and cleaning (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Mortman found no case-controlled study showing higher success (Mortman, 2011) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Limitations:** forward-firing fibres miss canal walls (no 360° coverage); energy past the apex; thermal damage (Nd:YAG can raise root surface 17-27 °C; limit about 7 °C); melting, carbonisation, ledging and perforation if used dry; cost; few clinical trials (Stabholz et al, 2016; Mortman, 2011).
**Conclusion:** lasers supplement, not replace, chemomechanical preparation (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## High-yield points
- Laser shaping causes ledges, zips and perforation; files stay essential.
- Near-infrared = deep photothermal kill; erbium = smear removal and irrigant activation.
- NaOCl penetrates about 130 μm; bacteria lie deeper.
- Fibre 1 mm short of WL, withdrawn helically, kept moving, after preparation to ISO 30/40.
- Erbium cavitation in microseconds versus diode in seconds.
- Root-surface rise above about 7 °C risks PDL damage.

## Common exam mistakes
- Writing that lasers shape canals as well as NiTi files.
- Presenting in vitro percentage kills as clinical success.
- Forgetting water/irrigant: lasing a dry canal is a mistake, not a technique.
- Swapping roles: Nd:YAG is the deep disinfector; erbium is the smear remover and activator.
