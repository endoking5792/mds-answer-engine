## 1. Introduction, properties and chemistry

### Explanation

Ozone (O3) is a triatomic molecule of three oxygen atoms with a molecular weight of 47.98 g/mol (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. Compared with oxygen it is 1.6 times denser and 10 times more soluble in water (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC], which is why it can be carried in water or oil. It is the third most potent oxidant after fluorine and persulfate (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC].

Its instability is the clinical problem: ozone cannot be stored and must be used at once because its half-life is about 40 minutes at 20 °C (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. In decomposing it passes through hydrogen trioxide to the even more powerful hydroxyl radical (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]; Kishen describes the same instability as liberation of singlet oxygen (Kishen, 2017) [SRC]. Both books describe a short-lived reactive oxygen species doing the killing; they name different species, and the two accounts are recorded side by side, not ranked.

Cohen's describes ozone in gaseous or aqueous form as active against bacteria, fungi, protozoa and viruses (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC].

**History.** Schönbein discovered ozone in 1839 (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]; Werner von Siemens designed an ozone generator in 1857 (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]; Fisch first used ozonated water in dentistry in 1930 (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Ozone (O3) is a triatomic, unstable allotrope of oxygen (MW 47.98 g/mol) and one of the strongest oxidants known, third after fluorine and persulfate (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015).

**Properties**
- 1.6 times denser and 10 times more soluble in water than oxygen.
- Cannot be stored: half-life about 40 min at 20 °C, so it is generated at the chair and used at once.
- Breaks down to oxygen, releasing reactive oxygen species: hydroxyl radical (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015); singlet oxygen (Kishen, 2017).
- Broad spectrum: bacteria, fungi, protozoa, viruses (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021).

**History:** discovered by Schönbein (1839); ozone generator designed by von Siemens (1857); ozonated water first used in dentistry by Fisch (1930).

## 2. Generation and delivery systems

### Explanation

**Generation.** In nature ozone forms when ultraviolet light or lightning splits oxygen; a clinical generator simulates lightning by an electrical discharge (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. Borges et al distinguish two supply principles: ozone produced from ambient air (low-dosage principle) or from pure oxygen supplied from a cylinder (high-dosage principle) (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC].

**Sealed-cup delivery (HealOzone type).** The gas reaches the tooth through a handpiece with a disposable silicone cup tip (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. After application the gas is suctioned off and converted back into oxygen (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; Bonsor and Pearson specify neutralisation through a platinum filter (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013) [SRC]. Baumann and Beer show a second-generation HealOzone developed with particular attention to root canal disinfection (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC], with elongated caps; the silicone cap isolates the access cavity and creates the vacuum (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC].

**Open-tip delivery.** Newer devices deliver ozone down a fine tip directly into the canal for very short exposures and need no seal around the tooth (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013) [SRC].

**Dose.** The books state the device output in different units, and these are recorded side by side:
- 4 g/m3 gas used in endodontics (Kishen, 2017) [SRC]; the same figure for HealOzone in Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh.
- About 2100 ppm for KaVo HealOzone (Sikri, 2020) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Generation:** an ozone generator passes oxygen through an electrical discharge, imitating lightning (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015). Source gas is room air (low-dosage) or medical oxygen (high-dosage) (Borges et al, 2020).

**Delivery systems**
1. **Sealed-cup system (HealOzone, KaVo):** handpiece with a disposable silicone cup sealed on the tooth; ozone is circulated, then sucked back and neutralised to oxygen (platinum filter) (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013). Elongated caps are made for access cavities.
2. **Open-tip systems:** a fine cannula delivers ozone directly into the canal for seconds; no seal needed.
3. **Ozonated water or oil**, which hold ozone and release it.

Output: 4 g/m3 (Kishen, 2017); about 2100 ppm (Sikri, 2020).

## 3. Forms of ozone used in dentistry

### Explanation

Ozone is applied to oral tissues as ozonated water, ozonated olive oil and oxygen/ozone gas (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015; Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]. Ozonated water and olive oil can trap and then release oxygen/ozone, which Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh call an ideal delivery system (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. Kishen reports a study claiming that ozone dissolved in oil can serve as an intracanal medicament (Kishen, 2017) [SRC], but adds that its flow, chemical stability and interaction with root dentine and obturating materials must be studied before endodontic use (Kishen, 2017) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Forms**
| Form | Use |
|---|---|
| Oxygen/ozone gas | Caries (HealOzone), canal disinfection |
| Ozonated water | Irrigant, mouth rinse, surgical irrigation |
| Ozonated olive oil | Proposed intracanal medicament, topical oral lesions |

Water and oil trap ozone and release it slowly (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015).

## 4. Mechanism of antimicrobial action

### Explanation

Cohen's gives the clearest sequence (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]:
1. The oxidant potential of ozone destroys the cell walls and cytoplasmic membranes of bacteria and fungi.
2. Ozone attacks glycoproteins, glycolipids and amino acids and inhibits the enzymatic control system of the cell (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC].
3. Membrane permeability rises, function ceases, and ozone then enters the cell and kills it.

Caprioglio et al add that ozone induces lysis of biofilm acid proteins (Caprioglio et al, 2018) [SRC]. Because the action is a non-specific oxidation, it acts without developing drug resistance (Kishen, 2017) [SRC].

**Why biofilms resist.** Kishen gives two reasons that ozone works on planktonic cells but much less within biofilm:
- the polymeric matrix forms a physical-chemical barrier against deeper penetration of dissolved ozone (Kishen, 2017) [SRC];
- oxidation products block the water channels of the biofilm (Kishen, 2017) [SRC].

This barrier explanation is the key to understanding the endodontic results in section 6 [Likely].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Mechanism of action** (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021)
1. Oxidation of the cell wall and cytoplasmic membrane.
2. Attack on glycoproteins, glycolipids and amino acids; enzyme systems inhibited.
3. Increased permeability, entry of ozone, cell death.
4. Lysis of biofilm acid proteins (Caprioglio et al, 2018).
5. No development of drug resistance (Kishen, 2017).

**Limitation:** in biofilm the extracellular matrix blocks penetration and oxidation products plug water channels, so effect falls sharply (Kishen, 2017).

## 5. Applications in conservative dentistry

### Explanation

**Caries management.** The aim is to arrest caries by oxidising cariogenic bacteria and so delay or avoid a restoration (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. In vitro, HealOzone exposure for 10-20 s reduced *S. mutans* and *S. sobrinus* in primary root caries lesions to under 1% of controls (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. Holmes reported that after 18 months 100% of primary root caries lesions had improved when ozone was followed by a remineralising solution (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. Sikri states that exposure of root caries to ozone for 10-40 s is effective (Sikri, 2020) [SRC] and that ozone stimulated remineralisation of incipient caries over 6-8 weeks (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]. Caprioglio et al go further: the carious dentine is sterilised, so it need not be removed (Caprioglio et al, 2018) [SRC], and ozone is useful for fissure, root and cervical caries (Caprioglio et al, 2018) [SRC].

The manufacturer's protocol given by Borges et al: ozone directly over the lesion for 20-120 s (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], then a remineralising solution with fluoride, calcium, zinc and phosphate, fluoride toothpaste and mouthwash, repeated after 3 and 6 months (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Application times differ between books: 20-120 s per surface (Sikri, 2020) [SRC], 10-60 s with a hermetic seal (Caprioglio et al, 2018) [SRC].

**Limitations in caries.** Ozone acts only on contact (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], so proximal lesions, hidden caries or lesions the cup cannot seal cannot be treated (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Reinfection can occur rapidly if the surface is not sealed (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. Borges et al conclude it is only an additional option for caries control (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]; Sikri's table rates its efficiency as doubtful (Sikri, 2020) [SRC]; Fontana et al say the evidence is still questionable (Fontana et al, 2016) [SRC] and quote the Cochrane Collaboration that there is no evidence ozone can reverse or stop decay (Fontana et al, 2016) [SRC]. The Cochrane review (three trials, 432 lesions in 137 participants, high risk of bias) found no reliable evidence that ozone stops or reverses caries (Rickard et al, 2004) [LIT]; that review was later withdrawn (Rickard et al, 2019, withdrawal notice) [LIT], so it should be quoted as a historical review, not current guidance.

**Dentin hypersensitivity.** A gaseous ozone trial (Dähnhardt et al) found no significant reduction in pain over placebo, and a triple-blinded HealOzone RCT (Azarpazhooh et al) confirmed it (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. Detail is in the dentin hypersensitivity answer.

**Before sealants and bonding.** Borges et al report good in vitro evidence for ozone before acid etching and placement of sealants and restorations (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC], with limited and partly contradictory clinical data (Borges et al, 2020) [SRC]. In laboratory studies, ozone improved the marginal seal of fissure sealants (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC] and did not affect immediate enamel and dentine bond strength (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC], but other studies found reduced bond strength, for example of a self-adhesive resin cement and of a dentine-composite interface (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. All bonding data are in vitro.

#### ✎ Exam core

**A. Caries management (HealOzone)**
- Rationale: oxidise cariogenic bacteria, arrest the lesion, allow remineralisation, delay restoration.
- Protocol: sealed cup over the lesion for 20-120 s (Borges et al, 2020); 10-40 s for root caries (Sikri, 2020); then remineralising solution (F, Ca, PO4, Zn), fluoride paste and rinse; recall and repeat at 3 and 6 months.
- Indications: occlusal pits and fissures, root caries, incipient smooth-surface lesions, cervical lesions; residual dentine after excavation.
- Limits: acts on contact only, so proximal and hidden lesions cannot be treated; reinfection if unsealed (Borges et al, 2020).
- Root caries reduced *S. mutans* to <1% of control in vitro (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015).
- Remineralisation of incipient caries reported over 6-8 weeks (Sikri, 2020); carious dentine treated with ozone is said not to need removal (Caprioglio et al, 2018).

**B. Dentin hypersensitivity:** RCTs showed no benefit over placebo (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015).

**C. Pre-sealant/pre-bonding disinfection:** in vitro, several studies show no loss of bond strength and better sealant margins; some studies show reduced bond strength (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015).

**D. Others:** minimally invasive paediatric caries treatment, cavity disinfection.

## 6. Applications in endodontics

### Explanation

Gas from HealOzone is already used clinically for endodontic treatment (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. The books describe three roles.

**1. Canal disinfection with gas or ozonated water.** Kishen sums the results up as inconsistent (Kishen, 2017) [SRC] and attributes this to unknown optimum concentration and duration. The pattern across the laboratory studies:
- Four in vitro studies compared ozone with 2.5% sodium hypochlorite and the results were controversial (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. Nagayoshi et al found similar activity with lower cytotoxicity of ozonated water (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]; Hems et al found sodium hypochlorite superior to ozonated water against *E. faecalis* (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC], and gaseous ozone had no effect on *E. faecalis* biofilm (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC].
- Baumann and Beer report the same Hems study: no effect on biofilm bacteria even after 3 minutes, whereas 5% sodium hypochlorite was significantly more effective (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC].
- Kishen: an antibacterial effect on planktonic *E. faecalis* but little effect in biofilm (Kishen, 2017) [SRC]; efficacy depended on concentration, strain, planktonic or biofilm growth and duration (Kishen, 2017) [SRC].
- In infected extracted human root canals, ozonated water, 2.5% sodium hypochlorite, 2% chlorhexidine and 20 minutes of gaseous ozone all failed to inactivate *E. faecalis* (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC].
- Ozonated water reduced *Candida albicans* immediately but showed no residual activity (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC], and did not neutralise endotoxin (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC].

All of these are laboratory or ex vivo studies; none is a clinical outcome study.

**2. Ozonated sodium hypochlorite (HealOzone in the canal).** Baumann and Beer state that ozone accelerates sodium hypochlorite even at low concentration (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. The procedure needs a well-shaped access cavity and adequate canal access (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]: canals are partly filled with sodium hypochlorite, cannulas inserted, the cap sealed and the generator run for 120 seconds (Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. Their evidence is two illustrated case reports with 12-month healing, which is case-level evidence only [Certain].

**3. Irrigant and medicament.** The DCNA 2026 review concludes that ozonated water is not a better disinfectant than NaOCl and CHX (Guruswamy Pandian et al, 2026) [SRC] and suggests it as a final irrigant instead of EDTA because it removes the smear layer (Guruswamy Pandian et al, 2026) [SRC]; that claim rests on a single cited study. Ozonated oil has been proposed as an intracanal medicament (Kishen, 2017) [SRC].

**Bonding to root dentine.** In vitro, ozone improved the shear bond strength of two root canal sealers, while gaseous ozone reduced adhesion of a self-adhesive resin cement used for fibre posts (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC].

**A laser-related caution.** George and Walsh warn that oxidants such as ozone quench bacterial fluorescence used to guide laser treatment; in their 2017 chapter scores take up to 24 h to recover (George and Walsh, 2017) [SRC]. The 2023 edition keeps the quenching warning (George and Walsh, 2023) [SRC] but no longer gives the 24 h figure.

**Systematic reviews.** A systematic review (seven ex vivo studies and one RCT) found ozone gave significantly less microbial reduction than sodium hypochlorite and did not improve it as an adjunct; it concluded ozone should neither replace nor complement sodium hypochlorite (Silva et al, 2020) [LIT]. A second review of seven studies on extracted teeth found ozonated water not better than sodium hypochlorite or chlorhexidine, though "almost comparable" (Shetty et al, 2022) [LIT].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Applications in endodontics**
1. **Root canal disinfection (gas):** HealOzone with elongated cannulas and silicone cap. Kills planktonic *E. faecalis*, but little effect on biofilm; 5% NaOCl significantly better (Hems et al, in Baumann and Beer, 2010). Efficacy depends on concentration, time, strain and growth mode (Kishen, 2017).
2. **Ozonated water as irrigant:** comparable to but not better than NaOCl or CHX (Guruswamy Pandian et al, 2026); proposed as final irrigant for smear layer removal; no residual antifungal action and does not neutralise endotoxin (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015).
3. **Ozonated NaOCl:** ozone activates NaOCl, reducing contact time; cannula in each canal and generator run for 120 s; needs a well-shaped access (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
4. **Intracanal medicament:** ozonated olive oil proposed (Kishen, 2017).
5. **Other:** retreatment cases (case reports only); *Candida* in canals (immediate kill, no residual effect).
6. **Caution:** ozone quenches bacterial fluorescence used for laser-guided endpoints (George and Walsh, 2017).

**Evidence:** a systematic review found ozone less effective than NaOCl and of no added value as an adjunct (Silva et al, 2020).

## 7. Toxicity, safety and contraindications

### Explanation

**Cell toxicity.** The endodontic gas concentration of 4 g/m3 is slightly less cytotoxic than 2.5% sodium hypochlorite (Kishen, 2017) [SRC]. Aqueous ozone up to 20 μg/mL showed essentially no toxicity to oral cells in vitro (Kishen, 2017) [SRC]. The Azarpazhooh and Limeback review highlighted biocompatibility with epithelial cells, gingival fibroblasts and periodontal cells (Kishen, 2017) [SRC].

**Gas toxicity.** The gas is a very effective sterilising agent but, at levels close to its toxic levels, damages normal tissue (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013) [SRC]; this is why sealed cups and neutralisers are used. Cohen's states that the hazards of ozone in endodontics have not been investigated (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC] and that patient and dentist exposure to the gas needs care (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]. In medicine, direct intravenous gas injection can cause air embolism (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC].

**Contraindications** (medical ozone therapy): acute alcohol intoxication, recent myocardial infarction, haemorrhage from any organ, pregnancy, hyperthyroidism, thrombocytopenia and ozone allergy (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Toxicity and safety**
- Biocompatible with oral epithelial cells, fibroblasts and PDL cells; aqueous ozone up to 20 μg/mL non-toxic in vitro; 4 g/m3 gas slightly less cytotoxic than 2.5% NaOCl (Kishen, 2017).
- Gas at levels close to its toxic levels damages normal tissue: use sealed cups, suction and neutralisers; avoid leakage into the operatory (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013). Hazards in endodontics not yet investigated (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021).

**Contraindications:** pregnancy, hyperthyroidism, thrombocytopenia, recent myocardial infarction, active haemorrhage, acute alcohol intoxication, ozone allergy (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015).

## 8. Advantages, disadvantages and current status

### Explanation

Cohen's summary is blunt: in vitro results were promising, clinical results the opposite (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) [SRC]. Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh note that despite promising in vitro evidence, clinical use has not reached a strong level of efficacy and cost-effectiveness (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC], and they call for well-designed double-blind randomised trials with adequate sample size (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015) [SRC]. The current place of ozone is therefore as an adjunct, not a replacement for sodium hypochlorite or for conventional caries control [Likely].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Advantages:** broad spectrum, no resistance, non-invasive, carious dentine need not be removed and no anaesthesia is needed in children (Caprioglio et al, 2018), biocompatible at clinical doses, short application.

**Disadvantages:** unstable (generated on site), costly equipment, acts only on contact, weak against biofilm, no residual effect, inhalation toxicity, inconsistent dosing protocols.

**Evidence on caries:** a Cochrane review (3 trials, 432 lesions) found no reliable evidence that ozone stops or reverses caries (Rickard et al, 2004); the review was later withdrawn, and textbooks still call the evidence questionable (Fontana et al, 2016).

**Conclusion:** in vitro results are promising but clinical results are not (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021). Ozone remains an adjunct; sodium hypochlorite remains the irrigant of choice and fluoride-based measures the basis of caries control.

## Critical evidence synthesis

**Claim: ozone is an effective root canal disinfectant.** The supportive data are planktonic and short-contact laboratory results (Kishen, 2017) [SRC], while every textbook-cited biofilm or infected-root model shows ozone inferior to sodium hypochlorite or ineffective (Mohammadi and Azarpazhooh, 2015; Baumann and Beer, 2010) [SRC]. The only systematic review that included a clinical trial reached the same conclusion and advised against using ozone either to replace or to supplement sodium hypochlorite (Silva et al, 2020) [LIT]. The Baumann and Beer healing cases cannot separate the effect of ozone from the rest of the treatment, so they do not answer the question [Certain]. On present evidence, ozone is a weak, biofilm-limited adjunct, and clinical efficacy is unproven [Likely].

## High-yield points
- O3: triatomic, MW 47.98, half-life about 40 min at 20 °C, third strongest oxidant.
- Three forms: gas, ozonated water, ozonated olive oil.
- Mechanism: oxidation of cell wall/membrane, glycoproteins, enzymes; no resistance.
- HealOzone: sealed silicone cup, gas sucked back and neutralised; 4 g/m3 (Kishen) versus about 2100 ppm (Sikri).
- Kills planktonic *E. faecalis*; little effect on biofilm; NaOCl superior.
- Cochrane ozone-caries review: no reliable evidence (later withdrawn).
- Hypersensitivity RCTs: no benefit over placebo.
- Ozone quenches fluorescence used to guide laser disinfection.

## Common exam mistakes
- Calling ozone "equal or superior to NaOCl"; the reviews say less effective and no added value.
- Presenting extracted-tooth or broth studies as clinical evidence.
- Forgetting that ozone acts only on contact, so proximal caries cannot be treated.
- Quoting a single device concentration as universal; books give 4 g/m3 and about 2100 ppm.
- Omitting toxicity and contraindications.
