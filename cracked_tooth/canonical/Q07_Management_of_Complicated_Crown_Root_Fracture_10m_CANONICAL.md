## 1. Definition and diagnosis

### Explanation

A crown-root fracture involves enamel, dentin and cementum (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]; it is complicated when the pulp is exposed. It made up 5% of permanent-tooth injuries in Andreasen's material (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. The line usually starts a few millimetres above the gingiva labially and runs obliquely below the crevice palatally (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. Because displacement is minimal, these fractures are frequently overlooked (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. Radiographs add little, because the oblique line is almost perpendicular to the beam (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]; the IADT suggests CBCT to show the fracture path and crown-root ratio (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC]. Since the fracture communicates with the mouth, fracture healing cannot be expected (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** A fracture involving enamel, dentin and cementum with exposure of the pulp (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007). 5% of permanent-tooth injuries.

**Features.** The line runs from a few millimetres above the labial gingiva obliquely below the palatal crevice; the coronal fragment is mobile but held by palatal PDL fibres. Radiographs rarely show the palatal extent (the line is perpendicular to the beam); CBCT shows the fracture path and crown-root ratio (Bourguignon et al, 2020). The fracture communicates with the mouth, so it cannot heal.

## 2. Management

### Explanation

Emergency care stabilises the fragment with an acid-etch splint (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC], or to the non-mobile fragment (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC], with definitive treatment within a few days (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. In immature teeth a partial pulpotomy is advantageous (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC]; in mature teeth removal of the pulp is usually indicated (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC]. Andreasen lists four definitive conservative alternatives (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. (1) Fragment removal allows gingival healing (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC], but only for superficial fractures not involving the pulp (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]; restore after 2-3 weeks (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]; re-fracture is frequent (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. (2) Surgical exposure by gingivectomy and osteotomy (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC], only when just the palatal aspect needs exposure (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. Check the lingual step for a secondary fracture (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]; labial migration of about 0.8 mm over 5 years follows (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. (3) Orthodontic extrusion, introduced by Heithersay in 1973 (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]: 2-4 mm for a central incisor and 4-6 mm for a lateral (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC], leaving a smaller cervical diameter (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. (4) Surgical extrusion, only with a complete root long enough for a post crown (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]; postponing RCT for 3-4 weeks gives better results (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]; 180° rotation may reduce the extrusion needed (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]; in Kahnberg's series all 19 teeth survived (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. The IADT adds intentional replantation with or without rotation (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC]. For unrestorable teeth in the young, root submergence keeps the alveolar width and height (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]. After growth an implant is a good alternative (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC]; in growing patients, space closure or autotransplantation (Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Emergency (Bourguignon et al, 2020; Andreasen, Andreasen and Tsukiboshi, 2007)**
- Stabilise the loose fragment to the adjacent teeth with an acid-etch splint; plan definitive care within days.
- Pulp: immature tooth, partial pulpotomy with calcium hydroxide or a non-staining calcium silicate; mature tooth, pulpectomy.
- Cover exposed dentin with GIC or bonded composite.

**Definitive options**

| Option | Indication | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Fragment removal and supragingival restoration | Superficial (chisel) fracture | Restore after 2-3 weeks; frequent re-fracture |
| 2. Surgical exposure (gingivectomy and osteotomy) | Only the palatal side needs exposure | Check the lingual step; 0.8 mm labial migration in 5 years |
| 3. Orthodontic extrusion (Heithersay, 1973) | Root long enough; aesthetics critical | Central incisor 2-4 mm, lateral 4-6 mm (crown-root ratio 1:1); smaller cervical diameter |
| 4. Surgical extrusion or intentional replantation, with rotation | Complete root, long enough for a post crown | Delay RCT 3-4 weeks; 180° rotation; all 19 teeth survived (Kahnberg) |
| 5. Root submergence | Unrestorable tooth in a young patient | Keeps ridge width and height |
| 6. Extraction | None of the above possible | Implant after growth; autotransplantation or space closure in growing patients |

**Restoration:** RCT, then post-retained crown or bonded fragment.

**Prognosis:** good periodontal stability after extrusion; surgical extrusion shows little or no resorption over 5 years.

**Follow-up:** 1 week, 6-8 weeks, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, then yearly for at least 5 years.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Enamel, dentin and cementum plus pulp; oblique, below the palatal gingiva.
2. Splint the fragment as an emergency; partial pulpotomy in immature teeth.
3. Four Andreasen options: fragment removal, surgical exposure, orthodontic extrusion, surgical extrusion.
4. Extrusion limit: 2-4 mm central, 4-6 mm lateral.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW

1. Oblique crown-root fracture line with the four treatment options.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Expecting the fracture to heal like a root fracture.
2. Using surgical exposure where the labial aspect would also need exposure.
3. Surgical extrusion of an immature or short root.
