## 1. Diagnosis

### Explanation

A horizontal root fracture involves dentin, cementum and pulp across the root (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC]; the line may be horizontal, oblique or both (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC]. Diagnosis depends entirely on radiographs (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC], with extra exposures at ±15° (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC] and a steep occlusal view (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** A fracture of the root involving dentin, cementum and pulp, running transversely or obliquely across the root (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007; Bourguignon et al, 2020).

**Aim of treatment.** Allow the pulp and PDL to unite the fragments, ideally with hard tissue, while preventing infection of the coronal pulp.

**Diagnosis.** The coronal fragment is mobile, extruded and lingually displaced; tender; sulcular bleeding. Diagnosis depends on radiographs: a parallel periapical, two more at ±15°, and an occlusal film; CBCT if needed. Sensibility may be negative at first.

## 2. Management

### Explanation

The principles are repositioning and immobilisation (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC]. Resistance on repositioning suggests a labial socket fracture (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC]. The IADT splint is passive and flexible for 4 weeks (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC], up to 4 months for cervical fractures (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC]. Rigid splints did worst (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC], and forced bands are contraindicated (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC]. Incomplete fractures in immature teeth need no fixation (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC]. No endodontic treatment at the emergency visit (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC]. Cervical fractures can heal (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC], and permanent proximal composite fixation is possible (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC]; a very mobile fragment above the crest is removed, with RCT and a post-retained crown (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC]. Necrosis usually affects the coronal fragment only (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC], so only that fragment is root filled (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC], with apexification if needed (Bourguignon et al, 2020) [SRC]. A negative early sensibility test does not mean necrosis (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC]. On extraction, the marginal socket wall must not be removed (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC]; a submerged vital apical fragment preserves the ridge (Andreasen, Andreasen and Cvek, 2007) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Emergency**
1. Local anaesthesia; reposition the coronal fragment digitally; check radiographically.
2. Passive, flexible acid-etch splint: 4 weeks for apical and middle-third fractures; up to 4 months for cervical fractures. No rigid splints or orthodontic bands.
3. No endodontic treatment at the emergency visit.
4. Incomplete fractures in immature teeth: no splint needed.

**By level of fracture**
- Apical third: usually heals; splint only if mobile.
- Middle third: reposition and splint 4 weeks.
- Cervical third: do not remove the fragment at once; splint up to 4 months or fix permanently to the neighbours with composite. If very mobile and above the crest: remove the coronal fragment, RCT, post-retained crown, with orthodontic or surgical extrusion or crown lengthening; otherwise extract, preserving the labial socket wall and possibly submerging the vital apical root.

**Pulp necrosis:** usually the coronal fragment only. Root fill the coronal fragment to the fracture line, with apexification (calcium hydroxide or an MTA barrier) at that level. Do not start RCT on a negative sensibility test alone.

**Expected healing (Andreasen):** (1) hard-tissue union when the pulp is intact; (2) connective tissue between the fragments after moderate pulp injury; (3) bone and connective tissue when injury precedes alveolar growth; (4) granulation tissue when the coronal pulp becomes infected, which is the only outcome needing endodontic treatment.

**Follow-up:** 4 weeks, 6-8 weeks, 4 months, 6 months, 1 year, then yearly for at least 5 years. Hard-tissue union cannot be confirmed before 3 months.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Reposition, then a flexible splint for 4 weeks (up to 4 months for cervical).
2. No RCT at the emergency visit; root fill the coronal fragment only if necrosis develops.
3. Cervical fractures can heal; do not remove the fragment straight away.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW

1. Incisor with a horizontal fracture, flexible splint, and coronal-fragment RCT ending at the fracture line.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Root filling the apical fragment.
2. Rigid splinting for 3 months by default.
3. Starting RCT at the first visit.
