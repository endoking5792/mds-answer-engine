## 1. Rationale and measures

### Explanation

Prevention is the key to managing VRF (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], because once present the root must be removed. Endodontic treatment is the commonest contributing procedure (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], by weakening the root as a structure (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC]. Torabinejad's two cardinal rules are to avoid excessive intraradicular dentin removal and to minimise internal wedging forces (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]. Excessive preparation predisposes to VRF (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], especially in danger zones where dentin thins (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC]. NiTi rotary and reciprocating files produce microcracks in vitro (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], hand files and the self-adjusting file did not (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], and the clinical link is unconfirmed (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC]. Rigid stainless-steel spreaders increase strain (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], NiTi spreaders need less force (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], and many accessory cones add risk (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]. Irrigants and medicaments may weaken dentin (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC], as may ultrasonic root-end preparation (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC]. Long or thick posts predispose (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], as does post corrosion (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC]; posts only when essential (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], small, passive and unthreaded (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC], cemented with a vent (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]. Crowns with a ferrule distribute strain better (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], with a 1.5-2 mm ferrule on sound dentin (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC], and preserving at least one coronal wall helps (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Rationale.** A VRF cannot be repaired, so prevention is the key (Berman and Tamse, 2021). Two cardinal rules: avoid excessive removal of intraradicular dentin, and minimise internal wedging forces (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

**1. Case assessment.** Recognise high-risk roots (oval premolars, mesial roots of mandibular molars, mandibular incisors) and danger-zone concavities; probe every tooth before treatment with a flexible probe.

**2. Access and preparation**
- Conservative access; preserve pericervical dentin.
- Avoid overenlarging canals and large tapers; respect danger zones.
- Flexible NiTi or minimally invasive systems (rotary files caused microcracks in vitro; the clinical link is unconfirmed).
- Avoid prolonged, concentrated irrigants and long calcium hydroxide dressings that weaken dentin.

**3. Obturation**
- Avoid excessive lateral compaction force; use NiTi finger spreaders, not rigid stainless-steel hand spreaders.
- Fewer accessory cones and spreader insertions; consider thermoplastic or single-cone techniques.

**4. Posts**
- Use only when needed for core retention.
- Small, passive, parallel-sided or fibre posts; never threaded or wedging; no excessive post-space preparation.
- Cement slowly with a vent; avoid corrodible metal posts.

**5. Coronal restoration**
- Cuspal coverage with a ferrule of 1.5-2 mm on sound dentin; preserve at least one coronal wall.
- Restore promptly after RCT.

**Why these measures work.** Fractures start where strain concentrates: on the inner wall at the buccal and lingual convexity of an oval root, where dentin is thinnest. Every procedure that removes dentin there, or pushes outward on the canal wall (spreaders, tapered posts, cement under pressure), adds strain that is stored and may cause a fracture months or years later. Preserving dentin and avoiding outward forces therefore attacks the mechanism, not just the symptoms.

**6. Retreatment:** endodontic retreatment removes further radicular dentin and adds strain (Tamse and Berman, 2019), so keep re-preparation minimal in thin roots and inspect the canal walls under the microscope for existing cracks during re-entry.

**7. Surgery and occlusion:** careful ultrasonic root-end preparation; relieve occlusal prematurities; manage bruxism.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Avoid excessive dentin removal and wedging forces.
2. NiTi finger spreaders; fewer accessory cones.
3. Posts only when needed: passive, small, unthreaded.
4. Ferrule 1.5-2 mm with cuspal coverage.
