## 1. Principle and diagnosis in multirooted teeth

### Explanation

In molars, VRF typically affects the mesial root of mandibular molars and the mesiobuccal root of maxillary molars (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC]. In mandibular molar mesial roots, the halo radiolucency involved the furcation in 63.3% (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC]. A furcation pocket may be either a VRF or a sinus tract from an apical abscess (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC]. An exploratory flap is indicated when the findings are equivocal (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Principle.** A vertical root fracture is a permanent pathway for bacteria into the periodontium, so the fractured root must be removed. In a multirooted tooth, only the fractured root need be removed, and the rest of the tooth can often be kept (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002; Krishnan and Swain, 2025).

**Roots commonly affected:** the mesial root of mandibular molars and the mesiobuccal root of maxillary molars (Berman and Tamse, 2021).

**Confirming the diagnosis**
- Narrow, isolated buccal or lingual pocket on the affected root; a coronal sinus tract.
- Halo or J-shaped radiolucency, often involving the furcation (63.3% in mandibular molar mesial roots; Tamse and Berman, 2019).
- A furcation pocket may be a VRF or a draining apical abscess; healing after infection control rules out VRF (Berman and Tamse, 2021).
- An exploratory flap shows the fracture line and dehiscence and identifies which root is fractured.

## 2. Management options

### Explanation

The only treatment is removal of the fractured root; in multirooted teeth this can be root amputation or hemisection (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]. Torabinejad's chapter illustrates a hemisected molar in which the fractured mesial segment was removed (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]. Cohen's shows a mesial root resection of a split root (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC]. After root amputation a new coronal restoration is often advised (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC]. A VRF limited to the apical part allows partial root removal (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC]: the root was shaved until the fracture disappeared (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC], and in another case both roots were root-end sealed with MTA (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC]. Extraction should follow as soon as possible when the tooth cannot be retained (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC], because bone loss grows with time (Tamse and Berman, 2019) [SRC] and early extraction makes implant placement easier (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC]. Attempted repairs are unreliable (Berman and Tamse, 2021) [SRC]. Case selection (enough bone around the remaining roots, a restorable crown, no fused roots or furcation fracture) and the sequence of RCT before resection are [RECALL] here.

#### ✎ Exam core

**1. Root amputation (resection)** (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002; Tamse and Berman, 2019)
- Indication: maxillary molar with a fractured mesiobuccal or distobuccal root.
- Complete RCT on the remaining roots, then remove the fractured root at the furcation through a flap; smooth the furcation and restore the crown.
- A new coronal restoration protecting the remaining tooth is often advised.

**2. Hemisection** (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002)
- Indication: mandibular molar with a fractured mesial or distal root.
- Section the tooth buccolingually through the furcation; extract the fractured half with its crown.
- Restore the remaining half as a premolar-like unit or as a bridge abutment.

**3. Apical VRF:** surgical removal of the fractured apical portion, with root-end resection beyond the fracture and an MTA root-end filling (Tamse and Berman, 2019).

**4. Extraction:** when several roots or the furcation are fractured, the remaining roots lack support, or the tooth is unrestorable. Extract early to limit bone loss and preserve the site for an implant (Berman and Tamse, 2021).

**Case selection [RECALL]:** separated roots (not fused), adequate bone around the retained roots, a restorable crown, good oral hygiene and patient consent.

**Not recommended:** bonding or cementing the fractured segments, or lasers; none is a reliable long-term solution (Berman and Tamse, 2021).

**Prognosis:** hopeless for the fractured root (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); the retained part depends on its periodontal support and restoration.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Remove only the fractured root: root amputation (maxillary molar) or hemisection (mandibular molar).
2. Common sites: mesial root of mandibular molars, mesiobuccal root of maxillary molars.
3. Apical VRF: resect beyond the fracture and seal with MTA.
4. Extract early when the furcation or several roots are fractured.
5. Repair or bonding of the fracture is not reliable.

## DIAGRAMS TO DRAW

1. Hemisected mandibular molar with the fractured mesial half removed.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Extracting the whole molar when one root could be resected.
2. Resecting a root before completing RCT on the remaining roots.
3. Offering adhesive repair as a definitive treatment.
