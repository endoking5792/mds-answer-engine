## 1. Rounded versus sharp internal line angles

### Explanation

An internal line angle is one whose apex points into the tooth (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]. All transitions between prepared surfaces are designed smooth and rounded to limit stress concentration (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; slightly rounded internal angles increase the tooth's fracture resistance (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC], and rounding the angles that face the restoration, such as the axiopulpal, increases the restoration's fracture resistance (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; the gingival margin trimmer is used to round the axiopulpal angle for this reason (Boushell and Donovan, 2019) [SRC]. Marzouk: amalgam angles rounded but definite (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC], and pear-shaped composite instruments give 90 degree margins without sharp internal angles (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]; external junctions are softened (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; avoiding stress from sharp angulations (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; enamel junctions very rounded (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC], decreasing stress concentration (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC] and frail corner rods (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC].

**Where definite or sharp angles are still wanted.** Hatchets sharpen internal line angles for direct gold (Boushell and Donovan, 2019) [SRC], and angle formers sharpen line angles and create retention for gold (Boushell and Donovan, 2019) [SRC]. In gold inlays Marzouk keeps definite angles between pulpal floor and walls (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC] but rounds the axiopulpal junction (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; Fan et al want internal angles well defined (Fan, Stevenson and Berry, 2013) [SRC] and Singh lists well-defined angles for inlays (Singh, 2020) [SRC], whereas Sturdevant's No. 271 bur avoids sharp internal angles (Sturdevant JR, 2019) [SRC] and the axiopulpal angle is bevelled (Sturdevant JR, 2019) [SRC].

**Brittle materials.** Ni-Cr preparations need more rounded angles (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC], with less taper to make up retention (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; cast ceramics very rounded (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; ceramic inlays rounded (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013) [SRC]; composite: avoid sharp angles (Garg and Garg, 2011) [SRC].

**Mechanism [RECALL].** At a sharp re-entrant corner tensile stress is concentrated; the smaller the radius, the higher the local stress, and cracks start there in brittle enamel, dentin and restorative materials. A rounded corner spreads the stress and is filled more completely, without voids.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** An internal line angle is the junction of two prepared walls whose apex points into the tooth (Boushell and Walter, 2019).

**Why round them (stress concentration).** A sharp internal corner concentrates tensile stress at its apex; cracks start there and run through the cusp or the restoration. Rounding spreads the load and lets the material adapt without voids.
- Rounded internal angles increase the tooth's resistance to fracture (Boushell and Walter, 2019).
- Rounding angles facing the restoration (axiopulpal) increases the restoration's resistance to fracture; gingival margin trimmers round the axiopulpal angle (Boushell and Donovan, 2019).

**Enamel and external junctions.** Junctions between enamel walls are made very rounded even when the dentinal junctions are angular, which lowers stress concentration and avoids frail corner rods (Marzouk et al, undated); any external junction of walls, obtuse or acute, is softened (Boushell and Walter, 2019).

**How it is achieved.** Burs with rounded corners (No. 271 for inlays: Sturdevant JR, 2019; elongated pear-shaped instruments for composite, giving 90° margins without sharp internal angles: Ahmed et al, 2019); gingival margin trimmers for the axiopulpal angle.

**By material.**

| Material | Internal angles | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Amalgam | Rounded but definite | Marzouk et al, undated |
| Composite | Avoid sharp angles | Garg and Garg, 2011 |
| Cast gold | Definite; axiopulpal rounded or bevelled | Marzouk et al, undated; Sturdevant JR, 2019 |
| Ni-Cr, cast ceramic | More to very rounded; less taper compensates | Marzouk et al, undated |
| Ceramic inlay | Rounded | Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013 |
| Direct gold | Sharpened with hatchets, angle formers | Boushell and Donovan, 2019 |

**Disagreement.** For gold inlays Fan, Stevenson and Berry (2013) and Singh (2020) want well-defined internal angles (seating, retention); Sturdevant JR (2019) uses a round-cornered No. 271 bur. All round the axiopulpal angle.

**Conclusion.** "Rounded" means definite but not sharp: sharpness is kept only where retention needs it (direct gold, gold inlays) and rounding is increased as the material becomes more brittle.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Rounded internal angles: tooth fracture resistance; rounded axiopulpal angle: restoration fracture resistance.
2. Amalgam: rounded but definite.
3. Direct gold: sharp angles for retention.
4. Ceramics and Ni-Cr: very rounded.
