# Discuss in detail treatment planning and concepts of tooth preparation for tooth coloured indirect restorations.

**Marks:** 25 · Essay

## 1. Introduction and the planning question

Tooth-coloured indirect restorations are composite or ceramic inlays, onlays, partial crowns (overlays), endocrowns and veneers, made outside the mouth and bonded with resin cement. Their success depends less on mechanical retention than on correct case selection, an adhesive-friendly preparation and a controlled bonding protocol. **Treatment planning sequence**
1. History: esthetic demand, parafunction (bruxism, clenching), caries risk, oral hygiene, ability to attend two visits or need for a single CAD/CAM visit.
2. Examination of the tooth: size of the lesion, remaining walls and cusps, marginal ridges, cracks, margin position (enamel or dentin, supra- or subgingival), pulp and endodontic status, periodontal health.
3. Occlusion: wear facets, opposing restorations, contact position relative to planned margins.
4. Decision direct versus indirect: direct is usually not advisable when more than half the tooth structure is lost (Sikri, 2017); deciding factors are lesion size, endodontic/periodontal status, compliance, esthetics and operator skill (Sikri, 2017).
5. Choice of restoration and material, then fabrication route (laboratory or chairside CAD/CAM). CAD/CAM is a means of fabrication, not a different restoration (Fasbinder and Neiva, 2019).

## 2. Indications and contraindications

| | Indications | Contraindications |
|---|---|---|
| Indirect composite inlay/onlay | Esthetic replacement of metal restorations (main indication); large defects; improving contacts and contours (Sikri, 2017) | Bruxism/clenching; dry field not possible; deep subgingival margins; poor hygiene (Sikri, 2017) |
| Ceramic inlay/onlay/partial crown | Esthetics with margins on enamel (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013); large lesions with undermined enamel; endodontically compromised teeth as an alternative to post-core-crown; metal allergy (Stappert et al., 2018) | Poor hygiene, too little structure for bonding, no moisture control, parafunction (Sikri, 2017); aggressive wear, opposing gold (Stappert et al., 2018) | Isolation is decisive: if the tooth cannot be isolated, an adhesive restoration should not be placed (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013).

## 3. Choice of material: composite or ceramic

| Situation | Preferred option |
|---|---|
| Two-/three-surface inlay of moderate width | Resin composite first (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013) |
| Isthmus over two-thirds intercuspal width | Ceramic ("a few authors", Sikri, 2017) |
| Any cusp covered | Ceramic onlay (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013) |
| Short molar crown | Ceramic onlay: 2 mm occlusal reduction, no axial retention needed (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013) |
| Root-treated molar with sound walls | Endocrown (Alani and Patel, 2025) |
| Discoloured, malformed anterior teeth | Porcelain/pressed ceramic veneer | Ceramic bonds more reliably (etched and silanated), while the composite-cement interface is a weak link (Sikri, 2017).

## 4. Concepts of preparation for posterior inlays and onlays

**Basic concept:** retention and resistance come mainly from adhesion, so the preparation is divergent, relatively nonretentive and as conservative as the defect allows (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013; Fasbinder and Neiva, 2019). Ceramic, composite and CAD/CAM preparations are essentially the same (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013). | Feature | Gold inlay | Composite/ceramic inlay/onlay |
|---|---|---|
| Outline | Narrow | Wider, defect-dictated |
| Wall taper | 2-6° | 10-12° (Sikri, 2017); "divergent" (Summitt's, Sturdevant's) |
| Line angles | Definite | Rounded |
| Bevels | Given | None |
| Retention aids | Grooves, ferrule | Rounded boxes only, no grooves; no ferrule | **Key dimensions (by source)**
- Isthmus at least 2 mm wide (Sikri, 2017; Fasbinder and Neiva, 2019).
- Depth: no less than 2.0 mm, axial 1.0-1.5 mm; ceramic ideally uniform 2.0 mm, axially 1.5 mm (Sikri, 2017). Summitt's: at least 1.5 mm central fossa, 2.0 mm over functional cusps.
- Cusp coverage when more than one-third of the incline is involved (Sikri, 2017); reduce at least 2.0 mm following cusp inclines.
- Proximal flare 90-100° (Sikri, 2017).
- Rounded internal angles reduce stress and help fabrication and milling (Stappert et al., 2018; Fasbinder and Neiva, 2019).
- Undercuts blocked out with resin composite base; GIC is weak as a base (Sikri, 2017) and is used only for block-out (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013).

## 5. Margin design

| Source | Recommended margin |
|---|---|
| Sikri, 2017 | Flat butt joint, 90° cavosurface, no bevel |
| Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013; Fasbinder and Neiva, 2019 | 90° butt joint; for esthetics, a 45° modification with a football-shaped diamond keeping ceramic bulk |
| Stappert et al., 2018 | No bevel; hollow-ground chamfer confined to marginal enamel | All agree on no bevel (thin ceramic or resin edges chip) and on supragingival enamel margins where possible. CAD/CAM onlays use no ferrule.

## 6. Veneers, endocrowns and newer concepts

**Veneers:** intra-enamel preparation; window design for direct composite veneers, butt-joint or incisal-lapping designs for porcelain (Heymann and Ritter, 2019). | Dimension | Heymann and Ritter, 2019 | Sikri, 2017 | Stappert et al., 2018 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Labial | 0.5-0.75 mm, 0.3-0.5 mm gingivally | Depth cuts 0.5-0.7 mm (maxillary) | 0.3-0.5 mm; minimum 0.4 mm |
| Incisal | - | At least 1.0 mm | 0.5-1.5 mm; 1.5 mm minimum ceramic |
| Margins | Chamfer at gingival crest | Enamel all round | Mainly enamel, no bevel | **Endocrown:** single piece engaging the pulp chamber, no post; at least 2 mm occlusal reduction, undercut-free chamber walls, supragingival margin, adhesive cementation (Alani and Patel, 2025). **Immediate dentine sealing:** freshly cut dentine is bonded immediately after preparation, before impression, reducing sensitivity and contamination (Alani and Patel, 2025; Donovan and Oliveira, 2019). **Cervical margin relocation (deep margin elevation):** a deep subgingival proximal margin is raised with composite so the indirect margin can be isolated (Alani and Patel, 2025).

## 7. Cementation implications

- Preparation design anticipates bonding: rubber dam, supragingival enamel margins and IDS on dentine.
- Resin cement only; dual-cure in posterior teeth (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013; Sikri, 2017).
- Ceramic intaglio: 8-12% hydrofluoric acid 1-3 min, then silane (Sikri, 2017). Composite intaglio: air abrasion and silane.
- Tooth: etch, prime, bond; seat, remove excess, cure; glycerin gel on margins.
- Failures are bulk fracture where material is under 2 mm thick and marginal wear (Fasbinder, Neiva and Robbins, 2013). **Conclusion.** Planning chooses the patient, tooth and material; preparation follows adhesive rules (divergence, rounded angles, uniform thickness, unbevelled enamel margins); exact figures differ between books.
