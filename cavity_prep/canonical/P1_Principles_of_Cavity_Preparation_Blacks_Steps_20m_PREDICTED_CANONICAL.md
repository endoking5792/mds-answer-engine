## 1. Concept and the two sequences

### Explanation

Black's steps are a systematic sequence (Summitt, 2013) [SRC] that Summitt's judges generally as appropriate today as when he formulated them (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. Harpreet Singh dates the principles to 1896 (Singh, 2020) [SRC] but on the same page also says Black gave his systematic approach "in the 1930s" (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; Black died in 1915, as Harpreet Singh's own chapter 1 states (Singh, 2020) [SRC], so the "1930s" statement is plainly wrong. The term "cavity preparation" has been changed to "tooth preparation" because of adhesive materials (Singh, 2020) [SRC].

Four versions of the sequence:
- **Black, as reported by Summitt's** (7 steps): outline, resistance, retention, convenience, remove remaining carious dentin (Summitt, 2013) [SRC], finish enamel walls and margins (Summitt, 2013) [SRC], and clean the preparation, Black's "toilet of the cavity" (Summitt, 2013) [SRC].
- **Marzouk** (8 steps, modified from Black) (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]: carious dentin removal comes before convenience form, and an eighth step is added, observing the biologic form (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; the steps can seldom be done in this sequence (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC].
- **Sturdevant's** (9 steps in two stages): initial (1-4) and final (5-9), including secondary resistance and retention forms (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC], external wall finishing (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC] and debridement and inspection (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; the order may change with deep caries (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC].
- **Harpreet Singh** follows Sturdevant's initial four (Singh, 2020) [SRC] and five final steps, ending with cleaning, inspecting, varnishing and conditioning (Singh, 2020) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** G.V. Black (father of operative dentistry) standardised cavity preparation as a sequence of steps; with adhesive materials the term is now "tooth preparation", but the sequence remains the basis of design (Summitt, 2013; Singh, 2020).

| Black's steps (as in Summitt, 2013) | Sturdevant's modern sequence (Boushell and Walter, 2019) |
|---|---|
| 1. Outline form | **Initial stage** 1. Initial depth and outline form |
| 2. Resistance form | 2. Primary resistance form |
| 3. Retention form | 3. Primary retention form |
| 4. Convenience form | 4. Convenience form |
| 5. Removal of remaining carious dentin | **Final stage** 5. Removal of defective restorative material and/or soft dentin |
| (not a separate step) | 6. Pulp protection |
| (not a separate step) | 7. Secondary resistance and retention forms |
| 6. Finishing enamel walls and cavosurface margins | 8. External wall finishing |
| 7. Toilet of the cavity | 9. Final procedures: debridement and inspection |

Marzouk et al (undated) place caries removal before convenience form and add an eighth step, "biologic form"; all books agree the order may change, e.g. deep caries is excavated early to protect the pulp.

## 2. Outline form

### Explanation

Outline form is the location the peripheries of the completed preparation will occupy (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. Sturdevant's principles: remove unsupported or friable enamel (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC], include all faults (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC], and place margins where they can be finished; factors include the restorative material (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; features include joining defects less than 0.5 mm apart (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]. Initial depth: Sturdevant's 0.2 mm inside the DEJ or 0.8 mm on root surfaces (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; Harpreet Singh 0.2-0.8 mm below the DEJ (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; Marzouk an average of 0.5 mm into dentin (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC].

**Extension for prevention: books disagree.** Black extended smooth-surface preparations to areas that are normally self-cleansing (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]. Marzouk keeps it, "cutting for immunity" (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC], because even successful restorations have a 20-120 micron microleakage space, even in clinically successful restorations (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. Sturdevant's says it has virtually been eliminated (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]. Summitt's: routine extension through fissures not known to be carious cannot be justified (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC]; seal them instead (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC]. Marzouk is the oldest of the books (pre-1990, references to 1984), which explains the difference.

#### ✎ Exam core

**1. Outline form:** the location of the preparation margins on the tooth surface, with initial depth (Marzouk et al, undated; Boushell and Walter, 2019).
- Principles: remove unsupported/friable enamel; include all faults; place margins where they can be finished.
- Factors: extent of caries, restorative material, esthetics, occlusion, adjacent contour, cavosurface design.
- Features: preserve cusps and marginal ridges, minimise faciolingual extension, enameloplasty, join defects <0.5 mm apart, restrict depth.
- Initial depth: 0.2 mm inside DEJ (0.8 mm on root) (Sturdevant's); 0.2-0.8 mm (Singh); 0.5 mm into dentin (Marzouk).
- **Extension for prevention:** Black extended margins to self-cleansing areas; Marzouk still supports it; Sturdevant's and Summitt's reject routine extension: fissures not carious are sealed instead.

## 3. Resistance and retention forms

### Explanation

Primary resistance form: shape and placement of walls and floors enabling tooth and restoration to withstand forces along the long axis (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; Marzouk's broader definition covers occlusal loading in general (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. Five principles: minimal extension, horizontal floors, rounded line angles, material thickness, cusp capping (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; amalgam 1.5-2 mm (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; cusp reduction considered at half and strongly recommended at two-thirds of groove-to-cusp distance (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; Harpreet Singh calls capping mandatory beyond two-thirds (Singh, 2020) [SRC] and warns against cup-shaped cavities (Singh, 2020) [SRC].

Primary retention form resists displacement by tipping or lifting forces (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; Marzouk: against masticatory dislodgement (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; Summitt's includes bonding (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]. It depends on the material (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]: amalgam walls converge occlusally (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; composite bonds (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; cast walls diverge 2-5 degrees per wall (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; the dovetail resists proximal displacement (Singh, 2020) [SRC]. Every preparation needs at least one principal means of retention (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; frictional retention depends on four factors (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; cement is the least effective auxiliary means (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. Marzouk's mechanics: floors at right angles to loading (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; crossing one marginal ridge at a quarter of the intercuspal distance loses about 10% of resistance to splitting (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. Many features that improve retention also improve resistance (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**2. Primary resistance form:** shape and placement of walls and floors that let tooth and restoration withstand masticatory forces along the long axis without fracture (Boushell and Walter, 2019).
- Minimal extension (strong cusps and marginal ridges).
- Box form with flat pulpal and gingival floors perpendicular to the long axis; avoid cup-shaped cavities (rocking, wedging).
- Rounded internal line angles.
- Adequate material bulk (amalgam 1.5-2 mm).
- Cusp capping: consider at half, usually at two-thirds of groove-to-cusp distance.
- Mechanics (Marzouk et al, undated): floors at right angles to loading avoid shear; retentive features must leave enough bulk of tooth; crossing marginal ridges progressively reduces resistance to splitting (Vale's experiment).

**3. Primary retention form:** form that prevents displacement by tipping or lifting forces; depends on the material (Boushell and Walter, 2019).
- Amalgam: walls converging occlusally; occlusal dovetail.
- Cast metal: near-parallel walls, 2-5° divergence per wall.
- Composite and GIC: micromechanical/chemical bonding.
- Marzouk et al (undated) divide retention into **principal** means (frictional retention from surface area, opposing walls, parallelism and proximity; dentin elasticity; undercuts; dovetail) and **auxiliary** means (grooves, internal boxes, pins, posts, etching, cement); every preparation needs at least one principal means, and cement is the least effective auxiliary means.
- Resistance and retention are prepared together, and features that improve one usually improve the other.

## 4. Convenience form and the final stage

### Explanation

Convenience form gives observation, access and ease of preparation and restoration (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; Marzouk includes modifications of the preparation or its instrumentation (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; Summitt's limits it where sound tooth would be removed (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]; clearance from the adjacent tooth is mandatory for glass-ceramic and cast gold (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC].

Final stage: firm dentin left in place remineralises and should not be removed (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; about 2 mm of bulk between pulp and metallic restorations is desirable (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; liners include calcium hydroxide, RMGI, zinc oxide-eugenol and MTA (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; each distinct area must be independently retentive (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC], using mechanical features or adhesive treatment of the walls (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; 90-degree margins for amalgam and glass-ceramic (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC], bevels for castings and some composites; unsupported enamel may be retained under bonded composite (Summitt, 2013) [SRC]; debridement with air-water syringe (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**4. Convenience form:** shape giving visibility, access and ease of preparing and restoring (Boushell and Walter, 2019), e.g. extension for access to deep caries, proximal clearance for matrices and impressions (mandatory for cast gold and ceramics), convenience points in direct gold. It must not sacrifice sound tooth unnecessarily (Summitt, 2013).

**Final stage** (Boushell and Walter, 2019; Singh, 2020)
5. **Removal of remaining caries/old material:** remove soft dentin, keep firm (remineralisable) dentin; in deep lesions selective removal to avoid exposure.
6. **Pulp protection:** liners (calcium hydroxide, RMGI, MTA) and bases; about 2 mm bulk between pulp and metal.
7. **Secondary resistance and retention:** grooves, coves, slots, pins, skirts, bevels, or adhesive treatment; each part independently retentive.
8. **Finishing external walls:** 90° butt joint for amalgam and ceramic; bevels for cast gold and some composites; remove friable enamel (unsupported enamel may be kept under bonded composite).
9. **Toilet of the cavity:** wash, gently dry (do not desiccate), inspect, disinfect or desensitise/varnish.

**Conclusion.** Black's sequence is still taught, but extension for prevention has been replaced by conservation and sealing, and retention form now depends on the material (Boushell and Walter, 2019; Summitt, 2013).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

**Claim: margins must be extended to self-cleansing areas (extension for prevention).**
- Textbook says: Marzouk supports it because microleakage spaces exist at every margin (Marzouk et al, undated).
- Other evidence: Summitt's cites strong evidence that carious dentin left under sealed fissures does not progress, so routine extension cannot be justified; Sturdevant's says the practice is virtually eliminated thanks to fluoride and prevention (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013; Boushell and Walter, 2019).
- Strength: moderate (clinical sealant studies cited by Summitt's).
- Conclusion: extend only to remove carious and undermined tooth; seal remaining fissures. [Likely]
- Clinical implication: smaller outlines conserve cusps and marginal ridges, improving resistance form.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Black's 7 steps: outline, resistance, retention, convenience, caries removal, finish walls, toilet.
2. Sturdevant's 9 steps: initial 1-4, final 5-9 (pulp protection and secondary R&R added).
3. Order is flexible: deep caries may be removed early.
4. Extension for prevention: Marzouk for, Sturdevant's and Summitt's against routine use.
5. Resistance: flat floors, rounded angles, bulk, cusp capping, minimal extension.
6. Retention depends on material: converge (amalgam), diverge 2-5° (cast), bond (composite).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Giving only four steps; the final stage (caries removal to toilet) carries marks.
2. Presenting extension for prevention as current practice without qualification.
3. Writing the same retention form for all materials.
4. Dating Black's steps to the 1930s (Black died in 1915).

## MNEMONIC

Sturdevant's nine steps: **"Old Rats Run Carefully; Rats Prefer Strong Fences Daily"** (Outline, Resistance, Retention, Convenience; Removal of caries, Pulp protection, Secondary R&R, Finishing walls, Debridement).
