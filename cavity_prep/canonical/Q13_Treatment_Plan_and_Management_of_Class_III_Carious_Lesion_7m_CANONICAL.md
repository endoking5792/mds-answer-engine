# Steps involved in treatment plan and management of a Class III carious lesion

**Marks:** 7 · Short note

## Answer A Class III lesion is a proximal lesion of an anterior tooth not involving the incisal edge (Boushell, 2019); management is diagnosis, risk-based planning and a conservative bonded restoration. **1. Diagnosis:** history; radiographs, visual inspection with transillumination, explorer (Boushell et al., 2019); assess cavitation, activity, caries risk, pulp vitality. **2. Treatment plan:** non-cavitated lesions: preventive and remineralising care; cavitated lesions: restore. Material: composite is the material of choice; amalgam rarely, mainly distal of canines when the preparation is extensive or moisture control is poor (Boushell, 2019). **3. Preliminary steps:** anaesthesia, occlusal check, shade selection before dehydration, rubber dam, pre-wedging (Ahmed et al., 2019). **4. Access:** lingual approach preferred (conserves facial enamel, shade less critical); facial when the lesion is facial, teeth are irregular or an old facial restoration is replaced (Ahmed et al., 2019). **5. Preparation**
- Entry close to the adjacent tooth with small round bur.
- Outline limited to the lesion; avoid the whole contact, facial surface and subgingival extension.
- Axial depth about 0.2 mm into dentin; gingivoaxial not over 0.75 mm on root.
- Selective caries removal; Pulp protection should be based on pulpal diagnosis and the remaining dentine; routine liners are not recommended solely because the lesion is deep (Schwendicke et al., 2026).
- 45° enamel bevel 0.5-2 mm; groove or cove only in large lesions (Ahmed et al., 2019). **Adjacent lesions:** prepare the larger outline first; restore the less accessible one first (Ahmed et al., 2019). **6. Restoration:** Mylar strip and wedge; etch, bond; composite in increments, cured through the strip (Ahmed et al., 2019). **7. Finishing:** flame-shaped finishing bur/diamond, discs and strips; check contact and occlusion. **8. Maintenance:** recall and preventive care. **Conclusion.** Successful Class III management combines lesion-directed diagnosis, conservative access, preservation of facial enamel, a sound adhesive protocol and careful finishing of proximal contact and contour.
