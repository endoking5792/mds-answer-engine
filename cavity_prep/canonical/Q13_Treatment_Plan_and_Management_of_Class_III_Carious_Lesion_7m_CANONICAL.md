## Explanation

Anterior proximal caries is found by radiographs, visual inspection with optional transillumination, or explorer (Boushell et al, 2019) [SRC]. Class III amalgam is rarely used (Boushell, 2019) [SRC] and is generally reserved for the distal surface of canines, with extensive preparation, cemental gingival margin or poor moisture control (Boushell, 2019) [SRC]; for esthetics it is rarely indicated on incisors and the mesial of canines (Boushell, 2019) [SRC]. Before preparation the shade is chosen before the tooth dehydrates (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC] and a wedge placed to help the contact (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]. Lingual access is always preferable unless it removes excessive structure (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC], because facial enamel is conserved (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]; facial access for facially placed lesions or irregular alignment (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]. Non-cavitated lesions are managed preventively before any cutting [RECALL]. The outline should not include the entire proximal contact (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]. Entry is as close to the adjacent tooth as possible without touching it (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]; initial axial depth 0.2 mm into dentin (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]; on root surface the gingivoaxial depth not more than 0.75 mm (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]. Bevel 45° and 0.5-2 mm wide on accessible enamel (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]; RMGI base only if remaining dentin thickness is under 1.5 mm (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]. Retention is almost exclusively by bonding (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]; gingival groove or incisal cove in large lesions (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]. Mylar matrix (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC], incremental curing for large restorations (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC], a flame-shaped carbide finishing bur or diamond for facial excess (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC], discs and strips for convex surfaces (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]. With approximating lesions the larger outline is developed first (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC] and the preparation with the least access is restored first (Ahmed et al, 2019) [SRC]. Class III defects lie on proximal surfaces of anterior teeth without affecting the incisal edge (Boushell, 2019) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** A Class III lesion is a proximal lesion of an anterior tooth not involving the incisal edge (Boushell, 2019); management is diagnosis, risk-based planning and a conservative bonded restoration.

**1. Diagnosis:** history; radiographs, visual inspection with transillumination, explorer (Boushell et al, 2019); assess cavitation, activity, caries risk, pulp vitality.

**2. Treatment plan:** non-cavitated lesions: preventive and remineralising care; cavitated lesions: restore. Material: composite is the material of choice; amalgam rarely, mainly distal of canines when the preparation is extensive or moisture control is poor (Boushell, 2019).

**3. Preliminary steps:** anaesthesia, occlusal check, shade selection before dehydration, rubber dam, pre-wedging (Ahmed et al, 2019).

**4. Access:** lingual approach preferred (conserves facial enamel, shade less critical); facial when the lesion is facial, teeth are irregular or an old facial restoration is replaced (Ahmed et al, 2019).

**5. Preparation**
- Entry close to the adjacent tooth with small round bur.
- Outline limited to the lesion; avoid the whole contact, facial surface and subgingival extension.
- Axial depth about 0.2 mm into dentin; gingivoaxial not over 0.75 mm on root.
- Selective caries removal; RMGI base if remaining dentin under 1.5 mm.
- 45° enamel bevel 0.5-2 mm; groove or cove only in large lesions (Ahmed et al, 2019).

**Adjacent lesions:** prepare the larger outline first; restore the less accessible one first (Ahmed et al, 2019).

**6. Restoration:** Mylar strip and wedge; etch, bond; composite in increments, cured through the strip (Ahmed et al, 2019).

**7. Finishing:** flame-shaped finishing bur/diamond, discs and strips; check contact and occlusion.

**8. Maintenance:** recall and preventive care.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Lingual access preferred.
- 0.2 mm into dentin; bevel 45°, 0.5-2 mm.
- Retention by bonding.
