## Explanation

Class V preparations lie in the gingival third of facial and lingual surfaces (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. **Amalgam:** Marzouk's Design 1 (conventional) is trapezoidal with the short side gingival; Design 2 adds a proximal "handle", Design 3 an occlusal "moustache", Design 4 multiple isolated boxes, Design 5 a furcation extension (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. Sturdevant's: axial depth 0.5 mm inside the DEJ and 0.75 mm in cementum; walls diverge facially so retention is needed: two grooves (incisoaxial and gingivoaxial) or four coves (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Summitt's: the trapezoidal gold-foil outline removed sound tooth; current outline for all materials is caries plus unsupported enamel (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC]. **Composite:** Garg and Garg name conventional (box, mainly root surface), beveled conventional (large lesion or replacement; 45° bevel 0.25-0.5 mm) and modified (small lesions; "scooped out") (Garg and Garg, 2011) [SRC]; Sturdevant's no longer uses these names: small lesions get no butt joints and no secondary retention; large ones combine bevelled enamel with 90° root margins; bevel about 45°, at least 0.5 mm wide; NCCL needs only debridement and roughening/bevelling of enamel (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Summitt's: no bevel on cementum; roughening sclerotic lesions did not increase retention in one trial (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC]; Marzouk's Design 2 for tooth-coloured materials is a non-mortise saucer, dish-shaped, for sensitive erosion/abrasion lesions (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. **GIC/RMGI:** like amalgam but no mechanical retention, no bevel, 90° butt joint about 1 mm deep; RMGI 96.4% retained at 5 years versus 51.5% composite in one study (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC]; erosion lesions need no instrumentation, only pumice and 10% polyacrylic acid conditioning (Mount and Ngo, 2016) [SRC].

**Disagreements:** Garg and Garg call microfill composite the material of choice for cervical flexure (Garg and Garg, 2011), whereas Overton et al (2013) say this recommendation "appears to have been refuted". Garg and Garg extend amalgam margins under the free gingiva in young patients (an extension-for-prevention remnant); Sturdevant's and Summitt's extend only to the lesion. Harpreet Singh prints "pulpal depth of 75 mm at the gingival wall" (Singh, 2020): plainly a misprint for 0.75 mm.

## ✎ Exam core

**Class V:** gingival third of facial or lingual surfaces (Ritter et al, 2019). Outline is now set by caries and unsupported enamel, not the old trapezoid (Overton et al, 2013).

| Material | Design |
|---|---|
| Amalgam | Trapezoid, short side gingival (Marzouk Design 1; variants: proximal handle, moustache, multiple boxes, furcation). Axial 0.5 mm inside DEJ, 0.75 mm in cementum; walls diverge, so two grooves or four coves (Ritter et al, 2019) |
| Composite: conventional | Box, 90°; lesion mainly on root |
| Composite: beveled conventional | Large lesion or replacement; 45° enamel bevel 0.25-0.5 mm (Garg and Garg, 2011) |
| Composite: modified | Small lesions; scooped out, no retention |
| GIC/RMGI (abrasion/erosion) | Minimal or no preparation; no bevel; 90° butt joint about 1 mm; polyacrylic acid conditioning (Overton et al, 2013; Mount and Ngo, 2016) |

No bevel on cementum; amalgam and glass ionomer get no bevel because of low edge strength (Overton et al, 2013). RMGI showed higher retention than composite in one trial (Overton et al, 2013).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Amalgam and GIC: no bevel (low edge strength).
- Microfill "material of choice" claim is disputed.
