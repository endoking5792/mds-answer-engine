## Explanation

Black introduced "extension for prevention" in his 1891 papers on enamel margins (Singh, 2020) [SRC] and set out his preparation method in his two-volume *Operative Dentistry* of 1908 (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; Torres and Schwendicke date his steps to 1908 and note they were established for amalgam, a nonadhesive material (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020) [SRC]. Marzouk explains the logic: margins had to lie in areas easily cleansable (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC] because every restoration has a microleakage space of 20-120 microns (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; "This principle is called" extension for prevention, or cutting for immunity (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. Sturdevant's records modifications by Bronner, Markley, Sturdevant and Sockwell (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]. Buonocore's acid etching (1955) and Bowen's composite shifted retention from mechanical to micromechanical (Singh, 2020) [SRC]. Black worked when caries aetiology was not understood (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020) [SRC]; with fluoride and prevention, extension for prevention "has virtually been eliminated" (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC] or "largely abandoned" (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020) [SRC], replaced by sealants, enameloplasty and conservative composite (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]. Mount et al note large restorations became larger at each replacement (Mount et al, 2016) [SRC], that Black's classification described the restoration not the lesion (Mount et al, 2016) [SRC], and that dentists can now be "physicians" first (Mount et al, 2016) [SRC]. Air abrasion and lasers have added instruments (Mount, Walsh and Brostek, 2016) [SRC] and chemomechanical caries removal is becoming more sophisticated (Mount, Walsh and Brostek, 2016) [SRC]. Composite preparations have rounded internal angles (Hilton and Broome, 2013) [SRC] and no extension for prevention (Hilton and Broome, 2013) [SRC]. The first objective of preparation is to conserve as much healthy tooth structure as possible (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]. Sturdevant's now speaks of tooth preparation, not cavity preparation (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Tooth preparation has moved from mechanically designed "cavities" for amalgam and gold to defect-dictated, adhesive, minimally invasive preparations.

**1. Black's era (1891-1908): surgical model**
- Black introduced "extension for prevention" (1891) and his systematic steps in *Operative Dentistry* (1908) (Singh, 2020; Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).
- Margins placed in self-cleansing, finishable areas because materials leaked (20-120 µm gap) (Marzouk et al, undated): "cutting for immunity".
- Steps designed for amalgam, a nonadhesive material (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020), so retention was mechanical and outlines large.

**2. Modifications of Black (mid-20th century)**
- Bronner, Markley, Sturdevant, Sockwell narrowed outlines and conserved cusps and marginal ridges (Boushell and Walter, 2019).

**3. Adhesive era (from 1955)**
- Buonocore's acid etch and Bowen's composite: retention mechanical to micromechanical (Singh, 2020).
- Rounded internal angles and defect-shaped, narrower outlines (Hilton and Broome, 2013).

**4. Preventive era: "prevention of extension"**
- Large Black-type restorations became even larger at each replacement, often ending in extraction (Mount et al, 2016).
- Black worked before caries aetiology was understood (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).
- Fluoride and caries control: extension for prevention virtually eliminated (Boushell and Walter, 2019), largely abandoned (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).
- Sealants, enameloplasty, preventive resin restorations replace extension; fissures sealed, not cut (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020).

**5. Minimal intervention (current)**
- Remove only the minimum tissue needed (Torres and Schwendicke, 2020); outline dictated by the lesion; no extension for prevention in composite preparations (Hilton and Broome, 2013).
- Lesion-based classification replaces Black's, which described restoration location (Mount et al, 2016).
- New instruments: air abrasion, lasers, chemomechanical caries removal (Mount, Walsh and Brostek, 2016).
- "Tooth preparation" replaces "cavity preparation" (Boushell and Walter, 2019).

| Era | Philosophy | Typical design |
|---|---|---|
| Black | Extension for prevention | Large, retentive |
| Adhesive | Bond replaces retention | Bevelled, rounded |
| MI | Preserve, seal, repair | Defect-only, slot/tunnel |


**Conclusion.** Black's sequence of steps still organises teaching, but its aim has changed: the first objective is now to conserve as much healthy tooth structure as possible (Boushell and Walter, 2019), with the dentist acting first as a physician who prevents and arrests disease (Mount et al, 2016).
