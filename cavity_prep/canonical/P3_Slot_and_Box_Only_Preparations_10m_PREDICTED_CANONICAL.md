## Explanation

Torres and Mehta use "proximal box" or "vertical slot" for access through the marginal ridge and "horizontal slot" for facial or lingual access (Torres and Mehta, 2020) [SRC]; Summitt's calls a Class II whose occlusal outline is limited to the marginal ridge a slot restoration (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC]. Sturdevant's indicates box-only when there is no occlusal caries, limited to a narrow contact and a ridge without occlusal contact; walls converge occlusally; grooves mandatory, 0.5 mm deep at the gingival point angle (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Garg and Garg add attrited teeth and give locks 0.5 mm gingivally and 0.3 mm occlusally (Garg and Garg, 2011) [SRC]. Summitt's: bur "falls" into caries, an enamel shell protects the neighbour, width about 1.5 mm, opposing grooves at least 0.5 mm deep are the only retention against proximal displacement (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC]. For composite: instrument parallel to the crown long axis, "No bevelling or secondary retention" (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; axial depth 0.2 mm inside the DEJ (Singh and Rajkumar, 2020) [SRC]; for glass ionomer a small box limited to the demineralised enamel, used when less than 2.0 mm of ridge remains, often keeping contact, without retention design (Mount and Ngo, 2016) [SRC]; the amalgam slot design is used for glass ionomer (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; fissures touching the outline are sealed (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC]. Facial slot: root caries gingival to the contact; amalgam if isolation is difficult; 0.75-1 mm gingivally, 1-1.25 mm occlusally; grooves at occlusoaxial and gingivoaxial line angles (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]; at least 2 mm enamel below the ridge (Torres and Mehta, 2020; Singh and Rajkumar, 2020) [SRC]; also when the adjacent tooth is missing (Garg and Garg, 2011) [SRC]. Evidence: slot may reduce fracture severity (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013); in vitro 18.09 kg versus 7.12 kg (Torres and Mehta, 2020); slot composites 70% at up to 10 years (Hilton and Broome, 2013) [SRC].

**Disagreement:** groove depth for amalgam box-only: 0.5 mm at the gingival point angle (Ritter et al, 2019), at least 0.5 mm full length (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013), 0.5 mm tapering to 0.3 mm (Garg and Garg, 2011).

## ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** A Class II preparation confined to the proximal surface, without an occlusal step. Through the marginal ridge it is a box-only or vertical slot; from the facial or lingual it is a horizontal slot (Torres and Mehta, 2020).

**1. Box-only (vertical slot)**
*Indications:* proximal lesion with no occlusal caries; narrow contact; marginal ridge without occlusal contact (Ritter et al, 2019); attrited teeth (Garg and Garg, 2011).

| Feature | Amalgam | Composite / GIC |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Through ridge until bur "falls" into caries; leave enamel shell | Pear or round bur parallel to long axis |
| Walls | 90°, converge occlusally, width about 1.5 mm | Dictated by lesion; axial 0.2 mm inside DEJ |
| Retention | Opposing grooves, at least 0.5 mm deep (mandatory) | None; no bevel (Ritter et al, 2019) |

Sources: Overton, Summitt and Osborne (2013); Singh and Rajkumar (2020); Mount and Ngo (2016) for GIC.

**2. Facial or lingual (horizontal) slot**
*Indications:* proximal root caries below the contact with recession; rotated teeth; adjacent tooth missing; at least 2 mm intact enamel below the ridge (Ritter et al, 2019; Torres and Mehta, 2020; Garg and Garg, 2011).
*Design:* depth 0.75-1 mm gingivally, 1-1.25 mm occlusally; 90° margins; grooves at occlusoaxial and gingivoaxial line angles; amalgam when isolation is poor (Ritter et al, 2019).

**3. Glass-ionomer slot:** when less than 2.0 mm of marginal ridge remains or the ridge is cracked, a small box limited to the cavitated enamel is cut through the ridge; contact with the adjacent tooth is kept where possible and no retention is needed (Mount and Ngo, 2016). The same slot design as for amalgam is used for glass ionomer (Ritter et al, 2019).

**Precautions:** protect the adjacent tooth (enamel shell, matrix strip); seal the uncut fissures (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013).

**Advantages:** marginal ridge and occlusal enamel kept; less fracture risk than extending through grooves (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013).

**Evidence:** in vitro, slot with locks resisted 18.09 kg versus 7.12 kg for occlusal dovetail (Torres and Mehta, 2020); slot amalgam over 58 years in service; slot composites 70% success up to 10 years (Hilton and Broome, 2013).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Amalgam box: grooves mandatory; composite box: no grooves, no bevel.
- Horizontal slot needs 2 mm enamel below the ridge.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

- Putting grooves in the axial wall.
