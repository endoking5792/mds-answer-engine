# Slot and box-only preparations

**Marks:** 10 · Predicted short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Definition → box-only preparation → facial/lingual slot → glass-ionomer modification → advantages and precautions.

## Definition

A **slot preparation** is a proximal-only Class II design without an occlusal step. Marginal-ridge access is a **vertical slot/box-only**; facial or lingual access is a **horizontal slot** (Torres and Mehta, 2020; Overton et al., 2013).

## 1. Box-only (vertical slot)

**Indications:** Proximal caries without occlusal involvement, narrow contact, or a marginal ridge without occlusal contact (Ritter et al., 2019).

**Design**
- Enter through the marginal ridge and extend gingivally to the lesion.
- Protect the adjacent tooth with a matrix or enamel shell.
- **Amalgam:** occlusally converging walls and opposing retention grooves. Reported groove depths vary from 0.3–0.5 mm (Ritter et al., 2019; Overton et al., 2013; Garg and Garg, 2011).
- **Composite:** conservative, lesion-oriented preparation; keep the instrument parallel to the crown's long axis. Routine grooves/bevels are unnecessary (Ritter et al., 2019; Singh and Rajkumar, 2020).

| Feature | Amalgam | Composite |
|---|---|---|
| Retention | Opposing grooves | Adhesive bonding |
| Walls | Slight occlusal convergence | Lesion-oriented |
| Bevel | Not routine | Not routine |

## 2. Facial or lingual (horizontal) slot

**Indications:** Root caries below the contact, recession, rotated teeth or a missing adjacent tooth; about 2 mm intact enamel should remain below the ridge (Torres and Mehta, 2020; Singh and Rajkumar, 2020).

**Design:** Access facially or lingually, preserving the ridge. Depth: 0.75–1 mm gingivally and 1–1.25 mm occlusally. For amalgam, add grooves at the occlusoaxial and gingivoaxial angles; useful when isolation is difficult (Ritter et al., 2019).

## 3. Glass-ionomer slot

For a cracked ridge or less than 2 mm remaining occlusogingivally, prepare a small box limited to demineralised enamel. Preserve contact where possible; mechanical retention is generally unnecessary (Mount and Ngo, 2016).

## Advantages and precautions

- Preserves occlusal enamel and marginal ridge.
- Avoids unnecessary extension through occlusal grooves and may reduce fracture severity compared with conventional Class II preparations (Overton et al., 2013).
- Protect the adjacent tooth and preserve supporting enamel and dentin.
- Seal non-carious fissures rather than extending them routinely.
- Select retention according to the restorative material.
