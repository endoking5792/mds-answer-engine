## 1. Principles common to all Class I amalgam designs

### Explanation

Sturdevant's conservative Class I: faciolingual width of no more than 1-1.5 mm and depth 1.5-2 mm considered ideal (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC] (as printed; the width figure reads as a minimal-isthmus target, not a ceiling for carious extension); relatively flat pulpal floor in sound tooth (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]; primary retention from slight occlusal convergence of opposing walls (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC], produced by the No. 245 bur (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]. Marginal ridge preserved at about 1.6 mm in premolars (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC] and 2 mm in molars (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]; Harpreet Singh agrees (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; when extending into the ridge the bur is tilted up to 10 degrees so the wall diverges (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]. Isthmus just wider than the bur (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC], since narrow isthmuses resist fracture (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]; Summitt's: wider amalgams make the tooth more fracture-prone (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC]. Butt joint: Marzouk right-angled (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; occlusal bevel contraindicated (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]; amalgam chips if its margin angle is under 80 degrees (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]. Thickness at least 1.5 and preferably 2.0 mm (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC]. Usually no secondary features are needed in Class I (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC].

**Disagreements.**
- *Flat floor:* Harpreet Singh and Sturdevant's want a flat floor; Summitt's says dentin is not removed simply to create a flat pulpal floor (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC].
- *Wall orientation:* converging (Sturdevant's, Harpreet Singh) versus parallel or slightly converging (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC].
- *Dovetails:* Harpreet Singh gives dovetails at mesial and distal ends of Class I for retention (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; Sturdevant's relies on convergence alone.
- *Fissures:* Harpreet Singh says all grooves and fissures must be included (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; Summitt's says routine extension through fissures not known to be carious cannot be justified (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC] and such fissures are sealed (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC].
- *Width:* quarter intercuspal distance does not weaken the tooth (Singh, 2020) [SRC] versus Sturdevant's "just wider than the bur".

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Class I preparations involve pits and fissures of occlusal surfaces of posterior teeth, the occlusal two-thirds of facial and lingual surfaces of molars, and lingual pits of maxillary incisors (Marzouk et al, undated; Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019). Design depends on caries extent, caries risk and tooth morphology.

**Common features** (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019; Singh, 2020; Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013)
- Depth 1.5-2 mm, pulpal floor just into dentin; amalgam at least 1.5 mm (preferably 2 mm) thick.
- Narrow isthmus (just wider than a No. 245 bur; at most one-fourth intercuspal distance).
- Walls converge occlusally (primary retention); rounded internal angles.
- Marginal ridge thickness ≥1.6 mm (premolars), ≥2 mm (molars); diverge mesial/distal walls when close to the ridge.
- 90° cavosurface (butt joint), no bevel; amalgam margin ≥80°.
- Gently curving outline; join defects <0.5 mm apart.

## 2. Conventional versus conservative designs

### Explanation

Marzouk's Design 1 (low caries risk): preparation width not over one-fourth intercuspal distance (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC], good hygiene (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. Design 2: wider, applying extension for prevention for high plaque and caries indices (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]. Marzouk supports extension for prevention (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; Sturdevant's says it is virtually eliminated (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]. Shapes: upper premolar dumbbell (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC] or butterfly (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; lower premolar pit-only "snake eyes" (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; upper molar mesial kidney shape when the oblique ridge is intact (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; H-shaped (Singh, 2020) [SRC], key-shaped mandibular first molar (Singh, 2020) [SRC], plus-shaped mandibular second molar (Singh, 2020) [SRC]. Summitt's links survival inversely to unsupported enamel (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC].

Fissurotomy burs are described by Garg and Garg as ultraconservative instruments (Garg and Garg, 2011) [SRC] for small caries and enlarging fissures (Garg and Garg, 2011) [SRC], used with suitable restorative materials (Garg and Garg, 2011) [SRC]; none of the books read describes a fissurotomy design for amalgam, so it belongs to sealant/composite, not to amalgam design.

#### ✎ Exam core

| | Conventional (Black; Marzouk Design 2) | Conservative (Marzouk Design 1; Sturdevant's; Summitt's) |
|---|---|---|
| Philosophy | Extension for prevention: all pits and fissures included to self-cleansing areas | Remove only caries and undermined enamel; seal non-carious fissures |
| Width | Wider (more than one-fourth intercuspal distance) | Just wider than the bur; ≤ one-fourth intercuspal distance |
| Indication | High caries risk, deep dentinal caries (Marzouk) | Low caries risk, good hygiene; now routine |
| Floor | Flat, uniform | Flat at initial depth; deeper only where caries extends |
| Result | Stronger seal of fissures, weaker cusps | Conserves cusps and marginal ridges |

**Outline shapes:** maxillary premolar dumbbell/butterfly; mandibular premolar circular ("snake-eye") if pit only; maxillary molar mesial C or kidney and distal I or heart shape with oblique ridge intact, H if joined; mandibular first molar key-shaped; mandibular second molar plus-shaped (Marzouk et al, undated; Singh, 2020).

Fissurotomy burs are used for ultraconservative opening of fissures with bonded materials, not as an amalgam design (Garg and Garg, 2011).

## 3. Tooth-specific designs and extensions

### Explanation

**Maxillary first molar, oblique ridge.** Separate mesial and distal preparations; join only if less than 0.5 mm of sound ridge remains (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; mesial C and distal I with ridge intact (Singh, 2020) [SRC]. Occlusolingual (distal oblique and lingual fissure): lingual extension not over 1 mm mesiodistally (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC], at the expense of the oblique ridge rather than centred over the fissure (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC], bur positioned 70:30 mesial to the pit (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC], distopulpal line angle not closer than 2 mm to the distal surface (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC], lingual axial wall 0.5 mm inside the DEJ (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC], and grooves if the walls diverge lingually (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC].

Not from locators: the exact direction of the premolar bur tilt is shown only in a Sturdevant's figure caption (bisector of the long axis and the perpendicular to the cusp-tip plane), not used as a locator.

**Mandibular first premolar** is in Sturdevant's list of other Class I amalgam preparations (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]. The bur is tilted (Singh, 2020) [SRC] so the preparation is at the expense of the facial cusp (Singh, 2020) [SRC], avoiding the large facial pulp horn (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; outline butterfly or Y (Singh, 2020) [SRC].

**Facial pit of mandibular molar** (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]: No. 245 perpendicular to the surface (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; Marzouk's Design 3 covers isolated facial/lingual pits and lingual pits of upper incisors (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; Design 4 when facial or lingual pits connect to the occlusal (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; Design 5 when cusps are undermined (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; Design 7 uses pins or posts (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC]; Design 8 is for endodontically treated teeth (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC].

Conservative technique: the bur is placed with its distal aspect over the distal pit (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC] and an initial depth of 1.5 mm is established (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]. Marzouk's Design 6 (cusp or marginal-ridge table) is for portions of or an entire cusp undermined or badly thinned by decay (Marzouk et al, undated) [SRC].

**Enameloplasty**: indicated when the fissure is no deeper than one-quarter to one-third of enamel (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]; Harpreet Singh: one-third or less (Singh, 2020) [SRC], without extending outline (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; cavosurface angle not over about 100 degrees (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]; not at occlusal contacts (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC].

**Extensive lesions**: less than 1 mm to pulp (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC]; cusp capping at half to two-thirds (Boushell and Walter, 2019) [SRC]; mandatory beyond two-thirds (Singh, 2020) [SRC]; Summitt's evaluates cusps beyond one-third intercuspal width (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013) [SRC]. Bonded amalgam for large Class I: benefits not substantiated (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Tooth-specific designs**
1. **Maxillary first molar (oblique ridge):** keep the ridge; separate mesial (C/kidney) and distal (I/heart) preparations; join (H) only if <0.5 mm of sound ridge remains or the ridge is carious. Occlusolingual extension for a distal oblique-lingual fissure: lingual extension ≤1 mm wide, cut at the expense of the oblique ridge (70:30 mesial), distopulpal angle ≥2 mm from distal surface, lingual axial wall 0.5 mm inside DEJ, grooves if walls diverge (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019; Singh, 2020).
2. **Mandibular first premolar:** small lingual cusp, large facial pulp horn; bur tilted (not parallel to the long axis) so the floor is cut at the expense of the facial cusp, keeping dentin over the pulp horn and supporting the lingual cusp; outline circular, butterfly or Y (Singh, 2020).
3. **Facial pit of mandibular molar / lingual pit of maxillary lateral incisor:** bur perpendicular to the surface, depth cut about 1.5 mm, small undercuts with No. ¼ bur if needed (Singh, 2020; Marzouk Design 3).
4. **Occlusofacial/occlusolingual (Marzouk Design 4):** pit connected to occlusal fissure; groove extension.
5. **Larger designs (Marzouk 5-8):** undermined facial/lingual cusps replaced; cusp tables; pins/posts; endodontic access designs.

**Marzouk's classification of Class I amalgam designs** (Marzouk et al, undated)

| Design | Location / indication |
|---|---|
| 1 | Occlusal; shallow caries, width ≤ one-fourth intercuspal distance, low caries risk |
| 2 | Occlusal; deeper caries, extension for prevention in high caries risk, cusps intact |
| 3 | Isolated facial/lingual pits of molars, lingual pits of maxillary incisors |
| 4 | Occlusal plus connected facial/lingual groove |
| 5 | Occlusal plus most of facial/lingual surface; undermined cusps replaced |
| 6 | Cusp or marginal-ridge "table" replacing part or all of a cusp |
| 7 | Extensive lesions retained by pins/posts |
| 8 | Endodontically treated teeth (access cavity as preparation) |

**Steps of the conservative preparation** (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019): punch cut with No. 245 bur parallel to the long axis into the most carious (usually distal) pit, to 1.5 mm; extend along fissures at constant depth following the DEJ; tilt the bur at the marginal ridges if needed; extend facially and lingually only to remove undermined enamel; enameloplasty of shallow fissure ends; then remove remaining caries, protect the pulp, finish walls and debride.

**Enameloplasty:** remove a shallow fissure (≤ one-quarter to one-third enamel depth) with a fine diamond, leaving a smooth saucer; no amalgam in the area; cavosurface ≤100°; not at occlusal contact (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019; Singh, 2020).

**Extensive Class I:** remove deep caries early; cap cusps when extension exceeds half (consider) or two-thirds (usually) of groove-to-cusp distance (Boushell and Walter, 2019); Summitt's evaluates cusps once width exceeds one-third of the intercuspal distance (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

**Claim: all pits and fissures must be included in a Class I amalgam (extension for prevention).**
- Textbook says: Harpreet Singh and Marzouk include all fissures.
- Other evidence: Summitt's cites strong evidence that sealed residual fissures do not progress and judges routine extension unjustified; Sturdevant's says extension for prevention has virtually been eliminated.
- Strength: moderate (sealant and sealed-restoration clinical studies cited by Summitt's).
- Conclusion: extend only to remove caries; seal or enameloplasty the rest. [Likely]
- Clinical implication: narrower restorations conserve cusps, which Summitt's links to lower fracture risk.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS

1. Depth 1.5-2 mm; isthmus just wider than bur, ≤1/4 intercuspal distance.
2. Marginal ridge ≥1.6 mm premolar, ≥2 mm molar.
3. Walls converge occlusally; 90° butt joint; no bevel.
4. Oblique ridge preserved unless <0.5 mm or carious.
5. Mandibular first premolar: tilt bur, cut at expense of facial cusp.
6. Enameloplasty: fissure ≤ 1/4-1/3 enamel thickness.
7. Seal non-carious fissures instead of extending.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

1. Crossing the oblique ridge routinely.
2. Bevelling the occlusal margin of amalgam.
3. Drawing a wide isthmus.
4. Presenting extension for prevention as current standard.
5. Forgetting the bur tilt in the mandibular first premolar.
