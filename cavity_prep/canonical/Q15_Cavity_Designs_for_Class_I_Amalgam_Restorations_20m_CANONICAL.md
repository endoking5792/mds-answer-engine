# Discuss various cavity designs for class I amalgam restorations.

**Marks:** 20 (assumed; original paper unmarked) · Essay

**Answer blueprint:** Introduction → principles common to Class I amalgam designs → conventional versus conservative designs → tooth-specific designs and preparation sequence → conclusion.

## 1. Principles common to all Class I amalgam designs

Class I preparations involve pits and fissures of occlusal surfaces of posterior teeth, the occlusal two-thirds of facial and lingual surfaces of molars, and lingual pits of maxillary incisors (Marzouk et al., n.d.; Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019). Design depends on caries extent, caries risk and tooth morphology.

**Common features** (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019; Singh, 2020; Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013)
- Depth 1.5-2 mm, pulpal floor just into dentin; amalgam at least 1.5 mm (preferably 2 mm) thick.
- Narrow isthmus (just wider than a No. 245 bur; at most one-fourth intercuspal distance).
- Walls converge occlusally (primary retention); rounded internal angles.
- Marginal ridge thickness ≥1.6 mm (premolars), ≥2 mm (molars); diverge mesial/distal walls when close to the ridge.
- 90° cavosurface (butt joint), no bevel; amalgam margin ≥80°.
- Gently curving outline; join defects <0.5 mm apart.

## 2. Conventional versus conservative designs

| | Conventional (Black; Marzouk Design 2) | Conservative (Marzouk Design 1; Sturdevant's; Summitt's) |
|---|---|---|
| Philosophy | Extension for prevention: all pits and fissures included to self-cleansing areas | Remove only caries and undermined enamel; seal non-carious fissures |
| Width | Wider (more than one-fourth intercuspal distance) | Just wider than the bur; ≤ one-fourth intercuspal distance |
| Indication | High caries risk, deep dentinal caries (Marzouk) | Low caries risk, good hygiene; now routine |
| Floor | Flat, uniform | Flat at initial depth; deeper only where caries extends |
| Result | Stronger seal of fissures, weaker cusps | Conserves cusps and marginal ridges |

**Outline shapes:** maxillary premolar dumbbell/butterfly; mandibular premolar circular ("snake-eye") if pit only; maxillary molar mesial C or kidney and distal I or heart shape with oblique ridge intact, H if joined; mandibular first molar key-shaped; mandibular second molar plus-shaped (Marzouk et al., n.d.; Singh, 2020).

Fissurotomy burs are used for ultraconservative opening of fissures with bonded materials, not as an amalgam design (Garg and Garg, 2011).

## 3. Tooth-specific designs and extensions

**Tooth-specific designs**
1. **Maxillary first molar (oblique ridge):** keep the ridge; separate mesial (C/kidney) and distal (I/heart) preparations; join (H) only if <0.5 mm of sound ridge remains or the ridge is carious. Occlusolingual extension for a distal oblique-lingual fissure: lingual extension ≤1 mm wide, cut at the expense of the oblique ridge (70:30 mesial), distopulpal angle ≥2 mm from distal surface, lingual axial wall 0.5 mm inside DEJ, grooves if walls diverge (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019; Singh, 2020).
2. **Mandibular first premolar:** small lingual cusp, large facial pulp horn; bur tilted (not parallel to the long axis) so the floor is cut at the expense of the facial cusp, keeping dentin over the pulp horn and supporting the lingual cusp; outline circular, butterfly or Y (Singh, 2020).
3. **Facial pit of mandibular molar / lingual pit of maxillary lateral incisor:** bur perpendicular to the surface, depth cut about 1.5 mm, small undercuts with No. ¼ bur if needed (Singh, 2020; Marzouk Design 3).
4. **Occlusofacial/occlusolingual (Marzouk Design 4):** pit connected to occlusal fissure; groove extension.
5. **Larger designs (Marzouk 5-8):** undermined facial/lingual cusps replaced; cusp tables; pins/posts; endodontic access designs.

**Marzouk's classification of Class I amalgam designs** (Marzouk et al., n.d.)

| Design | Location / indication |
|---|---|
| 1 | Occlusal; shallow caries, width ≤ one-fourth intercuspal distance, low caries risk |
| 2 | Occlusal; deeper caries, extension for prevention in high caries risk, cusps intact |
| 3 | Isolated facial/lingual pits of molars, lingual pits of maxillary incisors |
| 4 | Occlusal plus connected facial/lingual groove |
| 5 | Occlusal plus most of facial/lingual surface; undermined cusps replaced |
| 6 | Cusp or marginal-ridge "table" replacing part or all of a cusp |
| 7 | Extensive lesions retained by pins/posts |
| 8 | Endodontically treated teeth (access cavity as preparation) |

**Steps of the conservative preparation** (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019): punch cut with No. 245 bur parallel to the long axis into the most carious (usually distal) pit, to 1.5 mm; extend along fissures at constant depth following the DEJ; tilt the bur at the marginal ridges if needed; extend facially and lingually only to remove undermined enamel; enameloplasty of shallow fissure ends; then remove remaining caries, protect the pulp, finish walls and debride.

**Enameloplasty:** remove a shallow fissure (≤ one-quarter to one-third enamel depth) with a fine diamond, leaving a smooth saucer; no amalgam in the area; cavosurface ≤100°; not at occlusal contact (Ahmed, Boushell and Wilder, 2019; Singh, 2020).

**Extensive Class I:** remove deep caries early; cap cusps when extension exceeds half (consider) or two-thirds (usually) of groove-to-cusp distance (Boushell and Walter, 2019); Summitt's evaluates cusps once width exceeds one-third of the intercuspal distance (Overton, Summitt and Osborne, 2013).

**Conclusion.** Class I amalgam design should preserve cusps and marginal ridges while providing the bulk, resistance and mechanical retention required by the extent of the lesion and the tooth's anatomy.
