## STUDY MODE

### 1. What a biofilm is, and why the community matters
A biofilm is a sessile multicellular community whose cells are firmly attached to a surface and enmeshed in a self-produced matrix of extracellular polymeric substance (EPS), mostly polysaccharide but also proteins and nucleic acids (Berman et al, 2021; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). Grossman's phrasing is "a community of microcolonies … surrounded by a matrix made of glycocalyx, which also attaches the bacterial cells to a solid substratum" (Gopikrishna, 2021). The ability to form biofilms is itself regarded as a virulence factor, and biofilm infections are estimated to account for 65–80% of bacterial infections in the developed world (Berman et al, 2021). In endodontics the important idea is the *community-as-pathogen* concept: the composition, abundance, interactions (synergism) and virulence factors of the whole community together define its pathogenicity, and the whole is more than the sum of its parts (Berman et al, 2021). [Certain]

### 2. Ultrastructure
- **Proportions.** Microcolonies make up about 15% of volume and the EPS matrix about 85%, with water channels between the microcolonies; dental biofilms can reach 300 or more cell layers (Berman et al, 2021; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019; Siqueira and Rôças, 2022). Grossman gives the chemical makeup of the matrix: protein <1–2%, polysaccharide 1–2%, DNA <1%, RNA <1%, and water up to 97% (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Microcolonies** can be single-species but are more often mixed, and are not randomly placed: populations are positioned for metabolic interaction (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- **Water channels** carry nutrients, wastes and signalling molecules (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).
- **EPS functions** (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022): adhesion ("biologic glue"), mechanical stability, accumulation of extracellular enzymes, keeping cells close, nutrient reserve in starvation, water retention, protection from phagocytes and host molecules, and protection from antimicrobials.
- **Heterogeneity.** Gradients of oxygen, pH, nutrients and cell density create microenvironments, so cells at the base may be dead or lysing while cells nearer the surface grow actively (Berman et al, 2021; Chávez de Paz, 2015).
- **Shape caveat.** Tower or mushroom microcolonies are described under low shear forces, which "do not normally occur in root canals"; the main structural and physiological features of endodontic biofilms "remain to be determined" (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022). So the classical mushroom drawing is borrowed from non-endodontic biofilms. [Certain]
- **Gene expression.** Cells in biofilms express 20–70% different genes from planktonic cells of the same species (Berman et al, 2021).

### 3. How a biofilm forms
Two parts (Chávez de Paz, 2015): (a) initial interaction, where a clean surface is coated by a *conditioning film* (water, salts, albumin, fibronectin), a planktonic cell makes weak reversible contact, then irreversible adhesion through pili, flagella and exopolymers, then co-adhesion of other species; (b) growth and development: monolayer, double layer, microcolonies, vertical expansion, maturation, with some cells detaching. Grossman condenses this to four stages: conditioning layer, planktonic cell attachment, detachment (seeding dispersal), growth and expansion (Gopikrishna, 2021).
Endodontic twist: in canals the biofilm usually does not start from saliva-borne cells. As the caries biofilm reaches the pulp and the pulp becomes necrotic, the biofilm advances apically, so canal-wall biofilm forms gradually behind the infection front (Berman et al, 2021; Svensäter and Bergenholtz hypothesis as described by Chávez de Paz, 2015).

### 4. Categories of endodontic biofilm
Grossman (Gopikrishna, 2021) lists four:
1. **Intracanal** biofilm on radicular dentine; E. faecalis forms one of the most therapy-resistant and prevalent examples.
2. **Extraradicular** biofilm on the cemental surface around the apex.
3. **Periapical** biofilm, independent of canal surfaces; Actinomyces and Propionibacterium propionicum form periapical lesions resistant to endodontic therapy.
4. **Biomaterial-centred** (foreign-body) biofilm on an artificial surface.

Prevalence (Ricucci and Siqueira, described in Berman et al, 2021): intraradicular biofilms in the apical segment of about 80% of canals in primary or post-treatment apical periodontitis; extraradicular biofilms in only 6%, nearly always with an intraradicular biofilm and with symptoms. Biofilms were more frequent with large lesions and cysts (cysts 95%, abscesses 83%, granulomas 69.5%). Flocs (aggregates in the lumen) are sometimes called "planktonic biofilms" (Berman et al, 2021). [Certain]

### 5. Mechanisms of antimicrobial resistance
Magnitude: antibiotic concentrations to kill biofilm bacteria are ~100–1000 times those for the same species in suspension (Berman et al, 2021; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).
There is "no generally agreed upon specific mechanism" (Chávez de Paz, 2015), and the three textbooks group them differently:

| Source | Grouping |
|---|---|
| Cohen (Berman et al, 2021) | (a) restricted penetration; (b) starved, stationary-phase cells less susceptible; (c) persister cells |
| Chávez de Paz (2015) | Physical (matrix barrier) + acquired: low-metabolism cells, stress-responding cells, persisters |
| Grossman (Gopikrishna, 2021) | (i) slow penetration; (ii) enhanced tolerance (cell density/quorum sensing, persisters); (iii) arrangement of cells (outer cells shield inner); (iv) reduced growth rate |

Endodontic detail from Chávez de Paz (2015): E. faecalis survives starvation through the stringent response ((p)ppGpp), which also affects its biofilm formation; dormant biofilm cells did not reactivate even after 96 h in fresh nutrients, whereas planktonic cells recovered in 2 h; root-canal isolates survived alkaline stress better as biofilms than as planktonic cultures; a persister population could explain survival through a calcium hydroxide dressing (that source's hypothesis). Matrix slows diffusion (an observed delay in penicillin delivery through a beta-lactamase-positive biofilm). [Likely]

### 6. Quorum sensing
Cell-to-cell communication through released chemical signals (autoinducers). As the population multiplies, the extracellular concentration rises; at a threshold ("quorum") the population changes gene expression together (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022). It regulates virulence, secondary metabolite production and biofilm formation. Pathogens may delay virulence factors until numbers are high enough to avoid alerting host defences (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022; Fouad, 2017).
Signals (Fouad, 2017): Gram-negative bacteria use AI-1, acyl homoserine lactones (LuxI/LuxR), mostly species-specific; Gram-positive bacteria use post-translationally modified peptides, for example competence-stimulating peptide (CSP, ComC → ComD/ComE) in streptococci, linked to DNA uptake; AI-2 (LuxS pathway) is a cross-species signal. A luxS mutant of S. oralis failed to form the mixed biofilm with A. naeslundii, and quorum-sensing inhibitors have potential as antibiofilm agents (Fouad, 2017). Disrupting quorum-sensing systems is also listed among the effects of community disruption by treatment (Berman et al, 2021; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). [Certain]

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS (compressed, one claim)
**Claim — apical periodontitis is a biofilm-related disease.** The textbooks agree and the supporting evidence is the histobacteriologic study of Ricucci and Siqueira (about 80% of canals with apical biofilm; cysts 95%) and a six-criterion framework in which apical periodontitis meets four (adherent bacteria, encased microcolonies, site-confined infection, host cells facing the biofilm) (Berman et al, 2021; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019). The fourth, failure of antibiotics despite susceptible planktonic cells, is argued from the avascular necrotic canal rather than shown in the study; the sixth (remission when the biofilm is disrupted) is supported only indirectly (successful teeth show no canal biofilm; negative cultures at filling relate to better outcome). Associations in descriptive specimen studies do not by themselves prove cause, and in roughly one canal in five no apical biofilm was found. Clinical implication: disinfection should target the surface-attached community, not only free-floating bacteria. [Likely]

### 1. Definition
A biofilm is a sessile multicellular microbial community whose cells are firmly attached to a surface and enmeshed in a self-produced matrix of extracellular polymeric substance (EPS), mostly polysaccharide with proteins and nucleic acids (Berman et al, 2021). Grossman defines it as a community of microcolonies surrounded by a glycocalyx matrix that attaches the cells to a solid substratum (Gopikrishna, 2021). Infected root canals are multispecies biofilms, and the community, not a single species, is the unit of pathogenicity: composition, abundance, synergy and virulence factors act together, so the whole is more than the sum of its parts (Berman et al, 2021).

### 2. Characteristics
- **Altered phenotype:** cells in a biofilm express 20–70% different genes from planktonic cells of the same species (Berman et al, 2021).
- **Organised community:** populations are spatially arranged for metabolic interaction, with food chains, local environment modification, collective protection, coaggregation, cell-cell signalling and horizontal gene transfer (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).
- **Heterogeneity:** gradients of oxygen, pH, nutrients and cell density create microenvironments; cells at the base may be dead or lysing while surface cells grow actively (Chávez de Paz, 2015).
- **Resistance:** antibiotic concentrations needed to kill biofilm bacteria are about 100–1000 times those for planktonic cells (Berman et al, 2021).
- **Disease behaviour:** biofilms provoke chronic, persistent inflammation proportional to cell density and species composition, and release planktonic cells that can start acute flare-ups (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- **Advantages to bacteria:** wider habitat range, greater metabolic diversity, protection from competitors, host defences and antimicrobials, facilitated genetic exchange and enhanced pathogenicity (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

### 3. Ultrastructure
- Microcolonies make up about 15% and the EPS matrix about 85% of volume; the microcolonies are separated by **water channels**; dental biofilms may reach 300 or more cell layers (Berman et al, 2021; Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).
- Matrix composition: polysaccharide, protein, DNA, RNA and lipid, with water up to 97% of the biofilm (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Water channels circulate fluid carrying nutrients, wastes and signalling molecules (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).
- EPS functions: adhesion ("biologic glue"), mechanical stability, store of enzymes and nutrients, water retention, protection from phagocytes and from antimicrobials (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- Caution: tower or mushroom microcolonies need shear forces that do not normally occur in canals, so the endodontic structure is still not fully described (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- **Draw:** tooth wall, conditioning film, microcolonies inside matrix, water channels, detaching planktonic cells.

### 4. Formation
Conditioning film (water, salts, albumin, fibronectin) forms on the surface; a planktonic cell makes weak reversible contact; adhesion becomes irreversible through pili, flagella and sticky exopolymers; other species co-adhere; the biofilm grows, matures and sheds cells (Chávez de Paz, 2015). Grossman lists four stages: conditioning layer, planktonic attachment, detachment (seeding dispersal), and growth and expansion (Gopikrishna, 2021). In canals the biofilm builds gradually behind the infection front as the pulp becomes necrotic (Berman et al, 2021).

### 5. Categories of endodontic biofilm (Gopikrishna, 2021)
1. **Intracanal:** on radicular dentine; E. faecalis forms one of the most therapy-resistant and prevalent examples.
2. **Extraradicular:** on the cemental surface around the apex; found in only about 6% of cases, nearly always with an intraradicular biofilm and with symptoms (Berman et al, 2021).
3. **Periapical:** independent of canal surfaces; Actinomyces and Propionibacterium propionicum form lesions resistant to endodontic therapy.
4. **Biomaterial-centred:** on an artificial surface (foreign-body biofilm).

Intraradicular biofilm is found in the apical segment of about 80% of canals with apical periodontitis, more often in large lesions and cysts (Berman et al, 2021).

### 6. Mechanisms of antimicrobial resistance
1. **Slow penetration:** the EPS matrix retards diffusion of agents (Gopikrishna, 2021).
2. **Slow growth and dormancy:** starved, stationary-phase cells are less susceptible; dormancy is likely in nutrient-poor treated canals, and dormant biofilm cells reactivated slowly (Berman et al, 2021; Chávez de Paz, 2015).
3. **Persister cells:** a survivor subpopulation that restarts growth once the agent is gone.
4. **Phenotypic subpopulations:** gradients produce low-metabolism and stress-responding cells (Chávez de Paz, 2015).
5. **Arrangement of cells:** outer cells shield inner cells from agents acting on the periphery (Gopikrishna, 2021).
6. **Density and quorum sensing:** enhanced tolerance at high cell density.
7. **Starvation response:** in E. faecalis the (p)ppGpp stringent response supports survival and biofilm formation (Chávez de Paz, 2015).

### 7. Quorum sensing
Quorum sensing is cell-to-cell communication through released chemical signals called **autoinducers**. As the population grows the extracellular concentration rises, and at a threshold (the quorum) the population changes gene expression together (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- Gram-negative bacteria: acyl homoserine lactones (AI-1, LuxI/LuxR), mostly species-specific.
- Gram-positive bacteria: modified peptides, for example competence-stimulating peptide in streptococci, linked to DNA uptake.
- AI-2 (LuxS pathway): a cross-species signal (Fouad, 2017).
- It regulates virulence, secondary metabolites and **biofilm formation**; pathogens may delay virulence factors until numbers are high enough to avoid alerting the host (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- Quorum-sensing inhibitors are potential antibiofilm agents (Fouad, 2017).

### 8. Clinical significance
Biofilms sustain primary and post-treatment apical periodontitis. Systemic antibiotics fail because the drug does not reach bacteria in the avascular necrotic space and biofilm cells are tolerant (Berman et al, 2021). Mechanical instrumentation with irrigation remains the main antibiofilm measure (Chávez de Paz, 2015); eradication is covered in a separate answer.
