### 1. Introduction
Endodontic microbiota (microflora) are the microorganisms in the root canal system and periradicular tissues. Infection develops in a previously sterile site after pulp necrosis from caries, trauma or leakage, so any species found may be a pathogen or at least part of the community's ecology; studies are cross-sectional and show association only (Berman et al, 2021). Bacteria enter through dentinal tubules, traumatic exposure, coronal leakage, resorption, periodontal tissues and anachoresis (Gopikrishna, 2021).

### 2. Classification
**A. By location and time of entry**

```
Endodontic infections
├── Intraradicular
│   ├── Primary    – first invaders of the necrotic pulp
│   ├── Secondary  – introduced after professional intervention
│   └── Persistent – survived treatment and starvation
└── Extraradicular – dependent on / independent of the intraradicular infection
```
(Berman et al, 2021). Persistent and secondary infections are mostly clinically indistinguishable.

**B. By phylum:** Firmicutes, Bacteroidetes, Spirochaetes, Fusobacteria, Actinobacteria, Proteobacteria, Synergistetes, TM7 and SR1; sequencing also finds low-abundance Tenericutes, Acidobacteria and others. Fungi and archaea are occasional (Berman et al, 2021).
**C. By Gram stain and oxygen need:** Gram-positive and Gram-negative cocci and rods, each as obligate or facultative anaerobes (Gopikrishna, 2021).
**D. Taxonomy:** black-pigmented Bacteroides are now Prevotella (saccharolytic) and Porphyromonas (asaccharolytic) (Gopikrishna, 2021).

### 3. Microbiota by type of infection
| Type | Features | Typical organisms |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Mixed, anaerobe-dominated; 10³–10⁸ cells; mean 10–20 species | Fusobacterium, Prevotella, Porphyromonas, Tannerella, Treponema, Dialister, Parvimonas, Filifactor, Streptococcus |
| Secondary | From intervention or leakage | Pseudomonas, Staphylococcus, E. coli, Candida, E. faecalis |
| Persistent | Fewer species (1–5 if well treated); Gram-positive | Streptococci, E. faecalis, Actinomyces, lactobacilli, Olsenella; Candida in up to 18% |
| Extraradicular | Periradicular invasion; abscess | Actinomyces, Propionibacterium propionicum |

- Lesion size rises with richness and load: about 12 taxa in lesions under 5 mm, about 16 in 5–10 mm, about 20 over 10 mm (Berman et al, 2021).
- Early infection is facultative-dominated; as oxygen falls and tissue-fluid proteins become the main nutrient, obligate anaerobes replace them (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- E. faecalis, Candida, Pseudomonas, Staphylococcus and enteric rods are not normal in primary infection, so their presence suggests secondary infection (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

### 4. Molecular methods of identifying (culturing-independent) the microflora
Culture cannot grow many species, so the root canal microflora is studied with molecular methods. The target is the 16S rRNA gene (conserved regions for broad-range primers, variable regions for species-specific primers and probes); a 98.5–99% identity to a database sequence is the usual species criterion (Fouad, 2017).
- **PCR and derivatives:** species-specific, nested, multiplex, touchdown, real-time (quantitative), reverse-transcriptase, broad-range with cloning and sequencing, and typing (AP-PCR).
- **DNA–DNA hybridization:** checkerboard, reverse-capture checkerboard, DNA microarray (HOMIM, about 300 oral taxa).
- **FISH:** in situ detection and spatial distribution.
- **Fingerprinting:** DGGE and T-RFLP.
- **Next-generation sequencing and metagenomics:** deep coverage including low-abundance taxa.
PCR detects about 10 cells against 10⁴–10⁵ for culture, and these methods detect uncultivated species; they detect dead cells and are often qualitative (Fouad, 2017; Berman et al, 2021).

### 5. Impact on knowledge of the microflora
More than 400 species have been reported in endodontic infections; about 45% only from molecular studies, 32% by culture alone and 23% by both. Nine phyla are represented (Firmicutes, Bacteroidetes, Spirochaetes, Fusobacteria, Actinobacteria, Proteobacteria, Synergistetes, TM7, SR1) (Berman et al, 2021).

### 6. Role of E. faecalis
**Features:** a Gram-positive facultative anaerobic coccus (Gopikrishna, 2021).
**Prevalence:** the most frequently detected species in root-filled teeth, up to 90%, and about nine times likelier than in primary infections; about 1% of total bacterial counts (range 0.1–100%) (Berman et al, 2021; Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
**Behaviour:** commonly recovered from multi-visit or open-drainage teeth, so it acts as a secondary invader that later becomes persistent; its source may be food (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
**Survival factors:**
- Invades dentinal tubules and escapes instruments and irrigants.
- Forms biofilm on canal walls.
- Resists calcium hydroxide, possibly through a proton pump that acidifies the cytoplasm (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).
- Enters a viable-but-non-cultivable state and survives nutrient scarcity.
- Can colonise as a single species.
- Tolerates extreme alkaline pH, salt, 10–45°C and 60°C for 30 minutes; produces lytic enzymes, gelatinase, aggregation substance and lipoteichoic acid; suppresses lymphocytes (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Treatment implications:**
- Instrumentation alone does not clear it, but a larger apical preparation (no. 30 to no. 40) reduced counts (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- Use NaOCl in large volume with activation; 6% NaOCl was most effective against old E. faecalis biofilm in dentine, and 2% CHX eliminated an E. faecalis biofilm in vitro (Berman et al, 2021).
- Calcium hydroxide alone could not kill it inside tubules, but combined with chlorhexidine or iodine potassium iodide it disinfected dentine in vitro (Berman et al, 2021).
- Prevent re-entry: aseptic technique, rubber dam, and a bacteria-tight coronal seal.

### 7. Conclusion
The endodontic microflora is a mixed community whose composition differs by type of infection and between individuals; molecular methods have shown it to be much more diverse than culture alone suggested, but have also introduced limits such as detection of dead cells, so culture and molecular methods are complementary.
