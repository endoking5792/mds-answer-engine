## STUDY MODE
**Why molecular methods.** Culture misses the large uncultivated majority, so molecular methods were adopted to identify microbes without cultivation. They have confirmed many culture-based findings and added new candidate pathogens: more than 400 species are known in endodontic infections, about 45% only from molecular studies (Berman et al, 2021).

**Target gene.** The 16S rRNA gene (18S for fungi) has conserved regions, used for broad-range primers, and variable regions carrying genus and species signatures, used for species-specific primers and probes (Fouad, 2017; Berman et al, 2021). A 98.5–99% identity to a database sequence is the usual species criterion (Fouad, 2017).

**Techniques (Fouad, 2017; Berman et al, 2021).**
- *PCR* amplifies DNA exponentially through repeated melting, primer annealing and extension. Detects as few as 10 cells, versus 10⁴–10⁵ for culture on non-selective media and 10³–10⁴ for DNA–DNA hybridization or immunology, so it is at least 10–100 times more sensitive.
- *Species-specific PCR*; *nested PCR* (two rounds with outer then inner primers); *multiplex PCR* (several species at once); *touchdown PCR* (higher specificity).
- *Real-time (quantitative) PCR* gives quantification, for example bacterial load before and after treatment (TaqMan or SYBR Green).
- *Broad-range PCR with cloning and sequencing* detects unexpected species; *PCR typing* (AP-PCR/RAPD) tracks the origin of strains.
- *DGGE and T-RFLP* fingerprint communities.
- *DNA–DNA hybridization:* checkerboard (many samples against many probes), reverse-capture checkerboard (oligonucleotide probes, can detect uncultivated species), and DNA microarrays (HOMIM, about 300 oral taxa).
- *FISH:* fluorescent rRNA probes detect intact cells in situ and show architecture and spatial relationships.
- *Next-generation sequencing* (pyrosequencing, Illumina) gives much deeper coverage than Sanger sequencing and finds low-abundance taxa; *metagenomics* studies all DNA.

**Advantages and limitations (Berman et al, 2021).** Advantages: detects cultivable and uncultivated species, high specificity and sensitivity, rapid, no strict anaerobic handling, usable during antimicrobial treatment, samples storable and transportable, detects dead organisms. Limitations: most assays are qualitative or semiquantitative (real-time PCR excepted), detect few targets (broad-range PCR, checkerboard and microarray excepted), miss unexpected species (broad-range PCR excepted), costly, biases and contamination risk in broad-range PCR. DNA from dead cells can persist in root canals for up to 2 years, giving false positives (Fouad, 2017).

**Five generations of studies (Berman et al, 2021):** culture, then increasingly broad molecular approaches up to high-throughput sequencing.

### Definition and need
Molecular biology methods identify microorganisms from their nucleic acid markers without cultivation. They were needed because many endodontic bacteria cannot be cultured. They confirmed culture findings and added new candidate pathogens; of more than 400 species reported, about 45% came only from molecular studies (Berman et al, 2021).

### Target
The 16S rRNA gene (18S for fungi). Conserved regions take broad-range (universal) primers; variable regions carry genus and species signatures for species-specific primers and probes. A 98.5–99% identity to a database sequence is the usual species criterion (Fouad, 2017).

### Techniques
1. **PCR and derivatives:** repeated melting, primer annealing and extension amplify the target exponentially. Variants: species-specific, nested (two rounds, outer then inner primers), multiplex (several species at once), touchdown (higher specificity), real-time (quantitative), reverse-transcriptase, broad-range with cloning and sequencing, and typing (AP-PCR).
2. **DNA–DNA hybridization:** checkerboard (many samples against many probes), reverse-capture checkerboard (oligonucleotide probes, can detect uncultivated species), DNA microarray (HOMIM, about 300 oral taxa).
3. **FISH:** fluorescent rRNA probes detect intact cells in situ and show architecture and spatial relationships.
4. **Fingerprinting:** DGGE and T-RFLP compare whole communities.
5. **Next-generation sequencing and metagenomics:** pyrosequencing and Illumina platforms give far deeper coverage than Sanger sequencing and reveal low-abundance taxa (Fouad, 2017; Berman et al, 2021).

### Sensitivity
PCR detects about 10 cells; culture needs 10⁴–10⁵; hybridization and immunology 10³–10⁴. PCR is at least 10–100 times more sensitive (Fouad, 2017).

### Advantages
- Detects cultivable and uncultivated species.
- High specificity and sensitivity; rapid (minutes to hours).
- No strict anaerobic handling; usable during antibiotic treatment.
- Samples can be frozen and DNA transported.
- Detects dead organisms (Berman et al, 2021).

### Limitations
- Mostly qualitative or semiquantitative (real-time PCR excepted).
- Few targets per assay (broad-range PCR, checkerboard and microarray excepted); may miss unexpected species.
- Costly; bias and contamination risk in broad-range PCR.
- DNA of dead cells can persist in canals for up to 2 years, so a positive result does not prove viability (Fouad, 2017).

### Impact
Infections are polymicrobial communities with high interindividual variability, in which no single species is specific for a disease form (Berman et al, 2021).

### Diagram
Sample → DNA extraction → (broad-range PCR → cloning/sequencing) or (species-specific PCR/hybridization) → identification.
