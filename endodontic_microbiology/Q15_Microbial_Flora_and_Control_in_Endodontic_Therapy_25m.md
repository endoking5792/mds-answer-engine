## STUDY MODE

### A. Flora (see Q3 for detail)
- **Source and entry:** the infected canal begins sterile; organisms enter through caries, cracks, leakage, resorption, periodontal tissues and anachoresis (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- **Primary infection:** mixed community dominated by anaerobes; 10³–10⁸ cells per canal, mean 10–20 species (Berman et al, 2021).
- **Secondary and persistent infection:** fewer species, mainly Gram-positive; E. faecalis commonest in root-filled teeth (Berman et al, 2021).
- **Extraradicular:** abscess, actinomycosis, apical biofilm (Berman et al, 2021).
- Bacteria are beyond the reach of host defences and systemic antibiotics, so apart from extraction, treatment must be a professional intervention with mechanical, chemical and ecological effects that kill them (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

### B. Control measures (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022 unless stated)
1. **Asepsis.** Treatment and retreatment must start from an aseptic field: tooth free of plaque and calculus, caries and defective restorations removed, access finished under rubber dam, field cleaned with hydrogen peroxide and disinfected with 2% iodine, 2% chlorhexidine or 2.5% NaOCl, and the chamber irrigated with 2.5% NaOCl. Breaches of asepsis cause secondary infections (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022). Dahlén adds sampling and field-disinfection details for culture (Fouad, 2017).
2. **Mechanical preparation.** Instrumentation with irrigation removes more than 90% of cultivable bacteria even without antibacterials and reduces counts 100–1,000-fold, but negative cultures are not predictable; larger apical preparation removes more bacteria.
3. **Chemical irrigation.** NaOCl is better than saline, mainly with larger preparations; at 0.5% and 5% the clinical effect was similar, with large volume and frequent exchange compensating for concentration. Chlorhexidine is a possible substitute of similar potency. The smear layer is removed with EDTA or citric acid, since it can harbour bacteria.
4. **Supplementary disinfection.** Ultrasonic activation of NaOCl, a finishing instrument, or a final 2% CHX rinse (with an intermediate rinse to avoid precipitate). Evidence for each is mixed (see Q2).
5. **Intracanal medication.** Calcium hydroxide with CPMC–glycerin or CHX for about 7 days; the paste is removed with the master apical file under irrigation, which improved disinfection.
6. **Obturation and coronal seal.** Obturation reduces space for community reorganisation and denies nutrients, but cannot entomb bacteria above a threshold; a bacteria-tight coronal seal prevents re-entry.
7. **Ecological control.** Partial elimination of a community can reorganise it toward more resistant species, so preparation is ideally completed in a single visit with medication; the paradigm is to eliminate as many members as possible and deny substrate to those that remain.
8. **Culture.** Grossman: pre-obturation culture is no longer recommended; Cohen and Dahlén: negative cultures at filling relate to better outcome, but it is impractical routinely (Gopikrishna, 2021; Berman et al, 2021; Fouad, 2017).
9. **Antibiotics.** Adjuncts only in systemic involvement, progressive infection, immunocompromise or persistent infection (see Q7).
10. **Follow-up.** Bacteria that survive can still be harmless if few, avirulent or sealed away; but those that survive starvation and reach critical load can cause failure (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS (compressed, one claim)
**Claim — a negative culture before obturation should be required.** Grossman says culture is no longer recommended because success did not differ with or without negative culture, it delays one-visit treatment and sampling errors give false results (Gopikrishna, 2021). Cohen and Dahlén report that canals negative at filling have a better outcome, and that cultivable bacteria persisting at filling relate to poorer long-term outcome and delayed healing (Berman et al, 2021; Fouad, 2017). The books therefore disagree on whether culture adds clinical value; both agree it is not a routine step. What can be concluded is that bacterial reduction before filling matters, not that culture is the way to confirm it. [Likely]

## EXAM MODE
**(Estimated writing time: ~39 minutes at 22 words/min; ~848 words)**

### 1. Introduction
The root canal system is sterile until the pulp becomes necrotic; bacteria then colonise it through caries, cracks, leakage, resorption, periodontal tissues or the blood. Because bacteria in the necrotic canal are beyond host defences and systemic antibiotics, apart from extraction the only treatment is professional intervention with mechanical, chemical and ecological effects that kill them (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022; Gopikrishna, 2021).

### 2. Microbial flora
- **Primary intraradicular infection:** mixed community dominated by anaerobes; 10³–10⁸ cells per canal; mean 10–20 species. Common genera: Fusobacterium, Prevotella, Porphyromonas, Tannerella, Treponema, Dialister, Parvimonas, Filifactor, Streptococcus and Actinomyces (Berman et al, 2021).
- **Secondary infection:** organisms introduced after intervention (E. faecalis, Pseudomonas, Staphylococcus, enteric rods, Candida) (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
- **Persistent infection:** fewer species (1–5 if well treated), mainly Gram-positive; E. faecalis commonest in root-filled teeth (Berman et al, 2021).
- **Extraradicular:** acute abscess, actinomycosis, apical biofilm.
- **Organisation:** biofilms on the canal walls, in ramifications and in dentinal tubules.

### 3. Control of the flora (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022)
**A. Asepsis (prevents secondary infection)**
- Tooth free of plaque and calculus; caries and defective restorations removed; access finished under rubber dam.
- Field cleaned with hydrogen peroxide and disinfected with 2% iodine, 2% chlorhexidine or 2.5% NaOCl; chamber irrigated with 2.5% NaOCl.

**B. Mechanical preparation**
- Instrumentation with irrigation removes over 90% of cultivable bacteria even without antibacterials, and reduces counts 100–1,000-fold; negative cultures are not predictable (the original early study found 80% of canals culture-positive immediately after instrumentation with water).
- A larger apical preparation removes more bacteria; crown-down technique with abundant irrigation; apical patency kept with small files.

**C. Chemical irrigation**
- 2.5% NaOCl after each instrument, at least 2 mL per instrument; large volumes, frequently renewed; concentration (0.5% to 5%) made little clinical difference.
- Chlorhexidine is a possible substitute of similar potency; EDTA or citric acid removes the smear layer, which can harbour bacteria and delay the effect of medicaments.
- Small needles to or near working length improve delivery.

**D. Supplementary disinfection**
- Ultrasonic activation of NaOCl, a finishing instrument, or a final 2% chlorhexidine rinse (with an intermediate rinse of alcohol, EDTA or saline to avoid precipitate). Evidence is mixed (see Q2).

**E. Intracanal medication**
- Calcium hydroxide with camphorated paramonochlorophenol and glycerin, or with 0.12–2% chlorhexidine, for about 7 days; removed with the master apical file under irrigation, which improves disinfection.

**F. Obturation and coronal seal**
- Obturation reduces space for community reorganisation and denies nutrients, but above a bacterial threshold it cannot entomb survivors; a bacteria-tight coronal seal prevents re-entry.

**G. Ecological principle**
- Partial elimination of the community can reorganise it toward more resistant species. The paradigm is to eliminate as many members as possible and deny substrate to those that remain; single-visit preparation with medication limits reorganisation.

**H. What the clinical studies show (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022)**
- Instrumentation with saline alone left only 28% of canals culture-negative in one study; larger apical size gave more reduction, and the benefit of NaOCl over saline appeared mainly with larger sizes.
- With NaOCl at 0.5–5%, 30% to about 80% of canals were negative; 2.5% NaOCl reduced bacterial levels 10²- to 10⁵-fold (95–99%); chlorhexidine gave comparable results (for example 80% versus 73% negative in one comparison), though results varied between studies.
- Ultrasonic irrigation for one minute after preparation raised the number of negative cultures, probably through cavitation, acoustic streaming and warming; ultrasonic instruments are no longer recommended for preparation because of procedural accidents.
- In retreatment, 35% (NaOCl) and 41% (chlorhexidine) of canals still had bacteria at filling, and the initial bacterial load influenced the result.
- Removing the calcium hydroxide paste with the master apical file under irrigation gave further reduction.

**I. Summary of the recommended sequence (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022)**
Instrumentation to a large size under rubber dam, antimicrobial irrigation with smear layer removal, supplementary disinfection in the same visit, calcium hydroxide paste between visits, then obturation and a coronal seal.

**J. Healing does not require sterility (Berman et al, 2021)**
Some lesions heal although bacteria were present at filling, because the bacteria died after filling (toxic material, no nutrients, disrupted ecology), were below the critical number or virulence, or lay where the periradicular tissues could not reach them.

### 4. Monitoring: culture
Grossman: culture before obturation is no longer recommended (no outcome benefit, prevents single-visit treatment, sampling error). Cohen and Dahlén: negative culture at filling is associated with a better outcome, but it is impractical for routine use (Gopikrishna, 2021; Berman et al, 2021; Fouad, 2017).

### 5. Antibiotics
Only as an adjunct: systemic involvement, progressive or persistent infection, immunocompromised patients; not for pulpitis or localised swelling (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

### 6. Why bacteria survive control measures
Biofilms; unprepared areas (about 10–50% of canal surface on micro-CT); isthmuses, ramifications and tubules; dormancy; protection by tissue remnants and dentine; adaptation to starvation. Survivors can be harmless if few, avirulent or sealed away (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

### 7. Conclusion
Control of the flora rests on strict asepsis, thorough chemomechanical preparation to adequate size, supplementary disinfection and medication where indicated, and a bacteria-tight seal. Complete sterility is not reliably achievable, so the aim is to reduce bacteria below the level that sustains disease.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Mixed anaerobic primary flora; Gram-positive persistent flora; E. faecalis in root-filled teeth.
- Instrumentation and irrigation remove over 90% of cultivable bacteria.
- NaOCl is the irrigant of choice; large volume and frequent exchange matter more than concentration.
- Calcium hydroxide 7 days, remove with master apical file.
- Antibiotics only with systemic signs.
- Culture before obturation: Grossman against; Cohen and Dahlén link negative cultures with better outcome.
