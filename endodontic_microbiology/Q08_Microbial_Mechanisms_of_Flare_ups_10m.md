## STUDY MODE
**Definition.** An acute exacerbation of a periradicular pathosis after starting or continuing non-surgical root canal treatment: severe pain and/or swelling a few hours to the next day after treatment (Berman et al, 2021; Siqueira and Rôças, 2022). It is an event, not a disease: a symptomatic apical periodontitis or acute apical abscess secondary to treatment. Incidence 2.5–16% (meta-analysis 8.4%). Risk factors: preoperative pain and infected canals (necrotic pulps, retreatment) (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

**Core idea.** Bacterial infection of the canal is the main cause of periradicular inflammation. A flare-up occurs when the host is suddenly challenged by more bacterial cells or products than it is used to, upsetting the balance between aggression and defence (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022). Three microbial situations produce this:

1. **Apical extrusion of infected debris.**
   - Forcing bacteria and products into the periradicular tissues during chemomechanical preparation is allegedly a major cause of postoperative pain. Two factors decide the response: quantity (bacterial load) and quality (virulence of the extruded bacteria). A small amount of debris with virulent clonal types can still cause exacerbation.
   - All instrumentation techniques extrude debris or bacteria in 70–100% of cases, in different amounts; crown-down techniques extrude less, which is why they are preferred.
   - Overinstrumentation adds mechanical injury; in retreatment, filling remnants and infected debris are pushed ahead of instruments and the solvents are cytotoxic (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
2. **Change in the intracanal microbiota or conditions (incomplete preparation).**
   - The canal holds a mixed community; incomplete preparation can remove inhibitory species and let previously inhibited, possibly virulent species overgrow.
   - Environmental change may activate virulence genes, so a previously asymptomatic tooth becomes symptomatic.
   - Teeth with preoperative symptoms are at higher risk because virulent clonal types are present.
   - An oxygen-entry theory (rise in redox potential favouring facultative overgrowth) is speculative with no supporting evidence (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022). Hence preparation should ideally be completed in one visit.
3. **Secondary intraradicular infection.** New organisms enter through a breach of the aseptic chain, between appointments or by coronal leakage; if they survive and are virulent and dense enough they cause acute inflammation, particularly after heavy inoculation or accidents such as overinstrumentation (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

**Organisms.** No study has consistently identified species, because flare-ups are uncommon; combinations of F. nucleatum with Porphyromonas and Prevotella species have been suggested (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).

**Non-microbial contributors (Cohen).** Preparation beyond the apex, incomplete pulp removal, overextended filling material, chemical irritants (irrigants, medicaments, sealers), hyperocclusion and root fracture; higher prevalence in females under 20, maxillary lateral incisors, mandibular first molars with large lesions, and retreatment (Berman et al, 2021).

**Management.** Remove the temporary restoration, irrigate with 2.5% NaOCl, re-check working length and patency (blood on paper points suggests overinstrumentation), medicate with calcium hydroxide paste, seal, prescribe NSAIDs; treat an abscess as such. Antibiotics are not recommended to prevent flare-ups (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022; Berman et al, 2021). Flare-up rates far above 2–5% in infected canals point to a problem in technique (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022). [Certain]

## EXAM MODE
**(Estimated writing time: ~14 minutes at 22 words/min; ~315 words)**

### Definition
An acute exacerbation of periradicular disease, with severe pain and/or swelling, within hours to a day after starting or continuing root canal treatment; a symptomatic apical periodontitis or acute abscess secondary to treatment. Incidence 2.5–16% (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

### Basic mechanism
A sudden increase in the number of bacterial irritants overcomes host defence at the periradicular tissue, producing acute inflammation (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

### Microbial mechanisms
**1. Apical extrusion of infected debris**
- Bacteria forced beyond the apex during preparation; response depends on load (quantity) and virulence (quality).
- 70–100% of cases show extrusion with every technique; crown-down extrudes less.
- Overinstrumentation and retreatment (filling remnants, solvents) increase the risk.

**2. Change in the intracanal microbiota or environment**
- Incomplete preparation removes inhibitory species and lets virulent species overgrow.
- Virulence gene activation after environmental change.
- Speculative: oxygen entry raising redox potential (no evidence).

**3. Secondary infection**
- Breach of aseptic chain, coronal leakage, or between-visit contamination.
- New virulent species colonise in sufficient numbers.

### Risk factors
Preoperative pain, necrotic pulp, retreatment, symptomatic apical periodontitis (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022); large lesions and young age (Berman et al, 2021).

### Associated bacteria
Possibly F. nucleatum with Porphyromonas and Prevotella species (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019).

### Non-microbial causes
Overinstrumentation, overfilling, irrigant extrusion, chemical irritants, hyperocclusion (Berman et al, 2021).

### Why symptomatic teeth are at higher risk
Teeth with preoperative symptoms often harbour virulent clonal types of pathogenic species; if treatment changes the environment or extrudes these organisms, inflammation can be exacerbated even by a small amount of infected debris (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

### Audit point
If flare-ups in a clinician's infected canals far exceed 2–5%, the technique and concepts should be reviewed (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

### Prevention and management
Crown-down preparation within canal limits, rubber dam and aseptic technique, complete preparation, and a coronal seal. Treat with re-irrigation (2.5% NaOCl), patency, calcium hydroxide, seal and NSAIDs; antibiotics only for systemic signs (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Three microbial mechanisms: extrusion of debris, incomplete preparation, secondary infection.
- Load (quantity) plus virulence (quality) of extruded bacteria.
- Extrusion in 70–100% of cases; crown-down extrudes less.
- Incidence 2.5–16%; meta-analysis 8.4%.
- Antibiotics do not prevent flare-ups.
