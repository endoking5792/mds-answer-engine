## STUDY MODE
**What it is.** Culture propagates microorganisms in the laboratory by supplying nutrients and suitable temperature, atmosphere, salt and pH. The steps are sample collection and transport, dispersion, dilution, cultivation, isolation and identification (Berman et al, 2021). Culture was the traditional method for studying the endodontic microbiota and has given most of the early knowledge on aetiology, composition, treatment effects and antibiotic susceptibility (Berman et al, 2021).

**Sampling and transport (Dahlén, in Fouad, 2017).** Aseptic control of the field matters most to avoid false positives: rubber dam, prewash with 30% hydrogen peroxide, disinfection with 10% iodine tincture, removal of caries and defective restorations, and a control sample of the field with iodine inactivated by 5% thiosulfate. The canal is flooded with sampling solution, a file is pumped to suspend bacteria, and charcoaled paper points absorb the liquid (the last point is the most important) and go into a transport medium (VMGA III) that keeps organisms viable but does not let them multiply and inactivates medicaments.

**Laboratory.** Liquid media (thioglycolate, trypticase or brain-heart infusion broth) are favoured for fastidious or dormant cells and are incubated up to 14 days; solid media (blood agar) incubated aerobically and anaerobically give diversity and semiquantification (Fouad, 2017). Grossman lists brain-heart infusion broth with 0.1% agar, trypticase soy broth, thioglycolate and glucose ascites broth (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Advantages.** Broad-range, finds unexpected species, quantifies viable cultivable organisms, allows susceptibility testing and physiological and pathogenicity studies, widely available (Berman et al, 2021).

**Limitations.** Many species cannot be cultured (40–80% of the species in human sites, including the mouth, are uncultivated), low sensitivity, contamination and transport dependence, days to weeks for anaerobes, costly and laborious, identification depends on phenotype and may be wrong (Berman et al, 2021). Molecular studies report about 45% of more than 400 endodontic species only by molecular methods, 32% by culture alone and 23% by both (Berman et al, 2021).

**Is it still used clinically? The books differ.**
- Grossman: culture before obturation is no longer recommended. No difference in success between negative-culture and no-culture obturation, it prevents one-visit treatment, sampling errors cause false results, and modern instrumentation makes culture unnecessary (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Dahlén (Fouad, 2017): a sample during treatment may be useful; absence of viable bacteria before filling improves the prognosis of treatment and retreatment, but there is little benefit in sampling primary infections.
- Cohen: canals with negative cultures at filling have a better outcome, and bacteria present at obturation raise the risk of adverse outcome (Berman et al, 2021).
The usual reconciliation is that culture is informative in research and refractory cases but impractical as a routine step. [Likely]

## EXAM MODE
**(Estimated writing time: ~15 minutes at 22 words/min; ~337 words)**

### Definition
Culture is the propagation of microorganisms in the laboratory under suitable nutrients, temperature, atmosphere, salt concentration and pH. It was the traditional way of studying the endodontic microbiota (Berman et al, 2021).

### Steps
1. Sampling and transport in an anaerobic, viability-preserving, non-multiplying medium.
2. Dispersion by sonication or vortex mixing, then dilution.
3. Cultivation on media under aerobic and anaerobic conditions.
4. Isolation of colonies, then identification by colony and cell morphology, Gram stain, oxygen tolerance and biochemical tests (Berman et al, 2021).

### Sampling technique (Fouad, 2017)
- Rubber dam, hydrogen peroxide prewash, iodine disinfection of the field; remove caries and old fillings.
- Optional control sample of the field, with iodine inactivated by thiosulfate to avoid false negatives.
- Flood the canal with sampling solution, pump with a file, absorb with charcoaled paper points (the last point is the most important), transfer to transport medium (VMGA III).

### Media
Brain-heart infusion broth, trypticase soy broth, thioglycolate, glucose ascites broth, and blood agar plates (Gopikrishna, 2021; Fouad, 2017). Liquid media suit fastidious or dormant cells and are incubated up to 14 days.

### Advantages
- Broad range; finds unexpected species.
- Quantifies viable cultivable organisms.
- Allows antibiotic susceptibility, physiological and pathogenicity studies.
- Widely available.

### Limitations
- Many species cannot be cultured (40–80% of species at human sites are uncultivated).
- Low sensitivity: culture on non-selective media needs about 10⁴–10⁵ cells, against about 10 for PCR.
- Contamination and transport dependent; anaerobes need expertise and days to weeks; identification by phenotype can be wrong.
- Of more than 400 endodontic species, about 32% were found only by culture, 45% only by molecular methods and 23% by both (Berman et al, 2021).

### Clinical use and controversy
- Grossman: culture before obturation is no longer recommended: no outcome benefit, it prevents one-visit treatment, sampling errors give false results (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Cohen and Dahlén: canals negative at filling have a better outcome; a high count of polymicrobial anaerobes during treatment should raise suspicion of leakage; little benefit in sampling primary infections (Berman et al, 2021; Fouad, 2017).

### Diagram
Flow: sample → transport → dilute → plate/broth → incubate → isolate → identify.
