## STUDY MODE

**Why the open apex changes the plan.** A tooth with an open apex still has a route for blood vessels and nerves to re-enter the pulp. Teeth with an apical foramen wider than 1.1 mm can revascularise after replantation. Reported rates lie between 18% and 40% (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.262). Revascularisation depends on the foramen size and the length of the canal (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.484-485), and on bacterial contamination (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.262).

**Pre-replantation antibiotic.** Soak the root for about 5 minutes in doxycycline, or cover it with minocycline, then replant (Trope, n.d., p.345; Kahler, n.d., p.2638). Andreasen cites tetracycline as possibly more than doubling the chance of revascularisation, but notes the finding comes from animal work and has not been verified in humans (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.475). Doxycycline at 1 mg in 20 mL saline is reported to double the chance in animals (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.477).

**Timing.** If the apex is wide open and replantation is within 3 hours of injury, the textbook view is to wait for revascularisation (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.477). A prolonged extra-alveolar period has the worst prognosis. Harty's says that "it might be beneficial to perform root canal treatment before replanting such a tooth", but the sentence is cut off in the extracted text, so its stated reason is not reproduced here (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.262).

**Where revascularisation fails.** If the pulp does not revascularise, root resorption can progress very quickly in incompletely formed roots, at up to 0.1 mm of root substance per day (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.477). Endodontic treatment must therefore start early, with calcium hydroxide placed to stop resorption and clear periapical inflammation (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.477).

**Contrast with a closed apex.** A closed-apex tooth cannot revascularise, so it needs root canal treatment early, before the necrotic pulp becomes infected (Kahler, n.d., p.2638; Trope, n.d., p.341).

**Follow-up.** Radiographs at 2 and 3 weeks. Review the signs of revascularisation and of resorption (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.477).

## EXAM MODE

**Principle.** An open apex keeps a route for the pulp to revascularise, so the first aim is to preserve the pulp when the conditions allow. Once revascularisation fails, or when the dry time has been long, the aim switches to early root canal treatment, before resorption and infection take hold.

**Assessment at the clinic.** Establish the apical foramen (open or closed), the extra-alveolar time and the storage medium. A wide-open apex with dry time under three hours is the case where revascularisation can be expected [Andreasen et al, 2007; Sigurdsson and Bourguignon].

**Pre-replantation.** Rinse visible contamination off with saline. Topical antibiotics are not supported: animal results were promising, but human studies have failed to show improved revascularisation [Fouad et al, 2020, p.336; Huang et al, 2024, PDF p.6]. Systemic amoxicillin or penicillin are the IADT first choices [Fouad et al, 2020, p.336]. The textbook doxycycline soak [Trope, n.d., p.345] is not an IADT recommendation.

**Replantation.** Rinse the socket with saline, reposition any displaced socket wall, and replant gently [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.475]. Splint for two weeks, or four if the alveolus is fractured [Fouad et al, 2020, p.334-335].

**Choosing the endodontic route.** The IADT aim for an immature tooth is revascularisation. If it fails, apexification or root canal treatment follows, started only with definite necrosis and infection [Fouad et al, 2020, p.335]. The three-hour rule is a textbook point [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.477].

**Monitoring.** Review at 2 weeks (splint removal), 1, 2, 3 and 6 months, one year, and yearly for at least five years [Fouad et al, 2020, p.337-338]. Resorption is very rapid in children; if it starts, begin endodontic treatment with calcium hydroxide for up to one month (p.335, p.337).

**Closed apex, for contrast.** No revascularisation is possible, so root canal treatment is needed within two weeks of replantation [Fouad et al, 2020, p.334]. Trope gives 7 to 10 days from injury [Trope, n.d., p.341].

**Patient information.** Explain that revascularisation is possible but not assured, with reported rates of 18 to 40%, and that root canal treatment may still be needed if the pulp does not respond or resorption begins [Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d.; Andreasen et al, 2007] [Likely].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Foramen larger than 1.1 mm: revascularisation possible; rates 18-40%.
- Soak 5 min in doxycycline (1 mg/20 mL) or minocycline cover; tetracycline evidence is animal-only.
- Replanted within 3 h with wide-open apex: await revascularisation.
- Prolonged dry time: worst prognosis; Harty's suggests RCT before replantation.
- Resorption can reach 0.1 mm/day if revascularisation fails.
- Closed apex: no revascularisation; root canal treatment within 7 to 10 days.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Treating an open apex like a closed apex, and starting root canal treatment at once.
2. Quoting tetracycline or doxycycline results from animal studies as proven in humans.
3. Forgetting that revascularisation depends on extra-alveolar time, not only on apex size.
4. Omitting the calcium hydroxide step when resorption starts without revascularisation.
5. Giving a revascularisation rate without its range (18-40%).
