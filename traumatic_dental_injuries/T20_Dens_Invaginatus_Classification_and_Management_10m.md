## STUDY MODE

**Definition.** The AAE glossary defines dens invaginatus, or dens in dente, as a developmental defect resulting from infolding of the crown (AAE, n.d., p.14). Harty's describes it as a deep pit into the pulp space that forms during tooth development and then becomes a route for infection into the pulp (Harty's, n.d., p.63). Grossman's lists it as dens in dente under developmental anatomy (Grossman's, n.d., p.284).

**Occurrence.** The endodontic radiology text notes that it usually affects maxillary lateral incisors (Endodontic Radiology, n.d., p.74). The aetiology is unclear, and the condition is considered an invagination of the dental papilla during development (p.74).

**Oehlers classification.** Endodontic Radiology describes three classes (p.80). Class I is an invagination limited to the crown, not extending beyond the cemento-enamel junction. Class II extends beyond the cemento-enamel junction into the root but ends as a blind sac. Class III extends deeper into the root, communicates with the external root surface and may reach the apical third. None of the three directly communicates with the pulp, according to the figure legend (p.80).

**Management.** Harty's notes that the severity of the invagination determines the management (n.d., p.63). The sources read do not state a treatment for Class I. Sealing or restoring the shallow crown pit is the common teaching [Guessing; not from the sources read]. Class II and III lesions carry a risk of pulp infection through the blind sac or a communication with the root surface, so the approach may need root canal treatment, sealing of the invagination, or surgery, depending on the extent (Harty's, n.d., p.63; Endodontic Radiology, n.d., p.80). The sources do not give a single algorithm, and the choice should follow the class and the pulp status.

**Radiographic assessment.** Radiographs show the infolding of the crown, and the class depends on how far the infolding extends (Endodontic Radiology, n.d., p.74-80).

## EXAM MODE

**Definition.** Dens invaginatus, or dens in dente, is a developmental defect resulting from infolding of the crown [AAE, n.d., p.14]. The infolding forms a deep pit into the pulp space during tooth development, which can become a route for infection into the pulp [Harty's, n.d., p.63]. It is a developmental anomaly, not a traumatic injury.

**Occurrence.** It usually affects maxillary lateral incisors [Endodontic Radiology, n.d., p.74]. The aetiology is unclear, and it is considered an invagination of the dental papilla during development [Endodontic Radiology, n.d., p.74]. It is distinguished from dens evaginatus, an extra cusp on the occlusal surface of a premolar, which is a projection rather than an infolding [Endodontic Radiology, n.d., p.74].

**Classification.** Oehlers' classes [Endodontic Radiology, n.d., p.80]. Class I is confined to the crown, not extending beyond the cemento-enamel junction. Class II extends beyond the junction into the root and ends as a blind sac. Class III extends deeper into the root, communicates with the external root surface and may reach the apical third. The figure legend states that none of the three directly communicates with the pulp [Endodontic Radiology, n.d., p.80].

**Pathway of infection.** The blind sac, or a communication with the root surface, can hold plaque and bacteria that reach the pulp or the periodontal tissues. Harty's notes that the severity of the condition determines the management [Harty's, n.d., p.63].

**Radiographic diagnosis.** Radiographs show the infolding of the crown, and the class depends on how far the infolding extends [Endodontic Radiology, n.d., p.74-80]. Grossman's lists dens in dente among the developmental anatomy of the pulp chamber [Grossman's, n.d., p.284].

**Management.** Harty's links management to severity [Harty's, n.d., p.63]. The sources read do not state a single treatment for each class. For Class I, sealing or restoring the shallow pit is commonly taught [Guessing; not stated in the sources read]. For Class II and III, the management must address infection through the blind sac or the root communication, and may involve sealing of the invagination, root canal treatment or surgery, depending on extent and pulp status [Harty's, n.d., p.63] [Likely]. The examiner should state that the sources do not give one algorithm.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Dens invaginatus: developmental infolding; usually maxillary lateral incisors.
- Oehlers classes I, II, III.
- None of the classes communicates directly with the pulp (per the figure legend).
- Management depends on class and pulp status.
