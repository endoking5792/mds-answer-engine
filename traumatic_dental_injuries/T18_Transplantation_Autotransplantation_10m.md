## STUDY MODE

**Definition.** Transplantation moves a tooth from one socket to another, in the same or another person (Harty's, n.d., p.225). Autotransplantation is a transplant within the same patient. Andreasen et al describe it as a well-documented method for anterior tooth loss, especially after trauma, because the graft has an osteogenic capacity that helps the new bone (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.756).

**Indication and timing.** The treatment is well suited to anterior tooth loss, and it works best in early childhood, when implants are contraindicated (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.756). A multidisciplinary plan is recommended for tooth loss after trauma, because long-term planning involves several specialties (p.756).

**Donor selection.** The main factors are the stage of root development and the design of an atraumatic graft technique (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.756-757). The donor should have a crown and root anatomy suited to the recipient site, and the graft may be rotated 45 or 90 degrees to achieve harmony. The donor should ideally come from the opposite side of the arch (p.757). For central incisor loss, most premolars are suitable except the mandibular first premolar. For lateral incisor loss, the mandibular first premolar is the optimal donor. Mandibular premolars are optimal for canine loss (p.757-761).

**Technique.** The donor is removed atraumatically, usually with a flap and removal of marginal bone (labially for mandibular premolars, palatally for maxillary premolars). The graft is placed in the recipient socket and fixed, often with a suture over the incisal edge (p.760). The aim is to protect the periodontal ligament and cementum during removal and placement.

**Outcome.** Across several studies, the success rate (no progressive root resorption) is 87 to 93%, and tooth survival is 90 to 98%. Complications such as pulp necrosis and external resorption occur in 10 to 20% of cases at a later date (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.770). Life-table analysis of immature transplants shows 5-year survival of 98 to 99% (p.770). Healing and pulp survival are best predicted by the stage of root development and apical diameter at grafting (p.773).

## EXAM MODE

**Definition and scope.** Autotransplantation moves a tooth to a new socket in the same patient, most often to replace a lost anterior tooth after trauma [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.756]. The repository wording "transplantation" is read this way; transplantation between people is outside this answer [Harty's, n.d., p.225].

**Indications and timing.** It is well suited to anterior tooth loss and works best in early childhood, when implants are contraindicated. A multidisciplinary plan is recommended after trauma [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.756].

**Donor selection.** The key factors are root development at the time of grafting and the donor's crown and root anatomy [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.756-757]. Most premolars suit central incisor sites, except the mandibular first premolar; the mandibular first premolar is the optimal donor for a lateral incisor; and the mandibular premolars are optimal for canine sites [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.757-761]. Maxillary first premolars should generally not be used [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.757]. Rotate the graft by 45 or 90 degrees for harmony, and take the donor from the opposite side of the arch where possible [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.757].

**Technique.** Remove the donor atraumatically, usually by a flap, with limited removal of marginal bone (labially for mandibular premolars, palatally for maxillary premolars). Protect the periodontal ligament and cementum throughout [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.760]. A suture over the incisal edge can hold a tooth with incomplete eruption in the short term, though its effect is limited [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.859, p.865] [Likely].

**Outcome.** Across several studies, success (no progressive root resorption) is 87 to 93%, and tooth survival is 90 to 98%. Complications such as pulp necrosis and external resorption occur in 10 to 20% later [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.770]. Life-table analysis of immature transplants gives 5-year survival of 98 to 99% [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.770]. The stage of root development and apical diameter at grafting are the strongest predictors of healing [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.773].

**Monitoring.** Review pulp and periodontal status for several years, because complications can appear late, and treat pulp necrosis or external resorption as they arise [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.770].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Autotransplantation: same patient, different socket.
- Best in early childhood, before implants.
- Donor: root development, crown anatomy, atraumatic removal.
- Success 87-93%; survival 90-98%; complications 10-20%.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Confusing transplantation with replantation.
2. Using a maxillary first premolar as a donor for central incisor sites.
3. Ignoring root development at the time of grafting.
