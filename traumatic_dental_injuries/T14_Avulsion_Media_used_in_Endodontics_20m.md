## STUDY MODE

**Why a medium is needed.** An avulsed tooth has lost its blood supply, and the PDL on the root decides periodontal healing. If the PDL stays moist and viable, healing is expected. Once it dries, the cells die and the root is exposed to inflammation and replacement resorption (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.485-486). A transport medium keeps PDL cells alive while the tooth is out of the mouth.

**The media named in the sources.**
- *Hank's balanced salt solution (HBSS):* Cohen's calls it "presently considered the best medium for this purpose" (Kahler, n.d., p.2637). P&P names it the most common storage medium (Paranjpe and Noblett, 2021, p.220). Grossman's lists it first (Grossman, n.d., p.417).
- *Viaspan and other tissue-culture media:* effective in vitro for several days, and conditioned media kept teeth for up to 96 hours with optimal healing in experimental work (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.475).
- *Tooth rescue box:* Harty's places the specialised box first, with milk or saline second (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.260).
- *Milk:* the second-best transport medium after the specialised kits (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.260). It keeps a high percentage of viable cells for at least 3 hours, with osmolarity and pH close to ideal (p.260). Cohen's places it first in its order of preference (Kahler, n.d., p.2635).
- *Saline:* a good experimental medium (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.485-486).
- *Saliva:* acceptable for shorter periods. Storage in saline or saliva did not significantly impair periodontal healing (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.485-486).
- *Propolis:* good effect reported, but in vivo studies are lacking (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.475).
- *Tap water:* least desirable. Its low osmolality causes rapid cell death (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.485-486; Kahler, n.d., p.2636).
- *Dry storage:* worst, causing cell necrosis and compromised healing (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.485-486).

**The inconsistency inside Cohen's.** Cohen's gives milk first in its order of preference (p.2635), yet calls HBSS the best medium in its clinical section (p.2637). Both statements should be given with their pages.

**Extra-alveolar time.** Harty's calls extra-alveolar time "the most critical factor influencing the survival of an avulsed and replanted tooth" (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.260). A dry time of 60 minutes is the point at which PDL survival is unlikely (Trope, n.d., p.343; Kahler, n.d., p.2637). Andreasen's 400-tooth follow-up found PDL healing in 73% of teeth replanted within 5 minutes and in 18% of teeth stored before replantation (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.485).

**Clinical implications.** The practical point is that the medium is a bridge, not a cure. A specialised medium keeps PDL cells viable for longer, milk and saline are acceptable for shorter periods, and saliva suits a patient who cannot get anything else at once. Water and dry storage should be avoided in every case, because they damage the PDL irreversibly and make healing unlikely (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.485-486; Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.260). The choice of medium therefore has to be made together with the time of arrival at the surgery, since a good medium cannot compensate for a long dry period (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.475).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

**Claim: does the medium matter more than the time out of the mouth?** The textbooks do not agree.

- *EVIDENCE:* Andreasen states that "storage media more than the length of the extra-alveolar period determine prognosis", citing experimental studies (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.485-487). The same passage notes that a larger clinical study found "a less pronounced effect" of medium (p.486-487). Harty's names extra-alveolar time as the most critical factor (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.260).
- *INTERPRETATION:* The experimental and clinical sources weigh medium and time differently.
- *LIMITATION:* The experimental work is animal or in vitro. The clinical study is observational. Neither isolates the medium, so no settled ranking follows.
- *Clinical implication:* Replant early, and use the best available medium when replantation is delayed.

## EXAM MODE

**Principle.** A medium keeps the PDL cells of an avulsed tooth alive while it is out of the mouth. The choice of medium matters, but the time outside the socket usually matters more. The clinician's first aim is to replant the tooth at once, and the medium is the fallback [Andreasen et al, 2007; Sigurdsson and Bourguignon].

**Preferred media.** The IADT lists storage media in descending order of preference: milk, Hank's balanced salt solution (HBSS), saliva and saline, and describes all four as suitable and convenient [Fouad et al, 2020, p.333]. A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis found HBSS the most effective medium for preserving PDL cell viability, on in vitro evidence [Blackledge et al, 2025, p.1, p.3]. The two sources agree that HBSS is the best-supported medium; the IADT places milk first for practical reasons.

**Practical alternatives.** Milk is widely available, keeps a high percentage of viable cells for at least three hours, and has osmolarity and pH close to ideal [Sigurdsson and Bourguignon]. Harty's ranks saline with milk as the second-best option, behind the specialised box [Sigurdsson and Bourguignon]. Saliva is the medium the patient can supply at once, in the vestibule or in a container the patient spits into [Andreasen et al, 2007; Kahler].

**Media to avoid.** Tap water is the least desirable medium. Its low osmolality produces rapid cell lysis and more inflammation on replantation [Andreasen et al, 2007; Kahler]. Dry storage is worse than any medium.

**Time.** The IADT figures are the operative ones. After 30 minutes of extra-alveolar dry time most PDL cells are non-viable [Fouad et al, 2020, p.333]. If the tooth has been kept in a suitable medium with less than 60 minutes of dry time, the PDL cells may be viable but compromised; with more than 60 minutes, they are likely non-viable [Fouad et al, 2020, p.333]. Harty's calls extra-alveolar time the most critical factor [Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.260].

**Where sources disagree.** Andreasen's experimental work says the medium matters more than the time. Its own larger clinical study finds the effect of medium less pronounced. Harty's makes time the most critical factor. Cohen's ranks milk first and calls HBSS best. Each position should be stated with its source [Likely].

**Transport at the scene.** Replant at once after a gentle rinse. If that is impossible, place the tooth in HBSS or milk, or in saline or saliva for a short time. Never use water. Get to the dentist promptly [Andreasen et al, 2007; Kahler].

**What the media do for the cells.** Harty's ties the value of milk to its osmolarity and pH, which sit close to the ideal for cell survival [Sigurdsson and Bourguignon]. Andreasen ties the harm from tap water to its low osmolality, which kills cells quickly [Andreasen et al, 2007]. A medium is therefore judged on two properties: whether it preserves cell viability over time, and whether it avoids the osmotic injury that water causes. Saliva and saline are useful for short periods, while the specialised media are designed to keep cells viable for longer.

**Applying the ranking.** A complete answer lists the media in order, from the specialised box or HBSS when they exist, through milk, to saline or saliva for short periods. It names water and dry storage as unacceptable. It then states the time limits that apply whichever medium is used: 60 minutes of dry time as the threshold of PDL survival, and the benefit of replanting within 5 minutes [Trope; Andreasen et al, 2007].

**Strength of the evidence.** Much of the medium data comes from experimental work, which Andreasen describes as "experimental studies" [Andreasen et al, 2007]. The one clinical comparison it cites found a "less pronounced effect" of medium [Andreasen et al, 2007]. The examiner should hear that the ranking rests on experimental data and on textbook opinion, not on a controlled clinical trial.

**What the patient should be told.** Replant at once if possible. If not, put the tooth in milk, saline or saliva, never water, and go to the dentist. Time out of the mouth is a risk factor whatever the medium is.

**Deciding how to store the tooth.** Storage matters most when the tooth cannot be replanted within minutes. The sequence is to rinse the root only if it is dirty, gently in milk, saline or saliva (Fouad et al, 2020, p.333), then place the tooth in the best medium available. The working limit is about an hour of extra-alveolar dry time, after which PDL survival is unlikely [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.475; Trope, n.d., p.343]. Because the medium cannot restore a dry root, the time to the dentist is part of the storage decision [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.475] [Likely].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- A medium keeps PDL cells alive; dry storage is worst.
- Water is least desirable (low osmolality, rapid cell death).
- Cohen's: milk first in order of preference, but HBSS best in its clinical section.
- Harty's: rescue box first, milk second, and extra-alveolar time most critical.
- Milk keeps viable cells for at least 3 h.
- 60 min dry: PDL survival unlikely.
- 73% vs 18% PDL healing (replanted within 5 min vs stored first).
- Textbooks disagree on whether medium or time dominates; state both.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Storing the avulsed tooth in water, or calling tap water acceptable.
2. Quoting Cohen's order of preference (milk first) without noting that its clinical section calls HBSS best.
3. Ranking media as if the evidence were settled, when Andreasen's experimental and clinical data disagree.
4. Confusing the 73% vs 18% finding (replantation timing) with a comparison of media.
5. Describing specialised media without noting that they are not usually available at the scene.

## AUDIT NOTE (IADT 2020, checked 2026-10-19)

**Audit note (IADT 2020; Blackledge et al, 2025, checked 2026-10-19).** The storage order in the IADT is milk, HBSS, saliva, saline (Fouad et al, 2020, p.333), which matches the milk-first ranking in Cohen's and differs from its clinical-section statement that HBSS is best. The 2025 meta-analysis supports HBSS in vitro (PDF p.1, p.3). Corrections: the 60-minute figure is the IADT management boundary, and the viability figure is 30 minutes; the earlier text presented 60 minutes as the point of PDL death. The Andreasen experimental comparison is a textbook point and is not claimed as IADT. The Andreasen 400-tooth figures (73% PDL healing within 5 minutes, 18% when stored) were first marked unverifiable in error; they are on Andreasen p.485 and are restored in the study text. The 'Viaspan' comparison was also removed because the IADT does not mention it.
