# P1 — Pulp treatment options for a complicated crown fracture of an adult central incisor (PREDICTED)

## STUDY MODE

**Context.** A complicated crown fracture exposes the pulp. In an adult the pulp is less reactive than in a child, but degenerative change makes capping less reliable with age. The choice of pulp treatment therefore depends on exposure size, time since injury, root stage and the state of the pulp (Berman et al, n.d., p.34-35).

**Options.** Andreasen et al describe pulp capping or partial pulpotomy for a vital pulp that is freshly exposed and bleeds, with calcium hydroxide or MTA over the exposure (2007, p.630). Pulpectomy is performed in mature teeth when conservative pulp treatment is not indicated, with removal of the pulp to 1 to 2 mm from the apex (p.624). Coronal or deep pulpotomy is used for larger or older exposures (Berman et al, n.d., p.34).

**Contraindications.** Capping and pulpotomy should not be performed where degenerative or inflammatory change is expected (Berman et al, n.d., p.35). Old or necrotic exposure points to root canal treatment. Where the restoration will need a post or a full crown, Kohli and Lee note that immediate pulpectomy may be needed (2026, p.612) [Likely].

**Outcome.** Both sources are narrative or clinical series, so any figure should be quoted with its source. Kohli and Lee report partial pulpotomy success above 95% when 2 to 4 mm of inflamed pulp is excised and a bioceramic material is placed; this figure is from a narrative review, and the textbook does not quantify success (2026, p.612). Cvek's protocol requires follow-up over at least three years (Berman et al, n.d., p.37).

## EXAM MODE

**Assessment.** Record the exposure size, the time since injury, the root stage, the bleeding at the exposure, and the response of the pulp to thermal and electrical tests on the injured and neighbouring teeth [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.304; Cohen's Pathways, Kahler, n.d., p.2560]. A freshly exposed, bleeding pulp in a vital tooth is a candidate for capping or partial pulpotomy [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.630].

**Mature teeth with a vital pulp.** Harty's states that partial pulpotomy is the first conservative treatment option for a crown fracture that exposes the pulp of a mature tooth [Harty's, n.d., p.257]. Harty's describes a shallow cavity of about 2 mm into the pulp chamber, with copious irrigation, then a calcium hydroxide cap, a resin-modified glass-ionomer dome and composite restoration [Harty's, n.d., p.255-256].

**Options by state of the pulp.** Direct capping or partial (Cvek) pulpotomy for a vital pulp with a fresh exposure, using calcium hydroxide or MTA [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.630; Berman et al, n.d., p.34-38]. Coronal or deep pulpotomy for a larger or older exposure [Berman et al, n.d., p.34]. Pulpectomy to 1 to 2 mm from the apex for a mature tooth in which conservative treatment is not indicated [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.624]. Root canal treatment for a necrotic pulp, and in a mature tooth where the whole clinical crown is lost, extirpation, root canal filling and a post-retained crown [Harty's, n.d., p.257]. The IADT prefers partial pulpotomy, including in complete roots [Bourguignon et al, 2020, p.320].

**Contraindications.** Capping and pulpotomy should not be done where degenerative or inflammatory change is expected. Age alone does not exclude conservative treatment [Berman et al, n.d., p.35].

**Success figures.** Kohli and Lee report partial pulpotomy success above 95% when 2 to 4 mm of inflamed pulp is excised and a bioceramic material placed [Kohli and Lee, 2026, p.612]. This is a narrative-review figure without a stated denominator, and the textbooks do not quantify success [Likely]. Krastl et al [2022] report 70-100% success in mature teeth [p.1007].

**Follow-up.** Test the pulp at 2 to 4 and 6 to 8 weeks, at 3, 6 and 12 months and yearly [Cohen's Pathways, Kahler, n.d., p.2560]. Cvek's protocol requires review over at least three years [Berman et al, n.d., p.37].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Choose pulp treatment by exposure size, time and root stage.
- Vital, fresh exposure: capping or partial pulpotomy.
- Pulpectomy to 1-2 mm from the apex in mature teeth.
- Follow up for at least three years.

**Audit note (IADT 2020, checked 2026-10-19).** The exam section now carries the IADT preference for partial pulpotomy or pulp capping in complicated crown fractures, including teeth with completed roots (Bourguignon et al, 2020, PDF p.7, printed p.320). The IADT separately recommends pulp removal in mature crown-root fractures (PDF p.9, printed p.322), so the answer distinguishes the fracture types. The Krastl et al (2022) success figures (90-100% immature, 70-100% mature) are observational and are labelled as such (PDF p.5).
