## STUDY MODE

**Problem.** An immature root with a necrotic pulp and apical periodontitis is difficult to treat. Trope identifies three problems: the infected canal cannot be disinfected with the standard protocol that uses aggressive instrumentation; filling is difficult because the open apex gives no barrier; and the thin roots are more prone to fracture (Trope, 2010, p.313).

**Approach.** Trope describes a disinfection that does not rely on instrumentation, followed by a hard-tissue barrier at the apex, so that the canal can be filled. A calcium hydroxide or MTA barrier is placed, and the canal is filled once the barrier has set (Trope, 2010, p.313-324). Berman's guide describes apexification with calcium hydroxide, with the second appointment two weeks to a month after the first (n.d., p.40).

**Revascularisation.** Where the pulp is vital and the apex is open, revascularisation is possible if the tooth is replanted early. The reported rate is 18 to 40% after replantation for foramen larger than 1.1 mm (Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.262). In completed roots revascularisation is rare (Andreasen et al, 2007, p.482).

**Regenerative procedures.** Kohli and Lee note that regenerative procedures may be considered where the pulp becomes necrotic in an immature tooth (2026, p.619). The approach is not described in detail in the sources read for this draft.

**Monitoring.** Kohli and Lee advise monitoring, with endodontic intervention if infection develops (2026, p.619). Andreasen et al note that, when inflammatory resorption appears in an immature tooth, endodontic therapy with calcium hydroxide is started at once to eliminate periapical inflammation and arrest the resorption (2007, p.477).

## EXAM MODE

**Problems.** An immature root with a necrotic pulp and apical periodontitis presents three problems. The infected canal cannot be disinfected with the standard protocol that uses aggressive instrumentation. Filling is difficult because the open apex gives no barrier. And the thin roots are more susceptible to fracture [Trope, 2010, p.313-314].

**Assessment.** Establish the open apex, the pulp status and any signs of infection. Revascularisation is the first aim where the pulp is still vital and the tooth was replanted early. The reported rate is 18 to 40% for foramina larger than 1.1 mm after replantation [Sigurdsson and Bourguignon, n.d., p.262]. In a completed root, revascularisation is rare [Andreasen et al, 2007, p.482].

**Disinfection.** Trope describes disinfection without aggressive root canal instrumentation, so that the apical tissue is not further damaged [Trope, 2010, p.313-314]. Berman's guide describes apexification technique with wide coronal access to include the pulp horns, debridement with 0.5 to 2.5% sodium hypochlorite or 0.2% chlorhexidine followed by saline, and a calcium hydroxide paste dressing [Berman et al, n.d., p.40].

**Apical barrier.** A calcium hydroxide or MTA barrier of about 3 to 4 mm is placed at the apex. Trope describes an MTA barrier left with a wet cotton pellet for at least six hours before the canal is filled [Trope, 2010, p.317] [Likely]. The barrier allows a hard-tissue plug to form and gives an apical stop for filling [Trope, 2010, p.313-317]. IADT: calcium hydroxide intracanal for up to one month (p.337); endodontics only if necrosis and infection are definite (p.335).

**Timing.** The second appointment for apexification is two weeks to one month after the first [Berman et al, n.d., p.40]. Calcium hydroxide is changed at intervals until a barrier has formed, and the time needed depends on the root stage and periapical status [Berman et al, n.d., p.40] [Likely].

**Regenerative options and monitoring.** Kohli and Lee advise monitoring, with endodontic intervention if infection develops, and mention regenerative procedures as an option where the pulp is necrotic in an immature tooth [Kohli and Lee, 2026, p.619]. The regenerative approach is not described in detail in the sources read for this answer. The IADT aim is revascularisation; if it fails, apexification or root canal treatment follows [Fouad et al, 2020, p.335].

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Immature necrotic tooth: disinfection without aggressive instrumentation.
- Apical barrier: calcium hydroxide or MTA.
- Revascularisation 18-40% for open apices after replantation.
- Second appointment 2 weeks to 1 month.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Instrumenting an immature canal aggressively.
2. Filling before a barrier has formed.
3. Expecting revascularisation in a completed root.
