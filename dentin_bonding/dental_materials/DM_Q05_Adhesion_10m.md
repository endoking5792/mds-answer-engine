## STUDY MODE

**Definition.** Adhesion is the interfacial molecular/atomic attraction between two dissimilar materials (adhesive and adherend) in intimate contact, holding them together. [RECALL] [Certain]

**Theories of adhesion (materials-science background):**
1. **Mechanical theory** — adhesion arises purely from interlocking of the adhesive into surface irregularities/porosities of the adherend (micromechanical retention — resin tags into etched enamel, resin infiltration into demineralized dentin collagen).
2. **Adsorption theory** — adhesion arises from interatomic/intermolecular forces (van der Waals forces, hydrogen bonding, and primary chemical bonds — ionic/covalent) established at the interface once intimate contact (wetting) is achieved.
3. **Diffusion theory** — applicable where polymer chains from the adhesive diffuse into the adherend (more relevant to polymer-polymer bonding than tooth-resin bonding, but conceptually complete for a theories-of-adhesion answer).
4. **Electrostatic theory** — charge transfer across the interface contributes a (generally minor) adhesive force in some systems.

**Requirements for adhesion to occur:**
- **Wetting** — the adhesive must flow and spread over the adherend surface (a low contact angle indicates good wetting); hydrophilic monomers like HEMA specifically aid wetting of moist dentin.
- **Clean surface** — free of contamination (saliva, blood, debris, residual temporary-cement eugenol).
- **Intimate contact/surface energy** — adequate surface roughness/porosity (created by etching) increases effective surface area and surface energy available for bonding.
- **Low viscosity of the adhesive** at the point of application, to penetrate micro-porosities/collagen meshworks effectively.
- **Dimensional/chemical stability of the bond over time** — resistance to hydrolytic and (for dentin specifically) enzymatic (MMP-mediated) degradation.

**Classification in a dental context:** micromechanical adhesion (dominant for enamel; a major component for dentin's hybrid layer) vs chemical adhesion (true primary bonds — e.g. 10-MDP-to-calcium ionic bond, glass-ionomer's polyacrylic-acid-to-calcium ionic exchange). Macromechanical retention (undercuts, grooves, pins) is mechanical retention, NOT true adhesion, and should be distinguished explicitly.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Four theories of adhesion: mechanical, adsorption, diffusion, electrostatic — name all four even if dental bonding relies mainly on mechanical+adsorption.
- Wetting (low contact angle) is the key prerequisite — hydrophilic monomers (HEMA) specifically serve this function on moist dentin.
- Macromechanical retention (undercuts/grooves) is NOT true adhesion — a frequently tested distinction.
- Dental adhesion = micromechanical + chemical components, working together in modern (MDP-based) systems.
