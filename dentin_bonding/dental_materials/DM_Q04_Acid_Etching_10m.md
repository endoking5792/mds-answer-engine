## STUDY MODE

**Definition.** Acid etching is the deliberate chemical treatment of a tooth surface (enamel and/or dentin) with an acid, to remove the smear layer and demineralize the surface to a controlled depth, creating a micro-retentive, resin-infiltratable surface — first described by **Buonocore (1955)** on enamel. [RECALL] [Certain]

**Etchants used:** phosphoric acid (30-40%, gel or liquid — the conventional/gold-standard etchant), maleic acid (~10%, a weaker alternative), citric acid (weaker, occasional use), and self-etching acidic functional monomers (10-MDP, 4-MET, phenyl-P — etch and prime simultaneously, no separate rinse).

**Effect on enamel:** phosphoric acid dissolves enamel preferentially by prism core or periphery (depending on crystallite orientation), producing a classic **Type I (prism-core-preferential), Type II (prism-periphery-preferential), or Type III (mixed/irregular)** etch pattern — a highly retentive, high-surface-area micro-porous surface for resin-tag formation. Etch time 15-20s; dried to a **frosty-white** clinical endpoint.

**Effect on dentin, now with real numbers.** Modern 35% phosphoric acid demineralizes dentin to only **~4-5µm** depth (Van Noort's, PDF p.135); Nakabayashi's original work targeted a 5µm depth specifically. **A striking historical contrast**: Marzouk's describes the classical protocol as 35-50% acid for a full **60 seconds**, etching to ~**25 microns** (5-6× deeper) — demineralization depth has been deliberately reduced over time to match what resin can reliably infiltrate, not increased. Dentin must be kept **moist**, not dried to the enamel endpoint. [SRC]

**Technique variants:** total-etch (simultaneous enamel+dentin etching), selective-etch (phosphoric acid on enamel margins only, gentler self-etch treatment on dentin), and self-etch (acidic monomer etches+primes simultaneously, no separate rinse).

**Clinical significance:** acid etching is the enabling first step for virtually all adhesive dentistry — composite/compomer bonding, sealants/PRRs, resin-bonded bridges/splints, ceramic/composite indirect-restoration cementation, orthodontic bracket bonding — without it, resin would have negligible retention on an unetched, smear-layer-covered tooth surface.

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

**Definition:** chemical demineralization/smear-layer treatment creating a micro-retentive resin-bondable surface (Buonocore, 1955).

**Etchants:** phosphoric acid (30-40%, gold standard) > maleic/citric acid (weaker) > self-etch acidic monomers (10-MDP etc., etch+prime simultaneously).

**Enamel effect:** Type I/II/III etch patterns; 15-20s; dried to frosty-white endpoint.

**Dentin effect:** smear layer removal/modification + demineralization exposing collagen meshwork; ~15s; kept MOIST, not dried.

**Technique variants:** total-etch (simultaneous) / selective-etch (enamel-only phosphoric acid + gentler dentin treatment) / self-etch (simultaneous etch+prime, no rinse).

**Clinical significance:** enabling first step for all adhesive-dentistry procedures.

- Enamel: Type I/II/III etch patterns, dried to frosty-white. Dentin: smear layer management + demineralization, kept MOIST.
