## STUDY MODE

An **acid-etched resin-bonded splint** is a periodontal/orthodontic/trauma-stabilization appliance in which adjacent teeth are splinted together (or a wire/mesh/fiber framework is attached) using the acid-etch–composite-resin bonding technique, rather than by mechanical means (wiring through drilled holes, banding, etc.). [RECALL] [Certain]

**Technique:** the enamel surfaces of the teeth to be splinted are cleaned, isolated, and etched with phosphoric acid (30-40%, 15-20s), rinsed, and dried to the frosty-white endpoint; a bonding resin is applied and light-cured; a splinting material (orthodontic wire, fiber-reinforced ribbon/mesh, or a direct flowable-composite bridge between teeth) is placed and incorporated into/covered by composite resin, which is then light-cured and finished. [RECALL] [Certain]

**Clinical indications:** stabilization of mobile teeth in periodontally compromised dentitions (splinting adjacent teeth to distribute occlusal load), stabilization of luxated/avulsed teeth after traumatic dental injury (semi-rigid splinting, typically 2 weeks for luxation, up to 4 weeks for root fracture, per standard trauma-management protocols), temporary stabilization in orthodontic retention (bonded lingual/palatal retainers), and splinting following periodontal surgery.

**Advantages:** purely enamel-based (minimally invasive, no tooth preparation/drilling required), esthetic, reversible, and relies entirely on the well-established, predictable enamel acid-etch bonding mechanism — Buonocore's original 1955 technique achieved **~20MPa** bond strength (Philips', PDF p.276), and this remains the basis of the splint's retention: a purely micromechanical resin-tag interlock, since enamel offers no collagen substrate for hybrid-layer formation. [SRC]

**Limitations:** bond can debond under heavy occlusal/functional load if not adequately designed; requires good moisture/isolation control during placement; may trap plaque/debris at the resin-tooth margin if not well-finished, risking localized gingival inflammation.

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~7 minutes)*

**Definition:** teeth splinted together via acid-etch composite-resin bonding, not mechanical fixation.

**Technique:** etch enamel (phosphoric acid, 15-20s) → rinse/dry to frosty-white → bonding resin → incorporate splinting material (wire/fiber ribbon/composite bridge) → cure → finish.

**Indications:** periodontally mobile teeth, traumatic luxation/avulsion stabilization, orthodontic bonded retainers, post-periodontal-surgery splinting.

**Advantages:** minimally invasive (enamel-only, no drilling), esthetic, reversible.
**Limitations:** debonding risk under heavy load, moisture-sensitive placement, plaque-trap risk if poorly finished.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Acid-etched resin-bonded splint = enamel-only, micromechanical bonding of a splinting material to adjacent teeth.
- Key indications: periodontal mobility, traumatic luxation/avulsion, orthodontic retention, post-surgical splinting.
- Entirely reversible, minimally invasive, esthetic — key advantages over mechanical splinting methods.
