## STUDY MODE

**What "adhesive restorations" means.** A restoration retained primarily or entirely by adhesive bonding to tooth structure, rather than by classical mechanical retention form (slots, undercuts, grooves, pins) — the category that acid-etch/resin bonding technology made possible starting from Buonocore's 1955 enamel work. [RECALL] [Certain]

**Progress as of this question's early-1990s context:**
1. **From enamel-only to dentin-inclusive adhesive restorations.** Early adhesive restorations (1960s-70s) were essentially enamel-bonded-only (etched-enamel composite restorations in Class III/V cavities, acid-etched resin-bonded splints/bridges) because reliable dentin bonding did not yet exist. By the early 1990s, dentin-bonding systems (3rd-generation multi-step etch-and-rinse, building on Nakabayashi's 1982 hybrid-layer concept) had become clinically available, extending adhesive restoration to full Class I/II/IV composite restorations relying on dentin bonding as well as enamel bonding. [RECALL] [Likely]
2. **Expansion of clinical indications.** Acid-etch/resin-bonded splints (periodontal/trauma stabilization), resin-bonded (Maryland) bridges (avoiding full-coverage crown preparation), and composite resin as a genuinely viable posterior restorative option (not just anterior esthetic use) — all representing the growing clinical scope of "adhesive restorations" by this era.
3. **Materials progress enabling this expansion:** improved composite resin filler technology (smaller particle sizes, better polish/wear resistance) working in tandem with improving bonding-agent reliability — the restoration and the adhesive advanced together, not independently.
4. **Remaining limitations at this era (honestly noted):** dentin bond strengths, while much improved over 1st/2nd-generation attempts, were still considered less reliable and lower than enamel bond strengths; postoperative sensitivity following dentin-bonded restorations was a genuine, actively-discussed clinical concern; and polymerization-shrinkage stress in bonded Class II composite restorations (a newly-relevant problem once composite began replacing amalgam in load-bearing posterior cavities) was an actively emerging research/clinical concern at this time.
