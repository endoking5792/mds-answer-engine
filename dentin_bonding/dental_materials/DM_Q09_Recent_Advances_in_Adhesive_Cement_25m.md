## STUDY MODE

**Adhesive (resin) cements — core concept.** Unlike zinc phosphate or glass-ionomer luting cements (which rely mainly on mechanical retention/weak ionic adhesion respectively), resin cements achieve adhesion via the same fundamental mechanisms as direct dentin bonding agents — micromechanical interlock (hybrid layer/resin tags) and, in MDP-containing formulations, genuine chemical bonding — applied to the cementation of indirect restorations (crowns, bridges, inlays/onlays, veneers, fiber posts). [RECALL] [Certain]

**Classification of resin cements, with real quantified properties (Craig's, PDF p.289-292):**
1. **Total-etch resin cements** — multi-step, most technique-sensitive, highest bond strength.
2. **Self-etch resin cements** — fewer steps.
3. **Self-adhesive resin cements** (a significant later development) — combine etching, priming, and cementation into one material. **Real chemistry** (Craig's p.291-292; named monomers per Perdigão p.157-158, 194-196: 4-META, PMGDM, phenyl-P, MDP, BMP, Penta-P): initial pH **~2**, shifting toward neutral as acid groups react with calcium from hydroxyapatite/ion-leachable fillers; flexural strength **50-100MPa**, compressive strength **200-240MPa**, film thickness **15-20µm**, enamel shear bond strength **3-15MPa** (lower than phosphoric-acid-etched bonds). RelyX Unicem (the first commercial self-adhesive cement) forms a very thin (~0.2µm) hybrid layer with limited smear-layer dissolution and needs sustained seating pressure for intimate adaptation. **A real, important clinical caution**: using a self-adhesive cement as a CORE-BUILDUP material (not just post luting) has been linked to glass-ceramic-crown cracking after long-term water storage, attributed to hygroscopic expansion — explicitly "cannot be recommended" for that use (Sterzenbach et al. 2014, cited in Perdigão's p.195-196). [SRC]

**Real resin-cement property comparison across all three categories** (Craig's, PDF p.289-290): degree of conversion 50-73% self-cure / 67-85% light-cure; compressive strength 180-300MPa; flexural strength 80-100MPa (ISO 4049 minimum 50MPa); elastic modulus 4-10GPa; film thickness 13-20µm (ISO max 50µm); shear bond strength to dentin 12-18MPa; polymerization shrinkage 2-5%. [SRC]

**Restoration-side adhesive protocol (a distinct, essential half of "adhesive cementation").** For silica-based ceramics: hydrofluoric-acid etching or airborne-particle abrasion, followed by a **silane coupling agent** (which chemically bridges the inorganic ceramic surface and the organic resin cement). For zirconia (which does not etch with HF acid and has no silica phase for silane to bond to): airborne-particle abrasion plus an **MDP-containing primer**, which bonds chemically to the zirconia surface via the same MDP-to-metal-oxide mechanism relevant to zirconia's chemistry.

**Advances specific to this sitting's era (mid-to-late 1990s):** improved dual-cure resin-cement chemistry (allowing adequate polymerization beneath relatively opaque/thick indirect restorations, where light-curing alone is insufficient); refinement of silane-coupling-agent chemistry for porcelain/ceramic bonding; and growing clinical adoption of resin-cemented all-ceramic restorations (made possible specifically by these adhesive-cementation advances, since all-ceramic restorations otherwise lack adequate mechanical retention form compared with metal-based restorations).

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~40 minutes)*

**I. Adhesive cement core concept:** resin cements bond via micromechanical + chemical mechanisms (like direct DBAs), applied to indirect-restoration/fiber-post cementation.

**II. Classification:** total-etch resin cement (multi-step, technique-sensitive) / self-etch resin cement (fewer steps) / self-adhesive resin cement (later development, single-step, acidic monomers within the cement itself).

**III. Restoration-side protocol (essential second half):** silica ceramics → HF-etch/airborne-particle abrasion + silane; zirconia → airborne-particle abrasion + MDP-containing primer (no silane, since zirconia has no silica phase).

**IV. Era-specific advances:** dual-cure chemistry refinement, silane-coupling-agent improvement, growing all-ceramic-restoration adoption enabled by these advances.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Adhesive/resin cements use the SAME fundamental bonding mechanisms as direct DBAs, applied to indirect-restoration cementation.
- Cementation is a TWO-SIDED adhesive protocol: tooth side (etch/bond) AND restoration side (silane for silica ceramics; MDP-primer for zirconia).
- Real resin-cement properties: DC% 50-85%, compressive strength 180-300MPa, flexural 80-100MPa, modulus 4-10GPa, film thickness 13-20µm, shrinkage 2-5%.
- Self-adhesive cements: initial pH~2, named monomers (4-META/PMGDM/MDP etc.), only ~0.2µm hybrid layer — and should NOT be used as a core-buildup material (glass-ceramic-crown cracking risk, Sterzenbach 2014).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Treating "adhesive cement" as identical to a direct-restoration dentin bonding agent without naming the restoration-side protocol, which is the genuinely distinct, essential second half of cementation adhesion.
- Using silane on zirconia (incorrect — zirconia needs an MDP-containing primer, not silane, since it lacks a silica phase).
