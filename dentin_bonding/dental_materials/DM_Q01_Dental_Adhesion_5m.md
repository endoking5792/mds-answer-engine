## STUDY MODE

**Adhesion**, in the materials-science sense, is the state in which two dissimilar surfaces (an adherend and an adhesive) are held together by interfacial forces, which may be mechanical (micromechanical interlocking into surface irregularities/porosities) or chemical (primary chemical bonds — ionic, covalent, or hydrogen bonding — across the interface), or a combination of both. [RECALL] [Certain]

**In dentistry**, adhesion specifically refers to the bonding of a restorative material (resin composite, resin cement, some glass-ionomer-family materials) to tooth structure (enamel and/or dentin) or to another material (metal, ceramic). [RECALL] [Certain]

**Types/mechanisms of dental adhesion:**
1. **Micromechanical adhesion** — the dominant mechanism for enamel (etched prism micro-porosities, resin tags) and a major component for dentin (resin-infiltrated collagen meshwork = hybrid layer).
2. **Chemical adhesion** — true primary bonding, e.g. the ionic bond between 10-MDP's phosphate group and calcium in hydroxyapatite (modern self-etch/universal adhesives), or glass-ionomer's polyacrylic-acid-to-calcium ionic exchange bond.
3. **Macromechanical retention** — not true adhesion but often discussed alongside it; undercuts, grooves, pins — classical mechanical retention independent of any interfacial bonding force.

**Requirements for good adhesion:** intimate contact (wetting) between adhesive and adherend surfaces, minimal contact angle, a clean surface free of contamination, adequate surface energy/roughness, and dimensional stability of the bond over time (resistance to hydrolytic/thermal/mechanical stress).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Adhesion = interfacial force, mechanical and/or chemical, between dissimilar surfaces.
- Micromechanical (resin tags/hybrid layer) is the dominant dental mechanism; chemical (MDP-calcium, GIC-calcium) adds a hydrolysis-resistant component.
- Macromechanical retention (undercuts/grooves) is NOT true adhesion — a common conceptual confusion worth distinguishing explicitly.
- Requires wetting, clean surface, adequate surface energy, dimensional stability.
