## STUDY MODE

**Definition and function.** DBAs are resin systems achieving a durable chemical/micromechanical bond between restorative materials and dentin — a wet, tubular, collagen-rich (30% organic) substrate, qualitatively different from enamel's near-fully mineralized structure. [RECALL] [Certain]

**Classification.** Etch-and-rinse (3-step: separate etch/primer/adhesive; 2-step: etch + combined primer-adhesive) vs self-etch (2-step: self-etch primer + adhesive; 1-step/all-in-one) vs universal/multi-mode (10-MDP-based, any strategy). Generation numbering (1st-8th) is now considered scientifically imprecise, overlapping, and not reliably tied to clinical performance.

**Chemical composition (materials-science emphasis):**
- **Hydrophilic monomer** — HEMA, essential for wetting moist demineralized dentin; also contributes to water sorption/plasticization of the cured resin (a durability-relevant property).
- **Crosslinking/hydrophobic monomer** — Bis-GMA (high viscosity, requires diluent monomers), UDMA (lower viscosity, good mechanical properties), TEGDMA (diluent, increases polymerization shrinkage).
- **Solvent** — acetone (fast-evaporating, but can cause rapid, uneven collagen collapse if not carefully handled), ethanol, or water-based systems; solvent choice affects clinical handling and technique sensitivity.
- **Acidic functional monomer** (self-etch/universal) — 10-MDP (forms ionic bond with hydroxyapatite calcium), 4-MET, phenyl-P.
- **Photoinitiator system** — camphorquinone + tertiary amine (the standard visible-light-cure system); some products use alternative initiators (e.g. Lucirin TPO) for reduced yellowing.
- **Fillers** (some modern systems) — nanofillers for improved mechanical properties and potentially reduced polymerization shrinkage of the adhesive layer itself.

**Properties relevant to clinical performance:** bond strength (shear/tensile/microtensile, measured in vitro), degree of conversion (incomplete polymerization leaves unreacted monomer, a biocompatibility and mechanical-property concern), water sorption/solubility (affects long-term hydrolytic stability), film thickness (thin, uniform films generally perform better than thick, uneven ones), and elastic modulus (an adhesive layer with appropriate flexibility can absorb polymerization-shrinkage stress from the overlying composite without failing at the still-maturing interface).

**Mechanism of bonding.** Smear layer management → acid demineralization exposing the collagen meshwork → hydrophilic resin infiltration forming the hybrid layer (Nakabayashi, 1982) + resin tags → polymerization; MDP-containing systems add ionic MDP-calcium chemical bonding, relatively hydrolysis-resistant.

**Biocompatibility considerations.** Residual unreacted monomer (especially HEMA, known to be cytotoxic at higher concentrations in vitro) can diffuse toward the pulp in deep cavities with minimal remaining dentin thickness; adequate polymerization/degree of conversion and appropriate use of liners in very deep preparations mitigate this risk.

**I. Definition/classification:** etch-and-rinse (3-/2-step) / self-etch (2-/1-step) / universal — not generation number.

**II. Composition:** HEMA (hydrophilic/wetting) + Bis-GMA/UDMA/TEGDMA (crosslinking/mechanical) + solvent (acetone/ethanol/water) + 10-MDP(self-etch/universal, chemical bonding) + photoinitiator ± nanofillers.

**III. Properties:** bond strength, degree of conversion, water sorption/solubility, film thickness, elastic modulus (stress absorption).

**IV. Mechanism:** smear layer management → demineralization → hybrid layer/resin-tag formation → polymerization ± MDP-calcium bond.

**V. Biocompatibility:** residual monomer (HEMA) cytotoxicity risk in deep cavities with minimal RDT — mitigated by adequate cure and appropriate liner use.
