## STUDY MODE

**Definition.** A dental bonding (adhesive) agent is a resin-based material that creates a durable chemical and/or micromechanical union between a restorative material (composite, compomer, some cements) and tooth structure (enamel and/or dentin). [RECALL] [Certain]

**Classification — by clinical strategy (the modern, preferred classification over "generation" numbering, since generation numbers overlap and don't reliably track clinical performance):**
1. **Etch-and-rinse (total-etch)** — separate phosphoric-acid etch step, rinsed, followed by primer ± adhesive application. Subdivided into **3-step** (etch, primer, adhesive — separate bottles) and **2-step** (etch, then a combined primer+adhesive "one-bottle" system).
2. **Self-etch** — an acidic primer/monomer etches and primes simultaneously, no separate rinse. Subdivided into **2-step** (self-etch primer, then separate adhesive resin) and **1-step (all-in-one)** (etchant+primer+adhesive combined in one bottle).
3. **Universal (multi-mode) adhesives** — a single product usable in etch-and-rinse, self-etch, or selective-etch mode, typically containing the functional monomer **10-MDP** for chemical bonding to tooth structure (and, often, compatibility with multiple restorative/ceramic substrates). [RECALL] [Certain]

**Composition (typical components across most systems):** a **hydrophilic monomer** (commonly HEMA, for wetting the moist, demineralized dentin surface), **crosslinking/hydrophobic monomers** (e.g. Bis-GMA, UDMA, TEGDMA, contributing mechanical strength and compatibility with the composite resin above), an **acidic functional monomer** where self-etching capability is needed (10-MDP, 4-MET, phenyl-P), a **solvent** (acetone, ethanol, or water, which must evaporate fully for proper bond strength and is a real source of technique sensitivity), and **initiators** for polymerization (camphorquinone/amine for light-cure; some systems are dual- or self-cure for use under indirect restorations). [RECALL] [Certain]

**Mechanism (brief):** removal/modification of smear layer → demineralization of the surface (acid) → infiltration of hydrophilic resin monomer into the demineralized collagen meshwork on dentin (forming the hybrid layer) or into the etched enamel-prism micro-porosities (forming resin tags) → polymerization, locking the resin-tooth interface in place; MDP-containing systems add a chemical (ionic, MDP-calcium) bonding component. [RECALL] [Certain]

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~14 minutes)*

**Definition:** resin-based material uniting restorative material to tooth structure, chemically and/or micromechanically.

**Classification (by strategy, not generation):**
| Category | Subtype | Steps |
|---|---|---|
| Etch-and-rinse | 3-step | Etch → Primer → Adhesive |
| Etch-and-rinse | 2-step | Etch → Primer+Adhesive combined |
| Self-etch | 2-step | Self-etch primer → Adhesive |
| Self-etch | 1-step (all-in-one) | Etch+Primer+Adhesive combined |
| Universal/multi-mode | — | One product, any of the above modes |

**Composition:** hydrophilic monomer (HEMA) + crosslinking monomer (Bis-GMA/UDMA) + acidic functional monomer (10-MDP etc., self-etch systems) + solvent (acetone/ethanol/water) + photoinitiator.

**Mechanism:** smear layer management → demineralization → resin infiltration (hybrid layer/resin tags) → polymerization ± chemical (MDP-calcium) bonding.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Bonding agents classified by etch STRATEGY (etch-and-rinse 3-step/2-step, self-etch 2-step/1-step, universal) — preferred over the imprecise "generation" numbering.
- Core composition: hydrophilic monomer (HEMA) + crosslinking monomer (Bis-GMA/UDMA) + solvent + (for self-etch/universal) acidic functional monomer (10-MDP).
- Mechanism: smear layer management → demineralization → resin infiltration → polymerization, ± MDP-calcium chemical bond.
- Solvent evaporation is a genuine technique-sensitivity point — incomplete evaporation weakens the bond.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES

- Describing the mechanism as purely micromechanical, omitting MDP's chemical bonding contribution in modern systems.
