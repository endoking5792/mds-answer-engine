## STUDY MODE

The core **principles** underlying successful dentin bonding (distinct from "mechanism," which describes the step-by-step process — see CD_Q01) are:

1. **Smear layer management principle.** The smear layer must be either removed (etch-and-rinse) or deliberately modified/incorporated (self-etch) — never simply ignored — since an untreated smear layer is itself a weak, poorly-bonded interposed layer. [RECALL] [Certain]
2. **Micromechanical retention via hybrid-layer formation.** Demineralization must expose a collagen scaffold that resin can infiltrate; the resulting resin-collagen hybrid layer (Nakabayashi) is the principal retentive mechanism on dentin, analogous to (but mechanistically distinct from) enamel's resin-tag interlock. [RECALL] [Certain]
3. **Wet bonding principle.** Because collagen collapses and loses its 3D porosity when desiccated, dentin must be kept moist between etching and resin application — a principle that directly reverses the older "etch and dry thoroughly" teaching once applied uniformly to both enamel and dentin. [RECALL] [Certain]
4. **Complete resin infiltration principle.** The depth of resin monomer penetration must match the depth of demineralization; any mismatch leaves vulnerable, unprotected collagen. [RECALL] [Certain]
5. **Chemical adhesion principle (where applicable).** Functional acidic monomers (10-MDP) can form a genuine ionic bond to residual calcium in hydroxyapatite, supplementing micromechanical retention with a chemical bond that is more resistant to hydrolytic breakdown. [RECALL] [Likely]
6. **Stress-compatibility principle.** The adhesive layer must have adequate elasticity/flexibility to absorb polymerization-shrinkage stress from the overlying composite without failing at the still-maturing bonded interface.
7. **Biocompatibility principle.** The adhesive and its components (residual monomer, solvent) must not provoke a sustained pulpal inflammatory response, particularly relevant in deep cavities with minimal remaining dentin thickness.
8. **Durability/degradation-resistance principle.** The bonded interface must resist both hydrolytic (water-driven ester-bond breakdown) and enzymatic (MMP-mediated collagenolytic) degradation over years of function — the central unresolved challenge in contemporary adhesive dentistry.

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

**Eight governing principles:**
1. Smear layer must be actively managed (removed or modified), never ignored.
2. Micromechanical retention via hybrid-layer formation (resin-collagen interlock).
3. Wet bonding — moist dentin preserves the collagen scaffold for infiltration.
4. Resin infiltration depth must match demineralization depth.
5. Chemical adhesion (MDP-calcium ionic bond) supplements micromechanical retention where present.
6. Adhesive layer must absorb polymerization-shrinkage stress (stress compatibility).
7. Biocompatibility — no sustained pulpal inflammatory response.
8. Durability — resistance to hydrolytic AND enzymatic (MMP) degradation, the central ongoing challenge.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- "Principles" ≠ "mechanism": principles are the governing rules (smear layer management, wet bonding, infiltration-depth matching, stress compatibility, biocompatibility, durability); mechanism is the step-by-step process.
- Wet bonding is a principle that directly reverses older "dry and etch" teaching, specific to dentin's collagen-collapse vulnerability.
- Durability against hydrolytic AND enzymatic (MMP) degradation is the central unresolved principle in contemporary adhesive dentistry.
