## STUDY MODE

### I. Bonding agents — classification and composition (condensed; full detail in CD_Q26)
Etch-and-rinse (3-step/2-step) vs self-etch (2-step/1-step) vs universal/multi-mode — classified by etch strategy. Composition: HEMA + Bis-GMA/UDMA + solvent + acidic functional monomer (self-etch/universal) + photoinitiator.

### II. Clinical techniques (the section this question specifically foregrounds)

**Total-etch (etch-and-rinse) technique:** apply 30-40% phosphoric acid to enamel AND dentin simultaneously → 15-20s (enamel)/15s (dentin) → rinse thoroughly → dry enamel to frosty-white, keep dentin MOIST (not desiccated) → apply primer (3-step) or combined primer-adhesive (2-step) → light-cure → place restorative material. **Self-etch technique:** apply the self-etch primer (2-step) or all-in-one adhesive (1-step) directly to the smear-layer-covered surface, allow the required dwell time (manufacturer-specified, typically 10-20s) for simultaneous etch+prime, **no separate rinse step**, light-dry gently to evaporate solvent, apply separate adhesive resin (2-step systems only), light-cure.

**Selective-etch technique:** phosphoric acid applied to enamel margins ONLY for 15-20s, rinsed, enamel dried to frosty-white; self-etch primer or universal adhesive then applied to the entire preparation (both the etched enamel and the unetched dentin) per the self-etch protocol above — combining enamel's preference for aggressive etching with dentin's preference for gentler treatment.

**Technique for universal adhesives specifically:** can be used in any of the three modes above at the operator's discretion; manufacturer instructions specify whether "active" scrubbing-in application (improving monomer penetration, particularly into dentin) is required versus passive application.

**Indirect-restoration bonding technique (a genuinely distinct clinical scenario):** for ceramic/composite indirect restorations, BOTH a tooth-side adhesive protocol (as above) AND a restoration-side protocol are needed — hydrofluoric-acid etching or airborne-particle abrasion plus a **silane coupling agent** for silica-based ceramics, or a different surface treatment (airborne-particle abrasion plus an MDP-containing primer) for zirconia, before cementation with a resin cement.

### III. Factors governing technique choice
Substrate (enamel vs dentin vs both; sound vs sclerotic/caries-affected dentin), restoration type (direct composite vs indirect bonded restoration vs core build-up vs fiber post), operator moisture-control confidence, and postoperative-sensitivity risk profile of the specific case — see this topic's CD_Q23 for substrate-specific technique modifications in detail.
