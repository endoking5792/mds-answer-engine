## STUDY MODE

Adhesion is the foundational technology enabling virtually all modern esthetic dentistry, because it permits **conservative, minimally-invasive, tooth-coloured restorations that would otherwise have no mechanical retention form**. [RECALL] [Certain]

**Applications specific to esthetic dentistry:**
1. **Direct composite restorations** (Class III/IV/V, diastema closure, peg-lateral build-ups) — rely entirely on enamel/dentin adhesion, since esthetic cavity designs deliberately avoid retentive grooves/slots that would be visible or require extra tooth removal.
2. **Laminate veneers** (direct or indirect porcelain/composite) — a thin shell bonded almost entirely by adhesion; near-zero mechanical retention form is possible or desirable.
3. **Resin-bonded (Maryland) bridges** — adhesion to etched enamel/metal substructure, avoiding full-coverage crown preparation.
4. **Enamel microabrasion/bleaching-adjunct restorations** and **fissure sealants/PRRs** performed for esthetic/preventive reasons.
5. **Ceramic bonding** (bonded inlays/onlays, crowns) — requires a parallel adhesive protocol on the ceramic side (hydrofluoric acid etch or airborne-particle abrasion + silane coupling agent) in addition to tooth-side adhesive bonding. **Real bond-strength figures** (Alani & Patel, PDF p.52): the double-bond (resin cement + substrate-specific primer) mechanism achieves **20-30 MPa, irrespective of which indirect-restoration material is used** (ceramic, metal, or composite), since the same underlying chemistry applies once each substrate's own correct surface treatment is used.
6. **Immediate Dentine Sealing (IDS)** — a genuinely important technique not in the original draft (Paul & Magne, cited in Sturdevant's, PDF p.191-192): a 4th-generation DBA is used to seal freshly-cut dentine immediately after tooth preparation for an indirect restoration, BEFORE the impression/digital scan and before final cementation — avoiding hybrid-layer collapse under impression-material seating pressure, and letting the dentine bond "mature" before definitive cementation. A direct esthetic/adhesive-quality benefit specific to indirect restorations. [SRC]
7. **Shade blending and translucency effects** — adhesive resin cements/luting agents with try-in shades allow fine control of the final restoration's optical appearance, something not achievable with purely mechanically retained restorations.

**Why adhesion specifically serves esthetics:** (a) permits minimal tooth reduction, preserving natural tooth colour/translucency gradients; (b) eliminates visible metal retentive features (pins, grooves); (c) a well-bonded margin minimizes the visible interface line and reduces marginal staining/microleakage-related discoloration over time; (d) enables incremental, layered restorative techniques matching natural enamel/dentin optical zones.

## EXAM MODE
*(Estimated writing time: ~16 minutes)*

**Applications:** direct composite esthetic restorations, laminate veneers, resin-bonded bridges, bonded ceramic inlays/onlays/crowns (with silane/HF-etch on the ceramic side, double-bond mechanism 20-30MPa regardless of restoration material), IDS for indirect restorations (seal dentine before impression, avoids hybrid-layer collapse under seating pressure), fissure sealants/PRRs, adhesive luting for shade control.

**Why adhesion enables esthetics:** minimal tooth reduction (preserves natural optical zones) → no visible mechanical retention features → tighter/less-staining margins → layered/shade-matched technique possible only with a reliable adhesive bond, not mechanical retention alone.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Adhesion enables esthetic dentistry by removing the NEED for mechanical retention features that would otherwise be visible or require extra tooth removal.
- Key applications: direct composites, veneers, resin-bonded bridges, bonded ceramics (needs silane/HF-etch on ceramic side too; double-bond mechanism gives 20-30MPa regardless of restoration material), IDS, sealants/PRRs.
- IDS (Immediate Dentine Sealing) = seal dentine right after prep, BEFORE impression — avoids hybrid-layer collapse under impression-seating pressure, lets the bond mature before cementation.
- Well-bonded margins reduce microleakage-related staining — an esthetic AND mechanical benefit.
