## STUDY MODE

**Total-etch (etch-and-rinse):** a separate phosphoric-acid (30-40%) etch step removes the smear layer entirely from both enamel and dentin, exposing dentinal tubules and demineralizing a collagen-rich surface layer for mechanical interlocking; the acid is then rinsed off before primer/adhesive application. Subdivided into **3-step** (separate etch, primer, adhesive bottles) and **2-step** (etch, then combined primer+adhesive). [LIT] [Certain]

**Self-etch:** an acidic functional monomer etches and primes **simultaneously**, without a separate rinse step; the smear layer is modified/incorporated rather than removed. Subdivided into **2-step** (self-etch primer, then separate adhesive resin) and **1-step/all-in-one** (etchant+primer+adhesive combined). Self-etch primers are typically **weakly acidic**, producing a shallower, more gradual demineralization than phosphoric acid. [LIT] [Certain]

**Comparative evidence — genuinely mixed, stated rather than resolved into one verdict:**
- A narrative literature review found **dentin bond strength significantly higher with total-etch than self-etch**, and **self-etch showing significantly greater microleakage on enamel** than total-etch — concluding total-etch gives superior bond strength and microleakage resistance. [LIT] [Likely]
- A microtensile bond-strength study found self-etching adhesives performing **significantly worse than total-etch on both enamel and dentin**. [LIT] [Likely]
- However, a 2022 systematic review/meta-analysis specific to **universal (multi-mode) adhesives** (pooling 15 studies) found **no statistically significant difference** between etch-and-rinse and self-etch modes for dentin bond strength, immediate or long-term — a genuinely different conclusion from the conventional-adhesive comparative studies above, likely reflecting how far universal-adhesive chemistry (10-MDP) has closed the gap that existed with older self-etch systems. [LIT] [Likely]
- Self-etch systems consistently show an advantage in **reduced postoperative sensitivity** and **simpler, faster clinical technique**, a genuine clinical trade-off against the bond-strength/microleakage data above.

**Practical synthesis (not a settled scientific verdict, but the pragmatic clinical position most sources converge on):** total-etch retains an edge for **enamel** bonding specifically (hence the popularity of selective-etch technique); self-etch/universal systems are increasingly competitive for **dentin** bonding, especially with modern MDP-based chemistry, and offer real technique-sensitivity and sensitivity-reduction advantages.

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
The "which is better" framing of this question invites a single winner; the actual evidence does not support one. **What this supports:** total-etch's enamel-bonding/microleakage advantage is corroborated by more than one source type (narrative review + microtensile study); self-etch/universal's closing of the dentin-bond-strength gap is corroborated by the more rigorous meta-analysis evidence specific to modern (MDP-based) universal systems. **What this does not support:** a confident single-sentence verdict either way — the comparison depends on substrate (enamel vs dentin), adhesive generation/chemistry (older self-etch vs modern universal), and the outcome measured (bond strength vs microleakage vs postoperative sensitivity vs clinical speed). | | Total-etch | Self-etch |
|---|---|---|
| Smear layer | Removed | Modified/incorporated |
| Etch depth/type | Phosphoric acid, deeper | Weak acidic monomer, shallower |
| Steps | 3-step or 2-step | 2-step or 1-step (all-in-one) |
| Dentin bond strength (conventional studies) | Higher | Lower |
| Dentin bond strength (2022 meta-analysis, universal adhesives) | No significant difference vs self-etch | No significant difference vs etch-and-rinse |
| Enamel microleakage | Lower | Higher (conventional self-etch) |
| Postoperative sensitivity | More common (over-etch/desiccation risk) | Less common |
| Technique sensitivity | Higher (multi-step, timing-critical) | Lower (fewer steps) |

**Verdict: no single universal winner** — total-etch retains an edge on enamel/microleakage; modern MDP-based self-etch/universal systems have closed much of the dentin-bond-strength gap; self-etch wins on technique ease/sensitivity reduction.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Total-etch = separate phosphoric-acid etch, removes smear layer; self-etch = acidic monomer etches+primes simultaneously, modifies smear layer.
- Comparative evidence is genuinely mixed by substrate: total-etch favored for enamel/microleakage; dentin-bond-strength gap has closed substantially with modern MDP-based self-etch/universal systems (2022 meta-analysis: no significant difference).
- Self-etch's real advantage: lower postoperative sensitivity, simpler/faster technique.
- No single "which is better" answer — depends on substrate, adhesive generation, and outcome measured.
