## STUDY MODE

### I. Enamel adhesion
Buonocore's 1955 acid-etch technique: phosphoric acid (30-40%) dissolves enamel preferentially by prism core/periphery (depending on crystallite orientation), producing Type I/II/III etch patterns and a highly retentive, high-surface-area micro-porous surface. Resin flows into these micro-porosities and polymerizes, forming **resin tags** — a purely micromechanical bond, since enamel's near-total mineral content offers no substrate for a hybrid-layer-type resin-collagen interlock. ### II. Dentin adhesion
Fundamentally different because dentin is wet, tubular, and collagen-rich. Smear layer management (removal in etch-and-rinse; modification/incorporation in self-etch) → acid demineralization exposing the collagen meshwork → hydrophilic resin infiltration forming the **hybrid layer** (Nakabayashi, 1982) + resin tags into tubules → polymerization; MDP-containing (self-etch/universal) systems add a genuine ionic MDP-calcium chemical bond. (Full classification/composition/mechanism detail as in this topic's CD_Q04/CD_Q15/CD_Q26.)

### III. Factors affecting adhesion to each substrate
Enamel: prism orientation/etch pattern, degree of mineralization (fluorotic/aprismatic enamel etches less predictably), contamination. Dentin: depth (superficial vs deep), sclerosis, caries-affected status, moisture control, smear layer variability (see CD_Q16/CD_Q23 for full detail).

### IV. Clinical applications of dental adhesion
Direct composite/compomer restorations, bonded indirect restorations (requiring a parallel restoration-side adhesive protocol — silane for silica ceramics, MDP-primer for zirconia), resin-bonded bridges, fissure sealants/PRRs, core build-ups, fiber-post cementation, amalgam bonding, orthodontic bracket bonding (a further enamel-adhesion application worth naming in a broad "dental adhesion" essay).

### V. Durability and the central unresolved problem
Both enamel and dentin adhesion are subject to long-term degradation, but the mechanisms differ: enamel bonds degrade mainly through mechanical fatigue/marginal breakdown of the resin-enamel interface over years; dentin bonds additionally face **hydrolytic and MMP-mediated enzymatic collagen degradation**, a genuinely intrinsic, unsolved durability limitation specific to the collagen-containing hybrid layer.
