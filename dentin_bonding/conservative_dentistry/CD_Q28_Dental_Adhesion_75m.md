## STUDY MODE

### I. Enamel adhesion
Buonocore's **1955** acid-etch technique (*J Dent Res* 34:849-853, confirmed per Sturdevant's/Philips' own bibliographies; Marzouk's independently states 1959 elsewhere in this topic's retrieval — a genuine, flagged discrepancy) produced an original enamel bond strength of **~20MPa** (Philips', PDF p.276). Phosphoric acid (30-40%) dissolves enamel preferentially by prism core/periphery, producing Type I/II/III etch patterns; resin flows into these micro-porosities to form **resin tags, real dimensions ~6µm diameter and 10-20µm length** (Philips', PDF p.276) — a purely micromechanical bond, since enamel's near-total mineral content offers no collagen phase for a hybrid-layer-type interlock. [SRC]

### II. Dentin adhesion
Fundamentally different because dentin is wet, tubular, and collagen-rich. Smear layer management (removal in etch-and-rinse; modification/incorporation in self-etch) → acid demineralization exposing the collagen meshwork → hydrophilic resin infiltration forming the **hybrid layer** (Nakabayashi, 1982) + resin tags into tubules → polymerization; MDP-containing (self-etch/universal) systems add a genuine ionic MDP-calcium chemical bond. (Full classification/composition/mechanism detail as in this topic's CD_Q04/CD_Q15/CD_Q26.)

### III. Factors affecting adhesion to each substrate
Enamel: prism orientation/etch pattern, degree of mineralization (fluorotic/aprismatic enamel etches less predictably), contamination. Dentin: depth (superficial vs deep), sclerosis, caries-affected status, moisture control, smear layer variability (see CD_Q16/CD_Q23 for full detail).

### IV. Clinical applications of dental adhesion
Direct composite/compomer restorations, bonded indirect restorations (requiring a parallel restoration-side adhesive protocol — silane for silica ceramics, MDP-primer for zirconia), resin-bonded bridges, fissure sealants/PRRs, core build-ups, fiber-post cementation, amalgam bonding, orthodontic bracket bonding. **A genuinely distinct, enamel-only adhesion application**: resin infiltration of white-spot/incipient lesions (named product Icon®, Alani & Patel PDF p.114) — a real, exact clinical protocol: sandblast/abrasive disc → HCl etch (Icon-etch) scrub **2 minutes** → rinse 30s → ethanol (Icon-dry) 30s, with repeat etch/dry cycles as needed → resin infiltrant 3 minutes, cure 40s → repeat infiltrant 1 minute, cure 40s → polish. [SRC]

### V. Durability and the central unresolved problem
Both enamel and dentin adhesion are subject to long-term degradation, but the mechanisms differ: enamel bonds degrade mainly through mechanical fatigue/marginal breakdown of the resin-enamel interface over years; dentin bonds additionally face **hydrolytic and MMP-mediated enzymatic collagen degradation**, a genuinely intrinsic, unsolved durability limitation specific to the collagen-containing hybrid layer.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- A bare "dental adhesion" title should cover BOTH enamel and dentin adhesion, not just dentin — a genuinely different scope from this topic's other, dentin-specific questions.
- Enamel adhesion is purely micromechanical (resin tags, no hybrid layer possible — no significant organic/collagen phase to infiltrate).
- Dentin adhesion is a resin-collagen hybrid mechanism, qualitatively different from enamel's.
- Orthodontic bracket bonding is a genuine, often-forgotten enamel-adhesion application worth naming in a broad essay.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Answering a bare "dental adhesion" title as if it only meant dentin bonding, omitting enamel adhesion's distinct mechanism entirely.
- Treating enamel and dentin adhesion as having the same durability-limitation profile, when dentin's collagen-containing hybrid layer specifically faces MMP-mediated degradation that enamel bonding does not.
