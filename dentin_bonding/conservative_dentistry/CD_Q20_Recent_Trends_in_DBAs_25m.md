### Part II — Recent trends
1. **Universal/multi-mode adhesives** — single-bottle, any-etch-strategy, 10-MDP-based, multi-substrate-compatible (zirconia/ceramics/metals); a 2022 meta-analysis found no significant dentin-bond-strength difference between etch-and-rinse and self-etch modes for this chemistry. [LIT] [Likely]
2. **MMP-inhibition strategies** (chlorhexidine, EDTA, targeted inhibitor monomers) to counter hydrolytic/enzymatic hybrid-layer degradation over time.
3. **Bioactive/remineralizing adhesives** (bioglass/calcium-phosphate fillers).
4. **Antibacterial monomers** (MDPB) targeting secondary-caries risk at the margin.
5. **Nanotechnology-enhanced formulations** (nano-hydroxyapatite/silica fillers) for improved mechanical properties/reduced nanoleakage.
6. **Biomimetic remineralization** (ethanol wet-bonding, proanthocyanidin/phosphoprotein analogues) — a fundamentally different durability strategy from resin infiltration alone.
7. **Refined selective-etch protocols** as the pragmatic contemporary clinical default.

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
The "recent trends" framing invites a note on evidence maturity: universal-adhesive evidence is comparatively robust (a real pooled meta-analysis exists); MMP-inhibitor, bioactive, and biomimetic-remineralization strategies are largely supported by in-vitro/experimental data at this stage, with clinical-trial-level, long-term outcome evidence still comparatively limited — a genuine maturity gradient worth stating rather than treating all "recent trends" as equally evidence-backed.
