## STUDY MODE

**Historical trajectory (brief):** Buonocore's 1955 acid-etch work on enamel → Bowen's early composite resin and bonding resin work (1960s) → progressively simplified, faster-acting dentin adhesives through the so-called "generations" (1st-7th/8th, depending on the source) → the current, dominant paradigm of **etch-and-rinse vs self-etch classification**, now further converging into **universal (multi-mode) adhesives**. **Current (contemporary) state:**
1. **Universal/multi-mode adhesives dominate new product development** — a single bottle usable in etch-and-rinse, self-etch, or selective-etch mode, typically built around the **10-MDP** functional monomer, which also permits bonding to zirconia, silica-based ceramics, and metals without a separate primer. [LIT] [Likely]
2. **The "generation" numbering system is now considered scientifically imprecise** — a 2018-era review notes generations overlap in time and composition, and there is a real misconception that a higher generation number automatically means better performance; most contemporary teaching and literature classifies by etch strategy instead. [LIT] [Likely]
3. **Evidence on self-etch vs etch-and-rinse performance is genuinely mixed, not settled**: a 2022 systematic review/meta-analysis of universal adhesives (Dental Materials Journal, pooling 15 studies) found no statistically significant difference between etch-and-rinse and self-etch modes for immediate OR long-term dentin bond strength, though bond strength in both modes declines over time (favoring self-etch mode's simpler procedure for long-term use, per that review's authors); other narrative-review-level evidence has found total-etch giving significantly higher dentin bond strength and lower enamel microleakage than self-etch, and some microtensile studies have found self-etch adhesives performing worse on both enamel and dentin than total-etch systems. This genuine inconsistency across study types should be stated plainly rather than resolved into one simple verdict. [LIT] [Guessing]
4. **Selective-etch (phosphoric acid on enamel, self-etch/universal system on dentin) is widely adopted** as a pragmatic compromise exploiting enamel's preference for aggressive etching and dentin's preference for gentler treatment.
5. **Recognition of hydrolytic/enzymatic (MMP-mediated) bond degradation over time** has driven research into MMP-inhibiting strategies (chlorhexidine, EDTA pretreatment) and more hydrophobic, hydrolysis-resistant resin chemistries — a genuine current research direction rather than a solved problem.

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
Textbook-level teaching often implies the field has settled on self-etch/universal adhesives as clearly superior or equivalent to total-etch. Retrieved evidence complicates this: the most rigorous available pooled analysis (a 2022 meta-analysis specific to universal adhesives) found genuinely no significant difference between modes for dentin bond strength, while separate narrative-review and microtensile-study evidence favors total-etch, particularly for enamel microleakage. **What this supports:** universal adhesives used in self-etch mode are a reasonable, evidence-consistent choice for dentin specifically, and the field's current direction is real. **What it does not support:** a confident claim that self-etch/universal technology has definitively surpassed total-etch performance across all substrates — enamel-specific evidence still tends to favor the more aggressive phosphoric-acid etch, which is exactly why selective-etch technique has become the pragmatic contemporary compromise rather than a pure self-etch or pure total-etch default.
