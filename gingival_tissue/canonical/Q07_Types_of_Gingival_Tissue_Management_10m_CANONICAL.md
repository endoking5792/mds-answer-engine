## 1. Definition and classification

### Explanation

Sikri defines gingival retraction as exposing the margins when making impressions of prepared teeth, i.e. deflecting the marginal gingiva away from the tooth; the goal is to displace the tissue reversibly so low-viscosity impression material can enter the sulcus (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Indications include subgingival caries, cervical abrasion/erosion, gingival polyp and subgingival finish lines; contraindications include poor oral hygiene, recession, bone loss and systemic disease affecting the gingiva (Sikri, 2017) [SRC].

The books classify differently, and the student should know which list they are writing. Sikri: mechanical, chemomechanical, surgical/electrosurgical, miscellaneous (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Rouse (Summitt's): mechanical, mechanical-chemical, rotary curettage, electrosurgery, laser troughing (Rouse, 2013) [SRC]. Goldstein et al: retraction cord, paste systems, conventional electrosurgery, bipolar technique, diode lasers (Goldstein et al, 2018) [SRC]. The question asks also for "chemical" as a group; Sikri treats chemicals only as cord medicaments (chemomechanical), so a separate "chemical" heading in the exam is a teaching convenience, not Sikri's scheme.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Gingival retraction (tissue management) is the reversible deflection of the marginal gingiva away from the tooth to expose the finish line for impression and restoration (Sikri, 2017).

**Indications:** subgingival caries or finish line, cervical abrasion/erosion, gingival polyp. **Contraindications:** poor oral hygiene, recession, bone loss, systemic gingival disease.

**Classification** (Sikri, 2017; Rouse, 2013 lists rotary curettage and laser troughing separately).

## 2. The methods, with indications and cautions

### Explanation

Mechanical: wooden wedges depress interproximal gingiva; cotton twills retract in 20-30 minutes; an oversized copper band filled with modelling wax pushes the tissue (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Chemical agents: vasoconstrictors (epinephrine), fluid coagulants (alum, aluminium chloride, tannic acid), surface coagulants and chemical cautery (zinc chloride), the latter causing ulceration and necrosis if left long (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Epinephrine is avoided in cardiovascular disease, hypertension, hyperthyroidism, diabetes, beta-blocker and antidepressant users (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Ferric sulphate is a better haemostatic than epinephrine (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Cords are colour-coded by thickness; in the double-cord method the second cord is wetted and removed after 8-10 minutes, the first staying in to reduce recoil of the gingival cuff (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Rouse calls the mechanical-chemical technique the most popular (Rouse, 2013) [SRC].

Surgical: rotary curettage ("gingitage") needs adequate gingiva and sulcus depth under 3.0 mm; gingivectomy is indicated for crown lengthening and interfering tissue; electrosurgery is surgical reduction of sulcular epithelium with a high-frequency electrode, using fully rectified undamped current, never touching metal restorations, avoided with pacemakers, and in thin gingiva may cause recession (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Lasers ablate with blood-free incisions (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Rouse states rotary curettage, electrosurgery and laser troughing are contraindicated in low-crest patients (Rouse, 2013) [SRC]. Miscellaneous (cordless): Magic Foam (expanding vinyl polysiloxane), Expasyl (about 0.5 mm retraction), Merocel, Gingitrac, Comprecap, Racegel and others (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Improper methods can permanently damage the attachment and cause recession (Goldstein et al, 2018) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

| Type | Examples | Indication | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanical | Plain cord, wooden wedge, cotton twill (20-30 min), copper band with wax, rubber dam | Healthy, well-supported gingiva | Excess pressure: trauma, recession |
| Chemical | Vasoconstrictor (epinephrine); fluid coagulants (alum, aluminium chloride, tannic acid); ferric sulphate; surface coagulants/cautery (zinc chloride) | Haemostasis and fluid control | Epinephrine: avoid in cardiac disease, hypertension, hyperthyroidism, diabetes, beta-blockers; zinc chloride causes necrosis |
| Chemomechanical (most popular) | Impregnated cord, single or double cord (colour-coded 000 to 3) | Routine subgingival finish lines | Remove cord wet; second cord out at 8-10 min |
| Surgical | Rotary curettage ("gingitage"), electrosurgery, laser, scalpel gingivectomy | Hyperplastic tissue, polyps, crown lengthening, bleeding | Curettage only if sulcus < 3 mm; electrosurgery: no pacemaker, no metal contact, recession in thin tissue; contraindicated in low crests (Rouse, 2013) |
| Miscellaneous (cordless) | Magic Foam, Expasyl (~0.5 mm), Merocel, Gingitrac, Comprecap, Racegel, Stay-put, cryosurgery | Atraumatic, multiple preparations | Little haemostasis (foam), cost |

**Double-cord technique:** a small first cord is packed to the sulcus base, then a larger cord soaked in haemostatic agent above it; the second cord is wetted and removed after 8-10 minutes and the impression made with the first cord in place, which reduces recoil of the gingival cuff (Sikri, 2017).

**Electrosurgery rules:** moist field, fully rectified undamped current at minimum output, light rapid strokes, never touch metallic restorations, avoid in pacemaker patients (Sikri, 2017).

**Comparison of schemes:** Rouse (2013) lists mechanical, mechanical-chemical, rotary curettage, electrosurgery and laser troughing; Goldstein et al (2018) list cord, paste systems, electrosurgery, bipolar technique and diode laser.

**Principles:** retraction must be reversible, must not detach the epithelial attachment, and bleeding must be arrested before impression (Sikri, 2017). Improper methods cause permanent recession (Goldstein et al, 2018).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Sikri's four groups: mechanical, chemomechanical, surgical/electrosurgical, miscellaneous.
- Chemomechanical (cord + astringent) is the most popular (Rouse, 2013).
- Ferric sulphate is better haemostat than epinephrine; epinephrine has systemic effects.
- Electrosurgery: pacemaker contraindication; avoid in thin tissue.
- Rotary curettage only with sulcus under 3 mm.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Listing only cords; forgetting surgical and cordless methods.
- Giving epinephrine cord without its systemic contraindications.
- Recommending electrosurgery or curettage in thin tissue or low crests.
