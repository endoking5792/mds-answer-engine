## 1. Definition and dentogingival complex

### Explanation

Biologic width is the "combined vertical dimension of the junctional epithelium and the supra-alveolar connective tissue"; this complex "acts as a seal around the cervical portion of the tooth and has a self-restoration capacity", and epithelium detached by probing or flossing reattaches in about 5 days; in health "the epithelial attachment terminates at the apical end of the junctional epithelium" (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Summitt's calls the two tissues together the dentogingival junction, "and their combined vertical dimension is termed the biologic width" (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC]. The term was coined by D. Walter Cohen (Rouse, 2013) [SRC]. The dentogingival complex is the biologic width plus the sulcus: 3.0 mm midfacially (2.0 mm biologic width + 1.0 mm sulcus) as "a simplified working concept", and 3.0-4.5 mm interproximally (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Biologic width is the combined vertical dimension of the junctional epithelium and the supra-alveolar (supracrestal) connective tissue attachment, coronal to the alveolar crest (Morelli and Neiva, 2019). It forms a biological seal around the cervical tooth and can repair itself: epithelial attachment separated by probing or flossing reattaches in about 5 days. In health the epithelial attachment ends at the apical end of the junctional epithelium (Morelli and Neiva, 2019). The term was given by D. Walter Cohen (Rouse, 2013).

**Dentogingival complex** = sulcus + junctional epithelium + connective tissue attachment. Working figure: 3.0 mm midfacially (2 mm biologic width + 1 mm sulcus) and 3.0-4.5 mm interproximally (Fruits et al, 2013).

## 2. Dimensions: Gargiulo and Vacek

### Explanation

Table 10.1 of Sturdevant's (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]: Gargiulo 1961: sulcus 0.69, JE 0.97, CT 1.07, BW 2.04 mm; Vacek and Gher 1994: sulcus 1.32, JE 1.14, CT 0.77, BW 1.91 mm. Gargiulo found "the connective tissue zone had the most consistent dimension" and "The junctional epithelium was the most variable area"; Vacek found "biologic width was greater in posterior teeth" and the "junctional epithelium was significantly longer in teeth with restorations" (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Summitt's gives Vacek's 1.91 mm as 1.75 mm anterior and 2.08 mm posterior, and Gargiulo's average 2.04 mm (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

| Component (mm) | Gargiulo et al 1961 | Vacek et al 1994 |
|---|---|---|
| Sulcus | 0.69 | 1.32 |
| Junctional epithelium | 0.97 | 1.14 |
| Connective tissue | 1.07 | 0.77 |
| Biologic width | 2.04 | 1.91 |

(Morelli and Neiva, 2019). Connective tissue is the most constant zone, junctional epithelium the most variable. Biologic width is greater posteriorly (Vacek: 1.75 mm anterior, 2.08 mm posterior) (Fruits et al, 2013).

## 3. Bone sounding and crest classification

### Explanation

Under anaesthesia "the probe penetrates the midfacial sulcus to contact the osseous crest, a process called bone sounding"; "In 85% of subjects, a 3.0-mm reading confirms existence of an optimum biologic width", and then "the preparation margin may be safely placed 0.5 mm below the existing gingival margin" (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC]. Rouse sounds the midfacial and both facioproximal line angles and classifies anterior teeth (Rouse, 2013) [SRC]: normal crest, 3.0 mm facially and 3.0-4.5 mm interproximally, 85% of anterior teeth, margins 0.5-1.0 mm into the sulcus facially and 0.5-2.5 mm interproximally; low crest, greater than 3.0/4.5 mm, 13%, "the most difficult of all crest positions to manage", options are an internally bevelled gingivectomy to put the gingival crest 3 mm coronal to bone, or margins at or above the gingiva with minimal retraction; high crest, less than 3 mm, 2%, "the greatest risk for violation of biologic width", allowing only an "equigingival or supragingival restorative margin" (Rouse, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Bone sounding (Kois).** Under local anaesthesia a periodontal probe is pushed through the sulcus to the alveolar crest at the midfacial and both facioproximal line angles; the reading = dentogingival complex (Fruits et al, 2013; Rouse, 2013).

| Crest type | Facial / proximal reading | Frequency (anterior teeth) | Margin implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | 3.0 mm / 3.0-4.5 mm | 85% | 0.5-1.0 mm into sulcus facially, 0.5-2.5 mm proximally |
| Low | >3.0 mm / >4.5 mm | 13% | Recession and black triangles likely; internally bevelled gingivectomy, or margin at/above gingiva with minimal retraction |
| High | <3.0 mm | 2% | Equigingival or supragingival margins only; crown lengthening if subgingival margin needed |

(Rouse, 2013)

## 4. Violation of biologic width and its consequences

### Explanation

Margins in or apical to the junctional epithelium disturb the seal, "leading to gingival inflammation, attachment loss and recession or pocket formation", and the width "can only be restored by apical re-establishment of the supracrestal connective tissue" (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Thin scalloped tissue tends to recede; thick flat tissue tends to form "a moat-like lesion" in bone with bleeding and pocketing (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Contact of the margin with the attachment "allows for bacterial colonization of the area", followed by chronic inflammation (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Sikri lists chronic inflammation, pocket formation and osseous defects (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. A 26-year study found 0.5 mm more attachment loss with subgingival margins (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]; bleeding risk was "twice as high for intrasulcular margins as for supragingival margins" (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Mechanism.** A margin in contact with the junctional epithelium or connective tissue below the sulcus allows bacterial colonisation, recruitment of inflammatory cells and chronic inflammation; the tissues then try to re-establish the width apically, at the cost of bone (Morelli and Neiva, 2019).

**Causes:** deep caries or fracture, subgingival crown margins for aesthetics or retention, overextended preparations.

**Consequences** (Morelli and Neiva, 2019; Sikri, 2017):
- Chronic gingival inflammation, bleeding, discomfort.
- Attachment loss and bone resorption as the body re-creates the width apically.
- Thin scalloped biotype: gingival recession, exposed margins.
- Thick flat biotype: pocket and crater-like (moat) bony defect.
- Intrasulcular margins doubled bleeding risk compared with supragingival margins (Fruits et al, 2013).

## 5. Margin placement rules

### Explanation

Books differ on the minimum crest-to-margin distance. Sturdevant's: beyond 1 mm into the sulcus is "potentially disruptive"; allow about 2 mm (attachment) + 1 mm (sulcus), plus "an additional 0.5–1 mm" safe distance; "the 3-mm distance is the minimum", and Wagenberg's group "suggested to aim for a 5-mm distance from bone" (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Summitt's Ch.1: margin no more than 0.5 mm into the sulcus, "no closer than 2.5 mm from the osseous" crest (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC]. Sikri: intracrevicular margins should "terminate at least 2.0 mm above the alveolar crest" (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Ricketts: subgingival margins "should not extend deeper than 0.5 mm from the free gingival margin" (Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]. With the crest 5.0 mm from the contact, 98% of papillae fill the embrasure (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC]. Proximal CEJ can scallop 3.5 mm incisal to the facial CEJ, so preparations must follow it (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

- Supragingival margins are preferred wherever aesthetics allow (Sikri, 2017).
- Subgingival margins: no deeper than 0.5 mm into the sulcus (Ricketts, 2011; Fruits et al, 2013); not more than 1 mm (Morelli and Neiva, 2019).
- Minimum distance margin-to-crest, as stated by each book:

| Source | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Sikri, 2017 | 2.0 mm |
| Fruits et al, 2013 | 2.5 mm |
| Morelli and Neiva, 2019 | 3 mm (aim for 5 mm during surgery) |

- Follow the gingival scallop proximally (proximal CEJ up to 3.5 mm incisal to facial CEJ) (Fruits et al, 2013).
- Assess biotype: thin scalloped tissue recedes, thick flat tissue forms pockets (Morelli and Neiva, 2019).
- Proximal contacts: when the alveolar crest is 5 mm from the contact point, 98% of papillae fill the embrasure (Fruits et al, 2013).

## 6. Correction of biologic width violation

### Explanation

If the complex is under 3.0 mm, "a mucogingival flap and osseous resection (a crown lengthening procedure) is generally recommended" (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC]. Crown lengthening is "removal of bone tissue with concomitant removal or repositioning of the soft tissue"; at least 3 mm of sound tooth is needed from the preparation margin to bone (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]; Overton removes bone so "3 mm of root surface separate the bone and the caries lesion" (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC]. Indications include subgingival caries, fracture, inadequate retention ("1.5–2 mm of ferrule is necessary") and altered passive eruption (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Limits: furcation position, reduced support, aesthetics, "inadequate crown-to-root ratio after surgery" (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Orthodontic extrusion: Camargo called forced eruption "the technique of choice" for isolated teeth in the aesthetic zone (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Healing time differs: Sturdevant's waits 6-8 weeks posteriorly for indirect work and 6-8 months in aesthetic areas (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]; Summitt's places posterior restorations 4-8 weeks after surgery (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC]. Gingivectomy removes only soft tissue; if it encroaches on the attachment "the tissue regrows to establish at least approximately 1 mm of junctional epithelium and 1 mm of sulcus", and it is not advised when keratinized tissue is insufficient (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Following extrusion, a supracrestal fiberotomy is commonly done to limit relapse [RECALL].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Indications for correction** (Morelli and Neiva, 2019): subgingival caries needing access, subgingival crown-root fracture, inadequate retention (a ferrule of 1.5-2 mm is needed), altered passive eruption and other aesthetic problems such as gingival overgrowth.

1. **Surgical crown lengthening** (flap + ostectomy/osteoplasty, apically positioned flap): re-creates at least 3 mm of sound tooth between preparation margin and bone (Morelli and Neiva, 2019; Overton et al, 2013). Contraindications: furcation exposure, reduced support, poor crown-root ratio, aesthetic compromise. Wait 6-8 weeks posteriorly (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) or 4-8 weeks (Overton et al, 2013); 6-8 months in aesthetic zones (Morelli and Neiva, 2019).
2. **Orthodontic (forced) extrusion:** technique of choice for single teeth in the aesthetic zone; may be followed by crown lengthening (Morelli and Neiva, 2019).
3. **Gingivectomy/gingivoplasty:** only when excess soft tissue (e.g. altered passive eruption) and adequate bone distance and keratinized tissue exist; otherwise tissue regrows (Morelli and Neiva, 2019).

## 7. Management of gingival tissues in conservative dentistry: overview

### Explanation

Importance: subgingival margins need adequate keratinized gingiva; Lang and Löe found 2.0 mm minimum (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC]. Gingivoplasty is absolutely contraindicated with an inadequate zone of attached gingiva (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC]. Non-surgical access: rubber dam with "A no. 212SA clamp" (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC]. Surgical access: miniflap not past the mucogingival junction; tissue attachment to GIC/RMGI "does occur" (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC]; Geristore is the "preferred material among clinicians" for subgingival restorations (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]; distal wedge on molars (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Provisionals: well adapted, tissue observed for several weeks (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Retraction: mechanical, chemomechanical or surgical; sulcus width 0.2 mm or more (Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; four requirements, and the secondary cord left 4 minutes for 0.2 mm (Rouse, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Importance:** healthy gingiva gives haemostasis, a dry field, accurate margins and impressions, stable aesthetics and long-term periodontal health. At least 2 mm of keratinized gingiva is desirable around restorations (Fruits et al, 2013).

**Management:**
1. **Preoperative:** oral hygiene, scaling, replacing overhangs; bone sounding; graft if keratinized gingiva is inadequate.
2. **Low or high crest:** plan margins and retraction according to crest type; avoid aggressive retraction where tissue is unstable (Rouse, 2013).
3. **During cavity preparation (access):** rubber dam with No. 212SA cervical clamp, wedges, cord; miniflap, gingivoplasty, flap, distal wedge, crown lengthening (Overton et al, 2013; Morelli and Neiva, 2019).
4. **Materials:** RMGI/Geristore for subgingival areas; smooth, overhang-free margins (Morelli and Neiva, 2019; Overton et al, 2013).
5. **Provisionals:** well adapted; healthy tissue under them confirms no violation (Morelli and Neiva, 2019).
6. **Gingival retraction for impressions:** mechanical (cords, rubber dam), chemomechanical (cord with astringent), surgical (rotary curettage, electrosurgery, laser) (Ricketts, 2011). Four requirements: horizontal displacement, vertical displacement, haemostasis, dry field (Rouse, 2013). Sulcus width at least 0.2 mm (Ricketts, 2011).

**Conclusion.** Respecting the biologic width during diagnosis, preparation, impression and restoration is the basis of periodontal health around restorations.

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

**Claim: a fixed minimum (2, 2.5 or 3 mm) of tooth above bone is mandatory.**
- Textbook says: 3 mm minimum, aim 5 mm (Morelli and Neiva, 2019); 2.5 mm (Fruits et al, 2013); 2.0 mm (Sikri, 2017) [SRC].
- Other evidence: in Vacek's sample 15% of teeth had margins under 2.0 mm from bone without attachment loss; a 2-year study of margins in the connective tissue zone found bleeding but "no signs of either gingival recession or bone resorption"; a review judged the evidence "primarily based on opinion and anecdotal cases" (Fruits et al, 2013) [SRC].
- Agreement or challenge: challenges the precision of any single number, not the principle.
- Strength: cadaver averages and short clinical studies; moderate at best. [Likely]
- Conclusion: the figure is a clinical safety margin derived from averages with wide individual variation; individual bone sounding is more reliable than a fixed number.
- Clinical implication: sound each tooth; when in doubt keep margins supragingival or create more room surgically.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- BW = JE + supracrestal CT; Gargiulo 2.04 mm, Vacek 1.91 mm.
- CT most constant; JE most variable; BW larger posteriorly.
- Dentogingival complex: 3 mm facial, 3-4.5 mm proximal.
- Crest types: normal 85%, low 13%, high 2% (Rouse).
- Margin-to-crest minimum: Sikri 2.0, Summitt 2.5, Sturdevant 3 mm.
- Correction: crown lengthening, orthodontic extrusion, gingivectomy (soft tissue excess only).
- Retraction needs 0.2 mm sulcus width.
