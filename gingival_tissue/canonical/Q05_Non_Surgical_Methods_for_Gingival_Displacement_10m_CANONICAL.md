## 1. Definition, aims and mechanical methods

### Explanation

Sikri defines the goal as to reversibly displace the gingiva so low-viscosity impression material can enter the sulcus and record the margin without tearing (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Goldstein lists four criteria: access beyond the margin, space for bulk of material, no seepage or haemorrhage during setting, and minimal toxicity; retraction must never be attempted on inflamed or swollen tissue (Goldstein et al, 2018) [SRC]. Rouse cites Laufer: no detectable impression distortion at sulcular widths of 0.2 mm or more, and the secondary cord should stay 4 minutes to reach that width (Rouse, 2013) [SRC].

Mechanical methods suit healthy gingiva with good blood supply and bone support (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Sikri lists wooden wedges (interproximal gingiva), cotton twills (retraction in 20-30 minutes), twills with fast-setting ZOE (better retraction but loss of attachment if left too long), rubber dam (cannot be used with elastomeric impression materials) and an oversized copper band filled with modelling wax (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Goldstein's two-appointment method prepares the tooth and fits a well-fitting temporary; by the next visit the tissue is healthy and needs little retraction (Goldstein et al, 2018) [SRC]. Cords are colour-coded by thickness; ends should lie at the axial angles; in the double-cord technique the second, soaked cord is removed wet after 8-10 minutes and the first stays during the impression, reducing recoil of the gingival cuff (Sikri, 2017) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Gingival displacement reversibly deflects the free gingiva laterally and apically so that impression material can record a subgingival finish line (Sikri, 2017). A sulcus width of about 0.2 mm avoids impression distortion (Rouse, 2013).

**Requirements** (Goldstein et al, 2018): access beyond the margin, bulk of material, no bleeding or seepage, non-toxic. Never retract inflamed tissue.

**Classification of non-surgical methods:** mechanical; chemomechanical; cordless pastes/foams (Sikri, 2017).

**A. Mechanical** (healthy gingiva) (Sikri, 2017)
- Wooden wedges: depress interproximal gingiva.
- Drawn cotton twills: 20-30 minutes; with fast-setting ZOE better retraction, but attachment loss if left long.
- Rubber dam: limited teeth; not with elastomers.
- Copper band filled with modelling wax; avoid excess pressure.
- Plain retraction cord, single or double.
- Custom provisional (two-appointment): well-fitting temporary lets tissue heal, so little retraction is needed (Goldstein et al, 2018).

## 2. Chemomechanical and cordless methods

### Explanation

Sikri's chemical groups: vasoconstrictors (epinephrine, retraction in 10 minutes, limited by systemic effects), fluid coagulants (alum, aluminium chloride, tannic acid; 10% alum considered superior to epinephrine), surface coagulants (6-8% zinc chloride) and chemical cautery (40% zinc chloride); ferric sulphate is a better haemostatic than epinephrine (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Goldstein reports Timberlake's rise in pulse and blood pressure with epinephrine (Goldstein et al, 2018) [SRC]; Ricketts calls aluminium chloride the least damaging astringent (Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]; Rouse calls aluminium sulfate one of the least irritating (Rouse, 2013) [SRC]. The two books name different salts (chloride vs sulfate); both are aluminium astringents and neither ranks the other. Cordless: Magic Foam is expanding PVS held by a cap (Ricketts, 2011) [SRC], removed at five minutes; Expasyl is kaolin with aluminium chloride; Gingitrac uses bite pressure; Comprecap caps hold cord deep (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Cords give greater reversible displacement than paste or foam (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**B. Chemomechanical** (cord soaked in an agent) (Sikri, 2017)

| Group | Agents | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Vasoconstrictor | epinephrine | systemic effects; raises pulse and BP (Goldstein et al, 2018) |
| Fluid coagulant | alum, aluminium chloride, tannic acid | aluminium chloride least damaging (Ricketts, 2011) |
| Haemostatic | ferric sulphate | better haemostasis; may stain gums |
| Surface coagulant / cautery | 6-8% / 40% zinc chloride | ulceration, necrosis |

**Double-cord technique:** thin cord first; larger soaked cord over it, removed wet after 8-10 minutes; impression with first cord in place, which limits recoil (Sikri, 2017). Cord ends at axial angles.

**Cord guidelines** (Sikri, 2017): cut the exact length; colour code indicates thickness (black 000 to red 3); pack with a blunt serrated instrument angled apically; remove in a wet field. A secondary cord left about 4 minutes gives a 0.2 mm sulcus (Rouse, 2013). Aluminium sulfate is among the least irritating chemicals (Rouse, 2013).

**C. Cordless systems**
- Magic Foam (expanding PVS) plus Comprecap; removed after 5 minutes.
- Expasyl: kaolin paste with aluminium chloride.
- Gingitrac: aluminium sulfate paste pushed by bite pressure.
- Compression caps (Comprecap) hold cord deep (Sikri, 2017).

**Conclusion.** Cords give greater reversible displacement than pastes or foams; cordless systems are gentler (Morelli and Neiva, 2019).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Target sulcus width 0.2 mm (Rouse, 2013).
- Never retract inflamed tissue (Goldstein et al, 2018).
- Epinephrine contraindicated in cardiac disease, hyperthyroidism, diabetes.
- Aluminium chloride: least damaging astringent (Ricketts, 2011).
- Double cord: first cord stays during impression.
- Rubber dam is incompatible with elastomers (Sikri, 2017).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Including electrosurgery, lasers or curettage (these are surgical).
- Using epinephrine cord without listing contraindications.
- Forgetting tissue health first (two-appointment provisional).
- Leaving the second cord in during the impression.
