## 1. Classification of methods

### Explanation

Sikri classifies gingival retraction as mechanical, chemomechanical, surgical/electrosurgical and miscellaneous (newer agents) (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Goldstein lists five methods: retraction cord, paste systems, conventional electrosurgery, bipolar technique and diode lasers (Goldstein et al, 2018) [SRC]. Surgical options in Sikri: rotary curettage ("gingitage", only where sulcus depth is less than 3.0 mm), gingivectomy, and electrosurgery (avoid with pacemakers) (Sikri, 2017) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Aim.** Reversibly displace the gingiva so impression material records the finish line without tearing (Sikri, 2017).

**Classification** (Sikri, 2017)
1. Mechanical: wedges, drawn cotton, rubber dam, copper band, plain cord, well-fitting provisional.
2. Chemomechanical: cords with epinephrine, alum, aluminium chloride, ferric sulphate, zinc chloride.
3. Surgical: rotary curettage (sulcus under 3 mm), gingivectomy, electrosurgery (not with pacemakers).
4. Miscellaneous / newer agents (below).

Goldstein et al (2018) list five: cord, pastes, electrosurgery, bipolar unit, diode laser.

## 2. Recent advances and evidence

### Explanation

Sikri's newer agents: Magic Foam (expanding vinyl polysiloxane, no haemostatic action), Merocel (swells on absorbing fluid), Expasyl (kaolin with aluminium chloride; 0.5 mm retraction), Gingitrac (bite pressure), Stay-put (cord with copper filaments), Comprecap (holds cord deep; aluminium chloride may interfere with polyether/PVS setting), Racegel (25% aluminium chloride gel), matrix impression system (traps viscous material in sulcus), lasers (photo-ablation, bloodless), cryosurgery (fibrosed tissue), decongestants (favourable but limited) (Sikri, 2017) [SRC]. Ricketts gives Expasyl up to 2 minutes (Ricketts, 2011) [SRC]. Goldstein: bipolar unit needs no external ground; 810 nm diode laser for soft tissue (Goldstein et al, 2018) [SRC]. Sturdevant: cords give greater reversible displacement; Expasyl had the most inflammation and slowest healing; Magic Foam was less effective than single cord where margins exceeded 2 mm; no study compares laser, electrosurgery, cord and cordless (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) [SRC]. Martins et al (2020) [LIT] meta-analysed 9 studies: most methods exceeded 0.2 mm, cords displaced more than cordless, and risk of bias was a concern. Sikri calls Merocel "significantly superior to retraction cords" without data; this conflicts with Sturdevant and Martins, so the claim is shown, not adopted.

#### ✎ Exam core

| Advance | Principle | Evidence maturity |
|---|---|---|
| Magic Foam + Comprecap | expanding PVS; no haemostasis | short-term clinical; weaker beyond 2 mm (Morelli and Neiva, 2019) |
| Expasyl | kaolin + aluminium chloride paste, 0.5 mm | clinical; most inflammation, slowest healing |
| Racegel | 25% aluminium chloride gel | manufacturer / textbook |
| Gingitrac | astringent paste, bite pressure | textbook |
| Merocel | swells with fluid | limited |
| Stay-put | cord with copper filaments | textbook |
| Matrix impression system | viscous material trapped in sulcus | technique report |
| Lasers (diode 810 nm) | photo-ablation, bloodless | clinical; no head-to-head trials |
| Bipolar electrosurgery | no external ground | limited |
| Decongestants | vasoconstriction | experimental; limited |
| Digital impressions | still need a visible, dry margin [RECALL] | evolving |

(Sikri, 2017; Goldstein et al, 2018)

**Evidence.** A meta-analysis of 9 studies found most techniques achieved over 0.2 mm, with cord giving more displacement than cordless, though risk of bias was high (Martins et al, 2020). No trial compares laser, electrosurgery, cord and cordless (Morelli and Neiva, 2019).

**Clinical notes.** Comprecap with aluminium chloride may interfere with setting of polyether or PVS (Sikri, 2017). Expasyl is left up to 2 minutes and rinsed away (Ricketts, 2011). Lasers give bloodless troughing with rapid healing; cryosurgery suits fibrosed tissue; decongestants are less effective than other techniques (Sikri, 2017).

**Conclusion.** Cord remains the reference standard; cordless systems are gentler and faster but displace less.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Four groups: mechanical, chemomechanical, surgical, newer (Sikri, 2017).
- Magic Foam: expanding PVS, no haemostasis.
- Expasyl: kaolin + aluminium chloride.
- Racegel: 25% aluminium chloride.
- Cords > cordless for displacement (Martins et al, 2020).
- 810 nm diode laser for soft tissue (Goldstein et al, 2018).
