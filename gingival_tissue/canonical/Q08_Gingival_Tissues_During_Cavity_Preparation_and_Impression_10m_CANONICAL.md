## 1. During cavity preparation

### Explanation

Sturdevant's recommends the No. 212 cervical retainer (Markley) for Class V preparations, a modified No. 212 for greatly extended gingival margins, and dulling its sharp tips to avoid damaging cementum (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Summitt's uses the 212SA clamp stabilised with modelling compound (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC]. Wedges are inserted snugly into the gingival embrasures (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC]. Where rubber dam is impractical, retraction cord moistened with a noncaustic haemostatic agent displaces the gingiva and helps prevent abrasion of the gingiva during preparation; the cord should not blanch the tissue, and inserting it early limits abrasion and bleeding (Ritter et al, 2019) [SRC].

Surgical access (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC]: a miniflap whose incisions do not pass the mucogingival junction, usually without sutures; gingivoplasty only if enough attached gingiva remains (an inadequate zone is an absolute contraindication; under 3 mm remaining calls for an apically positioned flap or graft); a mucoperiosteal flap when two adjacent teeth need surgical access; after posterior crown lengthening, restore at 4-8 weeks. For a noncarious lesion, finishing short beats an overhang in a healthy sulcus (Overton et al, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Aim:** access and dryness without lacerating gingiva or violating the attachment.

1. **Rubber dam** retracts tissue; add a **No. 212 cervical retainer** for Class V (Ritter et al, 2019), stabilised with modelling compound (Overton et al, 2013); modified 212 for deep margins; tips dulled to protect cementum.
2. **Wedges** inserted snugly into gingival embrasures (Ritter et al, 2019) depress the interproximal gingiva (Sikri, 2017).
3. **Retraction cord** (with noncaustic haemostatic) when dam is impractical: placed early, sized so the gingiva does not blanch; prevents abrasion and seepage (Ritter et al, 2019).
4. **Surgical access** (Overton et al, 2013):
   - Miniflap: short incisions within keratinised gingiva, not beyond the mucogingival junction; sutures usually unnecessary.
   - Gingivoplasty: only if adequate attached gingiva remains (inadequate zone = absolute contraindication); if less than 3 mm attached gingiva would remain, use an apically positioned flap or graft.
   - Mucoperiosteal flap/crown lengthening when two teeth or the biologic width are involved; restore posteriors at 4-8 weeks.
5. **Avoid laceration:** sharp, controlled instrumentation; no overhangs; finishing short is better than overhanging into a healthy sulcus.

## 2. During impression procedures

### Explanation

Rouse lists four requirements: horizontal displacement for bulk of material, vertical displacement to expose the area apical to the finish line, arrest of bleeding, and dry tissues (Rouse, 2013) [SRC]. Defects fell to no detectable difference at sulcular widths of 0.2 mm or more, and the secondary cord should stay 4 minutes; aluminium sulfate is among the least irritating chemicals; the secondary cord is removed wet; ferric compounds inhibit polyether set; latex gloves inhibit PVS (Rouse, 2013) [SRC]. Crest relationships: normal (3.0 mm facially, 3.0-4.5 mm interproximally) is stable; low crest is the hardest, needing minimal retraction; high crest needs minimal retraction; curettage, electrosurgery and laser troughing are contraindicated in low crests (Rouse, 2013) [SRC]. Sikri gives 8-10 minutes for the second cord, against Rouse's 4 minutes; neither is ranked here (Sikri, 2017; Rouse, 2013) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Four requirements** (Rouse, 2013): horizontal displacement, vertical displacement, haemostasis, dry field. A sulcus width of **0.2 mm** or more gives defect-free impressions.

**Double-cord technique:** thin dry primary cord placed during finish-line preparation; astringent (aluminium sulfate) then a larger secondary cord; left about 4 min (Rouse, 2013; Sikri, 2017 says 8-10 min); removed wet; primary stays during impression. Cord ends at axial angles (Sikri, 2017).

**Dry field check:** the field must be dry before material is mixed; if not, chemicals and secondary cord are replaced, because the sulcus closes as time passes (Rouse, 2013).

**Haemostasis:** ferric solution on cut tissue if bleeding persists (inhibits polyether); avoid latex contact with PVS.

**Crest relationship** (bone sounding):
| Crest | Free gingiva to bone | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | 3.0 mm facial, 3.0-4.5 mm proximal | Routine double cord |
| Low | Greater | Margins supragingival/equigingival, light thin cords; no curettage, electrosurgery or laser |
| High | Less than 3 mm | Small cords, minimal retraction |

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- No. 212 retainer for Class V; dull its tips; stabilise with compound.
- Gingivoplasty: absolute contraindication = inadequate attached gingiva.
- Four requirements of retraction; 0.2 mm sulcus width.
- Remove cord wet; ferric inhibits polyether.
- Low crest: minimal retraction, no troughing.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Writing only impression retraction and ignoring the cavity-preparation half.
- Forgetting wedges and rubber dam as tissue protection.
- Using electrosurgery in low-crest or thin tissue.
