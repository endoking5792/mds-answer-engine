## STUDY MODE

### S1. Idea and rationale
The sandwich technique places GIC as the dentine replacement and composite as the enamel replacement, so each material is used where it is best (McCabe and Walls, 2008; Mount et al, 2016). Composite shrinks and its dentine bond is hard to keep, while GIC bonds to dentine by ion exchange and to composite after cut-back and etching (Mount, 1994).

### S2. Two kinds
Open sandwich: GIC forms part of the axial or gingival wall and is exposed to the mouth. Closed sandwich: composite covers the GIC completely (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013). Summitt reserves the open type for Class II and V restorations extending below the cemento-enamel junction, with a resin-modified GIC covered by composite (Hilton et al, 2013).

### S3. Cautions
Pickard says closed sandwich restorations are more prone to long-term failure at interfaces (Banerjee and Watson, 2011); etching GIC too early or too long damages it (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013).

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 8 minutes.)*

### 1. Definition
Restoration in which GIC replaces dentine and composite replaces enamel (McCabe and Walls, 2008; Mount et al, 2016).

### 2. Types
- **Open:** GIC exposed at the gingival or axial wall (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013); used where the margin lies on cementum or dentine (Hilton et al, 2013).
- **Closed:** GIC completely covered by composite (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013).

### 3. Rationale
Composite shrinks 1-5%; the dentine bond is hard to maintain; GIC bonds to dentine, releases fluoride, and reduces the composite needed (Mount, 1994; Mount et al, 2016).

### 4. Technique
1. Condition dentine 10 seconds and place the strongest radiopaque GIC (Mount et al, 2016).
2. After setting, cut back about 2 mm to expose enamel margins (Mount et al, 2016).
3. Etch enamel and auto-cure GIC 15 seconds (resin-modified GIC: enamel only) (Mount et al, 2016).
4. Apply enamel bond; place composite in increments (Mount et al, 2016).

### 4A. Indications
Subgingival Class II and Class V margins on dentine or cementum, erosive lesions, and deep proximal amalgam restorations with GIC in the gingival half (Mount et al, 2016; Hilton et al, 2013; Mount, 1994). Leave GIC exposed only at a subgingival margin without enamel (Mount et al, 2016).

### 5. Advantages and limits
Fluoride exposure, less shrinkage, tissue-tolerant GIC at the margin (Mount, 1994); closed type may fail at interfaces (Banerjee and Watson, 2011).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- GIC = dentine replacement; composite = enamel replacement (McCabe and Walls, 2008).
- Open (GIC exposed) against closed (fully covered) (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013).
- Cover all tubules, at least 1 mm (Mount, 1994).
- Etch GIC 15 s only (Mount et al, 2016).
