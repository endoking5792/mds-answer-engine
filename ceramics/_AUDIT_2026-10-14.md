## A. Corrections to existing drafts (draft claim → what sources say)

1. **Porcelain-denture origin date**: not 1774/"Duchateau". MICH: fused porcelain for teeth introduced **1789**; a baked-porcelain complete denture in a single block first shown by **Nicholas Dubois de Chemant, 1788** ("A Dissertation on Artificial Teeth," 1797). PHIL corroborates: de Chemant's **1789 patent** refining Duchateau's **1774** "mineral paste teeth" — so 1774 (Duchateau) is Duchateau's own earlier work, and de Chemant's own denture/patent is 1788/1789. State both names/dates distinctly rather than collapsing into one 1774 fact.
2. **Land's porcelain crown/furnace date**: commonly cited as 1886 (porcelain inlay/crown patent) vs MICH's explicit 1889 date for Land's gas/gasoline furnace contribution — these may be two different contributions by Land; cite both with their own attribution rather than picking one.
3. **Weinstein metal-ceramic patent**: it is a **patent**, not a journal study — US Patent No. 3,052,**7**82 (one reader transcribed "3,052,982"; MICH's bibliography gives 3,052,782), granted **Sept 11, 1962**, filed earlier. PHIL notes there were in fact **two separate 1962 Weinstein patents** (one on feldspathic porcelain formulation/CTE control, one on alloy composition for chemical bonding) — do not collapse into a single undifferentiated "1962 Weinstein" fact.
4. **Fracture-toughness "ladder" (soda-lime glass → leucite-reinforced → lithium disilicate)** is edition-dependent, NOT one fixed set of numbers:
   - CR: 0.78 → 1.2 → 2.75 MPa·m^0.5
   - PHIL: 0.75 → 1.3 → 3.3 MPa·m^0.5 (also gives comparators: enamel 0.7–1.3, dentin 3.1, resin composite 0.8–2.5 MPa·m^0.5)
   - MICH (older data): lithium disilicate 3.2 MPa·m^0.5
   - VN/BON frame strengthening via **flexural strength** (MPa), not fracture toughness, with different numbers again (see #6below).
   **Cite the edition and figure together ("Craig's latest ed. gives X; Phillips' 12th ed. gives Y") rather than stating one number as *the* value.** Leucite content at the "reinforced" stage is also a range, not one percentage: CR gives 35–40% (sintered)/35–55% (heat-pressed/CAD); PHIL/VN cite ~34 vol% for IPS Empress specifically.
5. **Zirconia tetragonal→monoclinic transformation volume change**: NOT a clean "~4%". VN gives no number at all (just "accompanied by an expansion"); PHIL gives **3–5%**. Do not state "~4%" as if it were a textbook figure — attribute a specific number only to PHIL's 3–5% range, or cite Chevalier et al. 2009 if a tighter number is needed (per CR's bibliography).
6. **Aluminous (McLean & Hughes) porcelain**: year is **1965** (not "1967" or "1765" — one OCR'd source garbled the year; McLean JW, Hughes TH, *Br Dent J* 117:251, 1965, is the correct citation). Alumina content **40–50 wt%** (VN/BON/PHIL/MICH agree). Flexural strength is reported differently by source: VN/BON ~120–150 MPa; PHIL ~131 MPa; MICH/CR ~139–145 MPa. Use a **120–150 MPa range**, not a single figure, and do not cite "~70 MPa" (unsupported by any of the five readers' sources). 5-year clinical fracture rate (McLean 1979, both PHIL and MCC cite): **2% anterior vs 15% molar** — directly supports "aluminous porcelain unsuitable for posterior teeth," with exact numbers.
7. **In-Ceram Alumina composition/strength is NOT a single settled figure across sources** — genuinely conflicting: VN says ~85% alumina post-infiltration, ~400–500 MPa full-crown claims; MICH (slip-cast description) gives alumina **>90%**, particle size 0.5–3.5µm, fired 4h/1100°C, glass-infiltrated with **lanthanum-containing** glass at 1150°C/4h, flexural strength **≈450 MPa**; PHIL gives core (pre-infiltration) **~62% alumina/20% zirconia/18% glass by weight** for In-Ceram ZIRCONIA specifically (not plain In-Ceram Alumina), ~620 MPa. CR's current edition **omits slip-cast/In-Ceram detail entirely** (pushed to an online supplement). **The commonly-quoted "70 vol% alumina, ~550 MPa" figure is NOT supported by any of the five readers' primary sources** — flag it as unverified/likely digest-corrupted if it appears in the draft, and present the range with per-source attribution instead of one number.
8. **Zirconia flexural strength**: not a flat "900 MPa". CR: 3Y-TZP 900–1500 MPa (measured 1087±173 MPa); translucent cubic-containing zirconia 600–700 MPa (measured 609 MPa, K_Ic 2.4 MPa·m^0.5). PHIL: 3Y-TZP ≈900 MPa, K_Ic **8–10.3** MPa·m^0.5 (a range, not ~8 flat); nondoped monoclinic zirconia K_Ic ≈2.06 MPa·m^0.5 (2.6 at full density). VN/BON: Y-TZP flexural strength **>1000 MPa** (VN) / **650 MPa** (BON) — another real cross-source discrepancy; state both with attribution. PHIL also gives a **non-monotonic grain-size effect**: strength rises 650→1000 MPa as grain size goes 0.9→1.4µm (a "critical" grain size), then FALLS to 750 MPa at 1.8µm — a genuinely counter-intuitive, examinable finding likely absent from the draft.
9. **Lithium disilicate / "lithium silicate" are not interchangeable terms.** CR explicitly classifies Vita Suprinity/Celtra Duo as **lithium silicate** (Li2Si2O5 + Li2SiO3, >50% crystallinity, ~420 MPa) as DISTINCT from pure lithium disilicate (65% crystallinity, 262±88 MPa hard-machined / 306±29 MPa heat-pressed per CR; VN gives 350–450 MPa; CR's own heat-pressed figure elsewhere is 360-400 MPa manufacturer data) — if the draft uses "lithium disilicate" as an umbrella term for both, correct it.
10. **Leucite ceramic MPa figures must be split by use-case**: metal-bonded leucite ceramic (for PFM veneering) is only **30–50 MPa** (VN); leucite-REINFORCED glass-ceramic for all-ceramic resin-bonded restorations (Empress I) is **up to 120 MPa** (VN) / specific figure 120-180 MPa range per MCC. These are not the same material despite sharing "leucite" in the name — a real, examinable distinction the draft should make explicit.
11. **Metal-ceramic market share figures are time-stamped, not one number**: PHIL (12th ed., "recently") = 60–70% of fixed restorations; a cited 1994 survey = ~90%; a different reader's source (likely an older digest) gave "70–80% circa 2011" — present as a declining-over-time trend with each figure's own year/edition, not a single fact.
12. **HF etch protocol for ceramic cementation is genuinely edition/era-dependent, not one number.** SIKRI (older, more conservative): 8–12% HF, 1–3 minutes (longer for feldspathic). AP (modern BDJ clinician's guide): 5% or 9.5% HF for only 10/20/60 seconds depending on ceramic. CR gives 2.5–10% HF, 1–4 minutes as its own range. **Present as "older protocol vs. modern protocol," both cited, not as a single correct answer** — and in every case, **zirconia/polycrystalline ceramics are NEVER HF-etched** (air/alumina-abraded + 10-MDP primer only — AP's Table 2.10, CR p.281-282, SIKRI's zirconia exception all agree on this point).
13. **Base under a ceramic restoration**: SIKRI explicitly says glass-ionomer is **NOT preferred** as a base under ceramic inlays/onlays (low compressive strength) — resin composite is recommended for its higher elastic modulus. If the draft states GIC as a universal base choice under any indirect restoration, this needs the ceramic-specific exception added.
14. **Resin-modified GIC luting cement**: SIKRI flags a specific hygroscopic-expansion risk under all-ceramic crowns specifically (can fracture the ceramic) — "use with caution," not a blanket-safe luting option for all-ceramic work.
15. **Resin cement cure mode for veneers**: STURD recommends **light-cured** (not dual-cured) resin cement specifically for porcelain veneer cementation, citing better colour stability and longer working time — correct any draft defaulting to dual-cure for veneers.
16. **Ceramic onlay/CAD-CAM preparation design explicitly rejects a ferrule/mechanical-retention design** (unlike cast-metal onlays) — STURD: a ferrule can cause under-milling of parallel walls and interferes with seating; a **butt-joint margin** is preferred, with an optional 45° "football diamond" modified butt-joint for esthetic blending. Correct any draft describing ceramic onlay prep as using mechanical retention grooves/boxes like a metal casting.
17. **Vacuum-firing date ("1949")**: NONE of the five readers' sources state this date explicitly in the chapters they read (PHIL/MCC describe the porosity-reduction EFFECT — ~4.6%→~0.5%, or air-fired 5.6% vs vacuum-fired 0.56% per CR/MICH citing Semmelman 1959 — without the 1949 date). If the draft states "1949," it needs a different/primary source citation or should be softened to "mid-20th-century."

## B. Gaps filled (substantial dedicated textbook content not in the review-article-sourced draft)

- **Toughening-mechanism physics** (CR pp.211-213; PHIL pp.440-454; VN): full mechanistic treatment of dispersion/crystalline reinforcement, ion-exchange/chemical tempering (molten KNO3 bath, K+ ~35% larger than Na+, compressive zone <100µm, >100% flexural-strength gain, **lost if ground/worn** — CR notes ion exchange is "no longer used for dental ceramics due to controllability issues"), thermal tempering (rapid quench, silicone oil preferred over air for dental use), and CTE-mismatch layering (core CTE deliberately higher than veneer to create protective residual compression).
- **ISO 6872** test standard (CR p.212): radioactivity, flexural strength, linear CTE, glass-transition temperature, chemical solubility protocols — mentioned by name in several drafts per earlier scope notes but likely not actually detailed.
- **Metal-ceramic bond failure classification** (CR p.221, Fig. 11.10): three failure-location types — adhesive (metal/oxide interface), cohesive-in-oxide, cohesive-in-ceramic (strongest bond, failure within the porcelain itself) — a clean, examinable classification scheme.
- **Metal-ceramic clinical survival data**: CR — 10-yr success ≈95%, most retreatments biological (tooth fracture/perio/secondary caries) not material failure, prosthesis/esthetic failure only 20% of single-unit retreatments, long-span FDPs (≥5 units) ≈2× failure rate of short-span. PHIL — 7.5-yr fracture rate as low as 2.3% (high-noble alloy), 7.5-yr survival 97.7%; molar crowns luted with GIC ~15% fracture within 7 years (a cementation/design interaction, not pure material failure).
- **Full named alloy composition/property table** (PHIL Table 18-5): 25+ commercial alloys (Au-Pt-In, Au-Pd-Ag, Pd-based, Ni-Cr, Co-Cr) with proof stress, elongation, VHN, modulus, CTE — richer than typical review coverage for the "alloys for metal-ceramic" question.
- **Connector/design biomechanics for all-ceramic FDPs** (PHIL pp.444-446): Quinn et al. 2010 minimum-connector-diameter model for 95%/20-yr survival of 3Y-TZP 4-unit bridges; a real documented case of a 3.5mm (vs recommended 4.0mm) connector failing at 1.5 years.
- **Captek capillary-casting metal-ceramic system** (PHIL/MCC): two-stage capillary infiltration (P-metal Au-Pt-Pd / G-metal Au-Ag), coping thickness 0.25mm vs 0.50mm conventional cast, bonds via a mechanical "bonder" layer rather than an oxide layer — a genuine mechanistic contrast worth adding to a bonding-mechanism answer.
- **Zirconia resin-bonding chemistry in full**: HF/silane do NOT work on zirconia (no amorphous silica phase); sandblast + MDP-based primers form Zr-O-P bonds; universal adhesives work best at pH 2.2-3.2 (CR p.281-282); AP's Table 2.10 gives the complete material-by-material intraoral bonding protocol (gold/base-metal, composite/resin-nanoceramic, fibre post, glass-ceramic, zirconia) in one place — likely the single most useful table for a cementation-protocol answer.
- **Full CAD/CAM systems chapter** (CR Ch.14; STURD Ch.22): 3-component model (scanner/software/fabrication), named scanners with their scan technology (3M True Definition, TRIOS, iTero-confocal, CEREC AC Bluecam-active triangulation, PlanScan-laser triangulation), 3/4/5-axis milling distinctions, digital-vs-elastomeric-impression comparison table (contacts 62% vs 46% "perfect," fit 92% vs 71%, occlusion 74% vs 48%), full chairside CEREC clinical workflow, history (Duret 1973, Mörmann/CEREC 1985), and the explicit design principle that undercuts should be physically blocked out with dentin substitute BEFORE scanning, not virtually blocked out in software (since the mill cannot reproduce an undercut and cement fills the gap left by virtual blocking).
- **Full clinical ceramic-inlay/onlay cementation protocol** (SIKRI, 12 numbered steps): try-in → rubber dam → HF etch → silane → phosphoric-acid tooth etch → adhesive/resin cement → seat under occlusal closure → cleanup → multi-direction light-cure → glycerin oxygen-block gel → minimal polish → avoid intraoral adjustment post-cementation.
- **Laminate veneer tooth-preparation KIT and full 6-step protocol** (SIKRI) — the single biggest gap-fill: named LVS (Laminate Veneer System) depth-cutting burs (LVS-1 0.3mm cervical, LVS-2 0.5mm incisal, LVS-3/4 proximal chamfer), 6-step sequence (labial reduction → interproximal reduction/extension → gingival/sulcular extension → finish-line establishment → incisal reduction [3 design options: chamfer/interlock, butt-joint, lingual-wrap] → lingual reduction), proximal margin placement options with pros/cons, and the point that temporization is usually NOT required for enamel-confined laminate preps.
- **STURD's own, independently detailed veneer-prep protocol** (facial reduction depth 0.5-0.75mm mid-facial tapering to 0.3-0.5mm gingival, done in 3 zones verified by diamond-striation merging; incisal reduction 1mm minimum/1.5mm preferred, gauged with a silicone index; named bur sequence — tapered rounded-end diamond tip Ø1-1.2mm as its own depth gauge, flame-shaped fine diamond for gingival dressing, 30-fluted carbide finishing bur used dry [Midwest #9803/Brasseler #7801], Dialite Porcelain Polishing Kit, porcelain polishing paste) — a second, independently-sourced full protocol that corroborates and extends SIKRI's.
- **Componeer (prefabricated composite veneer) technique** (SIKRI): split rubber-dam technique, thickness 0.3mm cervical→0.7mm incisal, contour-guide sizing, 37% phosphoric acid 15s etch, two-layer 5th-gen bonding protocol, named placer/flash-removal instruments — a named commercial technique almost certainly absent from a review-article-sourced draft.
- **Intraoral veneer repair, two distinct protocols**: direct composite veneer (roughen/micro-etch → etch → bond → composite, SIKRI and STURD agree) vs porcelain veneer (ONLY ~10% buffered HF gel under rubber dam, NEVER full-strength HF intraorally, OR sandblast/micro-abrasion kit, then silane/adhesive/composite; large fractures need full replacement) — both SIKRI and STURD give this with matching safety caveats.
- **Full luting-agents chapter content** (SIKRI Ch.12): 11 numbered clinical requirements with numeric targets (≥70 MPa compressive strength, <25µm film thickness, modulus hierarchy dentin 18-30 GPa < casting alloys 90-220 GPa / ceramics 55-230 GPa), comparative data across zinc phosphate/polycarboxylate/GIC/RMGIC/resin cements with named products (RelyX Unicem, Appeal, Nexus 2, Panavia, C&B Metabond), "seating vs retention" mechanics, and the concept of cement "venting."
- **Provisional restorations for veneers specifically are a distinct sub-case**: must be fabricated AND placed simultaneously intraorally (cannot be removed/trimmed/recemented like crown provisionals, too thin to survive handling), bonded via only a 2mm spot-etch rather than full-surface bonding (STURD) — a nuance a generic crown/bridge-provisional-only draft answer would miss entirely.
- **Shade-matching full clinical protocol** (SIKRI Ch.7): shade BEFORE prep, remove lipstick/bright clothing, assess at eye level in 5-second windows, compare under multiple lighting culminating in natural daylight, "Value > Hue/Chroma" principle, 5 structured methods of communicating shade to the lab. PHIL adds the formal physics: metamerism defined, shade-tab systematic-mismatch problem (tabs thicker/more translucent than real crowns, necks deliberately higher-chroma), and the energy-balance equation (incident = scattered+reflected+absorbed+transmitted+fluoresced).
- **Decision framework for indirect-vs-direct / which-indirect-material** (MOUNT pp.217-221): concise, directly quotable comparison of gold/ceramic/PFM/composite/GIC by indication, explicitly addressing when a laminate veneer vs partial-coverage onlay vs full crown is the right choice.
- **Mandibular anterior teeth are "rarely indicated" for veneers** (STURD) — thin facioincisal portion, occlusal-attrition risk, porcelain wears opposing enamel; and tetracycline-stained teeth need 5-15% opaque porcelain for masking, beyond which esthetic vitality is lost and a full crown may be preferable to a veneer.
- **Abrasiveness/wear science** (PHIL): Al-Hiyasat et al. 1998 carbonated-beverage wear study; glassy/glass-dominated ceramic hardness >460 kg/mm² vs enamel 343 kg/mm² (ceramic wears opposing enamel preferentially); crystalline ceramics 1200-1500 kg/mm² (even higher wear risk) — explains why porcelain denture teeth opposing natural dentition are avoided in favour of polymer teeth.
- **APF/fluoride chemical attack on glazed porcelain** (PHIL): 1.23% APF or 8% SnF2 roughens glazed feldspathic porcelain within 4 minutes via selective Na+ leaching; 0.4% SnF2/2% NaF has no significant effect — a clinically actionable threshold for a biocompatibility/durability answer.
- **Captek/Procera/Dicor historical process narratives** in rich, citable detail (see C below).

## C. High-value new facts (by likely question/topic)

**History**: Duchateau 1774 "mineral paste teeth"; de Chemant 1788/1789 first single-block baked-porcelain denture + patent; Fonzi ~1806-08 first baked-porcelain single tooth with platinum hooks, 26 shades (also "terrometallic tooth" 1808 per PHIL); Pincus (Beverly Hills) built porcelain veneers for Hollywood actors incl. Shirley Temple in the 1930s, retained by denture powder, "Building mouth personality" *J Calif Dent Assoc* 1938; Rochette 1975 first resin-bonded ceramic to etched enamel; Horn 1983 first HF-etched leucite veneer bonded with phosphoric-acid-etch + composite; Land, *Dental Cosmos* 1903 porcelain-crown description; Weinstein US Patent 3,052,782 (1962, two separate patents per PHIL); Etruscan dentures ~700 BC; Planteau introduces porcelain teeth to US 1817; Peale's Philadelphia baking process 1822; S.S. White Co. founded 1844; Duret 1973 (first CAD/CAM concept), Mörmann/CEREC 1985 (first clinical CAD/CAM system).

**Composition**: dental porcelain omits kaolin entirely per VN (0%, vs 50% household porcelain; ~65% feldspar/25% quartz/0% kaolin) — BON gives a differing simplification (3-5% kaolin, 70-85% feldspar, 12-25% quartz); use VN as primary, flag BON's kaolin % as a simplification. Typical oxide composition (VN Table 3.4.2): silica 63%, alumina 17%, boric oxide 7%, potash 7%, soda 4%, other 2%. Average particle size ~25µm, multimodal distribution for packing density. Firing shrinkage ~20% volumetric (biscuit bake); 10-20% linear per BON/PW.

**Classification tables ready-made for an exam answer**: VN's resin-bonded-ceramics table — Feldspathic (sintered: Vitadur; CAD-CAM: Vita Blocks) / Leucite-reinforced (sintered: Mirage, Fortress, Optec-HP; hot-pressed: Empress I; CAD-CAM: Procad) / Fluormica (cast: Dicor; CAD-CAM: Macor) / Lithium disilicate (hot-pressed: Empress II; CAD-CAM: e.max CAD). PW's fusing-temperature classification: High-fusing 1288-1371°C (denture teeth); medium-fusing 1093-1260°C; low-fusing 871-1066°C (veneering ceramic/all-ceramic). PHIL's Table 18-2 (by firing temp, with an added "Ultralow-fusing <850°C" category, e.g. Duceram LFC 1992 — hydrothermal glass, nonbridging OH groups, 400-500nm crystal particles enhancing opalescence, "self-healing" 1µm hydrothermal surface layer). PHIL Table 18-3: explicit primary/secondary/contraindication indication matrix across 9 ceramic classes.

**Zirconia**: ~3 mol% yttria for 3Y-TZP (VN/BON); theoretical density 6.08 g/cm³, clinical-grade requires >98.7% of theoretical density (CR); cubic zirconia more translucent (isotropic) but non-transformable → lower toughness/strength than 3Y-TZP; low thermal conductivity → delamination risk with fast heating/cooling of veneering porcelain, addressed by slower firing schedules; grinding/abrasion on AGED 3Y-TZP can trigger a beneficial surface t→m transformation (compressive stress) but risks long-term defects — "extreme care" with manufacturer instructions (CR); regeneration heat treatment (900°C, 1h) reverses surface monoclinic back to tetragonal after grinding (PHIL); zirconia-toughened alumina (ZTA) 70-90wt% alumina/10-20wt% zirconia, doubles alumina's strength, 2-4× toughness via stress-induced transformation (PHIL); coping thickness reducible to 0.4-0.5mm with translucent grades (CR); low-temperature/hydrothermal degradation occurs 200-400°C in lab studies, mechanism ("possibly OH⁻ attack") still not fully established (PHIL) — useful nuance against overclaiming a settled mechanism.

**Lithium disilicate**: heat-pressed at 910-920°C (CR)/920°C (VN, lower than leucite's 1180°C); final microstructure ~65vol% interlocking prismatic crystals, 2-5µm length × 0.8µm diameter, ~1vol% residual porosity (CR); CAD blocks machined in a partially-crystallized state (lithium metasilicate + lithium disilicate nuclei) then crystallized by post-mill firing 850°C/10min (CR); zirconia-containing lithium silicate variant ~10wt% ZrO2, crystallized 840°C/8min (CR).

**Leucite ceramics**: tetragonal↔cubic transformation at 625°C, 1.2vol% unit-cell expansion, explaining tetragonal leucite's high CTE (>20×10⁻⁶/°C) (CR); 10-20vol% leucite in conventional metal-ceramic porcelains controls CTE match to alloy; heat-pressed ingots 1150-1180°C, ~20min dwell, 9vol% residual porosity (CR).

**Metal-ceramic alloys/bonding**: three controlling factors for bond — mechanical interlocking, interatomic/chemical bonding across the oxide-porcelain interface, residual stress magnitude (CR/PHIL/MCC all agree); "degassing" is explicitly a misnomer — it forms oxide, doesn't degas (PHIL); oxide colour indicates composition (NiO-rich = dark grey, Cr2O3-rich = greenish; Cr2O3-rich oxides may bond poorly, needing a bonding agent) (CR); silver-containing noble alloys can cause porcelain "greening" via ionic Ag diffusion (CR); Ni-Cr alloys sometimes contain <0.9% beryllium for castability/bond, but Be release during grinding/polishing is a known carcinogen risk to the dental technician, release rate 5-6× the bulk composition (VN) — occupational-safety fact likely missing from drafts; titanium-ceramic bonding is poor above 800°C (excessive oxidation), Ti's CTE is only 9.6ppm/°C requiring specially formulated low-expansion ceramics (VN); actual CTC-mismatch stress calculation — a 1.7×10⁻⁶/K difference generates ~280 MPa shear stress cooling from 954°C; "thermally compatible" defined as Δα ≤0.5×10⁻⁶/K (PHIL); real bite-force data: 890N anterior typical max, 2224N posterior typical max, 4337N record clench (PHIL); design rule: metal-ceramic junction ≥1.5mm from centric occlusal contacts, shoulder depth ≥1.2mm (CR); porcelain repair — silane-only repair is "temporary" without a metal-bonding agent; silica-coat+silane or tin-coat+acidic primer for base-metal surfaces (CR).

**Aluminous porcelain process**: platinum-foil electroplated with tin, oxidized in furnace → continuous SnO2 film → reduces subsurface porosity/microcracks on the fit surface (PHIL).

**Shade/optics**: dentin-porcelain translucency 18-38%, enamel-porcelain translucency 45-50% (CR Table 11.3, Brodbelt et al. 1980); opalescence requires crystal particle size ≈ wavelength of light; both ZrO2 and Y2O3 additions increase opalescence in base dentin ceramics (CR).

**Biological**: lithium-disilicate ceramics show measurable initial in-vitro cytotoxicity that diminishes over several weeks as "surface corrosion" subsides (PW, citing Giordano et al.) — citable biocompatibility fact.

**CAD/CAM specifics**: lute-line width 60-150µm, can wear to half that depth (~50µm clinically-relevant marginal defect) (MCC); design/milling time ~10-25min design + 5-10min milling (MCC); three Y-TZP CAD-CAM workflows — green/compacted state, partially-sintered state, fully-sintered/hard-state milling (PHIL); sintering shrinkage of machined zirconia ~20-25% (CR).
