## STUDY MODE
"Porcelain" strictly means the kaolin-quartz-feldspar family; the question is answered as advances in dental ceramics that began from porcelain. Four problem statements drove change: low strength of feldspathic porcelain, veneer chipping, opacity of strong cores, and the lack of chairside speed.

1. **Strengthened glass-ceramics.** Leucite (35-45 vol%) then lithium disilicate (about 65-70 vol%, 450-550 MPa biaxial) (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022; Nejat, 2024). Lithium silicate and zirconia-reinforced lithium silicate (about 10 wt% zirconia) and virgilite-containing blocks (CEREC Tessera, 700 MPa as quoted by the manufacturer) widen indications (Nejat, 2024).
2. **Zirconia generations.** 3Y (strong, opaque), then 4Y and 5Y (more cubic phase, more translucent, 550-850 MPa), then strength- and translucency-graded blocks that combine 3Y cervical and 5Y incisal zones in one disc (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022; Nejat, 2024).
3. **Monolithic and CAD-on designs.** Monolithic zirconia and lithium disilicate remove the veneer-chipping weak link; the CAD-on technique fuses a lithium disilicate cap to a zirconia coping with a fusion glass-ceramic, giving fatigue survival of 591,071 cycles versus 16,428 for porcelain-veneered zirconia in one laboratory study (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).
4. **Polymer-infiltrated and resin-matrix ceramics.** About 86 wt% ceramic network infiltrated with about 14 wt% polymer; dentin-like modulus, flexural strength about 150 MPa (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).
5. **Digital fabrication.** Subtractive CAD/CAM of precrystallised or fully crystallised blocks and presintered zirconia; additive manufacturing of zirconia and lithium disilicate is emerging but data are mixed (flexural strength 934-1071 MPa in one study versus 320 MPa printed against 915 MPa milled in another) (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).
6. **Adhesive science.** Material-specific protocols: HF and silane for glass-ceramics; alumina blasting and phosphate monomer primers for zirconia (Nejat, 2024).

Advances are not all gains: translucent zirconia loses strength and transformation toughening; fully crystallised lithium disilicate blocks and 3D-printed ceramics have shorter clinical records (Nejat, 2024).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 330; about 15 min)**

### Introduction
Dental porcelain began as feldspathic glass, strong in compression but weak in tension. Advances have aimed at greater strength, preserved translucency and faster, more accurate fabrication.

### Advances
1. **Glass-ceramics:** leucite-reinforced (Empress), lithium disilicate (e.max, about 450-550 MPa), zirconia-reinforced lithium silicate.
2. **Translucent zirconia:** 3Y to 4Y to 5Y; gradient (strength- and colour-graded) discs.
3. **Monolithic restorations and CAD-on:** removes veneer chipping.
4. **Resin-matrix/PICN ceramics:** dentin-like modulus, easy milling and repair.
5. **CAD/CAM:** chairside and laboratory milling; precrystallised blocks.
6. **Additive manufacturing:** printed zirconia and lithium disilicate; experimental.
7. **Bonding:** HF and silane for glass-ceramics; air abrasion and MDP for zirconia.

### Comparative gain
| Era | Material | Gain | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1960s | Feldspathic | Esthetics | Weak |
| 1990s | Leucite, LDS | Strength, press | Anterior or single unit |
| 2000s | 3Y zirconia | Very strong | Opaque, chipping |
| 2010s+ | 4Y/5Y, graded | Translucent | Lower strength |

### Conclusion
The trend is from strongest-possible to best-balanced: choose the ceramic for the site, thickness and optical need.

- PICN: 86/14 wt% ceramic/polymer.
- Printed zirconia data are inconsistent.
