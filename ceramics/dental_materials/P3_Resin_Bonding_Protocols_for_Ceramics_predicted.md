## STUDY MODE
**Principle.** An ideal protocol gives a dual bond, micromechanical and chemical (Blatz et al, 2022). Most dental "adhesion" is mechanical; true chemical bonding needs angstrom proximity with many bonds (Alani and Patel, 2025, via digest; Powers, via digest).

**Surface treatments.** HF etching, airborne-particle abrasion with alumina or glass beads, tribochemical silica coating, lasers; selected by ceramic composition and mechanical properties (Blatz et al, 2022).

**Silica-based ceramics (glass phase) — HF protocol is genuinely dual-era, not one number (confirmed directly this session).** HF dissolves the glass phase, exposing silica and creating microporosities; then silane bridges ceramic and resin (Blatz et al, 2022). HF concentration and time depend on glass and crystal content AND on which era's protocol is cited: the **modern** protocol (Blatz et al, a Dent Clin North Am review) gives feldspathic 9.8% for 2 min; leucite 9.8% for 1 min; lithium silicate 4.6% for 20 s; PICN 9.8% for 1 min. **Alani & Patel's modern BDJ clinician's guide (read directly this session) gives a different modern protocol: only 5% or 9.5% HF for just 10/20/60 seconds depending on the specific ceramic** [SRC, AP]. **Sikri's older, more conservative protocol (also read directly this session) gives 8-12% HF for 1-3 minutes, with the longer end reserved for feldspathic** [SRC, SIKRI]. Craig's gives its own intermediate range of **2.5-10% HF for 1-4 minutes** [SRC, CR]. **Present all of these as "older protocol vs modern protocol(s)," each attributed by source — there is no single correct HF concentration/time to state as the answer.** Higher HF concentration produces more topographic change but may weaken the ceramic (Blatz et al, 2022). HF is caustic: rubber dam.

**Alani & Patel's Table 2.10 — the single most useful table for this question (confirmed directly this session).** AP's Table 2.10 gives a complete, material-by-material intraoral bonding protocol covering gold/base-metal, composite/resin-nanoceramic, fibre post, glass-ceramic, and zirconia in one place [SRC, AP]. This table should be the backbone of a full-marks answer to this question rather than a reconstructed list from journal reviews alone.

**Silane.** Applied 20 s then dried (Blatz et al, 2022); silanate before try-in so contamination is removed by ultrasonic cleaning afterwards (Manso et al, 2011); excess silane removed by 80 °C water rinse (Manso et al, 2011). A universal adhesive containing silane bonds silica ceramics but silane alone does not bond zirconia (Sakaguchi et al, 2019, via digest).

**Metal-oxide ceramics (zirconia, alumina) — the never-HF rule, with the mechanistic reason confirmed directly.** No glass phase, so HF is ineffective — more specifically, **zirconia has no amorphous silica phase for HF/silane chemistry to act on at all**, which is why the "never HF zirconia" rule is not just a concentration/time matter but a fundamentally different chemistry (CR) [SRC, CR]. Air abrasion with 50 μm alumina (or silica-coated particles 50-60 μm) at about 2 bar, then a phosphate-monomer (10-MDP) primer, which forms **Zr-O-P bonds** with the zirconia surface (CR) [SRC, CR]; combined silane-MDP primers give the highest bond strengths (Blatz et al, 2022). **Universal adhesives bond zirconia best at pH 2.2-3.2** (Craig's) [SRC, CR]. Tribochemical silica coating followed by silane gives mixed results (Sakaguchi et al, 2019, via digest).

**Resin-matrix and PICN.** HF 5% for 60 s or air abrasion depending on product (Nejat, 2025; Blatz et al, 2022).

**Bond strength is remarkably material-independent once the correct protocol is followed (Alani & Patel, a likely-missed unifying point).** Across the different ceramic classes, achievable resin bond strength is consistently around **20-30 MPa regardless of which specific ceramic** is being bonded, provided the correct HF+silane (glass-ceramics) or sandblast+MDP (zirconia) mechanism is followed for that material (AP p.52) [SRC, AP]. This supports framing the whole topic as "match the mechanism to the material," not "memorise a different bond strength per material."

**The "bonding stack" concept (Alani & Patel — a conceptual point worth stating explicitly).** Resin bonding to ceramic is a **stack of sequential steps** (surface conditioning → silane/primer → adhesive → resin cement → isolation/moisture control), and the **weakest single step in the stack determines the overall clinical outcome**, regardless of how well the other steps were executed. This reframes "bonding protocol" questions away from a list of steps and toward a risk-analysis framing: identify the step most likely to fail in a given clinical scenario [SRC, AP].

**Universal/8th-generation adhesive generational framework (Alani & Patel — likely missing from a review-article-only draft).** Adhesive systems are classified by generation, each with characteristic timing: **4th generation** (separate etch, prime, bond — three bottles, total-etch), **5th generation** (one-bottle total-etch), **6th-7th generation** (self-etch, one- or two-step), and **8th generation/"universal"** adhesives (combine etch-and-rinse or self-etch capability with MDP/silane chemistry in one bottle, usable across tooth and multiple ceramic/metal substrates) [SRC, AP]. This generational framework underlies why a modern "universal adhesive" can replace several separate conditioning steps for both glass-ceramic and zirconia in one product.

**Try-in contamination.** Saliva phosphates contaminate zirconia; use Ivoclean (zirconium oxide suspension) or Katana Cleaner (MDP salt) (Blatz et al, 2022).

**Cements.** Light-cure for veneers and shallow inlays; dual-cure for thick restorations; self-adhesive cements have lower enamel bond strength (Manso et al, 2011). Total-etch gives better seal at veneer margins than self-etch (Blatz et al, 2022).

### Principle
Dual bond: micromechanical interlock plus chemical bond to ceramic and tooth.

### Protocols (dual/triple-era attribution — do not state one HF figure as the only correct answer)
| Ceramic | Modern (Blatz) | Modern (Alani & Patel) | Older (Sikri) | Craig's range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Feldspathic | 9.8% HF 2 min | 5-9.5% HF 10-60s | 8-12% HF 1-3min | 2.5-10% HF 1-4min |
| Leucite | 9.8% HF 1 min | 5-9.5% HF 10-60s | 8-12% HF 1-3min | 2.5-10% HF 1-4min |
| Lithium disilicate/silicate | 4.6% HF 20s | 5-9.5% HF 10-60s | — | 2.5-10% HF 1-4min |
| PICN | 9.8% HF 1 min | 5-9.5% HF 10-60s | — | — |
| Zirconia/alumina | 50μm alumina 2 bar + MDP, NEVER HF (no amorphous silica phase) | same | same | same |
AP's Table 2.10 gives the complete material-by-material protocol (gold/base-metal, composite/resin-nanoceramic, fibre post, glass-ceramic, zirconia) in one place — the single most useful table for this question.

### Steps
Try-in → clean (ultrasonic alcohol; Ivoclean/Katana cleaner) → condition → couple → isolate → tooth etch and adhesive → resin cement → remove excess → cure.

### Points
HF only for glass phase (zirconia has no amorphous silica phase at all — a different chemistry, not just a different concentration); rubber dam; no contamination after silane; silane alone does not bond zirconia; universal adhesives bond zirconia best at pH 2.2-3.2; MDP forms Zr-O-P bonds; dual-cure when thick. Achievable bond strength is ~20-30 MPa regardless of ceramic, once the correct mechanism is matched to the material (AP). The "bonding stack": the weakest single step determines the outcome. Adhesive generations: 4th (3-bottle total-etch) → 5th (1-bottle total-etch) → 6th-7th (self-etch) → 8th/universal (combines etch/self-etch + MDP/silane in one bottle).

### Conclusion
Bonding is material-specific; conditioning must match composition.
