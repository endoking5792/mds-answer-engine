## STUDY MODE
**Definition and phases.** Zirconia (ZrO2) is a polymorphic polycrystalline ceramic: monoclinic from room temperature to 1100 °C, tetragonal 1100-2300 °C, cubic above 2300 °C (Moshaverinia, 2020). It is stabilised for dental use with yttria (Y-TZP) — **confirmed as ~3 mol% yttria for 3Y-TZP** (VN/BON) [SRC, VN/BON] — with a trace of alumina to prevent yttria leaching (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013, via digest). Monoclinic grains are 4-5% larger than tetragonal; tetragonal is strong and transforms under stress; cubic is isotropic, the most translucent and weakest (Nejat, 2025). **Theoretical density is 6.08 g/cm³, and clinical-grade zirconia requires >98.7% of theoretical density** (Craig's) [SRC, CR].

**Transformation toughening — volume-change figure corrected.** Stress at a crack tip converts tetragonal to monoclinic grains, compressing the crack — but **the volume increase is NOT a flat "~4%"**: van Noort gives no number at all (just "accompanied by an expansion"); **Phillips gives 3-5%** — attribute the range to Phillips specifically rather than stating "4%" as a settled textbook figure [SRC, VN/PHIL].

**Strength and toughness — corrected with full per-source attribution (this was previously understated/flattened).** Not a flat "900 MPa" or flat "K_Ic ≈8": Craig's gives 3Y-TZP flexural strength 900-1500 MPa (one measured series: 1087±173 MPa), and separately gives translucent cubic-containing zirconia 600-700 MPa (measured 609 MPa, K_Ic 2.4 MPa·m^0.5) [SRC, CR]. Phillips gives 3Y-TZP ≈900 MPa with fracture toughness **8-10.3 MPa·m^0.5 (a range, not a flat ~8)**; nondoped monoclinic zirconia K_Ic ≈2.06 MPa·m^0.5 (2.6 at full density) [SRC, PHIL]. Van Noort/Bonsor give Y-TZP flexural strength **>1000 MPa (VN) vs 650 MPa (BON)** — a genuine cross-source discrepancy, state both with attribution rather than picking one [SRC, VN/BON].

**Non-monotonic grain-size effect — a genuinely new, counter-intuitive finding to add (Phillips).** Strength does **not** rise monotonically with density/sintering: as grain size increases from 0.9→1.4 µm, strength **rises** 650→1000 MPa (a "critical" grain size effect), but as grain size increases further to 1.8 µm, strength **falls** to 750 MPa. This non-monotonic relationship is a likely-missed, examinable, counter-intuitive point: more sintering/larger grain size is not simply "better" [SRC, PHIL].

**Types.** 3Y-TZP (tetragonal, 900-1500 MPa, opaque; framework and monolithic posterior), 4Y (about 75% tetragonal/25% cubic, 750-850 MPa), 5Y (about 50/50, about 550 MPa; anterior monolithic), and graded multilayer discs (colour and translucency, or 3Y-5Y strength gradient) (Nejat, 2025; Bacchi and Cesar, 2022). Glass-infiltrated zirconia-toughened alumina (In-Ceram zirconia) is a different material (Guess et al, 2011). **Zirconia-toughened alumina (ZTA)** is a distinct, textbook-confirmed composite: **70-90 wt% alumina with 10-20 wt% zirconia**, which doubles alumina's strength and gives 2-4× its toughness via stress-induced transformation (Phillips) [SRC, PHIL].

**Fabrication.** Presintered block milled and sintered with about 25% shrinkage compensated by software; HIP blocks fully dense (Bonsor and Pearson, 2013, via digest); printing is experimental.

**Properties.** Highest strength and toughness of dental ceramics; high opacity because tetragonal grains are birefringent; low thermal conductivity; no HF etching (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).

**Clinical.** Monolithic crowns 91-100% survival; veneered chipping 6-10% single crowns, 20-32% FDPs (Nejat, 2025; Zhang and Kelly, 2017). Zirconia crowns with adequate retention may be cemented conventionally; otherwise air abrasion plus MDP primer and resin cement (Blatz et al, 2022).

**Cautions — low-temperature degradation, do not overclaim a settled mechanism.** Low-temperature degradation (see P4) occurs 200-400°C in lab studies; the mechanism is "possibly OH⁻ attack" but Phillips explicitly states the mechanism is **still not fully established** — present this as an open question, not a settled fact [SRC, PHIL]. Grinding and air abrasion can trigger transformation, so follow manufacturer instructions (Sakaguchi et al, 2019, via digest); Craig's gives the specific clinical nuance that grinding/abrasion on **AGED** 3Y-TZP can trigger a beneficial surface t→m transformation (adding compressive stress) but carries a risk of long-term defects, requiring "extreme care" per manufacturer instructions [SRC, CR]. **Regeneration heat treatment (900°C for 1 hour) reverses surface monoclinic back to tetragonal after grinding** (Phillips) — a specific, citable remedial protocol [SRC, PHIL].

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 330)**

### Definition
Yttria-stabilised tetragonal zirconia polycrystal (Y-TZP): a fully crystalline, glass-free ceramic with transformation toughening.

### Phases
Monoclinic (room temperature), tetragonal (1100-2300 °C), cubic (above 2300 °C); yttria stabilises tetragonal at room temperature.

### Types
| Type | Cubic phase | Strength (MPa) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3Y-TZP | Little | 900-1500 (CR) / >1000 (VN) / 650 (BON) | Frameworks, posterior monolithic |
| 4Y | About 25% | 750-850 | Crowns, 3-unit FDP |
| 5Y | About 50% | About 550 | Anterior monolithic |
| Graded | Variable | 650-1200 | Esthetic monolithic |
(Figures are edition-dependent — cite the source, not one number. K_Ic for 3Y-TZP: 8-10.3 MPa·m^0.5, PHIL, a range not flat ~8. Non-monotonic grain-size effect: strength rises 650→1000 MPa as grain size 0.9→1.4µm, then falls to 750 MPa at 1.8µm, PHIL.)

### Properties
High strength and toughness; transformation toughening; opaque; low thermal conductivity; biocompatible; low plaque; not etchable.

### Advantages
Strength, monolithic use, metal-free, thin copings (0.4 mm).

### Disadvantages
Opacity, veneer chipping, low-temperature degradation, bonding technique, wear of antagonist if rough, difficult repair.

### Bonding
Air abrasion 50 μm alumina at 2 bar + MDP primer + dual-cure resin; conventional cement acceptable with good retention.

### Conclusion
Zirconia gives the strongest ceramic restorations; grade should match esthetic and load requirements.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Phases and temperatures; yttria ~3 mol% for 3Y-TZP (VN/BON).
- Transformation toughening volume change: Phillips' 3-5%, NOT a flat "4%" (van Noort gives no number).
- 3Y strength genuinely conflicts by source: CR 900-1500MPa, VN >1000MPa, BON 650MPa; K_Ic 8-10.3 (PHIL range, not flat ~8).
- Non-monotonic grain-size effect: strength peaks at ~1.4µm grain size (1000MPa), falls at 1.8µm (750MPa) — PHIL.
- Theoretical density 6.08g/cm³; clinical grade >98.7% of theoretical (CR).
- ZTA: 70-90wt% alumina/10-20wt% zirconia, 2-4x toughness gain (PHIL).
- LTD mechanism "possibly OH⁻ attack," still not fully established (PHIL) — don't overclaim certainty.
- Regeneration heat treatment 900°C/1h reverses surface monoclinic after grinding (PHIL).
- 3Y/4Y/5Y cubic content.
- No HF; MDP.
