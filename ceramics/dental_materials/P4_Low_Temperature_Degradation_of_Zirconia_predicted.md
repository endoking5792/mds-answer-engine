## STUDY MODE
**Definition.** Slow, spontaneous tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation of zirconia in a moist environment, called low-temperature degradation (LTD), hydrothermal degradation or ageing.

**Mechanism — temperature range and transformation-% figures corrected against primary sources.** Phillips (read directly this session) gives the laboratory temperature range as **200-400°C** (a narrower, more specific range than the previously-cited 150-400°C) at which the transformation begins at the surface and progresses into the bulk; each transformed grain expands, stressing neighbouring grains and causing microcracking [SRC, PHIL]. **The associated transformation volume change is NOT a flat "~4%": van Noort gives no number at all (just "accompanied by an expansion"); Phillips gives a 3-5% range** — if this answer states "4%" anywhere, attribute it specifically to Phillips' range, not to a settled consensus figure [SRC, VN/PHIL]. Surface roughness increases, grains fragment, and microcracks form (Anusavice et al, 2013, via digest). **Phillips explicitly states the mechanism is "possibly OH⁻ (hydroxyl) attack" but is still NOT fully established** — this session's direct read confirms the mechanism remains genuinely open, not merely "unresolved" in a softer sense; do not present any single mechanistic explanation as settled fact [SRC, PHIL].

**Why it matters.** The same transformation that toughens a crack tip becomes a liability at the surface; it could roughen the surface, increase antagonist wear and weaken the restoration over the long term [Likely].

**Risk modifiers.** Grain size (transformability rises with grain size), yttria content, cubic content, surface treatment (grinding and air abrasion can trigger transformation) (Sakaguchi et al, 2019, via digest). Reports differ on translucent grades: Zhang and Kelly (2017) note that cubic/tetragonal zirconia (Zpex Smile) is also subject to LTD; Blatz et al (2022) state that 4Y/5Y are less susceptible to LTD than 3Y-TZP and have no transformation toughening; 3D-printed zirconia showed a higher rate of phase transformation and LTD (Nejat, 2025). This is a genuine contradiction between sources.

**Management — a clinically important, previously-understated nuance (Craig's, read directly).** Grinding/abrasion specifically on **AGED** zirconia (zirconia that has already been in service and undergone some ageing) can trigger a **beneficial** surface t→m transformation that adds a protective layer of compressive stress — but this carries a real **risk of introducing long-term defects**, so Craig's explicitly calls for **"extreme care"** and strict adherence to manufacturer instructions when adjusting aged zirconia restorations, rather than treating grinding as a uniformly safe or uniformly harmful intervention [SRC, CR]. A specific **regeneration heat treatment — 900°C for 1 hour — reverses surface monoclinic phase back to tetragonal after grinding** (Phillips), giving a concrete remedial protocol a laboratory can apply [SRC, PHIL]. Beyond this: follow manufacturer instructions for grinding and surface treatment; avoid aggressive airborne-particle abrasion; polish after adjustment; accelerated ageing in tests is not equal to clinical ageing.

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 280)**

### Definition
Spontaneous t → m transformation of Y-TZP at the surface in the presence of moisture at low temperature (200-400 °C in vitro, per Phillips — a narrower range than the previously-cited 150-400°C).

### Mechanism
Surface transformation → volume expansion (Phillips' 3-5% range, NOT a flat "4%"; van Noort gives no number at all) → stress on neighbours → microcracks → roughness, grain pull-out. Mechanism: "possibly OH⁻ attack" but explicitly still NOT fully established (Phillips) — do not overclaim certainty.

### Factors
Grain size, yttria content, cubic content, residual stress from grinding or abrasion, moisture and temperature.

### Consequences
Surface roughening, antagonist wear, strength loss (long term; clinical incidence not established).

### Prevention
Controlled sintering, small grains with enough stabiliser, avoid aggressive surface treatment, polish. Grinding/abrasion on AGED zirconia can trigger a beneficial surface t→m compressive layer but risks long-term defects — "extreme care" per manufacturer instructions (Craig's). Regeneration heat treatment 900°C/1h reverses surface monoclinic back to tetragonal after grinding (Phillips).

### Status
Not fully resolved; modern translucent grades are claimed to be less susceptible but sources disagree.

### Conclusion
LTD is a recognised long-term question for zirconia; it is minimised by composition and handling but its clinical significance remains unproven.
