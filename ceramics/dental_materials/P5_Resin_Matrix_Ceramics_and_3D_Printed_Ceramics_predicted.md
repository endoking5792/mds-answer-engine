## STUDY MODE

### Resin-matrix ceramics
**Definition.** Hybrid materials with an organic matrix highly filled with ceramic; ADA CDT 2013 classes materials with predominantly inorganic content as ceramics, so these count (Moshaverinia, 2020; Nejat, 2025). More than 50% inorganic.

**Types.**
- *PICN (Vita Enamic):* partially sintered feldspathic network (86 wt%, 75 vol%) infiltrated with dimethacrylate (UDMA and TEGDMA) by capillary action; dual interpenetrating networks; modulus about 30 GPa, hardness 1.7 GPa, strength about 150-159 MPa (Zhang and Kelly, 2017; Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).
- *Resin nanoceramics (Lava Ultimate, Cerasmart):* heavily filled resin (80-90 wt%) with silica and zirconia nanoparticles and nanoclusters cured at high temperature and pressure; the continuous phase is the resin; nanoclusters give polish retention but voids absorb fluids (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).

**Properties.** Not brittle; shock-absorbing; easy millability and edge stability (under 0.5 mm); easy adjustment and repair; no post-milling firing (Zhang and Kelly, 2017; Nejat, 2025). Lower strength than lithium disilicate at 1.0-1.5 mm but similar fatigue at endocrown thickness (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022). Little clinical data on longevity (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).

**Surface treatment.** PICN: 5% HF 60 s or 9.8% HF 1 min, plus silane; resin nanoceramics: air abrasion 25-50 μm alumina, or HF for Cerasmart; silica-coated sandblasting for resin nanoceramic onlays (Nejat, 2025; Blatz et al, 2022; Alani and Patel, 2025, via digest).

**Indications.** Inlays, onlays, veneers, crowns, implant crowns, endocrowns (Nejat, 2025).

### Additive manufacturing (3D printing)
**Methods.** Vat polymerisation (DLP, SLA) and 3D gel deposition for zirconia; resin-ceramic printing (e.g. permanent crown resins) (Nejat, 2025).
**Evidence.** Printed zirconia flexural strength comparable to milled in some studies (934-1071 vs 1007 MPa) but lower in another (320 vs 915 MPa; aged 281 vs 573); marginal and internal discrepancies larger than milled but within the clinically acceptable range; higher rate of phase transformation and LTD, lower hardness, more roughness and porosity (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022; Nejat, 2025).
**Problems.** Slurry viscosity, bubbles, powder agglomerates, interlayer defects, nesting angle and supports, debinding and sintering (Nejat, 2025).
**Potential.** Complex geometry, graded properties, less waste, multiple shades (Nejat, 2025).
**Clinical data.** Essentially absent.

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 330)**

### Resin-matrix ceramics
| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| PICN | 86 wt% porous feldspathic network infiltrated with polymer | Vita Enamic |
| Resin nanoceramic | Nanofilled resin (80-90 wt%) | Lava Ultimate, Cerasmart |

Properties: elastic modulus near dentin; shock-absorbing; easy milling and repair; no firing; lower strength; ageing of polymer; limited clinical data.
Bonding: HF and silane (PICN); air abrasion (nanoceramic).
Indications: inlays, onlays, veneers, crowns, implant crowns, endocrowns.

### 3D-printed ceramics
Vat polymerisation printing, then debinding and sintering; benefits: geometry, graded structures, less waste; drawbacks: porosity, interlayer defects, lower hardness, variable strength, no long-term data.

### Conclusion
Resin-matrix ceramics bridge ceramics and composites for conservative, easily repaired single units; printed ceramics are promising but still experimental.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Enamic 86 wt% ceramic / 14 wt% polymer; modulus about 30 GPa.
- Resin-matrix counted as ceramic when more than 50% inorganic (CDT 2013).
- Printed zirconia: inconsistent strength, porosity.
