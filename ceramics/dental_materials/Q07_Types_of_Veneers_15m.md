## STUDY MODE

### Definition
A veneer (laminate) is a thin layer of tooth-coloured material, ceramic or composite, bonded to the facial surface of a tooth to correct colour, shape or minor alignment while preserving enamel (Sikri, 2017, via digest).

### Classification
**1. By material**
- *Ceramic veneers:* (a) conventional powder-slurry feldspathic porcelain, hand-layered on refractory dies; (b) heat-pressed ceramic (leucite or lithium disilicate, e.g. IPS Empress); (c) CAD/CAM machined (e.g. CEREC) (Sikri, 2017, via digest). Feldspathic veneers show about 95.5% survival at 10 years, with fracture the main complication (Nejat, 2024). Layered feldspathic veneers can be made thinner than pressed and in multiple colours; they are indicated for tetracycline or discoloured preparations and for conservative cases in young patients with large pulps (Moshaverinia, 2020).
- *Composite veneers:* direct (freehand, transparent crown form, or palatal silicone index technique) or indirect (laboratory) (Torres, 2020, via digest). The silicone-index technique builds the lingual shell first, then layers opaque halo, dentin mamelons and enamel from the labial aspect.
- *Gold veneers:* for palatal wear of upper anterior teeth; bonded with resin cement after sandblasting; the wear itself prepares the tooth (Ricketts and Bartlett, 2011, via digest). "Double veneers" (gold palatal plus ceramic labial) are not generally recommended.

**2. By technique:** direct (chairside) versus indirect (laboratory or CAD/CAM).

**3. By thickness and preparation design** [RECALL]: minimal/no-preparation, window, feather, incisal overlap designs. (No source for preparation designs was available in this session; see the preparation answer in the Conservative Dentistry file.)

### Case-selection principles (Sikri, 2017, via digest)
1. Bond to enamel, not dentin; more dentin exposed favours a full crown.
2. Remove old restorations; minor defects can be built up first.
3. Avoid heavy occlusal load or parafunction.
4. Severe discolouration cannot be masked without thicker prep or opaque ceramic.
5. Veneers close spacing well; avoid in crowding (orthodontic correction first).
6. Lower anterior teeth: crowns are usually better.

### Material thickness (manufacturer data, Nejat, 2024)
Lithium disilicate 0.4-0.7 mm; feldspathic 0.2-0.7 mm; zirconia 0.4 mm.

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 540; about 25 min)**

### Introduction
A veneer is a thin tooth-coloured facing bonded to the facial surface, conserving enamel.

### Classification
| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| Material | Porcelain (feldspathic layered, pressed, CAD/CAM), composite (direct, indirect), gold (palatal) |
| Technique | Direct, indirect |
| Thickness | Ultra-thin, conventional |

### Porcelain veneers
- **Layered feldspathic:** best optics, thinnest, hand built; survival about 95% at 10 years; fracture-prone.
- **Pressed (Empress, e.max Press):** stronger; monochromatic, staining or cut-back.
- **CAD/CAM (CEREC, e.max CAD):** chairside; blocks with colour gradient.

### Composite veneers
- **Direct:** freehand, transparent crown form, palatal silicone index; repairable and inexpensive; wear and staining.
- **Indirect:** laboratory heat/pressure cure; better polymerisation, polish and wear.

### Gold veneers
Palatal tooth-wear cases; often combined with full crowns; sandblast plus adhesive resin.

### Indications
Discolouration (non-bleachable), fractured or worn incisal edges, diastema closure, malformed or peg lateral, enamel hypoplasia, minor misalignment.

### Contraindications
Insufficient enamel, severe crowding, parafunction, poor oral hygiene, large restorations and heavily discoloured teeth where masking is impossible.

### Advantages and disadvantages
| Ceramic | Composite |
|---|---|
| Colour-stable, strong when bonded | Repairable, cheaper, reversible |
| Irreversible, costly, lab | Stains, wears, less durable |

### Conclusion
Veneer choice is decided by enamel availability, discolouration, occlusion and cost; porcelain for durability, composite for conservation and reversibility.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Bond to enamel; dentin-heavy preps need crowns.
- Feldspathic veneers about 95.5% at 10 years.
- Veneers fix spacing, not crowding.
- Gold veneers: palatal wear.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Listing only porcelain.
2. Not stating enamel as the bonding substrate.
