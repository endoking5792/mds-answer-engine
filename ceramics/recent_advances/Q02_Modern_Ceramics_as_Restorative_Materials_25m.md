## STUDY MODE

### "Critically evaluate" frame
Describe what "modern" means (post-2010 materials and processing), then evaluate against criteria (strength, optics, bonding, wear, longevity, evidence base), and give a verdict.

### What counts as modern
Lithium disilicate and its derivatives (fully crystallised blocks, virgilite, nano-crystal), ZLS, translucent and gradient zirconia (3Y → 4Y → 5Y), resin-matrix ceramics (PICN, resin nanoceramic), CAD-on structures and printed ceramics (Nejat, 2025; Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).

### Evaluation
**Mechanical.** Lithium disilicate 400-530 MPa (manufacturer/journal figures; Craig's own figures for the same material, read directly this session, are 262±88 MPa hard-machined/306±29 MPa heat-pressed — cite the source with the figure, since these ranges reflect different test methods/editions, not a contradiction to resolve by picking one); ZLS about 370-420 MPa; 3Y zirconia 1150-1200 MPa (manufacturer; Craig's gives 900-1500 MPa, Phillips ≈900 MPa with K_Ic 8-10.3 — a range, not a flat ~8); 4Y 750-850; 5Y 550; PICN about 150 MPa (Nejat, 2025; Bacchi and Cesar, 2022). In fatigue testing translucent zirconia outperformed ZLS and lithium disilicate; PICN performed worse than lithium disilicate at 1-1.5 mm but similar in endocrowns (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022). Zirconia's high toughness comes from transformation toughening, which cubic-rich grades lose (Nejat, 2025); the associated **tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation volume change is Phillips' 3-5% range, not a flat "~4%"** (van Noort gives no number at all) — attribute this specifically rather than stating it as settled [SRC, VN/PHIL].
**Optical.** Glass-ceramics: excellent; higher translucency lowers masking; monolithic translucent ceramics need opaque luting or opaque composite on discoloured substrates; veneered zirconia and CAD-on mask best (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).
**Wear.** Zirconia (3Y, 5Y) wore less than enamel when polished and abraded antagonists less than glass-ceramic; lithium disilicate, feldspathic and PICN wore more than enamel; roughness is the predictor (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).
**Fit.** Marginal and internal fit similar across milled materials; milled zirconia more precise than printed (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).
**Bonding.** Glass-ceramics bond reliably (HF + silane); zirconia bonding relies on air abrasion and MDP primers and is more technique-sensitive; resin bonding raises flexural strength of thin translucent zirconia (Blatz et al, 2022).
**Clinical evidence.** Monolithic zirconia crowns 91-100% survival; lithium disilicate singles high; FDPs of glass-ceramic poor; ZLS partial coverage 80% at 5 years (molars 69%); lithium silicate 1-year data; printed ceramics laboratory only (Nejat, 2025). Veneered zirconia chips 6-10% at 3-5 years (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
**Evidence gaps.** Multilayer zirconia integrity not evaluated (Zhang and Kelly, 2017); no credible long-term Enamic data (Zhang and Kelly, 2017); LTD of cubic zirconia (Zhang and Kelly, 2017) versus reports of lower susceptibility than 3Y (Blatz et al, 2022): contradictory.

### Verdict
Modern ceramics have extended indications and simplified workflow, but laboratory properties outrun clinical evidence; no material is universally best, and each needs matching to substrate, load and optics.

---

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim — "modern" ceramics solve chipping and fit but not the strength-translucency trade-off.** Monolithic zirconia and CAD-on remove veneer chipping (0% vs 7.61% for metal-ceramic implant crowns; 591,071 vs 16,428 cycles) (Nejat, 2025; Bacchi and Cesar, 2022), while translucent grades lose roughly half of 3Y strength and toughness (Zhang and Kelly, 2017). INTERPRETATION: the trade-off has been moved to a lower strength at higher translucency; case selection still decides. LIMITATION: the survival and fatigue data differ in model and follow-up; manufacturer strength values are not method-comparable. [Likely]

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 900; about 40 min)**

### Introduction
Modern dental ceramics are engineered materials produced by pressing, milling or printing that aim to combine esthetics, strength and bonding.

### Classification
| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| Glass-matrix | Lithium disilicate, ZLS, lithium silicate, virgilite blocks |
| Polycrystalline | 3Y, 4Y, 5Y, graded zirconia |
| Resin-matrix | PICN (Enamic), resin nanoceramic |
| Combinations and processes | CAD-on, printed ceramics |

### Evaluation
| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Strength | LDS 400-530 (manufacturer) / 262-306 (CR, by processing route); ZLS 370-420; 3Y 1150-1200 (manufacturer) / 900-1500 (CR) / ≈900, K_Ic 8-10.3 (PHIL range, not flat ~8); 5Y 550; PICN about 150 MPa |
| Fatigue | Translucent zirconia > ZLS > LDS (laboratory) |
| Optics | Best with glass-ceramics; zirconia masks best |
| Wear | Polished zirconia low antagonist wear; roughness decisive |
| Fit | All clinically acceptable; milled better than printed zirconia |
| Bonding | Glass-ceramic reliable; zirconia technique-sensitive |
| Clinical evidence | Monolithic zirconia 91-100%; LDS singles high; ZLS limited; printed none |

### Merits
Conservative adhesive preparation (veneers 0.4-0.7 mm); monolithic restorations without chipping; digital workflow; improved translucency and gradients; broad indications.

### Demerits and gaps
Translucent zirconia weaker and may degrade at low temperature; ZLS not clearly stronger than LDS; PICN and printed materials have little long-term data; bonding is technique-sensitive; manufacturer data not comparable; cost.

### Selection
Esthetic zone with enamel: glass-ceramic; discoloured or posterior: lithium disilicate or zirconia; bruxism: 3Y zirconia; chairside single visit: CAD/CAM blocks.

### Conclusion
Modern ceramics greatly widen options, but their evidence base for the newest grades is short; clinicians should choose by indication and treat manufacturer claims critically.
