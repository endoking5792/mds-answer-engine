## STUDY MODE

### Functions (Sikri, 2017, via digest)
Preserve pulp vitality; ensure gingival health; prevent passive eruption and mesial drift; maintain occlusal function; support esthetics and psychology during treatment. Because laboratory delay or occlusal acclimatisation may prolong the interim phase, a provisional is never a throwaway.

### Requirements
| Category | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Biological | Non-irritant; seals and insulates; good margin fit, contour, smooth surface; correct contacts |
| Mechanical | Adequate strength; protects the prepared tooth |
| Esthetic | Acceptable colour, contour, translucency |
| Convenience | Short chairside time, easy manipulation and removal, tolerated, inexpensive |

### Techniques and materials
Pre-preparation impression filled with a self-cured bis-acryl composite (e.g. Protemp) dispensed through a double-helix mixing tip; test-mix on a pad; remove the matrix at the rubbery stage before full polymerisation to avoid locking into interdental undercuts; if the provisional stays on the tooth, ease it off promptly with an instrument beneath the contacts; if it comes away in the impression, allow full polymerisation first; trim flash with high-speed diamond or discs; adjust margins and occlusion ideally outside the mouth; confirm esthetics with the patient; cement with a non-eugenol temporary luting cement (Ricketts and Bartlett, 2011, via digest).
Other materials [Likely]: acrylic (PMMA, PEMA), preformed crowns, CAD/CAM PMMA.

### Occlusion check
The thickness of the temporary acts as a diagnostic guide: acrylic showing through indicates inadequate reduction (Sikri, 2017, via digest).

### Why non-eugenol
Eugenol can interfere with polymerisation of resin cements used for definitive bonded ceramics [Likely; standard teaching, not in the sources read].

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 310; about 14 min)**

### Definition
A provisional (temporary/interim) restoration is a short-term restoration placed between preparation and cementation of the definitive restoration.

### Functions
Pulp protection; gingival health; prevents drifting and supraeruption; occlusal function; esthetics; allows assessment of occlusion, shape and shade.

### Requirements
- Biological: non-irritant, sealed, good margins, smooth.
- Mechanical: strength, protects tooth.
- Esthetic: shade and contour.
- Convenience: easy to make, remove and repair.

### Materials and technique
Bis-acryl composite (matrix technique), PMMA/PEMA acrylics, preformed crowns, CAD/CAM PMMA.
Technique: impression before preparation → prepare → fill matrix with bis-acryl → seat → remove at rubbery stage → trim → check margins and occlusion → cement with non-eugenol cement.

### Problems
Marginal leakage, gingival irritation, fracture, debonding, discolouration, occlusal change.

### Conclusion
A good provisional protects the tooth and tissues and is a trial of the final restoration.
