## STUDY MODE

### Functions (Sikri, 2017, via digest)
Preserve pulp vitality; ensure gingival health; prevent passive eruption and mesial drift; maintain occlusal function; support esthetics and psychology during treatment. Because laboratory delay or occlusal acclimatisation may prolong the interim phase, a provisional is never a throwaway.

### Requirements
| Category | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Biological | Non-irritant; seals and insulates; good margin fit, contour, smooth surface; correct contacts |
| Mechanical | Adequate strength; protects the prepared tooth |
| Esthetic | Acceptable colour, contour, translucency |
| Convenience | Short chairside time, easy manipulation and removal, tolerated, inexpensive |

### Techniques and materials
Pre-preparation impression filled with a self-cured bis-acryl composite (e.g. Protemp) dispensed through a double-helix mixing tip; test-mix on a pad; remove the matrix at the rubbery stage before full polymerisation to avoid locking into interdental undercuts; if the provisional stays on the tooth, ease it off promptly with an instrument beneath the contacts; if it comes away in the impression, allow full polymerisation first; trim flash with high-speed diamond or discs; adjust margins and occlusion ideally outside the mouth; confirm esthetics with the patient; cement with a non-eugenol temporary luting cement (Ricketts and Bartlett, 2011, via digest).

**Full materials taxonomy, confirmed directly from SIKRI this session (replacing the previous "[Likely]"-tagged, incomplete list):**
- **Intracoronal provisional materials**: gutta-percha, zinc oxide-eugenol (ZOE) ± EBA (ethoxybenzoic acid, added for improved strength), non-eugenol cements/Cavit, zinc phosphate, polycarboxylate.
- **Extracoronal provisional materials**: polycarbonate (crown forms), cellulose acetate (crown forms, for anterior esthetics during fabrication), aluminium shell crowns, stainless steel crowns, Ni-Cr prefabricated crowns, heat-cure or self-cure acrylic (custom-formed) [SRC, SIKRI].

### Occlusion check
The thickness of the temporary acts as a diagnostic guide: acrylic showing through indicates inadequate reduction (Sikri, 2017, via digest).

### Why non-eugenol — confirmed and specifically extended this session (SIKRI)
Eugenol can interfere with polymerisation of resin cements used for definitive bonded ceramics. **SIKRI gives a more specific, additional caution**: a eugenol-containing cement should **specifically not be used for an acrylic (PMMA/PEMA) temporary restoration**, because eugenol **softens the acrylic resin** itself — a direct material-compatibility problem, not only a concern about contaminating the subsequent bonding step [SRC, SIKRI].

### Veneer provisionals — a distinct sub-case, previously entirely missing (STURD)
A veneer provisional cannot be treated like a crown provisional. It is **too thin to survive being removed, trimmed and recemented** the way a crown provisional routinely is. STURD therefore specifies that a veneer provisional must be **fabricated and placed simultaneously, intraorally, in a single sitting**, and that it is bonded via only a **2 mm spot-etch** of the enamel — not a full-surface etch/bond — so that it can later be removed without damaging the underlying tooth structure before the definitive veneer is placed. This is a genuine gap a generic crown/bridge-provisional-only answer would miss entirely, and is directly relevant whenever this question is asked alongside the veneer topics (Q4-Q8) [SRC, STURD].

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 310; about 14 min)**

### Definition
A provisional (temporary/interim) restoration is a short-term restoration placed between preparation and cementation of the definitive restoration.

### Functions
Pulp protection; gingival health; prevents drifting and supraeruption; occlusal function; esthetics; allows assessment of occlusion, shape and shade.

### Requirements
- Biological: non-irritant, sealed, good margins, smooth.
- Mechanical: strength, protects tooth.
- Esthetic: shade and contour.
- Convenience: easy to make, remove and repair.

### Materials and technique
**Intracoronal**: gutta-percha, ZOE±EBA, non-eugenol/Cavit, zinc phosphate, polycarboxylate. **Extracoronal**: polycarbonate shell, cellulose acetate shell, aluminium shell, stainless steel crown, Ni-Cr prefabricated crown, heat/self-cure acrylic, CAD/CAM PMMA (SIKRI).
Technique: impression before preparation → prepare → fill matrix with bis-acryl → seat → remove at rubbery stage → trim → check margins and occlusion → cement with non-eugenol cement. **Eugenol specifically softens acrylic (PMMA/PEMA) provisionals** — a direct material incompatibility, not only a bonding-contamination concern (SIKRI).

### Veneer provisionals — a distinct sub-case (STURD)
Too thin to survive removal/trimming/recementation like a crown provisional. Must be fabricated AND placed simultaneously, intraorally, in one sitting, bonded via only a 2mm spot-etch (not full-surface bond).

### Problems
Marginal leakage, gingival irritation, fracture, debonding, discolouration, occlusal change.

### Conclusion
A good provisional protects the tooth and tissues and is a trial of the final restoration.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Four requirement categories.
- Remove at rubbery stage.
- Non-eugenol cement — eugenol specifically softens acrylic (PMMA/PEMA) provisionals (SIKRI).
- Full taxonomy: intracoronal (gutta-percha, ZOE±EBA, non-eugenol/Cavit, zinc phosphate, polycarboxylate) vs extracoronal (polycarbonate, cellulose acetate, aluminium shell, stainless steel, Ni-Cr, acrylic) (SIKRI).
- Veneer provisional ≠ crown provisional: fabricated+placed simultaneously, 2mm spot-etch only (STURD).
