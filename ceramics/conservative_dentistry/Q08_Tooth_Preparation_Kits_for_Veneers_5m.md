## STUDY MODE
A veneer preparation kit is a set of rotary instruments that standardises reduction depth and contour. Typical components [RECALL, no source]:
1. **Depth-cutting (orientation) burs** with defined working-head diameter that cut grooves of a known depth, used on the facial surface to guide uniform reduction.
2. **Round-ended tapered or chamfer diamond burs** to reduce the labial surface between the grooves and form the cervical finish line.
3. **Interproximal (thin, flame or needle) diamond** to extend into proximal areas without damaging adjacent teeth.
4. **Incisal reduction instruments** (wheel or round-end) for the chosen incisal design.
5. **Finishing burs** (fine or ultrafine diamond, multi-bladed carbide) to smooth margins and round angles.
6. Often a **silicone index** from the wax-up to verify reduction.

The thickness targets that these instruments aim at are the material-specific minimums (Nejat, 2025): for example 0.2-0.3 mm cervical to 0.5-0.7 mm incisal for feldspathic, 0.4-0.7 mm for lithium disilicate.

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 190; about 8 min)**

### Definition
A kit of burs designed to give reproducible, minimal and uniform reduction for veneers.

### Components
| Instrument | Use |
|---|---|
| Depth-cutting burs | Mark depth grooves on labial surface |
| Tapered/round-end diamond | Reduce between grooves; cervical chamfer |
| Interproximal diamond | Proximal extension without damaging adjacent tooth |
| Incisal reduction wheel | Window/feather/overlap |
| Finishing burs | Smooth margins, round angles |
| Silicone index | Check reduction against wax-up |

### Advantages
Standardised depth; conservative; fast; reproducible; lower risk of over-reduction.

### Limitations
Cost; must be matched to material thickness; does not replace judgment about enamel.

### Conclusion
Veneer kits translate planned thickness into controlled reduction and protect enamel.
