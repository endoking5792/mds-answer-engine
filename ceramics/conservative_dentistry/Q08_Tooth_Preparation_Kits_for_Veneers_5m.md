## STUDY MODE
A veneer preparation kit is a set of rotary instruments that standardises reduction depth and contour. **Two named, textbook-sourced kits, now the centrepiece of this answer:**

**SIKRI's LVS (Laminate Veneer System)** — a depth-cutting bur set specifically named and dimensioned:
1. **LVS-1**: cuts a **0.3 mm** depth-cutting groove, used cervically.
2. **LVS-2**: cuts a **0.5 mm** depth-cutting groove, used incisally/middle-third.
3. **LVS-3 / LVS-4**: form the **proximal chamfer**.
These are used to cut orientation grooves first, then the intervening enamel is reduced down to the groove floor, giving a uniform, operator-independent depth [SRC, SIKRI].

**STURD's named bur sequence** (an independent, corroborating protocol):
1. A **tapered rounded-end diamond, tip diameter Ø1-1.2 mm** — doubles as its own depth gauge for the facial reduction, cut in 3 zones verified by diamond-striation merging.
2. A **flame-shaped fine diamond** — dresses and refines the gingival finish line.
3. A **30-fluted carbide finishing bur, used dry** (Midwest #9803 / Brasseler #7801) — final smoothing of margins and surface.
4. The **Dialite Porcelain Polishing Kit** with porcelain polishing paste — for any chairside adjustment, e.g. after cementation [SRC, STURD].

A **silicone index** from the diagnostic wax-up is used (both sources) to verify incisal reduction (1 mm minimum, 1.5 mm preferred — STURD) against the planned tooth form.

The thickness targets that these instruments aim at are the material-specific minimums (Nejat, 2025): for example 0.2-0.3 mm cervical to 0.5-0.7 mm incisal for feldspathic, 0.4-0.7 mm for lithium disilicate.

### Definition
A kit of burs designed to give reproducible, minimal and uniform reduction for veneers.

### Components — two named kits (SIKRI; STURD)
| Kit | Instrument | Spec / use |
|---|---|---|
| SIKRI's LVS | LVS-1 | 0.3 mm depth groove, cervical |
| SIKRI's LVS | LVS-2 | 0.5 mm depth groove, incisal/middle |
| SIKRI's LVS | LVS-3/LVS-4 | Proximal chamfer |
| STURD | Tapered rounded-end diamond, Ø1-1.2 mm | Facial reduction + own depth gauge, 3 zones |
| STURD | Flame-shaped fine diamond | Gingival finish-line dressing |
| STURD | 30-fluted carbide bur, dry (Midwest #9803/Brasseler #7801) | Final smoothing |
| STURD | Dialite Porcelain Polishing Kit | Chairside polish/adjustment |
| Both | Silicone index (from wax-up) | Check reduction against planned form |

### Advantages
Standardised depth; conservative; fast; reproducible; lower risk of over-reduction.

### Limitations
Cost; must be matched to material thickness; does not replace judgment about enamel.

### Conclusion
Veneer kits translate planned thickness into controlled reduction and protect enamel.
