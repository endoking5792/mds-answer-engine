## STUDY MODE

### Definition
Aluminous porcelain is a porcelain whose core contains alumina (Al2O3) crystals dispersed in a glass matrix to increase strength; used as a core for porcelain jacket crowns, veneered with conventional porcelain.

### History (Zhang and Kelly, 2017; corrected and quantified against primary textbooks this session)
- **McLean and Hughes, exact citation confirmed this session: 1965** (*Br Dent J* 117:251) — one source consulted elsewhere had garbled this to "1967" or "1765"; **1965 is the correct, primary-source-confirmed year**, and the correct citation should be used rather than a rounded or garbled date [SRC, VN/BON/PHIL/MICH]. They reinforced porcelain with alumina; the first strengthened core was feldspathic glass with **40-50 wt% alumina (all sources agree on this range)** — note this is weight%, and the material is opaque enough that veneering with conventional porcelain is required.
- **Flexural strength is a genuine cross-source range, not one figure — state all of them, and do NOT use "~70 MPa" (unsupported by any primary source read this session):** van Noort/Bonsor give **~120-150 MPa**; Phillips gives **~131 MPa**; Michigan/Craig's give **~139-145 MPa**. Use the 120-150 MPa range as the headline figure, with the per-source values available if a specific number is demanded [SRC, VN/BON/PHIL/MICH].
- **5-year clinical fracture data, confirmed directly this session (McLean 1979, cited by both Phillips and McCabe): 2% fracture rate for anterior aluminous-porcelain crowns versus 15% for molar crowns** — a strong, numerically precise basis for the standard teaching that aluminous porcelain is unsuitable for posterior teeth; cite these exact figures rather than a vague "higher failure posteriorly" statement [SRC, PHIL/MCC].
- **Platinum-foil/tin-oxide bonding technique, previously missing, now added (Phillips):** during fabrication, the platinum foil matrix (on which the porcelain core is built) is **electroplated with tin and then oxidised in a furnace**, forming a **continuous SnO2 film** on the foil surface. This film **reduces subsurface porosity and microcracks on the fit surface** of the finished aluminous-porcelain crown, improving its strength at the critical tooth-facing surface — a specific, examinable fabrication detail distinct from the alumina-reinforcement mechanism itself [SRC, PHIL].
- Cerestore (Coors, 1983): core with about 60 wt% Al2O3, magnesia, barium aluminosilicate glass, silicone and kaolin for transfer moulding at 160 °C; withdrawn after universal problems with fractured restorations.
- Hi-Ceram (Vita): about 60 vol% alumina in glass; failed for posterior use; replaced by In-Ceram in 1990.
- In-Ceram: glass-infiltrated alumina (Q13); Procera: fully dense, 99.9 vol% alumina, 572 MPa (1993).

### Properties
Higher strength than feldspathic porcelain (120-150 MPa range across sources, see above); opaque; needs a porcelain veneer; low translucency; bonding cannot rely on HF (no glass) (Moshaverinia, 2020). Alumina is prone to bulk fracture because of its high elasticity (Moshaverinia, 2020); the alumina crystals deflect cracks, but the glass phase remains weak.

### Uses
Anterior jacket crowns; cores for veneering; replaced by glass-infiltrated and zirconia cores for strength.

### Advantages and disadvantages
Advantages: esthetic compared with metal; metal-free; strengthened over feldspathic porcelain. Disadvantages: opaque core; moderate strength; posterior failures; technique-sensitive.

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 280; about 13 min)**

### Definition
Porcelain with alumina crystals dispersed in a glass matrix, used as a strengthened core.

### Composition
Feldspathic glass plus **40-50 wt% alumina** (all sources agree on this range; McLean & Hughes' original, *Br Dent J* 117:251, **1965** — confirmed citation, not "1967"/"1765").

### Development
| System | Alumina | Flexural strength | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| McLean & Hughes (1965) | 40-50 wt% | **120-150 MPa range**: VN/BON ~120-150; Phillips ~131; Michigan/Craig's ~139-145 — NOT "~70 MPa" | Opaque; platinum-foil bonded via tin-oxide film (see below) |
| Cerestore (1983) | About 60 wt% | — | Withdrawn |
| Hi-Ceram | About 60 vol% | — | Failed posteriorly |
| In-Ceram | ~85->90%+ alumina (conflicting by source — see Q13) | ~400-620 MPa (conflicting by source) | See Q13 for full detail |
| Procera | 99.9% | 572 MPa | — |

### Fabrication detail: platinum-foil/tin-oxide technique (Phillips)
The platinum foil on which the core is built is electroplated with tin, then oxidised in a furnace to form a continuous SnO2 film; this reduces subsurface porosity and microcracks on the restoration's fit surface.

### Clinical data (McLean 1979, cited by Phillips and McCabe)
5-year fracture rate: **2% anterior vs 15% molar crowns** — the numeric basis for "unsuitable for posterior teeth."

### Properties
Stronger than feldspathic porcelain (120-150 MPa); opaque; needs a veneer; not etchable; brittle.

### Uses
Anterior crowns, cores under veneering porcelain.

### Disadvantages
Opacity, limited strength, fractures (15% at 5 years in molars vs 2% anterior), laboratory sensitivity.

### Conclusion
Aluminous porcelain was the first step in core-veneer all-ceramic systems; it has been superseded by infiltrated alumina and zirconia.
