## STUDY MODE

### Dicor
First dental glass-ceramic: a castable glass with fluormica crystals as sheets or plates in a "house of cards" microstructure that interlocks; flexural strength about 229 MPa for the machinable form (Dicor MGC, fluormica about 70 vol%, toughness 1.2 MPa·m^0.5) (Zhang and Kelly, 2017). It was cast by the lost-wax technique and ceramed; used for anterior crowns (Ricketts and Bartlett, 2011, via digest). Molar crowns failed at 4-6% per year; it was withdrawn because of poor mechanical performance (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).

### In-Ceram (Vita)
A glass-infiltrated ceramic core made by slip-casting. An alumina slurry is condensed on a porous refractory die that shrinks on firing, so the coping separates easily. It is lightly sintered (1120 °C for 10 hours in Guess et al; 4 hours at 1100 °C in the digest of Craig, 2002: sources differ) to a porous skeleton, then infiltrated with low-viscosity lanthanum glass (1150 °C for 4 hours in the digest) (Guess et al, 2011; Craig, 2002, via digest). The result is an alumina (about 70 vol%) and glass interpenetrating network, flexural strength about 450-550 MPa, toughness 3.6 MPa·m^0.5 (Zhang and Kelly, 2017). The core is veneered with feldspathic porcelain of matched expansion.

Variants:
| Variant | Composition | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| In-Ceram Alumina | Almost pure alumina | Strongest of the glass-infiltrated alumina-based; first all-ceramic system for single units and 3-unit anterior bridges (Guess et al, 2011) |
| In-Ceram Spinell | Alumina plus magnesia (MgAl2O4) | More translucent, lower strength (350 MPa, toughness 2.5) |
| In-Ceram Zirconia | Zirconia-toughened alumina (about 67 vol% crystalline) | Higher strength (700 MPa, toughness 3.5), more opaque |

Indications: copings for anterior and posterior crowns; In-Ceram alumina for 3-unit anterior bridges; In-Ceram zirconia for posterior crowns (Ricketts and Bartlett, 2011, via digest).
Limitations: opacity; laborious slip-casting; brittle, both phases brittle so only crack deflection; glass infiltration cannot be etched effectively, so resin bonding is limited [Likely]; largely replaced by zirconia (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 370; about 17 min)**

### Dicor
- **Type:** castable glass-ceramic (Corning/Dentsply), the first dental glass-ceramic.
- **Composition:** fluormica crystals in glass matrix; plates interlock.
- **Method:** lost-wax; casting; ceramming.
- **Strength:** about 229 MPa.
- **Use:** anterior crowns.
- **Status:** withdrawn; 4-6% annual failure of molar crowns.

### In-Ceram
- **Type:** glass-infiltrated, slip-cast all-ceramic core (Vita, 1990).
- **Method:** alumina slurry condensed on a porous die → sintering → infiltration with lanthanum glass → veneering porcelain.
- **Structure:** interpenetrating alumina (about 70 vol%) and glass; strength about 450-550 MPa.

| Variant | Property | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Alumina | Strong | Anterior crown, 3-unit anterior bridge |
| Spinell | Translucent, weaker | Anterior single crown |
| Zirconia | Strongest, opaque | Posterior crown |

### Advantages
Good strength, accuracy, no metal, low shrinkage.

### Disadvantages
Opaque; slip-casting is technique-sensitive and slow; brittle; limited bonding.

### Comparison
| Feature | Dicor | In-Ceram |
|---|---|---|
| Process | Casting and ceramming | Slip-casting and infiltration |
| Structure | Glass-ceramic (fluormica) | Interpenetrating network |
| Strength | About 229 MPa | About 450-550 MPa |
| Status | Withdrawn | Largely replaced by zirconia |

### Conclusion
Dicor introduced glass-ceramics but failed clinically; In-Ceram introduced high-strength infiltrated cores and paved the way to zirconia.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Dicor: fluormica, cast, ceramed, withdrawn.
- In-Ceram: alumina scaffold plus lanthanum glass; three variants.
- Spinell more translucent; zirconia stronger.
