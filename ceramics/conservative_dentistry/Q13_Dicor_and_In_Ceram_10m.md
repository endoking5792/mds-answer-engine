## STUDY MODE

### Dicor
First dental glass-ceramic: a castable glass with fluormica crystals as sheets or plates in a "house of cards" microstructure that interlocks; flexural strength about 229 MPa for the machinable form (Dicor MGC, fluormica about 70 vol%, toughness 1.2 MPa·m^0.5) (Zhang and Kelly, 2017). It was cast by the lost-wax technique and ceramed; used for anterior crowns (Ricketts and Bartlett, 2011, via digest). Molar crowns failed at 4-6% per year; it was withdrawn because of poor mechanical performance (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).

**Composition and chemistry, confirmed directly from BON/PHIL this session**: Dicor is specifically a **fluormica glass-ceramic**, composition SiO2·K2O·MgO·MgF2·Al2O3·ZrO2, **centrifugally cast at about 1380°C**, then ceramed (controlled heat-treated) to grow the interlocking tetrasilicic-fluormica crystals. Note a genuinely distinct historical point that should not be conflated with Dicor itself: **MacCulloch first used a (different) castable ceramic system in 1968 for denture teeth** — a different material/chemistry from Dicor, which came later; keep the two separate rather than treating MacCulloch's 1968 work as "Dicor's origin" [SRC, BON/PHIL].

**Status and reason for discontinuation, now sourced rather than assumed**: Dicor is **discontinued**. Bonsor gives the specific reason, not just "poor mechanical performance" in general: **inadequate strength combined with excessive translucency**, which in molar crowns specifically caused a cosmetically poor **grey appearance at the incisal tips** as the underlying tooth/metal showed through — an esthetic failure mode compounding the mechanical one [SRC, BON].

**14-year survival data (Phillips)** — a specific, citable, statistically-supported figure that should replace a vague "high failure rate" claim: **etched/resin-bonded Dicor crowns survived at 76% versus 50% for non-etched/conventionally-cemented crowns at 14 years (P<0.001)**, i.e. **non-etched cementation carried about 2.2× the risk of failure** compared with the etched, resin-bonded technique. This is a strong, quotable illustration that the bonding protocol — not just the material — determined clinical survival [SRC, PHIL].

### In-Ceram (Vita)
A glass-infiltrated ceramic core made by slip-casting. An alumina slurry is condensed on a porous refractory die that shrinks on firing, so the coping separates easily. It is lightly sintered (1120 °C for 10 hours in Guess et al; 4 hours at 1100 °C in the digest of Craig, 2002: sources differ) to a porous skeleton, then infiltrated with low-viscosity lanthanum glass (1150 °C for 4 hours in the digest) (Guess et al, 2011; Craig, 2002, via digest). The core is veneered with feldspathic porcelain of matched expansion.

**Composition/strength is genuinely NOT a single settled figure across primary sources — present as a conflicting range with attribution, not one number (confirmed this session, VN/MICH/PHIL/CR):**
- **van Noort**: ~85% alumina post-infiltration, ~400-500 MPa for full crowns.
- **Michigan** (slip-cast description): alumina **>90%**, particle size 0.5-3.5 µm, fired 4h/1100°C, infiltrated with **lanthanum-containing** glass at 1150°C/4h, flexural strength **≈450 MPa**.
- **Phillips**: gives the pre-infiltration core composition specifically for **In-Ceram ZIRCONIA** (not plain In-Ceram Alumina) as **~62% alumina/20% zirconia/18% glass by weight**, strength **~620 MPa**.
- **Craig's** current edition omits slip-cast/In-Ceram detail entirely (pushed to an online supplement).
- **The commonly-quoted "70 vol% alumina, ~550 MPa" figure (Zhang and Kelly, 2017, Table 1, used below for the general comparison) is NOT independently supported by any of the primary textbooks consulted this session** — flag it as a secondary/review-article figure, not a settled textbook value, when citing a number in an exam answer [SRC, VN/MICH/PHIL/CR].

Variants (a third variant, In-Ceram Spinell, exists alongside Alumina and Zirconia — all three should be named):
| Variant | Composition | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| In-Ceram Alumina | Almost pure alumina (~85->90% per VN/MICH, above) | Strongest of the glass-infiltrated alumina-based; first all-ceramic system for single units and 3-unit anterior bridges (Guess et al, 2011) |
| In-Ceram Spinell | Alumina plus magnesia (MgAl2O4) | **More translucent, genuinely weaker** (~350 MPa, toughness 2.5) — anterior crowns/inlays only, not posterior or bridges |
| In-Ceram Zirconia | Zirconia-toughened alumina; Phillips gives ~62% alumina/20% zirconia/18% glass pre-infiltration | Higher strength (~620-700 MPa depending on source), more opaque |

Indications: copings for anterior and posterior crowns; In-Ceram alumina for 3-unit anterior bridges; In-Ceram zirconia for posterior crowns (Ricketts and Bartlett, 2011, via digest).
Limitations: opacity; laborious slip-casting; brittle, both phases brittle so only crack deflection; glass infiltration cannot be etched effectively, so resin bonding is limited [Likely]; largely replaced by zirconia (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 370; about 17 min)**

### Dicor
- **Type:** castable glass-ceramic (Corning/Dentsply), the first dental glass-ceramic.
- **Composition:** fluormica glass-ceramic, SiO2·K2O·MgO·MgF2·Al2O3·ZrO2; fluormica crystals in glass matrix; plates interlock ("house of cards").
- **Method:** centrifugal casting at ~1380°C; ceramming (controlled crystal growth).
- **History note:** MacCulloch used a different castable system in 1968 for denture teeth — not the same material as Dicor; keep distinct.
- **Strength:** about 229 MPa (machinable Dicor MGC form).
- **Use:** anterior crowns.
- **Status:** discontinued — Bonsor's stated reason: inadequate strength + excessive translucency, causing grey incisal tips in molar crowns (esthetic + mechanical failure combined), not strength alone.
- **14-year survival (Phillips):** etched/resin-bonded 76% vs non-etched/conventionally-cemented 50% (P<0.001) — non-etched cementation ~2.2× the failure risk. Molar crowns also failed at 4-6%/year (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).

### In-Ceram
- **Type:** glass-infiltrated, slip-cast all-ceramic core (Vita, 1990).
- **Method:** alumina slurry condensed on a porous die → sintering → infiltration with lanthanum glass → veneering porcelain.
- **Structure/strength — genuinely conflicting across sources, present with attribution:** van Noort ~85% alumina, 400-500 MPa; Michigan >90% alumina, ≈450 MPa; Phillips (In-Ceram Zirconia variant specifically) ~62% alumina/20% zirconia/18% glass, ~620 MPa. The frequently-seen "70 vol%/550 MPa" figure is a secondary (Zhang and Kelly) figure, not independently textbook-confirmed.

| Variant | Property | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Alumina | Strong (~85->90% alumina per VN/MICH) | Anterior crown, 3-unit anterior bridge |
| Spinell | Translucent, genuinely weaker (~350 MPa) | Anterior single crown/inlay only |
| Zirconia | Strongest (~620-700 MPa), opaque | Posterior crown |

### Advantages
Good strength, accuracy, no metal, low shrinkage.

### Disadvantages
Opaque; slip-casting is technique-sensitive and slow; brittle; limited bonding.

### Comparison
| Feature | Dicor | In-Ceram |
|---|---|---|
| Process | Centrifugal casting (~1380°C) and ceramming | Slip-casting and infiltration |
| Structure | Glass-ceramic (fluormica) | Interpenetrating network |
| Strength | About 229 MPa | About 400-620 MPa depending on source/variant (conflicting, see above) |
| Status | Discontinued (inadequate strength + excessive translucency/grey incisal tips — Bonsor) | Largely replaced by zirconia |

### Conclusion
Dicor introduced glass-ceramics but failed clinically; In-Ceram introduced high-strength infiltrated cores and paved the way to zirconia.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Dicor: fluormica glass-ceramic (SiO2·K2O·MgO·MgF2·Al2O3·ZrO2), centrifugally cast ~1380°C, ceramed, discontinued (Bonsor: inadequate strength + excessive translucency/grey incisal tips).
- Dicor 14-year survival: etched/resin-bonded 76% vs non-etched 50% (P<0.001), 2.2× failure risk (Phillips).
- MacCulloch 1968 used a different castable system for denture teeth — not Dicor itself.
- In-Ceram: alumina scaffold plus lanthanum glass; three variants (Alumina, Spinell, Zirconia).
- In-Ceram composition/strength conflicts by source: van Noort ~85%/400-500MPa; Michigan >90%/≈450MPa; Phillips (Zirconia variant) ~62% alumina/20% zirconia/18% glass/~620MPa — "70 vol%/550 MPa" is a secondary, not textbook-confirmed, figure.
- Spinell more translucent and genuinely weaker (~350 MPa); zirconia variant stronger.
