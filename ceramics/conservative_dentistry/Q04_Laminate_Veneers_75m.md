## STUDY MODE

### Definition and concept
A veneer or laminate is a thin layer of ceramic or composite bonded to the facial surface to change colour, form or position while conserving tooth structure (Sikri, 2017, via digest). Success rests on **bonding to enamel**: dentin bonding is less reliable and the rigid ceramic on flexible dentin transfers stress to the interface, so the more dentin exposed, the stronger the case for a crown (Sikri, 2017, via digest).

### Types
Ceramic: layered feldspathic, pressed (leucite or lithium disilicate), CAD/CAM; composite: direct or indirect; gold: palatal (Sikri, 2017; Ricketts and Bartlett, 2011, via digest). Feldspathic veneers: 94% at 12 years (Zhang and Kelly, 2017); 95.5% at 10 years, fracture the main complication (Nejat, 2025; Blatz et al, 2022); Empress veneers 94.4% at 12 years (Guess et al, 2011).

### Indications
Discoloured teeth not responding to bleaching (tetracycline, fluorosis, non-vital), fractured or worn incisal edges, diastema closure, malformed teeth (peg lateral), enamel hypoplasia, minor malalignment, replacement of failing composite restorations [Likely].

### Contraindications (SIKRI; STURD)
Insufficient enamel; heavy occlusal load or parafunction (full crown or high-strength opaque ceramic); deep intense discolouration that cannot be masked without deep preparation; crowding (veneers close spacing, not crowding); mandibular anterior teeth (small, thin enamel, difficult access, crowns usually better); poor oral hygiene.

**Confirmed and quantified directly from primary textbooks this session**: STURD independently states mandibular anterior veneers are **rarely indicated** — the facioincisal dimension is thin, leaving little room for reduction plus ceramic thickness, occlusal attrition exposes the margin, and porcelain wears opposing (usually maxillary) enamel. For masking, STURD gives a specific numeric threshold: **5-15% opaque porcelain is sufficient to mask tetracycline staining; beyond ~15% opaque content the veneer looks flat and esthetic vitality is lost** — at that point a full crown with a more opaque core is the better compromise, not a veneer pushed past its masking limit [SRC, STURD].

### Planning (Goldstein et al, 2018, via digest)
Smile analysis: facial rules (thirds, fifths), incisal plane following the lower lip curve, phonetics (f/v), anterior guidance, embrasures with connector areas of 50%, 40% and 30%, golden proportion 62% or recurring esthetic dental (RED) proportion 62-80% (70% for normal length), central incisor width-to-length 75-78%, zenith positions (central 1 mm distal, lateral 0.4 mm distal, canine on axis). Diagnostic wax-up and mock-up (silicone index) approve form before reduction [Likely, standard practice, source not read].

### Shade and masking
Shade before preparation; stump colour influences thin veneers; more opaque ceramic or opaque luting agent for discoloured substrates (Moshaverinia, 2020; Bacchi and Cesar, 2022). Cement shade matters for thin veneers (Manso et al, 2011: light-cure cements give colour stability).

### Preparation
Conservative, enamel-confined. Manufacturer minimum thicknesses (Nejat, 2025): feldspathic 0.2-0.3 mm cervical, 0.5 mm middle, 0.5-0.7 mm incisal; leucite 0.6-0.7 mm; lithium disilicate 0.4-0.6 mm cervical, 0.5-0.6 mm middle, 0.5-0.7 mm incisal; ZLS 0.4-0.6 mm; 3Y zirconia 0.4 mm. Margin supragingival where possible, finish lines in enamel, rounded internal angles, no undercuts [Likely].

**Two independently-sourced full preparation protocols, now filling the previous gap:**

**SIKRI's LVS (Laminate Veneer System) kit and 6-step sequence.** A named depth-cutting bur kit: **LVS-1** (0.3 mm depth, for cervical orientation grooves), **LVS-2** (0.5 mm depth, for incisal/middle-third grooves), **LVS-3/LVS-4** (proximal chamfer-forming burs). The 6 steps: (1) **labial reduction** — cut orientation grooves with the LVS burs, then reduce between them; (2) **interproximal reduction/extension** — extend the finish line interproximally, either breaking or retaining the contact depending on diastema-closure need; (3) **gingival/sulcular extension** — margin placed at or just within the gingival crest; (4) **finish-line establishment** — a smooth chamfer; (5) **incisal reduction**, with three design options: chamfer/incisal-overlap (ceramic wraps the incisal edge and a few mm of palatal — strongest, best for lengthening), butt-joint (ceramic meets enamel at the edge — simplest, for minimal change), or lingual-wrap/window (edge left on natural tooth — most conservative, least retentive); (6) **lingual reduction** only if the incisal design requires palatal coverage. SIKRI notes **temporisation is usually not required** for a purely enamel-confined laminate preparation [SRC, SIKRI].

**STURD's independently-sourced protocol** (converges with and extends SIKRI's): facial reduction **0.5-0.75 mm at mid-facial, tapering to 0.3-0.5 mm gingivally**, cut in **three zones** (gingival, middle, incisal) and verified by **diamond-striation merging** (when the depth-cutting bur's rotational marks from adjacent zones just touch, the correct depth has been reached); incisal reduction **1 mm minimum, 1.5 mm preferred**, gauged with a **silicone index** cut from the diagnostic wax-up. Named bur sequence: a **tapered rounded-end diamond, tip diameter Ø1-1.2 mm**, used both to cut depth grooves and as its own depth gauge; a **flame-shaped fine diamond** for dressing the gingival finish line; a **30-fluted carbide finishing bur, used dry** (Midwest #9803 / Brasseler #7801) for final smoothing; and finally the **Dialite Porcelain Polishing Kit** with porcelain polishing paste for any chairside adjustment after cementation [SRC, STURD].

### Impression, provisional, laboratory
Elastomeric or digital impression; laboratory fabrication on refractory dies, pressing or milling (Sikri, 2017, via digest).

**Veneer provisionals are a distinct sub-case, not a small crown provisional (STURD):** a veneer provisional is too thin to survive being removed, trimmed and recemented the way a crown provisional can. It must therefore be **fabricated and placed simultaneously, intraoral, in a single sitting**, and it is bonded with only a **2 mm spot-etch** (not full-surface etching/bonding) so that it can later be removed without damaging the underlying enamel. For an enamel-confined prep, full temporisation is often unnecessary at all (SIKRI, above); where a provisional is used, this spot-etch, single-sitting technique applies [SRC, STURD].

**Componeer technique (SIKRI)** — a prefabricated composite veneer, offered as a conservative/same-visit alternative to laboratory ceramic: placed with a **split rubber-dam technique**; shell thickness tapers **0.3 mm cervically to 0.7 mm incisally**; sized against a contour guide; **37% phosphoric acid, 15 s** enamel etch; a **two-layer, 5th-generation bonding protocol**; seated and finished with named placer and flash-removal instruments supplied with the kit [SRC, SIKRI].

### Try-in and bonding (Blatz et al, 2022; Manso et al, 2011)
1. Try-in with water-soluble try-in paste for shade and fit (esthetics, fit, occlusion).
2. Clean ultrasonically in alcohol for 5 min.
3. Etch ceramic: feldspathic 9.8% HF 2 min; leucite 9.8% HF 1 min; lithium silicate/disilicate 4.6% HF 20 s; rinse and dry.
4. Silane 20 s, dry. Manso et al advise silanating before try-in, since the HF-treated surface is hydrophilic and prone to contamination, and cleaning ultrasonically after try-in. **Role of silane clarified (STURD):** silane's main clinically-demonstrated benefit for veneer cementation is **reducing marginal leakage/microleakage**, rather than being the primary source of retention — primary retention comes from the micromechanical HF-etch pattern plus resin-composite interlock; silane supplements rather than substitutes for that mechanical bond [SRC, STURD].
5. Isolation; tooth: 35% phosphoric acid (total-etch gives better seal under veneers than self-etch), adhesive.
6. **Light-cure resin luting agent, specifically recommended over dual-cure for veneers** (STURD, corroborating Manso/Blatz above) for extended working time, colour stability and easy excess removal; remove excess before light-curing for 20 s each side. Self-adhesive cements show lower enamel bond strength and shade range, limiting use for veneers (Manso et al, 2011).
7. Finish margins; check occlusion and excursions; polish. Oxygen-blocking before final cure (Alani and Patel, 2025, via digest).

### Complications and maintenance
Fracture (main complication), debonding (rare when bonded to enamel), marginal discolouration, post-operative sensitivity (dentin exposure), colour mismatch, gingival inflammation from overhangs or retained cement. Maintenance: recall, occlusal splint for parafunction, polish only with ceramic-safe pastes.

### Repair
Two distinct, substrate-specific protocols, both matching between SIKRI and STURD:
- **Direct composite veneer repair:** roughen/micro-etch the composite surface → etch → bond → add composite in increments → finish/polish.
- **Porcelain veneer repair:** only **~10% buffered HF gel applied under rubber dam** (never full-strength intraoral HF) **OR** mechanical sandblast/micro-abrasion as an alternative to HF, followed by silane → adhesive → composite. Large fractures are not repaired — they need full veneer replacement [SRC, SIKRI/STURD]. See Q7 for the complete repair protocol including the zirconia/metal-substrate cases.

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim — bonded to enamel, ceramic veneers are among the most predictable indirect restorations.** Survival of 94-95.5% at 10-12 years for feldspathic and pressed leucite veneers (Zhang and Kelly, 2017; Guess et al, 2011; Nejat, 2025; Blatz et al, 2022) and the principle that enamel bonding drives retention (Sikri, 2017, via digest) support this. INTERPRETATION: failures cluster in dentin-exposed, parafunctional and large-restoration cases. LIMITATION: the cited series are retrospective, from experienced operators, with various definitions of success; no randomised comparison of preparation designs was available in the sources read. ## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 2,000; about 90 min)**

### Definition
A laminate veneer is a thin layer of tooth-coloured ceramic or composite bonded to the labial surface to alter colour, shape or alignment with minimum tooth removal.

### Classification
| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| Material | Porcelain (layered feldspathic, pressed, CAD/CAM), composite (direct, indirect), gold |
| Technique | Direct, indirect |
| Thickness | Ultra-thin, conventional |

### Indications
- Non-bleachable discolouration (tetracycline, fluorosis, non-vital teeth).
- Fractured, worn or malformed teeth (peg lateral); enamel defects.
- Diastema and small spaces.
- Minor malalignment and shape correction; smile design.

### Contraindications
- Insufficient enamel or large existing restorations.
- Bruxism or heavy occlusal load.
- Severe discolouration needing deep reduction (beyond ~15% opaque porcelain, esthetic vitality is lost — STURD; consider a crown instead).
- Crowding (orthodontic correction first).
- Mandibular anteriors — STURD: rarely indicated (thin facioincisal dimension, attrition exposure, wears opposing enamel).
- Poor hygiene, active caries or periodontal disease.

### Principle
Bond to enamel; preserve enamel; the more dentin exposed, the more a crown is indicated.

### Case planning
Smile analysis (lip line, incisal plane, proportions: golden 62% / RED 70%, embrasures 50-40-30%, zeniths), diagnostic wax-up and mock-up, shade selection before preparation, occlusal analysis, patient expectations.

### Steps in fabrication (maxillary anteriors)
1. Diagnosis, wax-up, mock-up, shade.
2. Preparation (two converging textbook protocols):
   - **SIKRI's LVS kit, 6 steps:** depth-cutting orientation grooves with LVS-1 (0.3 mm, cervical) and LVS-2 (0.5 mm, incisal/middle) burs → labial reduction between grooves → interproximal reduction/extension → gingival/sulcular extension → finish-line establishment → incisal reduction (chamfer/overlap, butt-joint, or lingual-wrap design) → lingual reduction if required. Temporisation usually not needed for an enamel-confined prep.
   - **STURD's protocol:** facial reduction 0.5-0.75 mm mid-facial tapering to 0.3-0.5 mm gingival, cut in 3 zones verified by diamond-striation merging; incisal reduction 1 mm minimum/1.5 mm preferred, gauged with a silicone index; bur sequence — tapered rounded-end diamond (Ø1-1.2 mm) → flame-shaped fine diamond (gingival finish line) → 30-fluted carbide finishing bur used dry (Midwest #9803/Brasseler #7801) → Dialite Porcelain Polishing Kit.
3. Impression (elastomeric or digital). Provisional only if needed: veneer provisionals are fabricated AND placed simultaneously intraorally in one sitting, bonded via only a 2 mm spot-etch (STURD) — not removed/trimmed/recemented like a crown provisional.
4. Laboratory: refractory die layering, pressing or milling. (Componeer alternative: prefabricated composite shell, split rubber-dam placement, 0.3→0.7 mm taper, 37% phosphoric acid 15 s, two-layer 5th-gen bonding — SIKRI.)
5. Try-in with try-in paste; evaluate fit, shade, margins.
6. Ceramic surface treatment: HF (9.8% 2 min feldspathic; 1 min leucite; 4.6% 20 s lithium silicate), ultrasonic clean, silane 20 s — silane's main role here is reducing marginal leakage, not primary retention (STURD); mechanical HF-etch micromechanical interlock is primary.
7. Tooth: isolation, 35% phosphoric acid, bonding agent.
8. Light-cure luting resin (STURD: specifically preferred over dual-cure for veneers); seat; remove excess; light-cure 20 s each surface.
9. Finish, polish, occlusal check.
10. Review and maintenance.

### Cement selection
Light-cure resin for colour stability and working time, and specifically recommended over dual-cure for veneers (STURD); dual-cure only if thick or deeper; self-adhesive cements not preferred (lower enamel bond, limited shades).

### Advantages
Conservative; excellent and stable esthetics; gingival tolerance; durable when bonded to enamel (about 94-95% at 10-12 years).

### Disadvantages
Irreversible; technique-sensitive; costly; brittle; difficult shade correction; repair challenging; masking limited for dark teeth.

### Repair (two substrate-specific protocols)
Direct composite veneer: roughen/micro-etch → etch → bond → composite (SIKRI/STURD). Porcelain veneer: ~10% buffered HF gel under rubber dam (never full-strength intraoral HF) OR sandblast/micro-abrasion, then silane → adhesive → composite; large fractures need full replacement, not repair (SIKRI/STURD).

### Complications
Fracture, debonding, marginal staining, sensitivity, gingival inflammation, colour mismatch, chipping from parafunction.

### Recent advances
Ultra-thin lithium disilicate and feldspathic CAD/CAM veneers (0.4-0.6 mm), gradient and multi-translucency blocks, digital smile design and wax-up, printed prototypes, MDP/silane combination primers, prefabricated composite shells (Componeer).

### Conclusion
Laminate veneers are the most conservative durable esthetic restoration when enamel is plentiful and load is low; success depends on case selection, preparation within enamel, shade planning and a strict bonding protocol.
