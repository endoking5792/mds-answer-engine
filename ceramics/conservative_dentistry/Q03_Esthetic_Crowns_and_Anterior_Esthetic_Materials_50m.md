## STUDY MODE

### Part A: esthetic crown restorations
An esthetic crown must match colour, translucency and form while resisting occlusal load and protecting the pulp and periodontium. The options, in order of history:

1. **Metal-ceramic (PFM):** a metal framework with layered porcelain. Esthetics best with a high-gold framework, but a low-modulus framework supports the veneer poorly and encourages chipping (Zhang and Kelly, 2017). Opacity and a grey cervical line limit esthetics; margins need 1.75-2.0 mm reduction (Sikri, 2017, via digest).
2. **Glass-ceramic crowns:** leucite-reinforced (anterior single crowns), lithium disilicate (all single units), ZLS (Nejat, 2025). Monolithic crowns are possible because they are translucent; staining and cut-back with porcelain give more characterisation. Lithium disilicate single crowns anywhere in the mouth are supported by long-term data (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
3. **Alumina and zirconia cores:** In-Ceram, Procera alumina; 3Y zirconia coping layered with feldspathic porcelain gives best masking of discoloured stumps and metal posts (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022) but chips (6-10% in 3-5 years) (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
4. **Monolithic translucent zirconia (4Y/5Y, gradient):** no veneer; 91-100% survival; chipping 0% versus 7.61% in implant crowns; best for posterior or bruxers with moderate esthetic demand (Nejat, 2025).
5. **CAD-on:** lithium disilicate cap on zirconia coping.
6. **Resin-matrix crowns (PICN, resin nanoceramic):** shock-absorbing; for implant and posterior crowns; limited data (Nejat, 2025; Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
7. **Printed ceramic and permanent resin crowns:** commercial products exist but evidence is limited (Nejat, 2025).

### Part B: decision framework (Moshaverinia, 2020)
| Factor | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Position | Anterior: translucent, lower strength; posterior: stronger |
| Substrate | More than 50% enamel and no discolouration: feldspathic; otherwise lithium disilicate; severe discolouration: zirconia core or opaque cement |
| Load | Medium to high: zirconia or bonded lithium disilicate |
| Design | Single unit versus splinted |
| Bonding | Zirconia only if retention is compromised |

### Part C: optical management
Stump effect: feldspathic highest, lithium disilicate medium-low, zirconia least (Goldstein et al, 2018, via digest). Monolithic ceramics cannot mask severe discolouration without opaque luting agent or opaque flowable composite (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022). Thickness ratio of core and veneer affects colour (Moshaverinia, 2020). Characterise internally first; surface stain last (Goldstein et al, 2018, via digest).

### Part D: anterior aesthetic restorative materials (advances)
**Composites.** Two system types: few-shade systems versus systems with a full range of opacities (dentin, enamel, incisal/translucent, plus tints and opaquers) enabling natural stratification; depth of colour built dark-to-light (Goldstein et al, 2018, via digest). Torres (2020, via digest) describes a four-layer sequence: opaque halo, dentin mamelons, translucent opalescent incisal layer, enamel shade; palatal silicone index for reproducible lingual wall; finishing and polishing control texture and thereby value and translucency. Nanocluster fillers (Lava Ultimate class) give polish retention (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
**Ceramic veneers and laminates:** feldspathic layered (95.5% at 10 years), pressed and CAD (Nejat, 2025); lithium disilicate veneers 0.4-0.7 mm minimum (Nejat, 2025).
**Gold veneers** for palatal wear (Ricketts and Bartlett, 2011, via digest).
**Resin-matrix and printed materials:** thin milled restorations under 0.5 mm possible with PICN (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
**Adhesive science:** HF and silane; zirconia MDP primers (Blatz et al, 2022).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim — for the esthetic zone, bonded glass-ceramic is the default and zirconia is the masking or high-load exception.** Feldspathic and lithium disilicate give the best optics and are bondable (Moshaverinia, 2020), but fail to mask severely discoloured stumps; veneered zirconia masks best but chips (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022; Zhang and Kelly, 2017). INTERPRETATION: the stump and the load decide. ### Introduction
Esthetic crowns and anterior restorations must reproduce the optical properties of enamel and dentin while being strong, biocompatible and conservative. Recent advances in ceramics and composites give a range of choices.

### A. Esthetic crown restorations
| Type | Features | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Metal-ceramic | Strong, long record; layered porcelain | Opaque, grey cervical line, chipping, more reduction |
| Leucite glass-ceramic | Best optics; anterior single crowns | Low strength |
| Lithium disilicate | 450-550 MPa; press/CAD; bonded; monolithic or cut-back | Moderate masking; FDP caution |
| ZLS | Translucent; chairside | Limited clinical data |
| Veneered zirconia | Best masking; strong | Chipping 6-10% at 3-5 years |
| Monolithic zirconia (3Y, 4Y/5Y, graded) | 91-100% survival; no chipping | Opacity (3Y); lower strength (5Y) |
| CAD-on | Fatigue-resistant | Complex fabrication |
| Resin-matrix | Shock-absorbing, easy repair | Limited data; ageing |

### B. Selection
Position, substrate, load, design, bonding. More than 50% enamel and no discolouration: feldspathic. Less than 50% enamel or discolouration: lithium disilicate. Posterior or bruxism: zirconia or thick bonded lithium disilicate. Severe discolouration or metal post: veneered zirconia, or opaque cement/composite layering.

### C. Recent advances in crown ceramics
- Translucent and graded zirconia (3Y → 4Y → 5Y; graded discs).
- Fully crystallised lithium disilicate blocks, virgilite blocks, nano-crystal blocks.
- CAD-on technique.
- Resin-matrix blocks.
- Digital shade management and chairside CAD/CAM.
- Additive manufacturing (experimental).

### D. Anterior aesthetic restorative materials
1. **Composite resin systems:** full opacity range (opaque, dentin, enamel, translucent), tints; stratification technique; palatal silicone index; texture and polish control value and translucency.
2. **Ceramic laminate veneers:** feldspathic (95.5% at 10 years), pressed, CAD/CAM; minimum 0.4-0.7 mm.
3. **Resin-matrix thin restorations.**
4. **Gold veneers** for palatal wear.
5. **Adhesion:** HF + silane, MDP primers.

### E. Technique points
Value is the most critical colour dimension; shade before preparation; shade with two light sources; communicate characterisation to the laboratory; adequate reduction (1.0-1.5 mm axial) for colour depth; polish after adjustment.

### Conclusion
No single esthetic material is best. Bonded glass-ceramic is the default for translucent, enamel-rich cases; zirconia is reserved for masking and load; composite offers a conservative, repairable option. Success follows from selection by substrate and load, optical planning and a disciplined bonding protocol.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Stump effect: feldspathic > lithium disilicate > zirconia.
- Veneered zirconia chips; monolithic zirconia does not (0% vs 7.61% metal-ceramic implant crowns).
- 50% enamel rule.
- Composite four-layer sequence: opaque halo, dentin, translucent incisal, enamel.
