## STUDY MODE

### Frame
"Recent advances" are best presented as responses to four problems: (1) brittleness, (2) the opacity of strong ceramics, (3) veneer chipping, and (4) fabrication accuracy and time. "Technical considerations" are the controllable variables once a material is chosen: design, thickness, processing, finishing, bonding and maintenance.

### Advances by problem
**Brittleness.** Lithium disilicate (about 65-70 vol%, 450-550 MPa biaxial) and its variants: fully crystallised blocks needing no firing (n!ce, GC Initial LiSi), blocks with virgilite lithium aluminosilicate crystals requiring one glaze firing (CEREC Tessera, 700 MPa, manufacturer data), nano-sized crystals with firing-controlled translucency (Amber Mill) (Nejat, 2025). Zirconia-reinforced lithium silicate (ZLS) with about 10 wt% zirconia and about 45 vol% glass; its mechanical advantage over lithium disilicate is not conclusive and the clinical data are limited (Nejat, 2025). Fatigue failure load: translucent zirconia 1740 N, ZLS 1187 N, lithium disilicate 987 N for simplified crowns (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).

**Opacity.**
- Zirconia generations (Nejat, 2025): 1st generation 3Y, monochromatic, opaque; 2nd generation polychromatic with 3Y, 4Y (about 75% tetragonal / 25% cubic) and 5Y (about 50% / 50%); 3rd generation with yttria varying within the disc (more cubic incisally, more tetragonal cervically) so that incisal layering with weak feldspathic porcelain becomes unnecessary.
- Translucent zirconias are weaker: 4Y 400-1000 MPa, 5Y about 550 MPa (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022; Nejat, 2025) and exhibit low-temperature degradation (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
- Birefringence of tetragonal grains limits translucency; sub-100 nm grains would be needed at full strength (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
- Masking: veneered zirconia or CAD-on mask severely discoloured substrates (A3.5, C4, metal) better than monolithic ceramics, which need opaque luting agent or high-value opaque flowable composite (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).

**Chipping.** Monolithic zirconia and lithium disilicate; CAD-on (lithium disilicate cap fused to zirconia coping with fusion glass-ceramic) with fatigue survival of 591,071 cycles versus 16,428 for porcelain-veneered zirconia and fracture load similar to monolithic translucent zirconia (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022). Monolithic zirconia on implant crowns 0% chipping versus 7.61% for metal-ceramic (Nejat, 2025).

**Fabrication.**
- Subtractive CAD/CAM: precrystallised blocks easy to mill; presintered zirconia.
- Pressed lithium disilicate has better fatigue performance and fracture toughness (2.5 versus 1.79 MPa·m^0.5) than milled in laboratory studies, due to more crystal phase and larger crystals (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).
- Additive: vat polymerisation (DLP, SLA) of zirconia; flexural strength comparable in some studies (934-1071 MPa) and much lower in another (320 vs 915 MPa); issues with slurry viscosity, bubbles, interlayer defects, post-processing (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022; Nejat, 2025).
- Precision: all methods clinically acceptable; milled more precise than printed zirconia and pressed lithium disilicate in one study (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022).

**Resin-matrix ceramics.** PICN (86 wt% ceramic / 14 wt% polymer), resin nanoceramics; shock-absorbing, easy to mill thinly (under 0.5 mm) and adjust; lower strength at conventional thickness but similar fatigue at endocrown thickness (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022; Zhang and Kelly, 2017); little clinical data (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).

### Technical considerations
1. **Material selection** by substrate, position, load, translucency (Moshaverinia, 2020).
2. **Minimum thickness** (manufacturer data, Nejat, 2025): lithium disilicate inlay/onlay 1.0 mm, veneer 0.4-0.7 mm, crown 1.0-1.5 mm; 3Y zirconia 0.4-0.5 mm; 4Y/5Y 0.8-1.0 mm; connector areas 7-9 mm² (3Y) to 12-16 mm² (4Y/5Y); lithium disilicate connector 16 mm². Zirconia framework thickness of 0.3 mm caused crown-abutment fractures (Guess et al, 2011).
3. **Veneering technique:** uniform support of veneer by framework design; veneer thickness over 2.5 mm from uniform CAD coping increases residual stress; slow cooling above glass transition; additional 6 min cooling is effective (Guess et al, 2011).
4. **Finishing:** adjust with fine diamonds under water, then full polishing sequence; highly polished zirconia gives low antagonist wear (Guess et al, 2011; Bacchi and Cesar, 2022). Avoid aggressive sandblasting and coarse burs (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
5. **Contact design:** avoid sharp cusps near crown edges (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
6. **Substrate:** high-modulus build-up and cement reduce flexure (Zhang and Kelly, 2017).
7. **Bonding** (Blatz et al, 2022, modern protocol): feldspathic 9.8% HF 2 min; leucite 9.8% HF 1 min; lithium silicate 4.6% HF 20 s; PICN 9.8% HF 1 min; all followed by ultrasonic alcohol cleaning 5 min and 20 s silane; Y-TZP 50 μm alumina at 2 bar plus MDP primer, NEVER HF (no amorphous silica phase for HF/silane to act on) [SRC]. This is genuinely one of several era-specific protocols, not the only correct one: Sikri's own (older, directly read this session) protocol gives 8-12% HF for 1-3 min; Alani & Patel's modern BDJ guide gives 5% or 9.5% HF for only 10/20/60s; Craig's gives 2.5-10% for 1-4 min [SRC, SIKRI/AP/CR] — present the range with attribution rather than picking Blatz's figures as the sole answer.
8. **Try-in contamination:** saliva phosphate contaminates the surface; clean with Ivoclean (zirconium oxide suspension) or Katana Cleaner (MDP salt) (Blatz et al, 2022).
9. **Cementation:** light-cure for veneers; dual-cure for inlays, onlays and thick restorations; total-etch gives better seal under feldspathic veneers than self-etch (Blatz et al, 2022); zirconia crowns of adequate retention can be cemented conventionally.
10. **Repair:** etchable ceramics repair intraorally; zirconia repair is difficult (Blatz et al, 2022; Nejat, 2025).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS
**Claim — recent advances shift the strength-translucency trade-off; they do not abolish it.** 4Y/5Y zirconia and gradient discs give translucency at 400-850 MPa instead of 1,200 MPa (Nejat, 2025; Zhang and Kelly, 2017), ZLS has not been shown stronger than lithium disilicate (Nejat, 2025), and printed ceramics show inconsistent strength (Bacchi and Cesar, 2022). INTERPRETATION: material choice remains case-specific; "newest" is not "best". LIMITATION: most evidence is in vitro, manufacturers' data differ in test method, and clinical follow-up for ZLS, 5Y and printed ceramics is short or absent. [Likely]

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 2,300; about 105 min)**

### Introduction
Dental ceramics have developed from brittle feldspathic porcelain to a spectrum of glass-ceramics, polycrystalline and resin-matrix materials made by milling, pressing and printing. The advances answer four problems: brittleness, opacity of strong ceramics, veneer chipping and fabrication accuracy. Technical considerations determine whether the material's potential is realised.

### I. Recent advances
**1. Glass-ceramics**
- Leucite-reinforced: 35-45 vol%; anterior and low-load.
- Lithium disilicate: 65-70 vol%, 450-550 MPa; press and CAD; veneers to molar crowns; short anterior FDP.
- Variants: fully crystallised blocks (no firing), virgilite blocks (CEREC Tessera), nano-crystal blocks.
- Zirconia-reinforced lithium silicate: about 10 wt% zirconia; translucent; limited clinical data (80% at 5 years in partial coverage; molars 69%).

**2. Zirconia**
| Generation | Yttria | Phases | Property |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 3Y | Mostly tetragonal | Strong (900-1200), opaque, monochromatic |
| 2nd | 4Y, 5Y | 75/25 and 50/50 tetragonal/cubic | Translucent, weaker (550-850) |
| 3rd | Graded | Cubic incisal, tetragonal cervical | Strength and translucency gradient |
Monolithic zirconia: 91-100% survival; low chipping.

**3. Structural designs**
Monolithic restorations; CAD-on (lithium disilicate on zirconia, fusion glass); layered porcelain only where esthetic need.

**4. Resin-matrix ceramics**
PICN (86/14 wt%), resin nanoceramic: dentin-like modulus, easily milled and repaired, fatigue acceptable at endocrown thickness; little clinical data.

**5. Digital fabrication**
Chairside and laboratory CAD/CAM; precrystallised blocks; presintered zirconia (about 25% shrinkage compensated); intraoral scanning; additive manufacturing of zirconia and lithium disilicate (promising, inconsistent strength, porosity, interlayer defects).

**6. Optical engineering**
Colour- and translucency-gradient blocks; digital shade analysis; masking strategies (veneered zirconia, CAD-on, opaque cement).

**7. Adhesion science**
Material-specific protocols, MDP primers, universal primers with silane plus MDP; zirconia cleaning agents.

### II. Technical considerations
| Domain | Consideration |
|---|---|
| Selection | Position, load, substrate (more than 50% enamel favours feldspathic), translucency |
| Thickness | Lithium disilicate 1.0-1.5 mm crown; 3Y zirconia 0.4-0.5 mm; 4Y/5Y 0.8-1.0 mm |
| Connectors | Lithium disilicate 16 mm²; 3Y zirconia 7-9 mm²; 4Y/5Y 12-16 mm² |
| Veneering | Uniform support; veneer under 2.5 mm; slow cooling |
| Finishing | Fine diamond with water, polish; avoid coarse burs |
| Occlusion | Blunt cusps, no sharp contacts near edges |
| Substrate | High-modulus core build-up and cement |
| Bonding | Glass-ceramic: HF + silane (modern 5-9.5%/10-60s vs older 8-12%/1-3min, both attributed); zirconia: 50 μm alumina at 2 bar + MDP, never HF |
| Try-in | Clean with Ivoclean or Katana Cleaner |
| Cementation | Light-cure veneers, dual-cure posterior; zirconia crowns with retention can be conventionally cemented |
| Repair | Etchable: HF/silane; zirconia: air abrasion/MDP |

### III. Limitations
Low-temperature degradation of zirconia; lower strength of translucent grades; short clinical follow-up for ZLS, 5Y and printed materials; manufacturer-reported strengths not comparable; bonding technique sensitivity; cost.

### IV. Clinical guidance
Use the weakest ceramic that meets the load requirement, so as to preserve optics; thicker where load is high; monolithic where esthetics allow.

### Conclusion
Advances in ceramics have given clinicians a spectrum from highly translucent adhesively bonded glass-ceramics to strong monolithic zirconia. Survival depends less on the brand than on correct selection, thickness, design, finishing and bonding.

- 3Y/4Y/5Y: about 0/25/50% cubic.

- Connector sizes by material.

- Clean try-in with Ivoclean/Katana cleaner, not saliva-contaminated bond.
