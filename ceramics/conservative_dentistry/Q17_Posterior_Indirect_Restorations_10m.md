## STUDY MODE

### Definition
Indirect restorations for posterior teeth are fabricated outside the mouth and cemented: inlays, onlays, partial-coverage and full crowns, endocrowns, in gold, ceramic, indirect composite or resin-matrix materials.

### Indications
More than half the tooth lost, wide proximal area, weakened cusps, need for accurate contact and anatomy, previously fractured direct restoration, root-filled molars (onlay), patient preference (amalgam replacement) (Sikri, 2017; Ricketts and Bartlett, 2011, via digest).

### Materials
| Material | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Cast gold (Type I-II inlays, III-IV crowns) | Gold inlay/onlay/crown | Longevity, low antagonist wear; unesthetic |
| Metal-ceramic | Crown, FDP | Strong; opaque |
| Lithium disilicate | Inlay to molar crown | Bonded; 450-550 MPa |
| Zirconia | Molar crown, FDP | Monolithic, high load |
| Indirect composite | Inlay, onlay | Repairable; wear product-dependent |
| PICN/resin-matrix | Single units | Shock-absorbing |

### Selection (Moshaverinia, 2020)
Posterior: stronger ceramics such as zirconia or bonded lithium disilicate; feldspathic porcelain is contraindicated posteriorly; bond lithium disilicate to tooth; zirconia bonded only if retention is compromised.

### Preparation
Single path of insertion; taper 10-20° posterior (about 10° for resin-luted); wall height 3 mm (molar 4 mm); no undercuts; rounded angles; ceramic thickness 1.0-1.5 mm (Sikri, 2017, via digest; Nejat, 2025).

### Cementation
Resin cement for ceramic and composite; conventional or resin for zirconia and metal with good retention.

### Survival
Empress inlays/onlays 90% at 8 years; lithium disilicate single units anywhere in mouth supported by long-term data; monolithic zirconia crowns 91-100%; veneered zirconia chips (Guess et al, 2011; Zhang and Kelly, 2017; Nejat, 2025).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 330; about 15 min)**

### Definition
Restorations made outside the mouth for posterior teeth and cemented: inlay, onlay, crown.

### Indications
Extensive loss, wide proximal area, cusp protection, root-filled tooth, failed direct restoration, esthetic or amalgam replacement.

### Types and materials
| Type | Material |
|---|---|
| Inlay | Gold, ceramic, indirect composite |
| Onlay | Gold, ceramic, composite |
| Crown | Metal-ceramic, zirconia, lithium disilicate |

### Selection
High load: gold, zirconia, bonded lithium disilicate; esthetic: lithium disilicate; economy and repair: indirect composite; avoid feldspathic posteriorly.

### Preparation
Single path, taper about 10° (10-20° posterior), walls at least 3 mm (4 mm molars), no undercuts, rounded angles, 1.0-1.5 mm thickness for ceramic.

### Cementation
Adhesive resin cement for ceramic and composite.

### Advantages
Strength, anatomy and contacts, longevity, tooth protection.

### Disadvantages
Cost, visits, more tooth removal, brittle ceramic, technique sensitivity.

### Conclusion
Posterior indirect restorations give durable, accurate restoration of large lesions; material choice follows load, esthetics and substrate.
