## STUDY MODE
The principle: **a dual bond**, micromechanical plus chemical, to both tooth and ceramic (Blatz et al, 2022). The ceramic surface is conditioned according to composition; the tooth by etch-and-rinse or self-etch adhesive.

### Selection of cement
Resin cement is routine for glass-ceramics and thin or low-retention restorations: better strength, lower solubility, esthetics; glass ionomer and resin-modified glass ionomer produce weaker ceramic restorations (Sikri, 2017, via digest). Zirconia crowns with adequate retention and thickness can be cemented conventionally (Blatz et al, 2022). Light-cure: veneers and shallow inlays (extended working time, colour stability); dual-cure: inlays, onlays and thick or opaque restorations; anaerobic cements for extended working time; self-adhesive cements: lower enamel bond strengths and limited shades, so less suited to veneers (Manso et al, 2011).

### Protocols (Blatz et al, 2022, Table 1)
| Ceramic | Steps |
|---|---|
| Feldspathic | Try-in; 9.8% HF 2 min; rinse; ultrasonic alcohol 5 min; silane 20 s; isolation; tooth pretreatment; light/dual-cure resin |
| Leucite-reinforced | 9.8% HF 1 min; then as above |
| Lithium silicate/disilicate | 4.6% HF 20 s; then as above |
| PICN | 9.8% HF 1 min; silane |
| Y-TZP | Ultrasonic alcohol clean; air abrasion 50 μm alumina at 2 bar; MDP primer; dual- or self-cure resin |
Sikri (2017, via digest): 8-12% HF 1-3 min (feldspathic longest); 30-35% phosphoric acid on tooth 10-15 s; Nejat (2025): manufacturer HF 5% for 10-60 s depending on product; zirconia air abrasion 25-110 μm alumina at 1-3 bar. Different sources give different concentrations and times; follow the manufacturer's instructions for the specific product.

### Special points
- Try-in contaminates the ceramic (saliva phosphates); silanate before try-in then ultrasonically clean after, or clean zirconia with Ivoclean or Katana Cleaner (Manso et al, 2011; Blatz et al, 2022).
- Excess silane removal: hot water rinse 80 °C 15 s (Manso et al, 2011).
- Isolation: rubber dam.
- Oxygen-blocking gel before final cure (Alani and Patel, 2025, via digest).
- HF is caustic: protect soft tissue.
- High-translucent zirconia benefits from resin bonding to raise flexural strength (Blatz et al, 2022).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 400; about 18 min)**

### Introduction
Cementation of all-ceramic restorations must give a durable seal and, for glass-ceramics, a bond that strengthens the restoration.

### Principle
Dual bond: micromechanical + chemical to ceramic and tooth.

### Cement choice
- Glass-ceramics, veneers, inlays/onlays: adhesive resin cement (light-cure for veneers, dual-cure for thick).
- Zirconia: resin cement with MDP; conventional cement acceptable with good retention.
- Avoid zinc phosphate and GIC for glass-ceramics.

### Protocol
| Ceramic | Surface treatment |
|---|---|
| Feldspathic | 9.8% HF 2 min + silane |
| Leucite | 9.8% HF 1 min + silane |
| Lithium disilicate | 4.6-5% HF 20 s + silane |
| PICN | HF 1 min + silane |
| Zirconia | 50 μm alumina 2 bar + MDP primer (no HF) |

### Steps
1. Try-in: fit, shade, occlusion.
2. Clean (ultrasonic alcohol; Ivoclean for zirconia).
3. Condition ceramic as above; silane 20 s.
4. Isolate with rubber dam.
5. Tooth: 35% phosphoric acid 15 s (enamel), adhesive.
6. Load resin cement; seat with light pressure.
7. Remove excess; light-cure; oxygen block.
8. Finish margins; check occlusion.

### Precautions
No contamination after silane; no HF on zirconia; protect soft tissue from HF; thin cement film.

### Conclusion
Material-specific conditioning plus adhesive resin cement gives durable all-ceramic restorations.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- HF: glass ceramics only; zirconia: air abrasion + MDP.
- Dual-cure for thick, light-cure for veneers.
- Rubber dam; remove excess before curing.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. HF on zirconia.
2. No try-in cleaning.
3. Self-adhesive cement for veneers.
