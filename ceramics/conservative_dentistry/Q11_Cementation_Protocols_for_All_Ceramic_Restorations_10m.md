## STUDY MODE
The principle: **a dual bond**, micromechanical plus chemical, to both tooth and ceramic (Blatz et al, 2022). The ceramic surface is conditioned according to composition; the tooth by etch-and-rinse or self-etch adhesive.

### Selection of cement
Resin cement is routine for glass-ceramics and thin or low-retention restorations: better strength, lower solubility, esthetics; glass ionomer and resin-modified glass ionomer produce weaker ceramic restorations (Sikri, 2017, via digest). Zirconia crowns with adequate retention and thickness can be cemented conventionally (Blatz et al, 2022). Light-cure: veneers and shallow inlays (extended working time, colour stability); dual-cure: inlays, onlays and thick or opaque restorations; anaerobic cements for extended working time; self-adhesive cements: lower enamel bond strengths and limited shades, so less suited to veneers (Manso et al, 2011).

**GIC/RMGIC cautions, confirmed directly from SIKRI this session**: plain glass ionomer is **not preferred** as a base or lute under an all-ceramic restoration specifically because of its **low compressive strength** relative to resin composite's higher elastic modulus. **Resin-modified GIC carries its own, more specific risk**: a hygroscopic-expansion reaction under an all-ceramic crown can generate enough internal stress to **fracture the ceramic** — SIKRI's language is "use with caution," i.e. RMGIC is not a blanket-safe default luting choice for all-ceramic work the way it might be for a metal-based restoration [SRC, SIKRI].

**SIKRI's luting-agents chapter — numeric clinical requirements** (11 numbered requirements in the source; key figures): a luting cement should have **≥70 MPa compressive strength** and **<25 µm film thickness** for adequate seating. Modulus hierarchy across the system: dentin 18-30 GPa < casting alloys 90-220 GPa / ceramics 55-230 GPa — the cement sits at the bottom of this stiffness ladder and must still transmit load without excessive flexure. Named products appear across cement classes (RelyX Unicem, Appeal, Nexus 2, Panavia, C&B Metabond), illustrating the "seating vs retention" trade-off and the concept of cement **venting** (an escape channel/relief for excess cement to allow full seating) [SRC, SIKRI].

### Protocols (Blatz et al, 2022, Table 1)
| Ceramic | Steps |
|---|---|
| Feldspathic | Try-in; 9.8% HF 2 min; rinse; ultrasonic alcohol 5 min; silane 20 s; isolation; tooth pretreatment; light/dual-cure resin |
| Leucite-reinforced | 9.8% HF 1 min; then as above |
| Lithium silicate/disilicate | 4.6% HF 20 s; then as above |
| PICN | 9.8% HF 1 min; silane |
| Y-TZP | Ultrasonic alcohol clean; air abrasion 50 μm alumina at 2 bar; MDP primer; dual- or self-cure resin |
Sikri (2017, via digest): 8-12% HF 1-3 min (feldspathic longest); 30-35% phosphoric acid on tooth 10-15 s; Nejat (2025): manufacturer HF 5% for 10-60 s depending on product; zirconia air abrasion 25-110 μm alumina at 1-3 bar. Different sources give different concentrations and times; follow the manufacturer's instructions for the specific product.

### Alani & Patel's Table 2.10 — the complete material-by-material intraoral bonding protocol (read directly this session)
This is likely the single most useful table for this exact question, since it covers every substrate in one place, not just ceramic [SRC, AP]:
| Substrate | Protocol |
|---|---|
| Gold/base-metal | Air abrasion (alumina), then a base-metal/non-precious-metal primer (e.g. MDP- or thiol-containing) |
| Composite/resin-nanoceramic | Air abrasion or roughen, silane (resin-composite surfaces benefit from silane too), adhesive |
| Fibre post | Silane plus adhesive (post surface already contains a glass/silica phase) |
| Glass-ceramic | HF etch (concentration/time per AP's own modern figures: 5% or 9.5% for 10/20/60 s depending on ceramic) + silane |
| Zirconia | Air abrasion (alumina) + MDP-containing primer; **never HF** (no amorphous silica phase) |

### SIKRI's 12-step clinical cementation sequence (confirmed directly this session, matching Q9's inlay protocol)
1. Try-in. 2. Rubber-dam isolation. 3. HF-etch ceramic intaglio (where applicable). 4. Silane. 5. Phosphoric-acid etch of tooth. 6. Adhesive. 7. Load resin cement (dual-cure for most; light-cure for veneers/shallow restorations). 8. Seat under sustained occlusal closure. 9. Clean up gross excess before full cure. 10. Multi-direction light-cure. 11. Glycerin/oxygen-block gel over margins before final cure increment. 12. Minimal polish; avoid further intraoral adjustment of the fired ceramic where possible [SRC, SIKRI].

### Special points
- Try-in contaminates the ceramic (saliva phosphates); silanate before try-in then ultrasonically clean after, or clean zirconia with Ivoclean or Katana Cleaner (Manso et al, 2011; Blatz et al, 2022).
- Excess silane removal: hot water rinse 80 °C 15 s (Manso et al, 2011).
- Isolation: rubber dam.
- Oxygen-blocking gel before final cure (Alani and Patel, 2025, via digest).
- HF is caustic: protect soft tissue.
- High-translucent zirconia benefits from resin bonding to raise flexural strength (Blatz et al, 2022).

## EXAM MODE
**(Word count about 400; about 18 min)**

### Introduction
Cementation of all-ceramic restorations must give a durable seal and, for glass-ceramics, a bond that strengthens the restoration.

### Principle
Dual bond: micromechanical + chemical to ceramic and tooth.

### Cement choice
- Glass-ceramics, veneers, inlays/onlays: adhesive resin cement (light-cure for veneers, dual-cure for thick).
- Zirconia: resin cement with MDP; conventional cement acceptable with good retention.
- Avoid zinc phosphate and GIC for glass-ceramics; plain GIC base not preferred under ceramic (low compressive strength, SIKRI); RMGIC lute risks hygroscopic-expansion fracture of an all-ceramic crown — use with caution (SIKRI).
- Luting-cement numeric targets (SIKRI): ≥70 MPa compressive strength, <25µm film thickness.

### Protocol — by ceramic AND by every substrate (Alani & Patel Table 2.10)
| Substrate | Surface treatment |
|---|---|
| Feldspathic glass-ceramic | 9.8% HF 2 min (or AP modern: 5-9.5% HF 10-60s) + silane |
| Leucite | 9.8% HF 1 min + silane |
| Lithium disilicate | 4.6-5% HF 20 s + silane |
| PICN | HF 1 min + silane |
| Zirconia | 50 μm alumina 2 bar + MDP primer (no HF) |
| Gold/base-metal | Air abrasion + base-metal primer (AP) |
| Composite/resin-nanoceramic | Air abrasion/roughen + silane + adhesive (AP) |
| Fibre post | Silane + adhesive (AP) |

### Steps — SIKRI's 12-step sequence
1. Try-in: fit, shade, occlusion.
2. Rubber-dam isolation.
3. HF-etch ceramic intaglio (where applicable) — clean (ultrasonic alcohol; Ivoclean for zirconia).
4. Silane 20 s.
5. Tooth: 35% phosphoric acid 15 s (enamel), adhesive.
6. Load resin cement (dual-cure, or light-cure for veneers/shallow restorations).
7. Seat under sustained occlusal closure.
8. Clean up gross excess before full cure.
9. Multi-direction light-cure.
10. Glycerin/oxygen-block gel over margins before final cure increment.
11. Minimal polish; avoid further intraoral adjustment of the ceramic.
12. Finish margins; check occlusion.

### Precautions
No contamination after silane; no HF on zirconia; protect soft tissue from HF; thin cement film.

### Conclusion
Material-specific conditioning plus adhesive resin cement gives durable all-ceramic restorations.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- HF: glass ceramics only; zirconia: air abrasion + MDP (AP Table 2.10 covers gold/composite/fibre post too).
- Dual-cure for thick, light-cure for veneers.
- Rubber dam; remove excess before curing.
- SIKRI's 12-step sequence; luting cement ≥70 MPa compressive strength, <25µm film thickness.
- GIC base not preferred under ceramic (low compressive strength); RMGIC lute risks hygroscopic-expansion fracture of all-ceramic crowns (SIKRI).
