## 1. Introduction and classification

### Explanation

Procedural accidents (mishaps) are unwanted or unforeseen events during root canal treatment that can affect prognosis (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]; they happen to experienced clinicians too (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. The question's three words map onto a useful idea from Attar and co-workers: most errors are "reversible" only if detected in time; ignored, they become irreversible in a "domino effect" (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025) [SRC]. Detection is therefore the hinge between prevention and correction.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Endodontic mishaps (procedural accidents) are unwanted or unforeseen events during root canal treatment that may affect prognosis; they occur with experienced clinicians as well as beginners (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002; Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Classification (by stage):** access (perforation, missed canals, wrong tooth); cleaning and shaping (blockage, ledge, transportation and zip, strip and apical perforation, separated instrument, irrigant extrusion); obturation (under- and overfilling, vertical root fracture); post space (perforation); any stage (aspiration, ingestion).

Early detection turns a correctable error into a corrected one; ignored errors lead to further errors ("domino effect") (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025).

## 2. Detection, correction and prevention of each mishap

### Explanation

**Detection signs** come from several chapters. Perforation: sudden pain when anaesthesia was adequate, sudden bleeding, burning or bad taste with NaOCl, malpositioned file, apex locator reading short (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]; an apex locator separates a bleeding canal from a perforation (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Apical perforation: fresh bleeding and sudden loss of the apical stop (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]; post-space perforation: fresh blood during preparation (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Ledge: a looser feel of the file, no tip binding, file tip off the canal on the radiograph (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC], or a "hard brick wall" (Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021) [SRC]. Separated instrument: a shortened file with a blunt tip and loss of patency (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Aspiration: a lost instrument followed by coughing or gagging; 87% are swallowed (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC].

**Correction** details are in Q01, Q05 and Q09: ledge bypass with a precurved No. 10 file (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC] or micro-Opener and slow precurved rotary files (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]; perforation sealed immediately with MTA (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC], or Geristore at the gingival margin (Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021) [SRC]; instrument removal with ultrasonics worked anticlockwise when one-third of the fragment can be exposed (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017; Machtou and Ruddle, 2019) [SRC], or bypass, which does not worsen prognosis in selected cases (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC].

| **NaOCl extrusion** | Sudden severe pain, swelling, ecchymosis, bleeding | Stop; saline; analgesics, steroids, cold compresses; drain visible swelling; no routine antibiotics (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) | Loose side-vented needle short of length; gentle pressure |

## 3. General prevention and communication

### Explanation

Grossman's general guidelines: rubber dam and high-power suction as the standard of care, working length by more than one method, single-use shaping files (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Harty's: glide path before engine-driven NiTi (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC]; Cohen's Ch.20: precurving and not forcing instruments (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]; Torabinejad's 6th edition: CBCT helps prevent most accidents (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Walton and Torabinejad: inform the patient of the incident, correction, alternatives and prognosis (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**General prevention (Gopikrishna, 2021; Terauchi and Renton, 2021; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021)**
1. Case assessment and referral of difficult cases.
2. Rubber dam with high-power suction.
3. Radiographs at each stage; CBCT for complex anatomy.
4. Straight-line access; magnification.
5. Working length by apex locator and radiograph, maintained.
6. Glide path; precurved, sequential files; never force; single-use NiTi.
7. Copious irrigation and recapitulation.
8. Inspect instruments before use; discard any that are unwound, bent or worn.
9. Know canal anatomy and its variations; anticipate difficulty from the preoperative radiograph.

**After a mishap:** stop, confirm (radiograph, apex locator), inform the patient of the incident, the correction, alternatives and prognosis, document, and refer if needed (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

**Critical point.** Most mishaps do not cause failure by themselves; the infected canal left beyond a ledge or fragment, or the periodontal breach of a perforation, does. Prognosis is good when detection is early and correction immediate (Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS (20 marks: one claim)

**"Separated instruments and ledges cause failure."** Cohen's says the separated instrument "in itself does not predispose the case to posttreatment disease"; Torabinejad's 6th edition says it affects outcome only where microbial control is compromised or a lesion preexists (Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. The mishap marks an uncleaned canal; detection and disinfection, not the object itself, decide outcome. [Likely]

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Reversible errors become irreversible if not detected ("domino effect").
- Perforation signs: bleeding, pain, burning with NaOCl, apex locator short.
- Ledge: hard stop or looser feel; separated file: blunt shortened file.
- Corrections: MTA for perforations; precurved small files for ledges; ultrasonics for fragments.
- Rubber dam prevents aspiration; 87% of dropped instruments are swallowed.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Writing only management and forgetting "detection" and "prevention", which the question names.
- Giving antibiotics routinely for NaOCl accidents.
- Omitting communication with the patient.
