## 1. Introduction and classification

### Explanation

Torabinejad's chapter names the commonest errors in this phase: ledge formation, artificial canal creation, root perforation, instrument separation and extrusion of irrigant (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]; Grossman's adds blockage, transportation and zipping (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Attar and co-workers separate reversible errors (blockage, separated instrument) from irreversible ones (ledge, zip, transportation, gouging, over-instrumentation), noting that irreversible errors usually follow reversible ones that were missed (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Cleaning and shaping must follow the original canal while removing tissue and debris; errors arise when instruments leave the canal path or are lost within it (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Classification**
- Reversible (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025): canal blockage; separated instrument.
- Irreversible: ledge; artificial canal; transportation, zip and elbow; strip perforation; apical perforation (over-instrumentation); irrigant extrusion.
Irreversible errors usually follow reversible ones left unrecognised ("domino effect").

## 2. Errors and their management

### Explanation

**Blockage** is debris packed apically (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; ledges and blocks often coexist (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. **Ledge**: Torabinejad's 6th edition: five causes (no straight-line access, no precurving, forcing large files, poor irrigation, packed debris), risk over 50% above 30 degrees of curvature; bypass with a precurved No. 10 file, a No. 3 Gates-Glidden anticurvature flare to see the ledge, or a precurved .02 rotary file at 100 rpm (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. **Artificial canal**: aggressive stainless steel use; seal perforations over 0.5 mm with MTA before renegotiating (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. **Transportation and zip**: AAE definitions (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC]; Ingle's Type I-III (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019) [SRC]. **Strip perforation**: bleeding after pulp removal (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; canals apical filled with MTA first (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. **Apical perforation**: sudden loss of apical stop; new working length 1-2 mm short (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. **Separated instrument**: hand 0.25%, rotary 1.68-2.4% (Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021) [SRC]; three approaches: remove, bypass, or fill to it (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. **NaOCl**: open apex, poor length control, no endodontic needle (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

| Error | Cause | Recognition | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blockage** | Dentinal debris packed apically; no recapitulation (Gopikrishna, 2021) | Loss of length; soft stop | Small precurved files, NaOCl/chelator, recapitulate with a patency file (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025) |
| **Ledge** (artificial irregularity on the canal wall impeding instruments to an otherwise patent canal; AAE) | No straight-line access, no precurving, large files forced into curves, poor irrigation, packed debris; risk above 30° curvature over 50% (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) | Hard stop short of length; file off the canal on radiograph | Precurved 08-10 file with directional stop, picking motion; anticurvature flare with No. 3 GG; precurved .02 rotary at 100 rpm; reduce; else fill to ledge and review (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) |
| **Artificial canal** | Boring past a ledge with stiff SS files (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) | Radiograph with file; bleeding if perforated | Relocate original canal (microscope, CBCT); seal perforation over 0.5 mm with MTA first |
| **Transportation, zip, elbow** | Files straighten in curves; rotation at length; large SS files above ISO 20 (Gopikrishna, 2021) | Loss of apical stop, reverse apical taper, bleeding | Ingle's Type I: reshape coronally; Type II: MTA apical barrier; Type III: surgery (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019); thermoplastic obturation |
| **Strip perforation** | Over-flaring inner (furcal) wall of curved roots | Bleeding after pulp removal (Gopikrishna, 2021) | Haemostasis; MTA repair; fill canal apical with MTA first (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) |
| **Apical perforation** | Instrumenting past the constriction | Sudden loss of apical stop, fresh bleeding, pain | New working length 1-2 mm short; apical seat or MTA barrier (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) |
| **Separated instrument** | Cyclic or torsional fatigue (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025); incidence hand 0.25%, rotary 1.68-2.4% (Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021) | Shortened blunt file; loss of patency | Remove if one-third can be exposed (ultrasonics); bypass; or fill to it; no staging platform beyond the curve (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) |
| **NaOCl extrusion** | Open or overprepared apex, poor length control, needle binding (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) | Sudden severe pain, swelling, ecchymosis | Saline, analgesics, steroids, cold compresses; drain swelling; antibiotics not routine (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) |

**General principles of management**
1. Stop as soon as resistance changes or length is lost; do not force.
2. Confirm with a radiograph (file in place, angled views), apex locator and, if needed, CBCT.
3. Use magnification; small precurved files, lubricant and irrigant.
4. Correct immediately while the error is still reversible; seal perforations at once with MTA.
5. If correction fails, clean and fill to the obstruction, inform the patient and recall; surgery or replantation if disease persists.
6. Refer complex cases to an endodontist (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

**Prognosis.** It depends on how much of the canal beyond the error remains uncleaned and infected, on the preoperative pulp and periapical status and on whether a perforation exists. Short, cleaned apical ledges and fragments separated late in a vital canal have a good prognosis; early errors in infected canals, and perforations near the crest, do worst (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021).

## 3. Prevention

### Explanation

Cohen's Ch.20: glide path prevents ledging and transportation; NiTi reciprocation lowers errors even in novices (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]. Grossman's: avoid aggressive large stainless steel files above ISO 20; NiTi used correctly is safest (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

1. Straight-line access and coronal flaring.
2. Accurate working length, re-checked during shaping.
3. Glide path to size 15-20 before rotary NiTi (Terauchi and Renton, 2021).
4. Precurved, sequential stainless steel hand files; avoid large SS files above ISO 20 in curves (Gopikrishna, 2021).
5. Flexible NiTi with non-cutting tips; reciprocation reduces errors even in beginners (Terauchi and Renton, 2021).
6. Never force or rotate files at length; balanced force.
7. Copious irrigation, lubrication, recapitulation with a patency file.
8. Single use and inspection of instruments.
9. Magnification and CBCT in complex canals.

**Conclusion.** Most cleaning and shaping errors start with loss of control of the instrument tip in a curved canal; early recognition and immediate correction keep them reversible.

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS (one claim)

**"NiTi instruments eliminate shaping errors."** Grossman's calls NiTi in the right protocol the safest method (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], and Cohen's reports fewer errors with reciprocation (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]; but rotary separation is more frequent than hand separation (1.68-2.4% vs 0.25%) (Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021) [SRC]. NiTi trades ledges and transportation for separation risk; a glide path reduces both. [Likely]

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Reversible (blockage, separation) vs irreversible (ledge, zip, perforation).
- Ledge risk over 50% above 30° curvature.
- Ingle's apical transportation Types I-III.
- Strip perforation: inner wall, bleeding after pulp removal, MTA.
- Separation: hand 0.25%, rotary 1.68-2.4%.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Putting the strip perforation on the outer wall (it is on the inner, furcal wall).
- Omitting the AAE definitions.
- Listing errors without recognition signs.
