## Explanation

The AAE defines the zip as a tear-drop shape formed at the foramen when a file extends through it and transports the outer wall, and the elbow as the narrow portion just coronal to a zip or transportation (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC]. Grossman's defines zipping as apical transportation of a curved canal from improper shaping and the elbow as "the narrowest portion of the zipped canal", where obturation usually stops (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Attar and co-workers credit "apical zip" to Weine and Kelly (1975) (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025) [SRC]. Strip perforation: complete penetration of the canal wall from excessive lateral removal, usually in curved roots or roots with invaginations (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC]; Hülsmann and Schäfer blame Hedström files, Gates-Glidden drills and especially Peeso reamers in curved mesial roots of lower molars (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2009) [SRC]; Grossman's: bleeding after pulp removal, MTA repair under the microscope (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; stripping perforations are usually inaccessible (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**1. Zipping**
- *Definition:* a tear-drop shape formed at the apical foramen when a file extends through it and transports the outer wall of a curved canal (American Association of Endodontists, 2012); term introduced by Weine and Kelly, 1975 (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025).
- *Cause:* stiff, large files taken to length and rotated in a curve; files straighten and cut the outer wall apically.
- *Result:* reverse apical taper, loss of the apical stop, extrusion of filling apically and an unfilled, leaking zip coronal to it; prognosis falls if a perforation follows.

**2. Elbow**
- *Definition:* the narrowest part of a curved canal immediately coronal to the zip (American Association of Endodontists, 2012; Gopikrishna, 2021).
- *Significance:* obturation stops at the elbow; the zip beyond is left unfilled and leaks.

**Management of zip/elbow (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019):** Type I (minor): reshape coronal to the foramen, conventional obturation; Type II (moderate): MTA apical barrier; Type III (severe): fill as well as possible, then surgery. Thermoplastic obturation is preferred (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**3. Strip perforation**
- *Definition:* complete penetration of the canal wall from excessive lateral removal of dentin, usually in curved roots or roots with concavities (American Association of Endodontists, 2012).
- *Site:* inner (furcal) wall of curved roots, e.g. mesial roots of mandibular molars; caused by over-flaring with Gates-Glidden drills, Peeso reamers or Hedström files (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2009).
- *Recognition:* bleeding after the pulp has been removed; apex locator reading short (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- *Management:* haemostasis; MTA repair under the microscope; fill the canal apical to the defect with MTA first (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021); poorer prognosis than direct perforations because it is inaccessible (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

**Prevention (all three):** precurved small files, no rotation at length, flexible NiTi, glide path, anticurvature filing (away from the furcation), limited Gates-Glidden and Peeso use in thin curved roots, sequential sizes without skipping, and stiff stainless steel files above ISO 20 avoided in curves (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Zip at foramen (outer wall), elbow at curve, strip on inner wall.
