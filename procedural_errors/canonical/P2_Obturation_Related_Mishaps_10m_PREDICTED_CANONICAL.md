## 1. Underfilling

### Explanation

Torabinejad's 6th edition: causes are a natural barrier, a ledge, insufficient flaring, a poorly adapted master cone and inadequate condensation; removal and retreatment addressing the cause is preferred; forcing gutta-percha with more spreader or plugger pressure "is not a solution and can fracture the root" (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Grossman's: loss of working length from dentinal mud; small files and NaOCl, then reseat the cone (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Definition:** root filling ending more than about 2 mm short of the radiographic apex or of the prepared working length [RECALL for the 2 mm figure]; voids and poor lateral adaptation are included.

**Causes (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021; Gopikrishna, 2021):** natural barrier (calcification); ledge or blockage (loss of working length from packed dentinal mud); insufficient flaring/taper; poorly fitting master cone; inadequate condensation.

**Management:**
- Before obturation: remove debris with small files and NaOCl, regain length, reseat the cone, check radiograph (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- After obturation: remove the filling and retreat, correcting the cause; do not force gutta-percha apically with extra pressure, which can fracture the root (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021).

## 2. Overfilling and overextension

### Explanation

Torabinejad's 6th edition: usually overinstrumentation through the constriction or lack of taper; an open apex gives no matrix; radiograph before cutting the excess, remove if sealer unset; apical surgery if failure (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Plotino and co-workers: warm gutta-percha overextends more than cold lateral condensation; little harm away from vital structures unless paraformaldehyde is present; nerve injury mostly with mandibular second molars; eugenol pastes not recommended; recovery depends on damage and speed of removal; extrusion into the antrum reported with many materials (Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025) [SRC]. Harty's: calcium hydroxide extruded into the inferior alveolar canal can cause permanent nerve damage (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Overfilling:** canal filled and material extruded beyond the apex. **Overextension:** material beyond the apex but canal not fully sealed [RECALL for the distinction].

**Causes:** over-instrumentation through the apical constriction; lack of taper or apical stop; open apex or resorption (no matrix); zipped foramen; excess condensation force; injected thermoplastic gutta-percha (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021; Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025).

**Consequences (Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025):**
- Usually little permanent harm if away from vital structures and the canal is sealed.
- Paraformaldehyde pastes: high risk of permanent damage.
- Inferior alveolar nerve (mostly mandibular second molars): paraesthesia, pain.
- Maxillary sinus: sinusitis; zinc-containing sealers linked to aspergillosis.

**Prevention:** accurate working length; tapered preparation with an apical stop; master cone radiograph; MTA apical barrier for open apices; controlled heat and pressure with warm techniques (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021).

**Management:** radiograph before removing excess; pull out the gutta-percha mass if the sealer has not set; observe minor extrusions; nerve involvement needs immediate removal and referral, since recovery depends on how quickly the cause is removed; apical surgery if failure (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021; Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025). Avoid eugenol pastes near the canal; refer calcium hydroxide extrusion into the inferior alveolar canal urgently (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017).

## 3. Vertical root fracture

### Explanation

Torabinejad's 6th edition: main cause post cementation, secondary cause overzealous condensation; narrow deep pocket, sinus tract, J-shaped radiolucency; CBCT limited by scatter; treatment root removal or extraction (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

- *Causes:* post cementation (main); excessive lateral/vertical condensation force in under- or over-prepared canals (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021).
- *Signs:* narrow, deep isolated pocket; sinus tract; lateral or J-shaped radiolucency.
- *Diagnosis:* visualisation by surgery or removing the restoration; CBCT (limited by scatter).
- *Treatment:* root resection (multirooted) or extraction; prognosis hopeless.


## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS (one claim)

**"Thermoplastic obturation is best for irregular canals."** Grossman's prefers it for zips and ledges (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]; Plotino and co-workers report a meta-analysis showing more overextension with warm than cold techniques (Plotino, Venturi and Grande, 2025) [SRC]. Use it with an apical stop or barrier. [Likely]

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Do not force gutta-percha: root fracture.
- Paraformaldehyde and eugenol pastes near the nerve are the dangerous overfills.
- VRF: post cementation, J-shaped radiolucency.
