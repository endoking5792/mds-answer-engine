## 1. Introduction and incidence

### Explanation

Incidence figures vary widely because studies differ in instrument, operator and definition: Cohen's Ch.10 gives 0.25% for hand and 1.68-2.4% for rotary instruments (Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021) [SRC]; Cohen's Ch.20 gives 0.4% to 23% for NiTi files and about 5% reported by endodontists, more often in mandibular molars (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]. Harty's: more common with inexperienced operators (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. Present the range rather than one number.

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Instrument separation is the fracture of a file, reamer, Gates-Glidden drill, lentulo or other instrument inside the canal. It blocks cleaning of the canal beyond it.

**Incidence:** hand files 0.25%, rotary 1.68-2.4% (Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021); NiTi reported from 0.4% to 23%, about 5% in endodontists' practice; more frequent in molars, especially mandibular molars (Terauchi and Renton, 2021).

## 2. Reasons for fracture

### Explanation

Cohen's Ch.20: improper use is the commonest cause (inadequate access, overuse, too much apical pressure, large instruments in curved canals), plus operator experience, speed, curvature radius, design, torque, manufacturing, alloy, motion, tooth type and absence of glide path; cyclic fatigue comes from alternating compressive and tensile stress on the outer side of a file rotating in a curve, without visible deformation; torsional failure when the tip binds and the shank keeps turning; cyclic fatigue predominates in curved canals, torsional failure can happen in straight ones (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]. Torabinejad's 6th edition: corrosion by NaOCl and manufacturing defects; NiTi fractures at lower force than stainless steel (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Attar and co-workers: overturning breaks hand files, and .06 taper files resist fracture less than .04 (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025) [SRC]. Harty's: curvature angle is a major factor (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**A. Mechanisms (Terauchi and Renton, 2021)**
1. **Cyclic (flexural) fatigue:** repeated tension and compression on the outer side of a file rotating in a curve; fracture without visible warning; mainly curved canals.
2. **Torsional failure:** tip binds while the shank continues to rotate; preceded by unwinding; can occur in straight canals.

**B. Operator and technique factors**
- Inadequate (non-straight-line) access; no glide path.
- Excessive apical force; forcing files; skipping sizes.
- Overuse and reuse; failure to inspect for unwinding (Gopikrishna, 2021).
- Overturning hand files (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025).
- Wrong speed or torque settings; inexperience (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017).
- Dry canal, poor irrigation.

**C. Canal factors:** acute, abrupt or coronal curvature (small radius, large angle); calcified and narrow canals; molars (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017; Terauchi and Renton, 2021).

**D. Instrument factors:** NiTi fractures at lower force than steel; larger size and taper (.06 weaker than .04); design, alloy and manufacturing defects; corrosion by NaOCl (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021; Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025).

## 3. Recognition and prevention

### Explanation

Torabinejad's 6th edition: a shortened file with a blunt tip and loss of patency are the clues; confirm with a radiograph; fatigue life is longer in liquid than in air; reciprocation extends instrument life (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Recognition:** shortened instrument with a blunt end; sudden loss of patency; radiograph (and CBCT) to locate the fragment; inform the patient and document (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021).

**Prevention:** straight-line access; glide path with hand files before rotary; manufacturer's speed and torque; light pressure, never force; work in a wet canal (fatigue life is longer in liquid); reciprocation in severe curves; limited use and discard of NiTi, single use (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021; Gopikrishna, 2021); inspect files.

## 4. Management at different levels

### Explanation

Cohen's Ch.20: orthograde mechanical (extractors, loops, post-removal systems, ultrasonics, laser) or chemical approaches, or surgery (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]. Torabinejad's 6th edition: remove, bypass, or prepare and fill to the fragment (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Ingle's: removable if one-third can be exposed (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019) [SRC]. Cohen's Ch.20: success is high before the curve, moderate at it and low beyond it; overall 33% to 95%; limit attempts to 45-60 minutes (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]. Attar and co-workers: no removal attempt in the apical third (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025) [SRC]. Ingle's ultrasonic technique: modified Gates-Glidden (cut at its widest diameter, 300 rpm) for a staging platform; ultrasonics dry, counterclockwise (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019) [SRC]. Torabinejad's 6th edition (Terauchi technique): trough on the inner wall, more than 180 degrees around; no staging platform beyond the curve (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Cohen's Ch.20: NiTi fragments fracture again under ultrasonics, and fragments can be pushed into bone (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Options:** (1) remove, (2) bypass, (3) prepare and fill to the fragment (retain), (4) surgery (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021). Mechanical, chemical or surgical (Terauchi and Renton, 2021).

**Decision factors:** level, length and diameter of the fragment; curvature; root thickness; instrument material; stage of preparation; periapical status. Removable if one-third (2-3 mm) of the fragment can be exposed (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019). Success: high before the curve, moderate at it, low beyond it; overall 33-95%; stop after 45-60 minutes (Terauchi and Renton, 2021).

| Level | Management |
|---|---|
| **Pulp chamber / orifice (head visible)** | Grasp with Steiglitz forceps, haemostat or modified needle holder (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) |
| **Coronal third (straight part)** | Straight-line access; staging platform with modified Gates-Glidden (cut at its widest diameter, about 300 rpm); ultrasonic tip worked dry and counterclockwise around the fragment until it loosens; microtube devices (Masserann, IRS, FRS) for larger fragments (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019; Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) |
| **Middle third / at the curve** | As above under the microscope; trough on the inner wall more than 180° around the fragment so it moves coronally (Terauchi technique); loop device; if not removable, bypass with small precurved files, braiding Hedström files to pull it out (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021; Terauchi and Renton, 2021) |
| **Apical third / beyond the curve** | No staging platform (ledge, perforation, dentin loss); attempt bypass; otherwise clean and fill to the fragment and review (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021; Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025) |
| **Beyond the apex / with lesion that persists** | Periapical surgery with root-end filling, intentional replantation, root amputation or hemisection |

**Steps of ultrasonic removal (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021)**
1. Plan with periapical radiographs and CBCT: length of fragment, curvature, wall thickness, lesion.
2. Straight-line coronal access; enlarge the canal to the fragment (Gates-Glidden or NiTi file).
3. Staging platform with a modified Gates-Glidden drill slightly wider than the fragment, at about 300 rpm, to expose its head.
4. Operating microscope; cotton pellets in other orifices.
5. Thin ultrasonic tip at the lowest power, in a dry canal (air from a Stropko syringe), moved counterclockwise around the fragment to trephine dentin and expose 2-3 mm (one-third) of it; in curves, trough on the inner wall.
6. The fragment loosens, unwinds, spins and jumps out; or it is engaged with a microtube or loop.
7. Radiograph to confirm; complete cleaning, shaping and obturation.

**Other methods (Terauchi and Renton, 2021):** chemical dissolution or electrochemical methods (experimental); Canal Finder/EndoPuls vertical-movement systems; laser irradiation; tube extractors bonded with cyanoacrylate.

**Precautions:** cotton pellets over other orifices; lowest ultrasonic power, intermittent use (NiTi re-fractures; heat above 10°C damages the PDL); never push the fragment apically (Terauchi and Renton, 2021; Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021).

## 5. Prognosis

### Explanation

Cohen's Ch.10 and Torabinejad's 6th edition agree: the fragment itself does not cause disease; outcome is affected only when microbial control is compromised or a lesion exists; best when a large instrument separates late near working length (Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Harty's: the alloy does not corrode but blocks disinfection (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. Ingle's: "Failing is not always failure" (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

- The fragment (noncorrodible alloy) does not itself cause failure; infection left beyond it does (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017; Roda, Gettleman and Johnson, 2021).
- Best: vital pulp, no lesion, large instrument separated late near working length. Worst: necrotic, infected canal with a small instrument separated early (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021).
- Bypassing and incorporating the fragment does not worsen prognosis in selected cases (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017).
- Removal that perforates or over-enlarges the root is worse than leaving the fragment.
- Recall; surgery if a lesion appears ("failing is not always failure"; Machtou and Ruddle, 2019).

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS (25 marks: one claim, short)

**"A broken instrument must always be removed."** Ingle's says virtually all can be removed with straight-line access and the microscope (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019) [SRC], but Cohen's Ch.20 gives success from 33% to 95%, low beyond the curve, recommends stopping after 45-60 minutes, and warns that NiTi fragments re-fracture and can be extruded (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]. Since outcome depends on infection rather than the fragment (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC], removal is indicated when it is safe; retention with disinfection and review is a valid alternative. ## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Incidence: hand 0.25%, rotary 1.68-2.4%; NiTi 0.4-23%.
- Cyclic fatigue (curves, no warning) vs torsional (binding, unwinding).
- Options: remove, bypass, retain and fill, surgery.
- One-third exposed = removable; high success before the curve, low beyond.
- Staging platform: modified GG cut at widest diameter; not beyond the curve.
- Ultrasonics dry, counterclockwise, lowest power; 45-60 minute limit.
