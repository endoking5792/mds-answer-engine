## 1. Why curved canals cause mishaps

### Explanation

The AAE explains transportation by "the tendency of files to restore themselves to their original linear shape" (American Association of Endodontists, 2012) [SRC]. Harty's: acutely curved and S-shaped canals stress instruments more, and "the more coronal the position of the curvature", the harder the canal; coronal curves stress the thicker parts of rotary files (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. Ingle's: canals are often more curved than their roots (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019) [SRC]. Torabinejad's 6th edition: over 30 degrees of curvature, more than half of canals were ledged; flexibility reduces transportation, while greater taper reduces flexibility (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC]. Attar and co-workers: holding a rotary file short of length grinds the outer wall as the file tries to straighten (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**Introduction.** Most root canals are curved, often more than their roots suggest, and in two planes (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019). Curvature is the main anatomical cause of instrumentation mishaps.

**Mechanism.** A file bent into a curve tries to return to its straight shape (elastic memory). It therefore cuts more on the **outer wall apically** and the **inner wall coronally** (American Association of Endodontists, 2012). Larger, stiffer and more tapered files resist bending more (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021); holding a rotary file short of length grinds the outer wall (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025).

**Risk factors**
- Degree of curvature: risk rises above 20 degrees; over 50% of canals ledged above 30 degrees (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021). Curvature is commonly measured by Schneider's method [RECALL].
- Position: coronal curves are hardest (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017).
- Small, long canals; S-shaped (double) curves (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).
- Stainless steel files above ISO 20, especially #25-30 (Gopikrishna, 2021; Berutti and Castellucci, 2025).

## 2. Mishaps in curved canals

### Explanation

**Zip and elbow:** Weine and Kelly (1975) introduced "apical zip" (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025) [SRC]; Grossman's calls the elbow the narrowest part of the zipped canal, where obturation stops (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. **Ledge:** always on the outer wall of the curve (Berutti and Castellucci, 2025) [SRC]. **Strip perforation:** Hülsmann and Schäfer attribute it to Hedström files and Gates-Glidden drills, and even more to Peeso reamers, in the curved mesial roots of lower molars (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2009) [SRC]; Grossman's: recognised by bleeding after pulp removal (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. **Separation:** Harty's calls curvature angle a major factor in fracture (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC]; most NiTi fragments are apical, and a staging platform must not be cut beyond the curve (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

**1. Canal transportation** (AAE): removal of canal wall on the outer curve in the apical half. Internal (within the canal) or external (foramen moved) (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**2. Zipping and elbow.** Zip: tear-drop widening of the apical foramen on the outer wall, with reverse apical taper (Weine and Kelly, 1975; Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025). Elbow: the narrowest point of the zipped canal at the curve, coronal to the zip; obturation stops there and the zip is underfilled and leaks (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**3. Ledge:** a step on the outer wall of the curve, short of length; loss of working length (Berutti and Castellucci, 2025).

**4. Artificial canal and apical perforation:** forcing past a ledge bores a new path through the outer wall (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

**5. Strip perforation** (AAE): lateral perforation on the **inner (furcal) wall** of a curved root from over-flaring, typically mesial roots of mandibular molars with Gates-Glidden drills, Peeso reamers or Hedström files (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2009); bleeding after pulp removal (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**6. Instrument separation:** cyclic fatigue from rotation around the curve; torsional failure from binding; curvature angle is a major risk factor; most NiTi fragments lie in the apical third, beyond the curve (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025; Hülsmann and Chong, 2017; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021).

**7. Blockage** from debris packed at the curve.

## 3. Management

### Explanation

Ingle's classifies apical transportation into three types with barrier and surgical options (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019) [SRC]. Ledge bypass is in Q09; strip perforation repair in Q05.

#### ✎ Exam core

| Mishap | Management |
|---|---|
| Transportation / zip | Ingle's Type I (minor): reshape coronal to the foramen, conventional obturation; Type II (moderate, wet canal): MTA apical barrier then backfill; Type III (severe): fill as well as possible and correct surgically, or extract (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019). Warm/thermoplastic obturation (Gopikrishna, 2021). |
| Ledge | Precurved 08-10 file, directional stop, picking motion; precurved .02 rotary at 100 rpm; reduce with short push-pull strokes; else fill to the ledge and review (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021). |
| Strip perforation | Haemostasis, protect canal, MTA repair under the microscope; fill canal apical with MTA (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021). |
| Apical perforation | New working length 1-2 mm short; MTA barrier. |
| Separated instrument | Beyond the curve: do not cut a staging platform; bypass or fill to it and review; surgery if a lesion persists (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021). Before the curve: ultrasonic removal. |

## 4. Prevention: shaping curved canals safely

### Explanation

Harty's: prepare a glide path before engine-driven NiTi; with hand files a reciprocal technique such as balanced force does better than filing; remove the "triangle" of dentin coronally to reduce overall curvature; in S-shaped canals prepare the coronal curve first, then the apical (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. Cohen's Ch.20: glide path, reciprocation and heat-treated NiTi reduce transportation (Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]. Attar and co-workers: "landed" files with radial lands stay centred (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025) [SRC].

#### ✎ Exam core

1. **Assess** curvature on angled radiographs or CBCT; anticipate both planes.
2. **Straight-line access and coronal flaring:** remove the coronal dentin triangle to reduce curvature (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017).
3. **Glide path** to size 15-20 with precurved small files before rotary NiTi (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017; Terauchi and Renton, 2021).
4. **Precurve** stainless steel files to the canal curve (Gopikrishna, 2021).
5. **Balanced force** for hand files rather than filing; never rotate files at length (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017).
6. **Flexible NiTi** with non-cutting tips; heat-treated alloys (controlled memory, Gold) and reciprocation centre better and cause fewer errors (Terauchi and Renton, 2021); landed files stay centred (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025).
7. Avoid large, stiff SS files and high taper apically; keep apical size modest.
8. **Anticurvature filing** away from the furcation; limited Gates-Glidden use in thin mesial roots.
9. **S-shaped canals:** prepare the coronal curve first, then the apical curve (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017).
10. Recapitulate, irrigate, discard fatigued files.

**Conclusion.** Curved canals need flexible instruments, a glide path and passive, controlled motion; most mishaps follow straightening of the canal by stiff instruments.

## CRITICAL EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS (one claim)

**"Rotary NiTi solves the problem of curved canals."** Harty's reports that rotary and reciprocating instruments maintained curvature well in severely curved canals, and Cohen's Ch.20 reports fewer errors with reciprocation and heat-treated alloys (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017; Terauchi and Renton, 2021) [SRC]; but curvature is also the main risk factor for NiTi fracture (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017) [SRC]. NiTi reduces transportation and ledging but not the need for a glide path and controlled use. [Likely]

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Files straighten: outer wall cut apically, inner wall coronally.
- Over 30° curvature: more than half of canals ledged.
- Zip at foramen, elbow at curve, ledge on outer wall, strip perforation on inner wall.
- Ingle's Type I-III transportation management.
- Prevention: glide path, precurving, balanced force, flexible NiTi, coronal curve first in S-canals.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
- Placing the strip perforation on the outer wall.
- Confusing zip with elbow.
- Writing management without prevention for curved canals.

## MNEMONIC
**"Outside apical, Inside coronal"**: where a straightening file cuts in a curve.
