## Explanation

"Iatrogenic" means caused by the operator. Torabinejad's chapter calls these procedural accidents (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC]; Grossman's notes they occur in experienced hands too and groups them by access, shaping and obturation (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Grossman's medicolegal point is that an instrument swallowed without a rubber dam is likely to lead to a lawsuit (Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC]. Management details are in Q01: MTA for perforations (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002; Gopikrishna, 2021) [SRC], precurved No. 10 file for ledges (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002) [SRC], remove/bypass/fill for fragments (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021) [SRC], Ingle's Type I-III for transportation (Machtou and Ruddle, 2019) [SRC].

## ✎ Exam core

**Definition.** Iatrogenic errors (procedural accidents) are unwanted events caused during root canal treatment that may affect prognosis (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); they occur with experienced clinicians as well (Gopikrishna, 2021).

**Classification and management (Gopikrishna, 2021; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021)**

| Stage | Error | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Wrong tooth; missed canal; crown/furcal perforation | Mark tooth before dam; locate canal (microscope); seal perforation with MTA after haemostasis |
| Cleaning and shaping | Blockage; ledge; transportation/zip; strip and apical perforation | Small precurved files; bypass ledge with precurved 10 file; Ingle's Type I-III (reshape, MTA barrier, surgery); MTA repair; new working length 1-2 mm short |
| | Separated instrument | Remove, bypass, or fill to it; surgery if lesion persists |
| | NaOCl extrusion | Saline, analgesics, steroids, cold compresses; antibiotics not routine |
| Obturation | Underfill | Retreat; do not force gutta-percha (root fracture) |
| | Overfill | Remove if sealer unset; observe; apical surgery if failure |
| | Vertical root fracture | Root resection or extraction |
| Post space | Perforation | Remove post, repair; or surgery |
| Any stage | Aspiration/ingestion | Immediate medical referral; 87% swallowed |

*(Walton and Torabinejad, 2002; Machtou and Ruddle, 2019; Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021)*

**Reversible vs irreversible:** blockage and separation are often reversible if detected early; ledge, zip and perforation are irreversible (Attar, Chogle and Hsu, 2025).

**Prevention:** rubber dam (aspiration is a medicolegal liability; Gopikrishna, 2021); straight-line access; accurate working length; glide path; precurved, sequential files; irrigation; magnification and CBCT.

**General management principles:** stop as soon as an error is suspected; confirm with a radiograph and apex locator; correct immediately under magnification; if correction fails, clean and fill to the obstruction and review; refer complex cases to an endodontist (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).

**Prognosis:** depends on how much infected canal is left untreated and on whether a perforation exists; errors in vital, uninfected canals that are corrected early rarely affect outcome (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021).

**Communication:** inform the patient of the error, correction, alternatives and prognosis; document (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002).
