## STUDY MODE

### S1. Definitions [basic science]
Mount defines a lining as a thin layer of neutral material that corrects a deficiency in cavity design or gives thermal protection, with no therapeutic effect expected unless there is exposure; a base is a dentine substitute for major dentine loss and also a way of bonding composite to dentine. The only difference between lining and base is the powder:liquid ratio [SRC:A191].

### S2. Why GIC is used [treatment relevance]
GIC is the only lining and base that bonds to dentine [SRC:B125]. Mount says it gives the most effective long-term seal to dentine, best used as the primary dentine sealant [SRC:A320]. Summitt says its mechanical properties, modulus and support allow it to serve as a base as well as a liner, and that zinc oxide-eugenol and zinc phosphate use has diminished [SRC:C186]. Craig notes a base thicker than 0.5 mm gives thermal protection because its conductivity is similar to enamel and dentine [SRC:B125].

### S3. Uses by restoration [clinical decision-making]
Under amalgam, minimal cavities need no lining, while cavities more than halfway to the pulp get a small amount of low powder:liquid (1.5:1) GIC lining [SRC:A321]. Under composite, GIC is a base in the sandwich technique [SRC:A112]. Under indirect restorations the lining is a convenience for impression and line of withdrawal [SRC:A322]. Over calcium hydroxide or MTA after pulp capping, GIC or resin-modified GIC gives an adhesive acid-resistant cover [SRC:C196] [SRC:C190].

### S4. Limits
Low powder:liquid lining GIC is not strong enough under composite unless the margin is entirely sound enamel; heavier cement (3:1) is used for major defects [SRC:A482]. Resin-modified liners can be cytotoxic in direct pulp contact (see Q8) [SRC:C188]. GIC is a poor base under all-ceramic restorations [SRC:C2158].

## EXAM MODE
*(Answer-book version. Estimated writing time at 22 words per minute: about 16 minutes.)*

### 1. Introduction
Type III glass ionomer cements are lining cements and bases; the difference between the two is only the powder:liquid ratio [SRC:A191] [SRC:A190].

### 2. Definitions
- **Liner:** thin layer, powder:liquid 1.5:1; corrects a deficiency in design, thermal protection, dentine seal [SRC:A191] [SRC:A190].
- **Base:** thicker, powder:liquid 3:1 or more; dentine substitute and a way of bonding composite to dentine [SRC:A191] [SRC:A190].

### 3. Why GIC
- Chemical bond to dentine; most effective long-term seal [SRC:B125] [SRC:A320].
- Fluoride release; radiopaque; thermal properties near enamel and dentine [SRC:B125].
- Strong enough to be a base as well as a liner [SRC:C186].
- Biocompatible when sealed and not in direct pulp contact [SRC:C178].

### 4. Types of material (Sikri's grouping by manufacturer)
| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Weak, low acidity | GC lining cement, Shofu liner [SRC:G10] |
| Stronger, more acidic | Ketac Bond, Shofu base, GC dentin cement [SRC:G10] |
| Strong even in a thin layer (light cure) | Vitrebond [SRC:G10] |
| Strength by Powers (24 hours) | GIC base compressive 70-210 MPa; resin-modified 150-200 MPa [SRC:B424] |

### 5. Uses
- Under amalgam: none in minimal cavities; small 1.5:1 lining if deep [SRC:A321].
- Under composite: base in the sandwich technique, covered by composite [SRC:A112].
- Under indirect restorations: undercut block-out, line of withdrawal, thermal protection [SRC:A322] [SRC:C185].
- After pulp capping with calcium hydroxide or MTA: GIC or resin-modified GIC over it [SRC:C190] [SRC:C196].
- Dentine substitute in large cavities [SRC:A376].

### 6. Technique
1. Isolate; remove caries; condition dentine 10 seconds [SRC:A395].
2. Place lining or base without pressure on dentine; cover all tubules, at least 1 mm for a base under composite [SRC:A385].
3. Let it set; cut back to expose enamel margins if composite follows [SRC:A415].
4. Do not place a calcium hydroxide sub-lining unless there is an exposure [SRC:A464] [SRC:A395].

### 7. Limitations
Low powder:liquid liner too weak under composite unless the margin is sound enamel [SRC:A482]; RMGIC cytotoxic in direct pulp contact [SRC:C188]; poor base under all-ceramic restorations [SRC:C2158].

### 8. Conclusion
GIC is now the standard adhesive liner and base: it bonds, seals and releases fluoride, and is chosen by powder:liquid ratio for the job.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Liner 1.5:1; base 3:1 or more; the only difference is ratio [SRC:A191].
- Only liner or base that bonds to dentine [SRC:B125].
- Base thicker than 0.5 mm gives thermal protection [SRC:B125].
- Not on exposed pulp; calcium hydroxide or MTA first [SRC:C190].
