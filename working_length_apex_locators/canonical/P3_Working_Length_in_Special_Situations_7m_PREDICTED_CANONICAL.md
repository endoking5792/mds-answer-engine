# Working length determination in special situations: curved canals, open apex, resorption, perforation, retreatment, multirooted teeth.

**Marks:** 7 · Short essay (predicted)

No method alone suffices; combine locator and radiograph (Jain, 2016).

| Situation | Problem and management |
|---|---|
| **Curved canals** | Shaping shortens the canal 1-2 mm; verify with a locator before and after (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021). |
| **Open apex** | Films hard to read; locators are of little use (Jain, 2016), problematic in immature roots (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021) but accurate even with incomplete root growth (Attin and Hoer, 2010). Use locator then undistorted radiograph; a size 30 paper point advanced to resistance is an alternative (Jain, 2016). Calcium hydroxide apexification: 2 mm short of the radiographic apex (Gopikrishna, 2021). |
| **Resorption** | Shorten by a further 0.5 mm, 2 mm or more if extensive (Weine, 2004); 1 mm with no loss, 1.5 mm with bone loss, 2 mm with bone and root loss (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); or stop 1-2 mm coronal to the highest sound root end (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011). Locators: unreliable if root end wide open (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011), not significantly affected (Peters et al., 2021). |
| **Perforation** | The locator reads "apex" at once (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012) or short (Nosrat et al., 2021). Apical perforation: new length 1-2 mm short, MTA barrier (Nosrat et al., 2021). |
| **Retreatment** | Old filling alters impedance: use both (Terauchi, Parirokh and Handysides, 2021); remove all gutta-percha first (Sikri, 2019). |
| **Multirooted teeth** | Place files in all canals before one film (Teixeira, Williamson and Shabahang, 2021); two canals in a root need a 20-30 degree angle; use one reference point, the mesiobuccal cusp in molars (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002). Isolate canals (Jain, 2016). |
