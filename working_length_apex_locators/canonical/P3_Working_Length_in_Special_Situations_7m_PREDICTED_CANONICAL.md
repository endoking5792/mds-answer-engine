# Working length determination in special situations: curved canals, open apex, resorption, perforation, retreatment, multirooted teeth.

**Marks:** 7 · Short essay (predicted)

No method alone suffices, so locator and radiograph are combined (Jain, 2016).

| Situation | Problem and management |
|---|---|
| **Curved canals** | Shaping straightens the canal, shortening it 1-2 mm; verify with a locator before and after shaping (Nosrat, Terauchi and Torabinejad, 2021). Flare first (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011). |
| **Open apex** | A wide foramen blurs films and locators read short or unreliably; use locator then undistorted radiograph (Jain, 2016). For apexification stay at least 2 mm short of the radiographic apex (Gopikrishna, 2021). |
| **Resorption** | Shorten by a further 0.5 mm, up to 2 mm if extensive (Weine, 2004); 1 mm with no loss, 1.5 mm with bone loss, 2 mm with bone and root loss (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); or stop 1-2 mm coronal to the most coronal sound root end (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011). Locators: unreliable if root end wide open (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011), not significantly affected (Peters, Peters and Basrani, 2021). |
| **Perforation** | The locator reads "apex" at once (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012) or short of length (Nosrat et al., 2021). Apical perforation: new length 1-2 mm short of it, MTA barrier (Nosrat et al., 2021). |
| **Retreatment** | Old filling alters impedance, so use both (Terauchi, Parirokh and Handysides, 2021); remove all gutta-percha first (Sikri, 2019). |
| **Multirooted teeth** | Place files in all canals before one film (Teixeira, Williamson and Shabahang, 2021); two canals in a root need a 20-30 degree angle; use one reference point, the mesiobuccal cusp in molars (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002). A locator helps where structures overlap and shows canals joining apically (Sikri, 2019); isolate canals (Jain, 2016). |
