# Factors that affect the accuracy of electronic apex locators and how to overcome them.

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay (predicted)

**Answer blueprint:** Introduction → Factors and remedies → Where books differ → Conclusion with verification.

## Introduction

An extra conducting path or a break in the circuit falsifies the impedance reading. A **false positive** ("apex" shown early) comes from a short circuit: metal restorations, gingival overgrowth, parapulpal pins, root fractures, lateral or false canals (Baumann and Beer, 2010). A **false negative** (no signal at the apex) comes from calcified canals or apical blockage by dentine chips (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2009), or secondary or tertiary dentine (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

## Factors and remedies

| Factor | Effect | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| File touching a metal restoration or crown; metal stopper | False short reading (Gopikrishna, 2021) | Remove it; plastic capillary tip (Munce et al, 2019); varnished shaft (Jain, 2016); silicone stopper (Baumann and Beer, 2010) |
| Fluid or saliva in chamber; loose clip | Short circuit | Dry the chamber with a cotton pellet (Baumann and Beer, 2010); check the clip (Weine, 2004) |
| Blood, exudate, remnant pulp | Inaccurate readings (Jain, 2016) | Remove tissue; dry a wet canal, moisten a dry one (Weine, 2004) |
| Open, immature or resorbed apex | Short readings (Jain, 2016); unreliable beyond a size 100 K-file (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012) | Snug-fitting file (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011); other methods (Jain, 2016) |
| Debris, calcification, lost patency | False negative | Patency, recapitulation and irrigation (Jain, 2016) |
| Old gutta-percha | Insulates (Sikri, 2019) | Remove it; a clean file regains accuracy (Roda et al, 2021) |
| Low battery, inexperience | Errors (Jain, 2016; Weine, 2004) | Check battery (below 50%); calibrate if required (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012) |

Pulp vitality (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012) and file alloy (Jain, 2016) do not matter.

## Where books differ

| Issue | Position |
|---|---|
| File size | Unaffected by canal or file size (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012); small files can reduce reliability, but in wide canals work as well as large ones (Jain, 2016) |
| Canal moisture | First two generations failed with conductive fluids (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012); impedance units work fairly well wet or dry (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); fourth needs a relatively dry canal, fifth struggles when dry (Jain, 2016) |
| Preflaring | May increase accuracy (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012); does not seem to affect it (Jain, 2016) |

## Conclusion

Errors are mostly preventable: dry chamber, insulated file, patent canal, snug file. Confirm radiographically (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014); repeat and radiograph if readings vary (Jain, 2016).
