# Electronic apex locators.

**Marks:** 25 · Essay

## Definition and principle

An **apex locator** is "An electronic instrument used to assist in determining the root canal working length or perforation" (American Association of Endodontists, 2012). The body completes the circuit: lip clip, oral mucosa, periodontal ligament, file (Jain, 2016). The resistance between periodontal ligament and oral mucosa is a constant **6.5 kΩ** (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011), though Baumann and Beer (2010) give 6 kΩ. Modern units measure canal impedance, highest at the apical constriction and lowest at the apical foramen (Baumann and Beer, 2010). They measure to the **apical foramen, not the radiographic apex** (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012); the foramen coincides with the anatomical apex in under 50% of roots. Books word the endpoint differently: the narrowest canal (Weine, 2004), or between constriction and foramen (Baumann and Beer, 2010).

## History

Custer proposed electronic measurement in 1918, Suzuki found the 6.5 kΩ constant in 1942 and Sunada brought it to the clinic in 1962 (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012). The Root Canal Meter dates from 1969 (Jain, 2016); Weine (2004) dates Inoue's resistance-type Sono-Explorer to that year. Inoue's change-in-frequency method followed in 1971 and Root ZX in 1992 (Jain, 2016).

## Classification (generations)

| Generation | Principle | Examples | Main limitation (Jain, 2016) |
|---|---|---|---|
| First, resistance | Direct current | Root Canal Meter | Dry canal; irrigants interfere; painful current |
| Second, impedance | Single-frequency alternating current | Sono-Explorer, Endocater | Calibration per tooth; canal free of conductive material |
| Third, frequency-dependent | Two frequencies together; difference greatest apically. Endex subtracts (1 and 5 kHz); Root ZX takes the ratio (8 and 0.4 kHz) (Peters et al, 2021) | Root ZX, Endex | Largely unaffected by electrolytes |
| Fourth | Several frequencies, one at a time; resistance and capacitance compared with stored data | Elements, Raypex 4 | Relatively dry canal needed |
| Fifth | Capacitance and resistance measured separately | Joypex 5 | Difficult in dry canals |
| Sixth, adaptive | Adjusts to canal moisture | DenApex | Clinical accuracy not yet established (Sikri, 2019) |

**Books number the generations differently:**

| Author | Scheme |
|---|---|
| Gopikrishna (2021) | Four: resistance; impedance; multiple-frequency (Endex); ratio (Root ZX) |
| Weine (2004) | Three types: resistance, impedance, frequency |
| Sikri (2019) | Six; the fifth is labelled "blue tooth technology" |
| Sono-Explorer | First generation (Weine, 2004; Sikri's Mark I); second (Jain, 2016; Gopikrishna, 2021; Sikri's Marks II and III) |
| Root ZX | Third (Jain, 2016; Weine, 2004; Sikri, 2019); fourth (Gopikrishna, 2021) |

## Technique

1. Rubber dam, access, estimated length from the pre-operative radiograph; irrigate, dry the chamber with a cotton pellet (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012).
2. Use a snug K-file (Sikri, 2019) with a silicone stopper, as a metal one can short-circuit (Baumann and Beer, 2010).
3. Check the battery and clips (Jain, 2016); advance until the display reads "apex"; passing the foramen changes the audible signal.
4. Record the length from the reference point, confirm radiographically and re-check during preparation (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).

From the "apex" reading: subtract 1 to 2 mm (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002); withdraw about 0.5 mm (Peters and Arias, 2025); work to the zero reading (Gulabivala and Ng, 2014).

## Accuracy

Accuracy within 0.5 mm of the foramen is reported as 90% to 100% (Siqueira et al, 2022). Walton and Torabinejad (2002) give 80% to 95% for impedance locators; Weine (2004) the "low 90 percentiles", within 1 mm of the cementodentinal junction. Root ZX gave 84% alone and 96% with a radiograph (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012). When locators err, the reading tends to be too long (Weine, 2004).

## Comparison with Ingle's radiographic method

In Ingle's method a file set to the pre-operative tooth length is radiographed and adjusted until its tip is 0.5 to 1 mm short of the radiographic apex (Jain, 2016).

| Feature | Electronic apex locator | Ingle's radiographic method |
|---|---|---|
| Endpoint | Apical foramen | Radiographic apex; a two-dimensional image (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012) |
| Accuracy | 90.4% in a randomised trial (Munce et al, 2019) | 82.1% (Munce et al, 2019); minor foramen found correctly 20% (anterior, premolar) and 11% (molar) (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012) |
| Radiation | None; usable in pregnancy (Jain, 2016) | Ionising; technique sensitive (Jain, 2016) |
| Superimposition | Unaffected by a dense zygomatic arch (Jain, 2016) | Zygomatic arch overlies first and second maxillary molar apices in about 20% and 42% (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012) |
| Special situations | Helps with gagging and multirooted teeth; detects perforations, fractures, resorption level (Jain, 2016; Sikri, 2019) | Complicated in multirooted teeth (Jain, 2016) |
| Anatomy | Cannot show canal width, curvature or number (Jain, 2016) | Shows and records root anatomy (Jain, 2016) |

## Limitations and contraindications

- **False readings** with metallic restorations, profuse bleeding, saliva on the file and open apices (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012); dry the chamber or insulate the file with a plastic capillary tip (Munce et al, 2019). **Calcified or blocked canals** and debris (Jain, 2016).
- **Open or immature apices** give short readings (Jain, 2016); a foramen larger than size 100 K-file was unreliable (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012).
- **Retreatment:** gutta-percha insulates (Sikri, 2019); accuracy returns with a clean file (Roda et al, 2021).
- **Pacemakers:** consult the physician (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012); resistance types are contraindicated (Sikri, 2019); studies found no interference, though manufacturers still warn (Munce et al, 2019).
- Cost, learning curve (Jain, 2016); current may be felt (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012).

## Recent advances

- **Locators in endodontic motors:** the Tri Auto ZX auto-reverses at working length (Jain, 2016); modern motors stop or reverse at length (Peters et al, 2021), comparably to separate locators (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2019). Weine (2004) criticises the Tri Auto ZX as errors tend long.

## Combining with radiographs

Guidelines recommend combining both (Jain, 2016); locators add to radiographs and do not replace them (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002). Gopikrishna (2021) recommends the combination but allows the locator alone; Gutmann and Lovdahl (2011) suggest serious consideration of locators as the primary means. If the radiograph shows the locator length well short of the apex, prefer the locator reading; if over 3 mm short, adjust and re-expose (Jain, 2016).

## Conclusion

Apex locators measure to the foramen without radiation, but readings need radiographic confirmation.
