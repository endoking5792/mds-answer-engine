# Radiographic working length determination: Ingle's method, the radiographic apex versus the apical constriction, errors and how to correct them, digital and CBCT measurement.

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay (predicted)

**Answer blueprint:** Ingle's method → Other methods → Radiographic apex versus apical constriction → Errors and corrections → Digital radiography → CBCT → Conclusion.

## Ingle's method

A file is radiographed in the canal; the commonest method (Attin and Hoer, 2010).
1. Measure the tooth on the preoperative film from a stable reference point; subtract 1 mm for distortion (Gopikrishna, 2021).
2. Expose with a stopped file, size 15 or more (Basrani, 2012).
3. Measure file tip to radiographic apex; add the shortfall or subtract the excess (Sikri, 2019).
4. Terminus: 0.5-1.0 mm short (Gopikrishna, 2021), 1.0-1.5 mm (Sikri, 2019) or 1-2 mm (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002). Repeat the film if over 1 mm short (Gopikrishna, 2021), over 2 mm beyond (Basrani, 2012) or the correction exceeds 3 mm (Teixeira, Williamson and Shabahang, 2021).

## Other methods

Coolidge-Kesel and Grossman (Sikri, 2019); Best and Bramante (Jain, 2016); Kuttler's method (Weine, 2004).

## Radiographic apex versus apical constriction

The constriction lies 0-4 mm, average 1 mm, from the apex (Attin and Hoer, 2010); the 1 mm rule is empirical (Teixeira et al., 2021). The radiographic apex is a fictitious structure varying with beam direction (Hülsmann and Schäfer, 2009); films may locate the constriction accurately in only 32% of canals (Barnett, 2017).

## Errors and corrections

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| Elongation, foreshortening | Modified paralleling (Teixeira et al., 2021) |
| Zygoma over molar apices (20%, 42%) | Shift 20-30 degrees; **SLOB rule** (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) |
| Overlapping canals | Mesial angle for premolars, maxillary molars (Teixeira et al., 2021) |
| Slipped stop, transfer error | Stop at right angle (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011); cut cones with a scalpel (Attin and Hoer, 2010) |
| Apical resorption | Subtract a further 0.5 mm (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012) |

## Digital radiography

RVG sensors give immediate, enhanceable, measurable images (Jain, 2016). They match film for root length but not with a size 10 file (Farman et al., 2019), and are not superior for working length (Malkhassian et al., 2012).

## CBCT

CBCT lengths correlated highly with electronic lengths, but more studies are needed before use for working length (Jain, 2016). Periapical imaging remains first choice; metal causes CBCT artefacts (Abramovitch and Fayad, 2021).

## Conclusion

Radiographs locate the constriction poorly; add an apex locator (Munce et al., 2019).
