# Radiographic working length determination: Ingle's method, the radiographic apex versus the apical constriction, errors and how to correct them, digital and CBCT measurement.

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay (predicted)

**Answer blueprint:** Ingle's method → Other methods → Radiographic apex versus apical constriction → Errors and corrections → Digital radiography → CBCT → Conclusion.

## Ingle's method

A file placed in the canal is radiographed; this is the most widely used method (Attin and Hoer, 2010).
1. Measure the tooth on the preoperative film from a stable reference point; subtract at least 1 mm (Gopikrishna, 2021).
2. Insert a stopped file of size 15 or more and expose (Basrani, 2012).
3. Measure file tip to radiographic apex; add the shortfall or subtract the excess (Sikri, 2019).
4. Terminus: 0.5-1.0 mm short (Gopikrishna, 2021), 1.0-1.5 mm (Sikri, 2019) or 1-2 mm (Walton and Torabinejad, 2002). Repeat the film if the change exceeds 1 mm (Gopikrishna, 2021), 2 mm (Basrani, 2012) or 3 mm (Teixeira, Williamson and Shabahang, 2021).

## Other methods

Coolidge-Kesel and Grossman (Sikri, 2019); Best and Bramante (Jain, 2016); Weine's method from Kuttler's data (Weine, 2004).

## Radiographic apex versus apical constriction

The constriction is the narrowest canal part, usually 0.5-1.0 mm short of the foramen (Malkhassian, Plazas and Nahmias, 2012), 0-4 mm from the apex with an average of 1 mm (Attin and Hoer, 2010). The 1 mm rule is therefore empirical (Teixeira et al., 2021): films located the constriction in only 32% of canals (Barnett, 2017).

## Errors and corrections

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| Elongation, foreshortening | Modified paralleling, not bisecting angle (Teixeira et al., 2021) |
| Zygoma over 20% and 42% of molar apices | Shift 20-30 degrees; **SLOB rule** (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011) |
| Overlapping canals | Mesial angle for premolars and maxillary molars (Teixeira et al., 2021) |
| Slipped stop, parallax | Stop at right angle (Gutmann and Lovdahl, 2011); cut cones with a scalpel (Attin and Hoer, 2010) |
| Apical resorption | Subtract a further 0.5 mm (Malkhassian et al., 2012) |

Curved canals lose about 1 mm in preparation; reaffirm the length (Sikri, 2019).

## Digital radiography

RVG and phosphor plates give instant, enhanceable images and on-screen measurement (Jain, 2016). Digital equals film for root length but is inferior with a size 10 file (Farman et al., 2019), and is not superior for working length (Malkhassian et al., 2012).

## CBCT

CBCT canal lengths correlated highly with electronic lengths, but more studies are needed before it is used for working length (Jain, 2016).

## Conclusion

Radiographs show anatomy but locate the constriction poorly; adding an apex locator reduces error (Munce et al., 2019).
