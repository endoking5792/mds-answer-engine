## Evidence and uncertainty notes

- **[RECALL] / unverified:** none stated as fact without a locator.
- **Interpretation / synthesis:** 'Bacterial number and virulence decide the response' compresses the quantitative and qualitative factors of Rôças and Siqueira. The four etiology headings (microbial, chemical, mechanical, psychological) follow the question; the books list the factors differently (Baumgartner and Rosenberg name mechanical, microbial, chemical, immunological, gender and psychological components), so the assignment of individual items to a heading is the drafter's grouping: debris extrusion and over-instrumentation appear under microbial/mechanical because Rôças and Siqueira treat extrusion of infected debris as a microbial mechanism whereas Teixeira and Cheung list over-instrumentation as a cause in its own right. No book states that psychological factors are an independent cause; Baumgartner and Rosenberg list psychological components and anxiety among factors that predispose to pain, and Sikri links dental phobia to flare-ups. The table of treatment factors summarises three positions, without ranking.
- **Conflicting sources:** (1) Incidence: Walton and Ruparel about 3% (controlled prospective studies); Teixeira and Cheung 2-20%, meta-analysis 8.4%; Gopikrishna 1.4-19%; Rôças and Siqueira 2.5-16%, meta-analysis 8.4%; Sikri (Ch.23, not used in the answer) 1-25%. Difference is study design and criteria, as Walton and Ruparel say. (2) Treatment as a cause: Walton and Ruparel find no definitive treatment risk factor; Baumgartner and Rosenberg say clinical studies have not linked incomplete preparation to flare-ups, though they note over-instrumentation and extrusion as causes elsewhere in the chapter; Teixeira and Cheung, Gopikrishna and Rôças and Siqueira list over-instrumentation, extrusion and incomplete preparation as causes. Shown side by side in a table. (3) Antibiotic prophylaxis: Teixeira and Cheung report an earlier study finding fewer flare-ups and later studies finding no effect; Walton and Ruparel, Rôças and Siqueira and Weine (Ch.3) say not useful; Weine allows cover for certain systemic conditions. The answer states 'not useful' with the two sources that say it plainly. (4) Sinus tract: Sikri and Baumgartner and Rosenberg both say a sinus tract protects; no conflict. (5) Terminology: Sikri Ch.23 calls the mid-treatment flare-up a Phoenix abscess; Grossman Ch.5 and Sikri Ch.6 define the Phoenix abscess as an acute exacerbation of an existing chronic lesion; the answer adopts Sikri Ch.23's synonym without ranking.
- **Numerical claims:** 3% (Walton and Ruparel PDF 177); 2-20% and 8.4% (Teixeira and Cheung); 1.4-19% (Gopikrishna Ch.7); 2.5-16% (Rôças and Siqueira); under 20 years (Teixeira and Cheung); 400-600 mg ibuprofen, 24-48 hours (Walton and Ruparel); 30 minutes rubber dam rest (Walton and Ruparel).
