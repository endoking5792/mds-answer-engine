## Evidence and uncertainty notes

- **[RECALL] / unverified:** the one-line conclusion ('no single technique is reliable; sequence primary block, test, supplement') is reasoning drawn from the chapters, not a quoted statement; the sentence that Malamed's presurgical NSAID is for surgery protocol (Table 18-8) rather than block success is interpretation (the answer words it neutrally); the grouping 'Patient preparation / Solutions / Primary / Supplemental / Delivery / Cautions' is a structuring choice.
- **Interpretation / synthesis:** pain control during cavity preparation (Conservative wordings absorbed) is covered only by the cold/EPT test before the procedure and the maxillary/mandibular primary techniques, because the textbooks' restorative-dentistry anaesthesia sections are the same material; the operative texts add nothing usable.
- **Conflicting sources:** (1) Vasoconstrictor in intraosseous injection: Sikri says not advised (rapid uptake, raised heart rate); Cohen reports epinephrine-containing lidocaine works with transient tachycardia and gives plain 3% mepivacaine as the option without tachycardia; Malamed recommends 1:200,000 epinephrine. Shown side by side in Cautions. (2) Bupivacaine duration in a mandibular block: Cohen about 4 hours (twice lidocaine) versus Sikri up to seven hours. (3) Intrapulpal duration: Reader et al 5 to 15 minutes versus Cohen 15 to 20 minutes (not shown in the answer; the answer uses 'short-lived'). (4) Premedication: Reader et al find preoperative analgesics give no significant advantage for block success; Malamed lists presurgical ibuprofen 600 mg in a surgical pain-management protocol (different purpose). (5) Articaine: Sikri Ch.4 says its effect is under an hour and not generally preferred, whereas Cohen and Ingle/Malamed report articaine buccal infiltration after a block as useful; not conflicted in the answer because the answer cites only the supplemental-infiltration data.
