# Discuss the role of antibiotic prescription in endodontic practice.

**Marks:** 20 · Long essay

**Answer blueprint:** Principle → Indications and non-indications → Drugs and doses → Prophylaxis → Resistance and stewardship → Local versus systemic → Prescribing around endodontic surgery → Conclusion

## Principle

Antibiotics are not curative drugs; the immune system cures infection and the drug helps it (Segura-Egea and Martín-González, 2022). They are no substitute for proper endodontic treatment (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019). A necrotic canal has no blood circulation, so a systemic drug cannot reach the canal reservoir; the critical decision is whether to prescribe at all (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021). Drainage improves circulation and drug delivery (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019).

## Indications and non-indications

| Not indicated in a healthy patient | Indicated |
|---|---|
| Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, symptomatic apical periodontitis, necrotic pulp with radiolucency, sinus tract, localised fluctuant swelling (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) | Systemic involvement: fever above 100°F, malaise, lymphadenopathy, trismus (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) |
| After surgery, to prevent flare-ups, after drainage of a localised swelling without cellulitis, fever or lymphadenopathy (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) | Progressive infection (increasing swelling, cellulitis, osteomyelitis) or persistent infection (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) |
| Irreversible pulpitis: no effect on pain in placebo-controlled trials (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) | Immunocompromised patients (Segura-Egea and Martín-González, 2022); replantation of avulsed teeth (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) |

Segura-Egea and Martín-González (2022) set the fever threshold at above 38° C (Hargreaves and Baumgartner, 2002: above 100°F or 38° C) and add a delayed prescription for an immunocompetent patient when drainage is not immediately available. Sikri (2019) tabulates acute apical abscess with a sinus, necrotic pulp and irreversible pulpitis as needing none.

## Drugs and doses

Choice is empirical because culture takes 7 to 14 days (Segura-Egea and Martín-González, 2022). Penicillins are first line, amoxicillin being preferred (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021). Doses as printed (loading, then maintenance):

| Drug | (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) | (Gopikrishna, 2021) |
|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | 1,000 mg, then 500 mg every 8 hours | 1000 mg, then 500 mg every 8 hours, 5–7 days |
| Amoxicillin with clavulanate | 1,000 mg, then 500 mg every 8 hours | 625 mg every 12 hours, 5–7 days |
| Penicillin VK | 1,000 mg, then 500 mg every 4 to 6 hours | not listed |
| Clindamycin (penicillin allergy) | 600 mg, then 300 mg every 6 hours | same, 5–7 days |
| Metronidazole | 1,000 mg, then 500 mg every 6 hours | same, 5–7 days |

Sikri (2019) prints amoxicillin 500 mg thrice a day and calls penicillin the antibiotic of choice. Metronidazole is added to amoxicillin if there is no improvement at 48 hours (Segura-Egea and Martín-González, 2022), not used alone (Sikri, 2019).
Hargreaves and Baumgartner (2002) differ: penicillin VK remains the first choice, 1000 mg then 500 mg every 6 hours for 7 days; metronidazole 500 mg every 6 hours is added if there is no improvement after 72 hours (48 hours above); clindamycin is 300 mg then 150 to 300 mg every 6 hours.

Duration differs: 7 days (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019); 5–7 days (Gopikrishna, 2021); 3 to 4 days (Segura-Egea and Martín-González, 2022); stop 1 or 2 days after resolution (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) against 2 to 3 days after resolution (Hargreaves and Baumgartner, 2002).

## Prophylaxis

Prophylaxis before endodontic treatment has no solid scientific basis; bacteraemia clears within minutes to 1 hour (Segura-Egea and Martín-González, 2022). It is reserved for impaired immunity and endocarditis risk: complex congenital heart defects, prosthetic valve or previous endocarditis (Segura-Egea and Martín-González, 2022). Sikri (2019) prints amoxicillin 2.0 grams one hour before, clindamycin 600 mg if allergic.

## Resistance and stewardship

Therapy is warranted in about 20% of infections but prescribed up to 80% of the time (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021). Beta-lactamase production was found in 36% of black-pigmented Prevotella (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021). Longer courses select more resistant strains (Segura-Egea and Martín-González, 2022). Clindamycin carries a risk of Clostridioides difficile infection (Segura-Egea and Martín-González, 2022).

## Local versus systemic

Systemic drugs fail in the necrotic canal; topical agents act there. Ledermix combines 1.0% triamcinolone with 3.0% demeclocycline (Sikri, 2019). A ciprofloxacin, metronidazole and minocycline dressing disinfects immature necrotic teeth (Segura-Egea and Martín-González, 2022).

## Prescribing around endodontic surgery

Cohen's chapter does not recommend antibiotics after endodontic surgery (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021); Reiser (2017) ties them to signs: temperature above 101°F, heart rate above 90, rapidly progressive swelling or cellulitis. Antibiotics may be needed in the immunosuppressed; for patients on antiresorptives they run from 1 day before to 3 to 7 days after surgery (Reiser, 2017). A patient already on antibiotics receives a different drug (Reiser, 2017).

## Conclusion

Antibiotics are an adjunct for spreading, systemic or persistent infection and the compromised host; drainage and debridement remain the treatment.
