# Failure of local anaesthesia in irreversible pulpitis: causes and supplemental techniques

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay (predicted)

**Answer blueprint:** Introduction → Causes of failure → First-line measures → Supplemental techniques → Conclusion.

## Introduction

In symptomatic irreversible pulpitis the inferior alveolar block succeeded in only 28% of first molars, 25% of second molars and 39% of premolars (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021). Mandibular molars are hardest to anaesthetise (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021).

## Causes of failure

1. **Poor penetration:** the solution may not reach the pulpal sensory nerves, especially in the mandible (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021).
2. **Central core theory:** outer fibres supply molars and inner fibres anterior teeth, so the solution may not reach all nerves (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021).
3. **Acidity of inflamed tissue:** less base form is available to enter the nerve (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021). This does not explain mandibular molar failure, as the injection site is distant (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021); Walton and Torabinejad (2002) add that it has no experimental support.
4. **Hyperalgesia:** nerves from inflamed tissue have altered resting potentials and lowered excitability thresholds (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021), so anaesthetic cannot prevent conduction (Sikri, 2019).
5. **Anxiety** and **inadequate waiting time** (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021).

## First-line measures

Test with cold before access; a response means supplemental anaesthesia is needed, but no response does not ensure it (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021). A second cartridge does not increase success (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021), nor did buffering (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021), though Malamed (2019) reports anecdotal benefit in hot molars.

## Supplemental techniques

| Technique | Success in irreversible pulpitis |
|---|---|
| Buccal articaine infiltration | 42% first molar, 48% second molar, 73% premolar (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021); unpredictable in endodontically involved teeth (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021) |
| Intraosseous | About 90%; 3% mepivacaine 80%, rising to 98% with a second injection (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021) |
| Intraligamentary | 50% to 96%, below intraosseous (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021; Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021) |
| Intraseptal | 29% (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021) |
| Intrapulpal | Last resort, painful, with back pressure (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021) |

Intraosseous, ligament and intrapulpal injections are contraindicated in a symptomatic necrotic tooth with apical abscess (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021). If all fail, place a temporary restoration and refer (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021).

## Conclusion

Test, then supplement in sequence, starting with intraosseous or ligament injection.
