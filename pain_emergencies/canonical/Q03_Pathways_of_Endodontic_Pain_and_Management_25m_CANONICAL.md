# Discuss in detail the pathways of endodontic pain and its management strategies.

**Marks:** 25 · Long essay

## Introduction

Pulpal and periapical inflammation accounts for about 90% of emergency visits with tooth pain as the chief complaint (Ruparel and Khan, 2019). The route is: **transduction** at pulpal nociceptors, **transmission** to the trigeminal brainstem nucleus, **modulation** there, and **perception** in the cortex. Inflammation alters every step; each is a treatment target.

## Pulpal innervation and nociceptors

Sensory supply comes from the trigeminal system, with cell bodies in the trigeminal ganglion (Ruparel and Khan, 2019).

| Feature | Aδ fibres | C fibres |
|---|---|---|
| Myelin | Myelinated | Unmyelinated |
| Pain | Sharp; dentinal sensitivity (Diogenes and Henry, 2012) | Dull, aching; pulpal inflammation (Diogenes and Henry, 2012) |
| Site | Pulp horn densely innervated (Ruparel and Khan, 2019) | Pulp proper (Ruparel and Khan, 2019) |

Both are polymodal nociceptors (Ruparel and Khan, 2019). Released substance P and CGRP mediate vasodilation and vascular permeability (neurogenic inflammation) (Ruparel and Khan, 2019).

## Theories of dentine and pulp pain

- **Hydrodynamic theory**, the most widely accepted: fluid movement in dentinal tubules activates nerve fibres (Ruparel and Khan, 2019), the Aδ fibres at the pulp-dentine junction (Sikri, 2019).
- **Odontoblast transduction theory:** odontoblasts act as sensory receptors (Ruparel and Khan, 2019).
- **Neural theory:** nerves in the tubules are directly activated by stimuli (Ruparel and Khan, 2019).

## Peripheral mechanisms in the inflamed pulp

- Bacterial endotoxin is recognised by receptors on nociceptors, which activates and sensitises them (Diogenes and Henry, 2012). Toll-like receptor activation sensitises TRPV1 (Ruparel and Khan, 2019), a channel that also detects temperatures above 43°C (Ruparel and Khan, 2019).
- Bradykinin, serotonin and substance P sensitise Aδ fibres and lower their threshold (Sikri, 2019). Prostaglandin E2 levels are more than 100-fold higher in irreversible pulpitis (Diogenes and Henry, 2012); the combined inflammatory mediators matter more than any single one (Diogenes and Henry, 2012). Nerve growth factor rises up to eightfold (Diogenes and Henry, 2012).
- Sodium channels Nav1.7 and Nav1.8 are upregulated in painful teeth (Ruparel and Khan, 2019).
- A sensitised nociceptor shows spontaneous depolarisation, a lowered threshold and increased after-discharges (Diogenes and Henry, 2012). Exudate raises tissue pressure and compresses nerve endings (Sikri, 2019).

The result is **hyperalgesia** (increased response to a painful stimulus) and **allodynia** (pain from a stimulus that was not noxious) (Diogenes and Henry, 2012): lingering thermal pain, spontaneous pain, tenderness to tapping.

## Central pathway

The primary afferent enters the brainstem at the pons and ends in the trigeminal sensory nucleus (Diogenes and Henry, 2012), whose subnuclei are oralis, interpolaris and caudalis (Diogenes and Henry, 2012); most nociceptive input reaches nucleus caudalis (Ruparel and Khan, 2019). Central terminals release glutamate and neuropeptides onto second-order neurons (Diogenes and Henry, 2012). The wide dynamic range type responds to tactile and noxious stimuli (Diogenes and Henry, 2012). Output crosses in the trigeminothalamic tract to the thalamus (Diogenes and Henry, 2012); the anterior cingulate cortex carries the affective component (Diogenes and Henry, 2012).

**Modulation:** the periaqueductal gray to rostral ventromedial medulla pathway (Diogenes and Henry, 2012); naloxone increases pain perception, showing endogenous opioid release (Diogenes and Henry, 2012).

**Central sensitisation:** inflammation increases excitability and receptive field of the wide dynamic range neurons (Diogenes and Henry, 2012), establishing central sensitisation (Ruparel and Khan, 2019). It can outlast the peripheral cause: about 11% of patients still have pain seven days after treatment, and preoperative pain predicts postoperative pain (Ruparel and Khan, 2019).

## Referred pain

Pain is felt in a site supplied by a different nerve (Ruparel and Khan, 2019). Central convergence of inputs on the same neurons underlies it (Diogenes and Henry, 2012; Sikri, 2019). Pulpal fibres project to several subnuclei, which may explain why patients cannot localise pulpitic pain (Ruparel and Khan, 2019). The source is found by reproducing the pain there, since stimulating the source elicits it (Ruparel and Khan, 2019), or by blocking it with local anaesthetic (Ruparel and Khan, 2019).

## Where books differ

| Issue | Positions |
|---|---|
| Dull ache | C fibres (Diogenes and Henry, 2012); may also be Aδ fibres that lost their myelin (Ruparel and Khan, 2019) |
| Throbbing | Synchronous firing with the heartbeat (Diogenes and Henry, 2012); no synchrony with arterial pulsation (Ruparel and Khan, 2019) |
| Relief by cold | Nociceptor threshold falls to body temperature, so cooling stops firing (Diogenes and Henry, 2012); gas in the infected pulp contracts (Sikri, 2019) |

## Management strategies

Treatment follows the **three Ds**: diagnosis, definitive dental treatment and drugs (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021; Sikri, 2019). It should remove peripheral mechanisms of hyperalgesia and allodynia (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021).

- **Diagnosis:** exclude referred and non-odontogenic pain.
- **Definitive treatment:** pulpotomy and pulpectomy substantially reduce pain (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021). Incision and drainage evacuates pus and decompresses the tissues (Sikri, 2019); occlusal reduction relieves mechanical stimulation (Sikri, 2019).
- **Local anaesthesia:** the only drugs that prevent impulses reaching the brain (Malamed, 2019). Long-acting agents delay postoperative pain (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021); by block they reduce it for 2 to 7 days (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021).
- **Analgesics:** NSAIDs block cyclooxygenase (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021) and lower periradicular PGE2 (Sikri, 2019). Pretreatment blocks developing hyperalgesia by reducing peripheral input (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021), given 30 minutes before as ibuprofen 400 mg, flurbiprofen 100 mg or acetaminophen 1000 mg (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021); Sikri (2019) gives ibuprofen 600 mg. In a flexible plan, an NSAID with acetaminophen gives nearly twice the response of the NSAID alone (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021); opioids are added only in rare severe pain (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021).
- **Corticosteroids:** intracanal Ledermix (triamcinolone with demeclocycline) gave less posttreatment pain than calcium hydroxide or no dressing (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021).
- **Antibiotics:** only for frank abscess (Sikri, 2019).

## Conclusion

Management therefore removes the cause, blocks the pathway with local anaesthesia, and suppresses sensitisation with NSAIDs and long-acting anaesthetics.
