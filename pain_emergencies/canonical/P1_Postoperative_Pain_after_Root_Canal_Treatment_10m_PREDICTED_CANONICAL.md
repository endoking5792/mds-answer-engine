# Postoperative pain after root canal treatment: causes, prevention and management

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay (predicted)

**Answer blueprint:** Incidence → Causes → Prevention → Management → Persistent pain

## Incidence

Mild soreness is common; severe pain or swelling is a **flare-up**, reported in 2% to 20% of cases (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021). Pain after cleaning and shaping (21.76%) exceeds pain after obturation (5.83%) (Nusstein et al, 2021), which comes mostly in the first 24 hours (Walton and Ruparel, 2021) and subsides in 2–5 days (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## Causes

| Group | Causes |
|---|---|
| Mechanical | Preparation beyond the apical terminus, over-instrumentation, debris pushed periapically, incomplete pulp removal (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) |
| Chemical | Irrigants, medicaments, sealers (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) |
| Obturation | Overextension, the highest incidence of discomfort (Walton and Ruparel, 2021) |
| Occlusal, structural | Hyperocclusion, missed canal, poor coronal seal, fracture (Gopikrishna, 2021) |
| Microbial | Introduced bacteria and products (Nusstein et al, 2021); persistent or secondary infection (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) |
| Patient | Preoperative pain or percussion sensitivity (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) |

Walton and Ruparel (2021) find no definitive treatment risk factors, whereas Teixeira and Cheung (2021) list procedural causes.

## Prevention

- **Debris control:** crown-down instrumentation and negative-pressure irrigation (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021).
- **Long-acting anaesthetic:** analgesia up to 8 to 10 hours after a block (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019); less pain for 2 to 7 days (Nusstein et al, 2021).
- **Analgesic:** ibuprofen 400–600 mg in the chair, then by the clock for 24 to 48 hours (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).
- **Occlusal reduction:** 80% of reduced irreversible-pulpitis teeth had no pain (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019); not beneficial if percussion is normal (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021).
- **Timing:** postpone obturation while acute apical periodontitis persists (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019).
- **Warning:** soreness is likely and settles in a day or two (Walton and Ruparel, 2021); antibiotics are not useful (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).

## Management

1. Reassure; it is the most important step (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).
2. Check the temporary restoration for traumatic occlusion (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).
3. Give analgesics; bupivacaine extends anaesthesia, and corticosteroids reduce inflammation (Walton and Ruparel, 2021). Never leave the tooth open (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).
4. Reassess the treatment if pain persists beyond 2–5 days (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## Persistent pain

Pain beyond 6 months occurs in 5%, from persistent or secondary infection, overfilling, perforation, missed canals, root fracture, nonodontogenic pain or central sensitization; infection needs retreatment or periradicular surgery (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
