# Postoperative pain after root canal treatment: causes, prevention and management

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay (predicted)

**Answer blueprint:** Incidence → Causes → Prevention → Management → Persistent pain

## Incidence

Severe pain or swelling is a **flare-up**, reported in 2% to 20% of cases (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) but 2% to 4% by Hargreaves and Baumgartner (2002), who find about 40% of patients have some pain. Pain after cleaning and shaping (21.76%) exceeds pain after obturation (5.83%) (Nusstein et al, 2021). Pain tends to occur in the first 24 hours (Walton and Ruparel, 2021); discomfort after obturation subsides in 2–5 days (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## Causes

| Group | Causes |
|---|---|
| Mechanical | Preparation beyond the apical terminus, over-instrumentation, debris pushed periapically (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) |
| Chemical | Irrigants, medicaments, sealers (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021) |
| Obturation | Overextension, the highest incidence of discomfort (Walton and Ruparel, 2021) |
| Occlusal, structural | Hyperocclusion, missed canal, poor coronal seal, fracture (Gopikrishna, 2021) |
| Microbial | Introduced bacteria and products (Nusstein et al, 2021); persistent or secondary infection (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022) |
| Patient | Preoperative pain or percussion sensitivity (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019) |

For flare-ups Walton and Ruparel (2021) find no definitive treatment risk factors, whereas Teixeira and Cheung (2021) list procedural causes; pretreatment pain is the best predictor (Hargreaves and Baumgartner, 2002).

## Prevention

- **Debris control:** crown-down instrumentation and negative-pressure irrigation (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021).
- **Long-acting anaesthetic:** analgesia up to 8 to 10 hours after a block (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019).
- **Analgesic:** for patients at risk, ibuprofen 400–600 mg in the chair, then by the clock for 24 to 48 hours (Walton and Ruparel, 2021), or 2 to 3 days (Hargreaves and Baumgartner, 2002).
- **Occlusal reduction:** in irreversible pulpitis 80% of reduced teeth had no pain, about twice the controls (Baumgartner and Rosenberg, 2019); not beneficial if percussion is normal (Teixeira and Cheung, 2021).
- **Antibiotics** are not useful (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).

## Management

1. Reassure, the most important step (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).
2. Check the temporary restoration for traumatic occlusion (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).
3. Give analgesics; bupivacaine extends anaesthesia, and corticosteroids reduce inflammation (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).
4. Reassess the treatment if pain persists beyond 2–5 days (Gopikrishna, 2021).

## Persistent pain

Pain beyond 6 months occurs in 5% in a systematic review (12% in another report), from persistent or secondary infection, overfilling, perforation, missed canals, root fracture, nonodontogenic pain or central sensitization; infection is managed by retreatment or surgery (Siqueira and Rôças, 2022).
