# Emergency treatment for 'Hot Tooth'.

**Marks:** 10 · Short essay

**Answer blueprint:** Definition → Why anaesthesia fails → Emergency management (confirm anaesthesia, supplemental techniques, pulpotomy or pulpectomy) → Premedication and analgesics

## Definition
A hot tooth is a tooth that is difficult to anaesthetise (Gopikrishna, 2021), usually with irreversible pulpitis (Sikri, 2019). Typically a mandibular first molar, the lip is numb yet access is acutely painful (Gopikrishna, 2021); heat or cold gives heightened pain and the tooth is tender to bite (Hülsmann and Chong, 2017).

## Why anaesthesia fails
Neuroinflammation shifts sodium channels on C fibres to tetrodotoxin-resistant forms, five times more resistant to lidocaine (Gopikrishna, 2021); these are sensitized by prostaglandins (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021).

## Emergency management
1. **Reassure** and give the standard block (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).
2. **Test with cold before access;** if it hurts, give a supplemental injection (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021).
3. **Supplemental techniques:**

| Technique | Points |
|---|---|
| Buccal articaine infiltration | 4% articaine 1:100,000, 0.9 to 1.2 mL, is among the most effective (Gopikrishna, 2021); not predictable in endodontically involved teeth (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021) |
| Intraligamentary | 0.2 mL slowly (Gopikrishna, 2021); immediate onset, about 23 minutes (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021) |
| Intraosseous | About 90% success as a supplement (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021) |
| Intrapulpal | When others fail; painful, lasts 15 to 20 minutes (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021) |
| Buffering | Quicker onset (Gopikrishna, 2021), but no better block success (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021) |
| Nitrous oxide 30 to 50% | Raised block success (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021) |

4. **Remove the inflamed pulp.** Complete cleaning and shaping if time permits; otherwise pulpotomy or partial pulpectomy of the largest canal, each over 90% successful in reducing pain. Partial pulpectomy, unlike pulpotomy, upregulates inflammatory mediators in severely inflamed teeth. Irrigate with NaOCl, place a dry cotton pellet and seal (Walton and Ruparel, 2021). With an open apex, pulpotomy with MTA or calcium hydroxide is preferred (Sikri, 2019).

## Premedication and analgesics
- An NSAID (ibuprofen 400 mg) or acetaminophen 1000 mg, 30 minutes before treatment, lessens postoperative pain (Nusstein, Drum and Khan, 2021). Analgesics before the block show no significant advantage for block success (Reader, Nusstein and Drum, 2021).
- Afterwards, 600 mg ibuprofen by the clock, first dose before the anaesthetic wears off; bupivacaine lessens later pain (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).
- Antibiotics are not indicated (Walton and Ruparel, 2021).
